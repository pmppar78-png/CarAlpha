Electric vehicles in Kentucky

For plug-in drivers, Kentucky has two road-funding charges, an annual ownership fee and a tax on public charging. Both came from House Bill 8 in 2022. The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center also lists a charger grant for government agencies, NEVI planning and rules for low-speed vehicles. It doesn't list a state rebate or tax credit for buying an electric car.

The ownership fee

Under KRS 138.475, the county clerk collects an electric vehicle ownership fee at first registration and each annual renewal. The fee applies to any vehicle with plug-in charging capability, whether it runs on electricity alone or combines a gas engine with electric power. Plug-in hybrids pay it, but conventional hybrids that can't plug in don't fall under this definition. Electric motorcycles and scooters have a separate fee.

The base fees in the statute are $120 for an electric vehicle and $60 for an electric motorcycle. The Department of Revenue adjusts them each year on the same schedule as the charging tax, rounding to the nearest dollar, and they can't drop below the base. The AFDC lists the 2025 amounts as $126 and $63. This fee is charged on top of the standard $11.50 registration fee. The Transportation Cabinet's fee page says the money goes to road and bridge work, since motor fuel taxes are a primary source of transportation funding and those revenues fall as fuel use drops.

The charging tax

House Bill 8 also created a tax on electricity used at EV charging stations installed on or after July 1, 2022. Under KRS 138.477, as summarized by the AFDC, charger owners and lessees pay an excise tax. They also pay a surtax if the station is on state property. In 2025, each was $0.032 per kilowatt-hour. The tax is added to the price the station charges. If a station gives electricity away, the operator still owes the tax. Operators report kilowatt-hours and pay monthly. The rate is adjusted each January by the change in the National Highway Construction Cost Index 2.0, capped at 5% up or down.

Charging infrastructure

A 2019 Public Service Commission decision, Case No. 2018-00372, held that an entity that owns or operates an EV charging station isn't a public utility. The Energy and Environment Cabinet offers a 50% cost match for Level 2 and DC fast chargers, funded by Kentucky's share of the Volkswagen Mitigation Trust. Only state and local government agencies are eligible. Kentucky also had to submit an updated EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan under the federal NEVI program each fiscal year from 2022 through 2026. The Cabinet's evcharging.ky.gov site has charging maps and planned routes.

Low-speed vehicles

Under KRS 186.010 and 189.282, a low-speed vehicle has four wheels and is designed to go no faster than 25 mph. It can run on an electric motor, a combustion engine or both. It may be driven on roads posted at 35 mph or less if its speed hasn't been modified. It can cross a faster road only at an intersection with a traffic signal. It must be titled, registered and insured, carry a VIN, and meet federal safety standards at 49 CFR 571.500. The driver needs a valid license.
