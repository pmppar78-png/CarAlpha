Nebraska's insurance requirement

Every vehicle registered in Nebraska needs a current automobile liability policy or another accepted proof of financial responsibility. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-387, you must show proof of insurance to register a vehicle, and the policy dates have to show coverage in effect on and after the registration date. The county treasurer can also check the state's motor vehicle insurance database (§ 60-3,136), which insurers report policy information to. By paying the registration fees, every person named on the registration certifies that coverage will be kept in force while the vehicle is driven and shown on demand (§ 60-390).

Minimum liability limits: 25/50/25

Section 60-310 defines an automobile liability policy as coverage from an insurer authorized in Nebraska with at least:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in an accident.
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in an accident.
  • $25,000 for damage to other people's property in an accident.

The same section bars a policy from excluding, limiting or reducing liability coverage just because the injured person is the named insured or lives in the named insured's household. Section 60-534 sets the same limits for policies used as proof under the Motor Vehicle Safety Responsibility Act, and requires them to cover permitted drivers anywhere in the United States or Canada.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Nebraska requires two more coverages on every liability policy for a vehicle principally garaged in the state. Under § 44-6408, the policy must include:

  • Uninsured motorist coverage of $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury caused by a driver with no insurance.
  • Underinsured motorist coverage at the same $25,000/$50,000 limits for injuries caused by a driver whose coverage is too low.

If you ask in writing, the insurer must sell higher limits under its rating plan, up to $100,000 per person and $300,000 per accident. After you've bought the coverage, the insurer doesn't have to remind you at renewal that higher limits are available, so ask in writing when you want more.

The insurance database

Insurers doing business in Nebraska send policy information to the DMV's database under § 60-3,137. Self-insurers and carriers providing financial responsibility under §§ 75-392 to 75-3,100 are exempt. Under § 60-3,138, the DMV may confirm whether someone has the required coverage under the Uniform Motor Vehicle Records Disclosure Act. It can't release coverage details for resale or solicitation, or as bulk lists. Owners of five or more vehicles can show proof with a fleet certificate covering every vehicle the named insured owns (§ 60-387).

Carrying proof

The Nebraska DMV says the registration certificate and proof of financial responsibility must be in the vehicle at all times. When you register or renew in person or by mail, bring an original insurance document, because photocopies aren't accepted. Online renewals are checked against the insurance database.

If an officer asks for proof and you can't produce it, § 60-3,167 gives you 10 days to show the prosecutor or county attorney that the vehicle was insured when you were stopped. Once you do, the citation is dismissed at no cost to you.

Penalties for driving uninsured

An owner who drives, or lets someone else drive, a vehicle that must be registered in Nebraska without coverage commits a Class II misdemeanor under § 60-3,167. Section 28-106 sets the maximum for that class at six months in jail, a $1,000 fine, or both. The owner is presumed to know when someone else is driving the vehicle. On conviction, the DMV also suspends three things until you file proof of financial responsibility and pay reinstatement fees:

  • Your driver's license.
  • The vehicle's registration certificate.
  • The vehicle's license plates.

You then have to keep proof of financial responsibility on file for three continuous years. Under § 60-528, that proof can be a certificate of insurance (the SR-22), a bond, a deposit of money or securities, or a certificate of self-insurance. Section 60-505.02 sets a $50 fee to reinstate a suspended license and a separate $50 fee to reinstate a suspended registration. If you later show the DMV that coverage was in force when you were cited, it reinstates your license, registration and plates at no cost and rescinds the filing order.

Average premiums

According to the 2024 Auto Insurance Database supplement from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, Nebraska's average combined premium for liability, collision and comprehensive was $1,345.35, compared with $956.86 in 2020. The NAIC's average expenditure for the state was $1,108.40. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 9.5% of Nebraska drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 42nd. Your own rate depends on your driving record, vehicle, location and coverage choices, so get quotes from more than one licensed insurer.
