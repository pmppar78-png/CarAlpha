Annual EV decal fee

Wyoming charges plug-in vehicles an annual decal fee on top of registration. Under Wyo. Stat. § 31-3-102(a)(xxiii) and (xxiv), the decal includes the bucking horse and rider emblem and costs:

  • $100 a year for an all-electric vehicle, as defined in § 39-17-301(a)(l)
  • $50 a year for a plug-in hybrid electric vehicle, as defined in § 39-17-301(a)(xxxviii)

The Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists the EV decal fee as $200 and says multipurpose vehicles and motorcycles are exempt, citing House Bill 166 of 2019. The amounts here come from the current statute text published by the Legislature. The decal is in addition to the county and state registration fees, including the $30 state fee for a passenger car.

Alternative fuel license tax

According to AFDC, starting in January 2026 Wyoming collects a license tax of $0.24 per gasoline or diesel gallon equivalent on all alternative fuel used, sold or distributed in the state, under §§ 39-17-104, 39-17-204, 39-17-303 and 39-17-304. Electricity is one of those fuels, and one gasoline gallon equivalent of electricity is 33.56 kilowatt-hours. Compressed and liquefied natural gas, propane and renewable diesel are also taxed.

Section 39-17-301 defines alternative fuels to include methanol, ethanol and blends of at least 85% alcohol, natural gas, propane, coal-derived liquid fuels, hydrogen, electricity, pure biodiesel, renewable diesel, other biologically derived fuels and P-series fuels.

Alternative fuel suppliers, refiners, distributors, terminal operators, importers and exporters need a $25 annual license from WYDOT under § 39-17-306. They also have to report alternative fuel gallons sold in Wyoming to WYDOT each month under § 39-17-307. Fuel exported by a licensed exporter is exempt from the license tax, and refunds have to be requested within a year of purchase.

Charging stations

Under § 37-1-101, as summarized by AFDC, an entity that owns, operates, leases or controls EV charging stations isn't a public utility. Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, WYDOT had to submit an annually updated EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026, describing how it intended to distribute NEVI funds.

Fleet replacement grants

The Wyoming Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) offers city and county governments grants of up to 40 percent of the purchase price to replace medium- and heavy-duty trucks and transit buses with new diesel or alternative fuel vehicles, EVs included. A separate DEQ program covers up to 25 percent of the price of a new bus that replaces a diesel school bus early. Both programs use Wyoming's share of the Volkswagen Environmental Mitigation Trust and Diesel Emissions Reduction Act funding.
