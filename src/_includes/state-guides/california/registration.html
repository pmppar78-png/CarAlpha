Deadlines

  • Moving in: if you bring a car that was registered in another state or country, you have to register it within 20 days of becoming a resident or bringing the car into California, according to the DMV's new-resident guide. The DMV treats you as a resident if you intend to live in California six months or more in a year. Paying resident tuition or claiming residency for any benefit counts too.
  • Private-party purchase: the buyer has 10 days to transfer ownership, and the seller has 5 days to report the sale to the DMV (DMV).
  • Transfer fees are due within 10 days of the sale. A late penalty applies after 30 days.
  • Renewals have no grace period. The DMV mails a notice about 60 days before expiration. If it doesn't arrive, use the online fee calculator or call the DMV. Don't just wait for it.

What registration costs

A California registration bill is a stack of separate charges, listed on the DMV's registration fees page:

  • Registration fee: $76. This includes a $3 alternative fuel and technology fee.
  • CHP fee: $34.
  • Transportation Improvement Fee, based on the vehicle's value: $33 under $5,000, $66 for $5,000 to $24,999, $132 for $25,000 to $34,999, $198 for $35,000 to $59,999, and $231 at $60,000 and up.
  • Vehicle License Fee (VLF): 0.65 percent of the purchase price or value. It drops over the first 11 renewal years, or until the car changes hands. It's deductible on your income taxes, because it takes the place of property tax on the car.
  • County and district fees, which vary.
  • Road Improvement Fee: $121 on model-year 2020 and newer zero-emission vehicles. See the California EV page.

On a $30,000 car, the VLF alone is about $195 in the first year, which is why California renewals often run several hundred dollars. Some owners don't pay the VLF at all: disabled veterans rated 100 percent service-connected, nonresident military stationed in California (and nonresident spouses), California and federally recognized Native American tribes and tribal members, and specialized transportation vehicles.

Other common fees include $15 for a title transfer, $28 for a title-only application, $28 each for replacement plates, stickers, or a registration card, and $28 to file Planned Non-Operation status for a car you're keeping off the road.

Late penalties

Penalties grow quickly. They combine a percentage of the VLF (and weight fee, if any) with flat registration and CHP late fees:

  • 1 to 10 days late: 10 percent, plus $10 and $10
  • 11 to 30 days: 20 percent, plus $15 and $15
  • 31 days to one year: 60 percent, plus $30 and $30
  • more than one year: 80 percent, plus $50 and $50
  • more than two years: 160 percent, plus $100 and $100

A car that has never been registered in California pays 40 percent of the VLF if it's up to a year late, 80 percent at one to two years, and 160 percent after that. That matters for new residents who miss the 20-day window. You can still file for Planned Non-Operation up to 90 days after expiration if the car wasn't driven at all, but the late penalties are still due.

Use tax

When you buy a car, you pay use tax on the price at registration (or the dealer collects it). The rate is 7.25 to 10.25 percent, depending on the city and county where the new owner lives. California dealers also have to show you a National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) report.

Smog checks

California's smog program applies at registration, at renewal in listed counties (the DMV lists 34 counties, among them Los Angeles, Orange, Sacramento, Alameda, Santa Clara, Fresno, and Ventura), and when ownership changes. You don't need a check if your car:

  • is fully electric
  • is gasoline-powered and model-year 1975 or older
  • is diesel and model-year 1997 or older, or over 14,000 pounds
  • runs on natural gas and weighs over 14,000 pounds
  • is a gas car less than eight model years old. Instead, you pay a $20 smog abatement fee.

Sellers have to give the buyer a valid smog certificate. If the car is less than four years old, the buyer pays an $8 smog transfer fee instead. If your renewal notice sends you to a STAR station, use one of the Bureau of Automotive Repair's listed stations. If the car fails, the BAR Referee Program and Consumer Assistance Program can help. Active-duty military and veterans don't get a smog exemption. Bringing a car in from out of state? It has to be certified to meet California's smog rules before it can be registered here.

What to bring

For a private-party purchase, bring the title signed by the seller on line 1. If the title has no space for mileage, add a REG 262 odometer form signed by both parties. You'll also need a bill of sale if the seller isn't the person named on the title, a smog certificate, and the fees and use tax. For a lost title, use form REG 227. Many dealers handle title and temporary registration for you.
