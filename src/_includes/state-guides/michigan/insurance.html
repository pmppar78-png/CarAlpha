Three required coverages

Michigan's Department of Insurance and Financial Services (DIFS) lists three coverages that every vehicle's policy must carry:

  • Personal injury protection (PIP). The no-fault coverage pays allowable expenses for your medical care, recovery and rehabilitation after an auto accident. It also includes wage loss, replacement services, and funeral and burial benefits.
  • Property protection insurance (PPI). It pays up to $1 million for damage your car does in Michigan to other people's property, such as buildings and fences, and to another person's properly parked vehicle. It doesn't pay for any other damage to cars.
  • Residual bodily injury and property damage liability (BI/PD). It covers you when you're legally responsible for someone's death, injury or damaged property. That includes pain and suffering, medical costs beyond the injured person's own policy limits, and crashes outside Michigan or with an out-of-state driver.

Liability limits: 250/500 by default, 50/100 at the lowest

For policies issued or renewed after July 1, 2020, the default BI/PD limits are $250,000 per person and $500,000 per accident, plus $10,000 for property damage in another state. You can choose lower limits, down to $50,000 per person and $100,000 per accident, by signing the DIFS Choice of Bodily Injury Liability Coverage Limits form. MCL 500.3009(5) requires insurers to use that form. DIFS Bulletin 2025-07-INS says insurers must also show, at application and renewal, every bodily injury option they sell and its price, including options beyond the two levels named on the form.

Six PIP medical choices

When you buy or renew a policy, you pick the most your insurer will pay per person, per accident, for PIP medical expenses:

  1. Unlimited coverage.
  2. $500,000.
  3. $250,000.
  4. $250,000 with exclusions. A named insured who has qualified health coverage other than Medicare, and household members with health coverage that covers auto injuries, can be excluded.
  5. $50,000. This is available if the named insured is on Medicaid and everyone else in the household has other qualifying coverage.
  6. No PIP medical coverage. This is available if the named insured has Medicare Parts A and B and household members are covered elsewhere.

Options 1 through 3 are open to everyone. Options 4 through 6 require proof. If you don't choose, don't send the documentation, or pick an option you don't qualify for, you get the default, which is unlimited PIP medical, and you're charged its premium. Wage loss, replacement services and funeral benefits stay in the policy even if you limit or opt out of PIP medical. DIFS says a qualified health plan counts for an opt-out only if its deductible is no more than a set amount, which its February 2025 bulletin put at $6,579.

Repairs to your own car

No-fault coverage doesn't pay to fix your car after a crash, except when it was properly parked and the other driver's PPI applies. Collision and comprehensive are optional. DIFS describes three collision types:

  • Broad form pays regardless of fault, and the deductible applies only when you're substantially (more than 50%) at fault.
  • Standard pays regardless of fault, and the deductible always applies.
  • Limited pays only when you're not substantially at fault.

Under the "mini-tort" rule, a driver 50% or more at fault can be sued for up to $3,000 of the other driver's uncovered vehicle damage, such as a collision deductible. That cap applies to accidents after July 1, 2020. Limited property damage liability coverage, sold as an option, pays these claims for you.

Driving without coverage

Under MCL 500.3102, an owner or registrant who drives, or lets someone drive, an uninsured vehicle on a public highway commits a misdemeanor. So does a driver who knows the owner has no coverage. The penalty is a fine of $200 to $500, up to a year in jail, or both. If you can't show proof of insurance for the date a citation is issued, the court may presume the vehicle was uninsured unless you prove otherwise. A nonresident whose vehicle isn't registered in Michigan must maintain no-fault security to drive it here more than 30 days in a calendar year. You also need proof of a valid Michigan no-fault policy to register a vehicle with the Secretary of State.

Thinking through the choices

Lower PIP medical limits reduce that part of your premium, but the insurer pays nothing above the limit you picked. Check what your health plan covers for auto injuries, including deductibles and network rules, before giving up coverage. If you lower your BI limits, remember that the liability coverage is what pays when someone you injure sues for amounts their own insurance doesn't cover.

Average premiums

The NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement lists Michigan's combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,791.22, up from $1,535.56 in 2020. The average expenditure was $1,630.72. The Insurance Research Council estimates that 22.3% of Michigan drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 4th.
