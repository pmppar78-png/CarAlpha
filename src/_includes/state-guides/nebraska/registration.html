When and where to register

Nebraska registers vehicles in the county where the vehicle has situs, which the Nebraska DMV describes as the county where it's kept most of the time. The DMV says a newly purchased vehicle must be registered, and sales tax paid, within 30 days of the purchase date. Registration goes through the County Treasurer, who collects the taxes and fees as the DMV's agent (§ 60-3,141). A Nebraska title must be issued first unless you're a nonresident or the vehicle is exempt from titling.

For a first registration, in person or by mail, bring:

  • An original proof of insurance (photocopies aren't accepted).
  • Proof that sales or excise tax was paid.
  • A signed copy of the lease, if the vehicle is leased.

Renewals can be done online, by mail or in person. Online renewals check your coverage through the insurance database. Plates, the registration certificate and validation tabs are mailed if you don't renew in person.

Registration periods

Registrations are staggered by month, and your expiration date is set by the month you bought the vehicle. Owners of two or more vehicles can choose to put them all on a January–December calendar year, or on one annual period starting in a month they choose. The registration certificate doesn't have to be displayed, but it must be kept in the vehicle.

What you pay

A Nebraska registration bill has four main parts: the motor vehicle tax, the motor vehicle fee, the registration fee, and several small fixed fees. The DMV's online Motor Vehicle Tax Estimator gives an estimate, and some localities add their own fees and taxes.

Motor vehicle tax

The motor vehicle tax is charged at the first registration and every year after until the vehicle is 14 years old. It's based on the manufacturer's suggested retail price when new, which never changes. Under § 60-3,187, a base tax is set by MSRP band. It starts at $25 for vehicles under $4,000, rises by $40 for each $2,000 step from $10,000 up, and tops out at $1,900 for $100,000 and over. A share of the base tax is charged each year: 100% in the first year, then 90%, 80%, 70%, 60%, 51%, 42%, 33% and 24%. It's 15% in years ten and eleven, 7% in years twelve and thirteen, and nothing after that.

A car with a $40,000 MSRP has a $700 base tax. That works out to $700 in its first year, $630 in its second and $105 in its tenth. The money is split among the county, schools and cities in the vehicle's tax district.

Motor vehicle fee

Under § 60-3,190, the motor vehicle fee goes to county and city roads. For automobiles, the base fee is $5 under $20,000 MSRP, $20 from $20,000 to $39,999, and $30 at $40,000 or more. Except for vehicles under $20,000, the full base fee applies in years one through five, 70% in years six through ten and 35% after that.

Registration fee and fixed charges

  • Registration fee: $15 for passenger and leased vehicles (§ 60-3,143).
  • Fixed fees on each registration: $2 for the DMV cash fund, $1.50 for the State Recreation Road Fund, and 50 cents for emergency medical services (§ 60-3,156).
  • County fee: $1.50 for residents.
  • Plates: $4.10 per plate on the DMV's fee page. Section 60-3,102 caps the plate fee at $8.
  • Replacements: $1 for a registration certificate and $2.50 for decals.

Electric vehicles pay a $150 alternative fuel fee and plug-in hybrids $75 at each registration (§ 60-3,191). See the Nebraska EV guide for details.

Title and sales tax

An original certificate of title issued by a county costs $10 under § 60-154. Nebraska's state sales tax on vehicles is 5.5%, and local governments can add up to 2.5% more. The DMV's 2025 revenue summary reports $407,557,351 in total motor vehicle sales tax that year. It also counted about 2,520,030 registered vehicles, of which about 1,053,810 were 14 or more years old and paid no motor vehicle tax.

Tax exemptions

Section 60-3,185 exempts certain vehicles from the motor vehicle tax and fee:

  • One personal vehicle of a qualifying disabled or blind veteran.
  • Vehicles owned by enrolled tribal members.
  • Vehicles of nonresident service members stationed in Nebraska, and their spouses.
  • Government vehicles.
  • Vehicles used only by qualifying charitable, religious or educational organizations.

Veterans and organizations apply to the County Treasurer from 15 days before to 30 days after the registration date and renew each year.
