What Florida requires

$10,000 PIP + $10,000 PDL: personal injury protection of $10,000 and property damage liability of $10,000. Bodily injury liability isn't required to register most private cars.

Florida is a no-fault state, so the minimum policy is built around your own coverage. According to the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), you must show proof of personal injury protection (PIP) and property damage liability (PDL) before you can register any vehicle with four or more wheels. The policy has to come from a company licensed in Florida. If you're moving in, your agent can switch your current coverage to a Florida policy. Taxis are different: they need $125,000/$250,000 bodily injury and $50,000 property damage.

Coverage must be continuous for the whole registration period, even if the car is parked, broken down or out of state. Members of the military stationed elsewhere may be exempt. A nonresident's car that has been in Florida for more than 90 of the last 365 days also needs Florida security under § 627.733.

What PIP pays

Under § 627.736, PIP covers you, relatives in your household, passengers, people driving your car, and pedestrians your car hits, whoever caused the crash. The $10,000 limit covers:

  • Medical benefits: 80% of reasonable, medically necessary expenses.
  • Disability benefits: 60% of lost gross income and earning capacity, plus the cost of hiring help for household work you can't do. These must be paid at least every two weeks.
  • Death benefits: $5,000 per person, paid on top of the $10,000.

Two rules can reduce or eliminate what you get. First, you must get initial treatment within 14 days of the crash. Second, if a doctor or other listed provider decides you didn't have an emergency medical condition, medical benefits are capped at $2,500 instead of the full $10,000. PIP also doesn't pay for massage therapy or acupuncture. See a doctor promptly after any crash, even if you feel fine.

Insurers must offer PIP deductibles of $250, $500 and $1,000 (§ 627.739). You can also exclude the lost-wage benefit, which makes sense if you're retired or don't have earned income. Each choice lowers your premium, and it applies only to you and your relatives in the household. The deductible and the wage exclusion don't reduce what passengers or pedestrians receive.

When bodily injury coverage is required

Although it's not required to register, bodily injury liability becomes mandatory in two situations:

  • To reinstate under the Financial Responsibility Law, you file an SR-22 showing bodily injury and property damage coverage. Under § 324.021, the minimums are $10,000 per person and $20,000 per crash for bodily injury, plus $10,000 for property damage. An at-fault driver who can't pay for the damage may face a civil judgment or have to post a security deposit.
  • After a DUI conviction (including a no-contest plea), § 324.023 requires $100,000/$300,000 bodily injury and $50,000 property damage. These limits are in addition to any other requirement.

Without bodily injury coverage, your own savings are the only thing protecting you if you seriously injure someone. FLHSMV defines it simply as coverage that "pays for injury or death to others."

Uninsured motorist coverage

Under § 627.727, any policy with bodily injury liability must also include uninsured motorist (UM) coverage at the same limits, unless the named insured rejects it in writing or chooses lower limits. The same coverage pays when the at-fault driver's limits are too low to cover your damages, or when their insurer goes broke. Insurers may sell a "non-stacking" version that limits you to the coverage on the car you were in.

Proof of insurance and penalties

Drivers must carry proof of PIP and PDL, and of bodily injury coverage if required, every time they drive. A paper card, an electronic card on your phone, a binder or the policy itself all count (§ 316.646). Showing an officer your phone doesn't give them permission to look at anything else on it. If you're the owner and you're cited, you must show the court that coverage was in force. Otherwise your license and registration will be suspended. Knowingly showing proof of a policy that has lapsed is a first-degree misdemeanor.

If your coverage lapses, FLHSMV can suspend your license and plate for up to three years, with no hardship license available. Reinstatement can cost up to $500. To avoid this, turn in your plate at a tax collector's office or service center before you cancel your policy. Do the same when you move away: don't cancel until the car is registered in your new state.

What Floridians pay

The NAIC's 2024 average premium supplement puts Florida's combined average premium at $2,174.55, compared with $1,469.92 four years earlier. That's an increase of nearly 48%. The average expenditure per insured vehicle was $2,017.97. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 20.6% of Florida drivers were uninsured, the 7th-highest rate in the country. That's a strong reason to keep UM coverage on your policy and leave the rejection form unsigned.

Getting help

The Department of Financial Services' Division of Consumer Services answers insurance questions and handles complaints about insurers. Call (850) 413-3089, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern, Monday through Friday. Questions about suspensions, reinstatement or registration go to FLHSMV.
