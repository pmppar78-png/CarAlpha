Pennsylvania's required coverage

Under 75 Pa.C.S. § 1786, every vehicle of a type that must be registered in Pennsylvania, and that is operated or currently registered, has to be covered by financial responsibility. Section 1702 sets the amounts, and the Pennsylvania Insurance Department's auto insurance guide lists three required coverages:

CoverageMinimum
Bodily injury liability, one person$15,000
Bodily injury liability, one accident$30,000
Property damage liability$5,000
First-party medical benefits$5,000

The guide notes that some companies offer a $35,000 combined single limit that meets the bodily injury and property damage minimums. Under § 1711, the $5,000 medical benefit applies to policies for registered vehicles other than motorcycles, motor-driven cycles, motorized pedalcycles and recreational vehicles not meant for highway use. It pays medical bills for you and others covered by the policy whatever the fault. Insurers must also make a policy with just the minimum coverages available.

Limited tort or full tort

Section 1705 requires insurers to offer a choice between two tort options. With full tort, you and household members covered by the policy keep an unrestricted right to sue for pain and suffering and other noneconomic damages caused by another driver. With limited tort, you can still recover medical and other out-of-pocket costs, but you can't sue for noneconomic loss unless the injury is a "serious injury": one resulting in death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement.

A limited tort policyholder keeps full tort rights when the at-fault driver:

  • is convicted of, or accepts Accelerated Rehabilitative Disposition for, driving under the influence in that accident
  • is driving a vehicle registered in another state
  • intends to injure himself or another person, or
  • hasn't maintained the required financial responsibility.

Full tort rights also remain for claims against businesses that design, make, repair or service vehicles over a defect, and for injuries suffered while riding in a vehicle other than a private passenger vehicle. If a policyholder doesn't answer the insurer's first notice and a second and final notice, the law presumes the full tort option was chosen.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Under § 1731, insurers must offer uninsured motorist (UM) and underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage with every liability policy, but buying it is optional. You reject it by signing the statutory rejection form. Under § 1734, you can ask in writing for UM and UIM limits equal to or lower than your bodily injury limits.

When more than one vehicle is insured, § 1738 stacks UM and UIM limits, so the limit applies separately to each vehicle and the totals add up. You can waive stacking, and the Insurance Department notes that you'll pay less if you do.

Optional coverages

The Insurance Department guide lists optional coverages that include higher medical benefits, income loss, funeral and accidental death benefits, collision and comprehensive. Extraordinary medical benefits pay medical and rehabilitation costs above $100,000, up to $1.1 million. For collision coverage, Pennsylvania law applies a $500 deductible unless you ask for a lower one.

Proof of insurance

You certify that you're insured when you register or renew, and PennDOT refuses registration if you don't or if the certification is false. PennDOT checks coverage through an online verification system and sends a notice if it finds no coverage. You must show proof to PennDOT or a police officer on request, and an insurance ID card on paper or on a phone counts. If an officer views electronic proof, the officer is limited to viewing what's reasonably needed to confirm coverage.

Penalties for driving uninsured

If PennDOT finds a vehicle wasn't insured as required, § 1786(d) has it suspend the registration for three months. If the owner drove or allowed the vehicle to be driven uninsured, the owner's driving privilege is suspended for three months too, and it isn't restored until the restoration fee is paid.

In place of serving the registration suspension, an owner may pay a $500 civil penalty plus the restoration fee and provide proof of insurance, once in a 12-month period. PennDOT's MV-70S lists the registration restoration fee at $119. Driving uninsured is also a summary offense under § 1786(f), with a $300 fine.

The registration suspension doesn't apply if you show the lapse lasted less than 31 days and the vehicle wasn't driven, if you're in the armed services and the coverage lapsed during temporary emergency duty while the vehicle sat unused, or if coverage ended with a seasonal registration.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in Pennsylvania was $1,395.99 (Table 5 of the Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement). The Insurance Research Council estimated that 11.0% of Pennsylvania drivers were uninsured, which ranked the state 36th.
