Oklahoma's Compulsory Insurance Law

Under 47 O.S. § 7-601(A), every owner of a motor vehicle registered in Oklahoma, other than a licensed used motor vehicle dealer, must keep security in force at all times for liability for bodily injury, death and property damage arising from the vehicle's ownership, maintenance, operation or use. A person driving a vehicle they don't own must keep their own security in force, unless the owner's coverage already covers them without excluding them. Section 7-601(B) adds that no motor vehicle may be operated in the state without that security.

Service Oklahoma's publication "Requirements for Oklahoma Drivers" lists the minimum liability limits:

CoverageMinimum
Injury or death of one person$25,000
Injury or death per accident$50,000
Property damage$25,000

That's written 25/50/25. These limits pay others when you're at fault. They don't pay for damage to your own car.

Carrying proof

Section 7-601(B)(1) requires proof of security to be in the vehicle at all times. You must show it to a law enforcement officer or a Department of Public Safety representative on request, and after an accident, to anyone affected who asks. That publication calls this document a security verification form and says your insurer, or your non-owner policy, provides it. It suggests writing down the other driver's insurance details, full name, driver license number and date of birth after a collision.

It warns that creating or counterfeiting a security verification form can lead to a felony conviction. Simply possessing a fraudulent one can bring a misdemeanor charge, a fine of up to $250 and a mandatory suspension of your driving privilege and vehicle registration.

Nonresidents are covered by § 7-601(B)(2) and (3). If your vehicle is registered in another state that requires compulsory liability insurance, meeting that state's law counts as meeting Oklahoma's.

Uninsured motorist coverage

Oklahoma's uninsured motorist law is 36 O.S. § 3636. An auto liability policy for a vehicle registered or principally garaged in the state must include uninsured motorist coverage, which protects you when you're hurt by the owner or driver of an uninsured or hit-and-run vehicle. The coverage must be at least the bodily injury minimums, and you can buy higher limits up to your own bodily injury liability limits.

Section 3636(C) widens what counts as "uninsured." It includes a vehicle whose insurer can't pay because it's insolvent, and a vehicle whose liability limits are less than the amount of your claim. That second piece means Oklahoma's UM coverage also works as underinsured coverage.

You can reject the coverage in writing under § 3636(G). The insurer must give you the offer on a separate form, set out in § 3636(H), with four choices:

  • UM limits equal to your bodily injury liability coverage
  • the minimum UM coverage of $25,000 per person and $50,000 per occurrence
  • UM coverage in an amount you write in, or
  • rejection of UM coverage.

Your choice stays valid for the life of the policy and its renewals with the same insurer until you ask in writing to add the coverage. Policies issued, renewed or reinstated after November 1, 2014 can't be stacked unless the insurer expressly provides for it. A policy may also call for arbitration of disputes on demand, and if arbitration doesn't produce an agreement within three months, you can sue the at-fault driver.

When you've reached a tentative settlement for the other driver's liability limits, § 3636(F) requires you to send certified-mail notice to your UM insurer, with documentation of your losses and authorization to get employer and medical reports. The insurer then has 60 days to substitute its own payment for the settlement amount if it wants to keep its right to recover from the at-fault driver.

Penalties for driving uninsured

Under 47 O.S. § 7-606(A)(1), an owner or operator who fails to comply with the Compulsory Insurance Law commits a misdemeanor. A conviction carries:

  • a fine of up to $250
  • up to 30 days in jail, or both, and
  • suspension of your driving privilege under § 7-605.

There's no violation, and no citation, if the officer confirms valid insurance through the online verification system. If the officer has probable cause to believe the vehicle is uninsured, it can be seized and towed. That can't happen if you produce what appears to be a valid security verification form that the officer can't disprove, or if the vehicle has an unexpired temporary plate.

An owner who files an affidavit under § 7-607 that a vehicle won't be driven, and then drives it or lets someone else drive it on public roads, faces a fine of up to $500, up to 30 days in jail or both, plus suspension. Antique and classic automobiles are excepted from that rule.

Under § 7-606(C), proof in court that a current security verification form showing liability coverage was in force at the time of the offense gets the charge dismissed. If you present it by the business day before your first scheduled court date, the dismissal comes without court costs.

Getting your license and plate back

Section 7-605 makes a suspension last until you pay the fees and file proof of insurance. A binder doesn't count; the proof must be an owner's or operator's policy. If you don't surrender your license within 30 days of the notice, you pay an extra $50. If you show the Department that coverage was in effect at the time of the alleged offense, the suspension is vacated without fees.

The Department of Public Safety's seizure notice lists what reinstatement takes:

  1. proof of current insurance in your name
  2. statutory fees of $350, by cashier's check or money order to the Department of Public Safety, plus $275 for each additional case, and
  3. clearing any other outstanding suspensions or revocations.

The same publication says that after a collision involving any injury or property damage of $300 or more, Service Oklahoma will suspend the driver license of an uninsured driver or owner.

What coverage costs

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners' 2024 Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement lists a combined average of $1,453.74 for liability, collision and comprehensive in Oklahoma (Table 5). That figure adds the three coverages together. It isn't the price of a minimum liability policy.

The Insurance Research Council estimated that 12.0% of Oklahoma drivers were uninsured, which put the state 27th in its ranking.
