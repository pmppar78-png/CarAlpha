Mississippi's compulsory liability law

Mississippi has required drivers to carry auto liability insurance since January 1, 2001. Governor Ronnie Musgrove signed House Bill 621 on February 23, 2000, setting up the compulsory system, and drivers must keep proof of coverage in the vehicle. The Mississippi Insurance Department (MID) regulates insurers and handles consumer complaints. The Department of Public Safety's Safety Responsibility Division enforces the compulsory liability law. Mississippi is an at-fault state: the driver who causes a crash is responsible for the damage, and that driver's liability coverage pays the other people's claims.

Minimum liability limits

The minimums went up on January 1, 2006, to:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to one person in a single accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury per accident
  • $25,000 for property damage per accident

These are written as 25/50/25. MID says you can meet the requirement in any of three ways:

  • a liability policy with at least those limits
  • a bond for the same amounts
  • a cash or security deposit equal to the minimums

MID calls a liability policy the most effective way for the vast majority of people to protect themselves. It also points out that 25/50/25 may not be enough at today's medical and repair prices, and that you're personally responsible for anything above your limits if you cause a crash.

Insurance cards and traffic stops

Insurers must give you an insurance card, and it has to be in the vehicle. Since July 1, 2013, under Senate Bill 2593, you can show the card on paper or as an electronic image on a phone or other device. MID notes that an officer can't stop you only to check for an insurance card, but can ask for proof during a stop for any other violation.

Penalties for not having proof

Under Miss. Code Ann. § 63-15-4(4), as quoted by the Department of Public Safety's Driver Service Bureau, failing to have the insurance card in the vehicle or on an electronic device is a misdemeanor. On conviction it carries a $100 fine and suspension of your driving privilege. The suspension lasts one year, or until the owner shows proof of insurance meeting the required limits and pays the fines, assessments and DPS reinstatement fees.

The Driver Service Bureau also handles crash-related suspensions. If insurance isn't listed on a crash report, the owner's license can be suspended. An owner who had coverage at the time but couldn't show it must send proof to the Bureau's Records Division within 60 days. An owner who got coverage after the crash must also send proof within 60 days. If you get a citation for no proof of insurance after a crash, you need to do two things: resolve the citation in court, and send proof of insurance to the Bureau. If you don't do both, your license will be suspended. The Bureau lists an accident suspension reinstatement fee of $100.

Optional coverages

Liability pays only for harm you cause to others. MID describes other coverages you can add:

  • Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM): lets you claim through your own insurer when a driver with no insurance, or too little, hits you.
  • Collision: pays to repair your car after an impact with a vehicle or object.
  • Comprehensive: covers theft, among other losses.
  • Medical payments: pays medical expenses for people hurt in an accident, regardless of fault.

MID adds that lenders usually require collision and comprehensive on financed cars. Raising deductibles lowers those premiums, and owners of older cars may decide to drop them.

Average costs and uninsured drivers

In the NAIC's 2024 Auto Insurance Database supplement, Mississippi's combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive is $1,542.36, up from $1,148.95 in 2020. The average expenditure was $1,314.59. The Insurance Research Council estimated 28.2% of Mississippi drivers were uninsured, which put the state first in its ranking. That figure makes UM/UIM coverage worth a close look here.

Shopping for a policy

MID suggests getting quotes from several agents and companies and tracking them on a worksheet. It also suggests asking about discounts for bundling home and auto, insuring more than one car, good students, drivers over 55 who finish a driving course, and safety or anti-theft equipment. Before you buy a car, check what it costs to insure, and remember that paying premiums on time keeps your coverage from being canceled.

After a crash

MID recommends calling the police, collecting the other drivers' names, addresses, registration and insurance details and license numbers, and noting the time, place, road conditions and damage. Notify your insurer promptly and keep copies of your claim forms. If a claim is denied or only partly paid, the insurer should explain why in writing. If you can't resolve it with the company, you can contact MID for help.
