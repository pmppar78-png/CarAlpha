The TERP light-duty rebate

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) runs the Light-Duty Motor Vehicle Purchase or Lease Incentive Program (LDPLIP) as part of the Texas Emissions Reduction Plan. The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) summarizes the program under Health and Safety Code § 386.154: a battery-electric or hydrogen fuel cell vehicle, or a plug-in hybrid with at least 4 kilowatt-hours of battery capacity, can qualify for up to $2,500. Compressed natural gas and propane vehicles can qualify for up to $5,000. One rebate is available per vehicle, and grants are awarded first come, first served.

TCEQ's program page currently lists the program as closed. The current round covers new vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating of 10,000 pounds or less bought or leased on or after September 1, 2025 and titled and registered in Texas. Applications go through TERP Online, and you must already have the vehicle when you apply.

EV registration fee

Transportation Code § 502.360, added by Senate Bill 505 in 2023, charges an extra fee for an electric vehicle with a gross weight of 10,000 pounds or less that runs only on electricity:

  • $400 for the first registration of a new vehicle
  • $200 at each later registration or renewal

The fee goes to the state highway fund. It doesn't apply to autocycles, mopeds, motorcycles or neighborhood electric vehicles.

Other state rules

  • Electric-only vehicles are exempt from emissions testing, including in Bexar County, where TCEQ schedules testing to begin November 1, 2026. In October 2026, local officials said the EPA had granted a stay of that start.
  • Under Transportation Code § 621.101, EVs and natural gas vehicles may exceed state gross weight limits by up to 2,000 pounds, up to 82,000 pounds total.
  • Under Local Government Code §§ 248.001–248.003, cities, counties and other political subdivisions can't restrict the use, sale or lease of a vehicle or fueling infrastructure based on its fuel source.
  • Neighborhood electric vehicles may use roads posted at 45 mph or less and may not go faster than 35 mph, under §§ 551.301–551.304.

Public charging rules

Under Utilities Code §§ 37.001–37.002, a charging provider whose equipment is used only to charge vehicles isn't treated as a retail electric utility. Inside ERCOT territory, § 42.0104 bars transmission and distribution utilities from directly owning or operating public chargers, though they can build make-ready infrastructure on the utility side of the meter.

Occupations Code Chapter 2311 has the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) set charger standards. Public chargers must show how the charging fee is calculated, the current rate and any surcharges, and must provide an itemized receipt on request. Unsafe chargers have to come off digital maps and be marked "out of order." TDLR registers and inspects chargers and takes complaints.

Utility programs

The AFDC lists utilities and private entities offering incentives in Texas, including AEP Texas, Austin Energy, Bandera Electric Coop, Liberty Utilities, Oncor, SWEPCO and Texas Gas Service. Check your provider's site for current terms.
