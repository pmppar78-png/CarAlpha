When you have to register

NRS 482.385 gives a new Nevada resident 30 days after moving in, or until getting a Nevada driver's license if that comes first, to register every vehicle they own and drive in the state. The same 30-day clock starts when someone takes a job in Nevada or enrolls a child in a Nevada public school. Active-duty service members, out-of-state students and migrant or seasonal farm workers are excepted. A violation is a misdemeanor with a $1,000 fine, although a citation can only be issued if the vehicle is stopped or the driver arrested for something else.

Visitors with a current out-of-state registration can drive in Nevada for up to 30 days in total in a calendar year without registering.

What to bring

The DMV's registration page lists the documents for a private-party or out-of-state purchase:

  • The vehicle title, or a security agreement from a lender. A bill of sale by itself isn't accepted.
  • Nevada Evidence of Insurance from a Nevada-licensed carrier, effective on or before the registration date, in the exact names going on the title.
  • A Nevada emissions inspection report, if the vehicle needs one.
  • The current odometer reading.
  • A Vehicle Inspection Certificate (VP 015) for a vehicle never titled or registered in Nevada.
  • Any tax exemption forms, plus your current registration if you're transferring plates.

Nevada dealers give buyers an Electronic Dealer Report of Sale number that lets them register online. Private-party buyers and new residents may qualify for the DMV's online Rapid Registration. Registrations in a trust's name, personalized plates, mopeds, motor homes and trailers still need an office visit.

Registration fee

Under NRS 482.480 and 482.482, the base fee depends on vehicle class and weight. The DMV's fee page lists $33 for a passenger car, motorcycle, moped or low-speed vehicle, with a $6 safety fee added for motorcycles. Trucks and buses pay $33 under 6,000 pounds, $38 from 6,000 to 8,499 pounds and $48 from 8,500 to 10,000 pounds. Travel trailers pay $27, and other trailers pay $12 or $24 depending on weight.

NRS 482.268 adds a 50-cent plate fee for the Fund for Prison Industries, plus a plate production fee set by DMV regulation.

Governmental Services Tax

The tax portion of the bill is the Governmental Services Tax under NRS Chapter 371, which funds local governments, school districts and the state General Fund. The calculation starts from the vehicle's original MSRP:

  1. The DMV valuation is 35% of MSRP and doesn't change for the life of the vehicle.
  2. That valuation is depreciated by 5% after the first year and 10% a year after that, down to a floor of 15%.
  3. The tax is 4% of the depreciated valuation, with a $16 minimum.

The DMV's example is a two-year-old car with a $20,000 MSRP. Its $7,000 valuation, depreciated 15%, becomes $5,950, so the tax is $238. Clark and Churchill counties add a voter-approved Supplemental GST of 1 cent per dollar for highway construction, $60 in the example, for $298 in total taxes.

County assessors can approve GST relief for some veterans, disabled veterans and surviving spouses, and blind residents. Active-duty members stationed in Nevada who are residents elsewhere are fully exempt, and tribal members living on tribal land can apply with form VP 154. An exemption has to be claimed when you register or renew; the DMV doesn't refund it later.

Title and sales tax

The fee page lists $28.25 for a first-time Nevada title. The DMV title page lists $28.50 for a change of ownership and $20 for converting an out-of-state title with no ownership change. Sales tax on a Nevada dealer sale is paid to the dealer on the purchase price. Private-party sales, family sales and gifts aren't subject to sales tax.

Emissions testing

The DMV requires annual emissions tests for vehicles in the urban areas of Clark and Washoe counties: gasoline vehicles from model year 1968 on and diesels up to 14,000 pounds GVWR. New vehicles are exempt until their fourth registration, and hybrids for five model years. Tests are valid for 90 days, or 180 days for Nevada dealer sales.

Late renewals

Nevada registrations expire on the printed date, and there's no grace period. A late renewal pays the next year in full, prorated fees and taxes for the lapsed period, $6 a month on the past-due registration fee and a 10% penalty on past-due GST. Late fees don't apply to time covered by a valid movement permit.
