New residents: 30 days

Under Va. Code § 46.2-662, a new Virginia resident whose passenger car, pickup, panel truck, moped, autocycle or motorcycle is registered for the current year in another state or country can drive it on those plates for the first 30 days of residency without registering it or paying Virginia fees. After that, a locality may adopt an ordinance imposing a penalty of up to $250 a year for as long as the vehicle stays unregistered.

The DMV's Welcome to Virginia brochure (DMV 140) sets out the steps in order: insure the vehicle, title it, and then register it, all within 30 days of moving. It lists some people who don't have to title or register here, including nonresident active-duty service members and activated or mobilized reserve and National Guard members who are the sole owners of a vehicle titled and registered in another state.

Titling the vehicle

To title a vehicle, the brochure says to bring:

  • A completed Application for Title and Registration (VSA 17A)
  • Proof of address
  • The manufacturer's certificate or statement of origin for a new vehicle, or the title signed over to you for a used one
  • For a vehicle from out of state, the title from the state where it was last titled, or a bill of sale and your current registration if that state didn't issue titles

Section 46.2-627 sets the statutory fee for an original title at $10 and the fee to record a supplemental lien and issue a new title at $6. The DMV Fees chart (DMV 201) lists $15 for an original, substitute, replacement or supplemental lien title, and the brochure also says to pay a $15 titling fee.

Motor vehicle sales and use tax

Virginia levies its motor vehicle sales and use tax under Va. Code § 58.1-2402. Since July 1, 2016, the rate has been 4.15% of the sale price of a vehicle sold in Virginia, and the same rate applies to a vehicle bought elsewhere and used or stored in the state. The DMV lists a minimum charge of $75.

According to the brochure, if you're titling a vehicle in Virginia for the first time and hold a valid assignable title or registration in your name from another state or a branch of the U.S. Armed Forces, you don't pay the tax. If you bought the vehicle in the past 12 months, you'll need proof that you paid sales and use tax elsewhere to get that exemption.

Registration fees

Section 46.2-694 sets the statutory annual fee for a private passenger car at $23 at 4,000 pounds or less and $28 above 4,000 pounds. The DMV 201 chart lists the amounts customers pay:

  • Passenger vehicle, 4,000 pounds or less: $30.75
  • Passenger vehicle, 4,001 to 6,500 pounds: $35.75
  • Pickup truck, 4,001 to 6,500 pounds gross weight: $35.75
  • Pickup truck, 6,501 to 10,000 pounds gross weight: $44.75
  • Motorcycle: $24.75
  • Autocycle: $21.75
  • Moped: $18.25

The chart's notes say three charges can be added. There's $2 for the emissions program if the vehicle is garaged in Arlington, Fairfax, Loudoun, Prince William or Stafford County, or in the cities of Alexandria, Fairfax, Falls Church, Manassas or Manassas Park. Localities in the DMV's Local Vehicle Registration Program may also charge their own local registration fees. A highway use fee may be added as well, and for an electric vehicle the chart lists it at $135.63.

The brochure says you register with a completed Application for Registration (VSA 14) and can choose one or two years, saving $2 with a two-year registration. The DMV 201 chart lists a $10 late fee, $2 for a replacement registration card and $2 to transfer a registration.

Annual safety inspection

Under Va. Code § 46.2-1157, any vehicle registered in Virginia and driven or parked on a highway has to be inspected at an official inspection station. The owner also has to correct any mechanical defects the inspection finds, and each day you're out of compliance counts as a separate offense. The DMV brochure says a vehicle must pass an annual safety inspection and display a valid sticker before it's driven in Virginia. For inspection details and station locations, it points to the Virginia State Police.

Section 46.2-1167 caps the charge at $20 for a passenger vehicle and $12 for a motorcycle or autocycle. No charge is mandatory, and a station can charge $1 to reinspect a rejected vehicle brought back within the rejection sticker's validity period. Under § 46.2-1157(E), an officer can't stop a vehicle for an expired inspection sticker until the first day of the fourth month after it expired.

Emissions inspection in Northern Virginia

The brochure says a vehicle must pass an emissions inspection if it's garaged in the five counties and five cities listed above. Autocycles, motorcycles and mopeds are excluded. New and demonstrator vehicles are exempt for four years, and used vehicles of the current and three previous model years are exempt for up to four years. An emissions certificate is valid for two years, and a vehicle registered here for the first time with a valid certificate from the past 12 months can be titled and registered without a Virginia test.

Under § 46.2-1182, a station in the enhanced program may charge up to $30, and you're entitled to one free retest at the same station within 14 days of a first failure. The DMV brochure, dated July 2024, says the test shouldn't cost more than $28, and it refers questions about emissions stations to the Department of Environmental Quality.
