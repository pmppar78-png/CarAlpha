CHEAPR: Connecticut's point-of-sale rebate

The Connecticut Hydrogen and Electric Automobile Purchase Rebate, known as CHEAPR, is run by the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP) under C.G.S. 22a-202. It's paid for by the Clean Air Act fee charged on new-car sales and registration renewals. The rebate comes off the price at the dealership, so you don't wait for a check. On October 15, 2026, DEEP raised the standard rebate for a new battery-electric car back up to $1,500. It had cut the rebate after a rush of buyers in 2025, ahead of the federal tax credit ending on October 1 of that year.

For purchases or leases on or after October 15, 2026, the rebates are:

  • New battery-electric or fuel-cell vehicle: a $1,500 standard rebate, plus $3,000 more with Rebate+ New, for up to $4,500.
  • New plug-in hybrid: a $500 standard rebate, plus $1,500 with Rebate+ New, for up to $2,000.
  • Used vehicles (Rebate+ Used only): $5,000 for a battery-electric car or $3,000 for a plug-in hybrid.

Your rebate is whatever amount is in effect on the date you buy or lease, even if you were prequalified earlier at a different amount.

Who qualifies and how

  • A new vehicle must have an MSRP of $50,000 or less, appear on CHEAPR's eligible vehicle list, be bought from a licensed Connecticut dealer, and be registered in Connecticut. DEEP also accepts applications for cars bought directly from a manufacturer with no Connecticut dealers, such as Tesla.
  • Leases must run at least 24 months.
  • Dealers don't have to participate, so confirm that yours does and that it has submitted your application. A car that wasn't on the list on the day you bought it can't get a rebate later.
  • The standard rebate is open to any Connecticut resident. Rebate+ requires prequalification first. You qualify if your household income is below 300% of the federal poverty level, you take part in an approved assistance program, or you live in an environmental justice community or a distressed municipality. Once approved, you get a voucher ID for the dealer to enter.
  • Each person can get two CHEAPR incentives, at least 24 months apart. Businesses and fleets aren't eligible yet.
  • Aftermarket conversions, scooters, ATVs, and low-speed vehicles don't qualify.

DEEP warns that the rebate may count as taxable income for federal and state purposes, so check with a tax professional. For questions, contact the program administrator, the Center for Sustainable Energy: 855-704-6350 or cheapr@energycenter.org (CHEAPR FAQ).

Registration and emissions

The DMV's fee schedule shows no separate EV surcharge. A regular electric passenger car pays the same $120 registration, $5 plate, $25 title, and $10 administrative fees as a gas car, plus the parks fee. It's also charged the $15 greenhouse gas fee if it's new with a certificate of origin. But the electric row has no Clean Air Act fee and no $40 emissions exemption fee. That's because cars that run only on electricity are permanently exempt from emissions testing under C.G.S. 14-164c (AFDC). Plug-in hybrids are tested like any other gas car once they're older than four model years.

Charging at home

Renters. Under C.G.S. 47a-13b (AFDC summary), a landlord has to approve a tenant's written request to install a charger in the tenant's assigned parking space. This now applies to every landlord, since the rule's last phase-in date, for owners of fewer than 50 units, was October 1, 2024. You pay for installation, upkeep, repairs, removal, electricity, and any damage. A charger you leave behind when the lease ends becomes part of the property.

Condos. Since October 1, 2022, condominium associations can't ban or restrict charger installation (C.G.S. 47-261g, AFDC). They can set reasonable rules, but those rules can't raise the cost or reduce the charger's performance. You'll need association approval, a licensed contractor, and a certificate of insurance, and you'll pay the electricity and maintenance costs.

Financing. The Connecticut Green Bank's Smart-E loans offer low-interest financing for Level 1 and Level 2 home chargers to people who own and live in the home (AFDC).

Public chargers

Only electric vehicles may park in a space with a public charger. A gas car parked there gets an infraction (C.G.S. 16-19eee and 16-19ggg).
