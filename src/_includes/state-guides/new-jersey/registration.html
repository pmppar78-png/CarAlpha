Passenger registration fees

The New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission sets a passenger car's registration fee by model year and weight. The weight class comes from the manufacturer's statement of origin, or failing that from the renewal application, the Branham Reference Book or the MVC's weight class manual. The current classes on the MVC fee page are:

  • Under 3,500 pounds: $46.50 for a model older than two years, or $59 for a model within two years
  • Over 3,500 pounds: $71.50 for a model older than two years, or $84 for a model within two years
  • A commuter van, defined as a vehicle with at least eight passenger seats used for a daily work commute, pays the same as the over-3,500-pound class

Older models have their own rows. Cars from 1971 through 1979 pay $38.50, $49.50 or $72.50 depending on weight, and 1970 or older models pay $35.50, $44.50 or $65.50. If the shipping weight is disputed or unavailable, N.J.S.A. 39:3-8 lets the MVC require a weigh-in on a certified scale. A wrong weight class, color, door count or model can be corrected at an agency on Form MVS-2, or for weight, by mail to the MVC in Trenton.

Discounts

Owners or lessees who are 65 or older, or who have a disability, pay $7 less in every passenger category. Passenger vehicles and non-commercial trucks registered to recipients of Pharmaceutical Assistance to the Aged and Disabled (PAAD), Supplemental Security Income (SSI) or Lifeline also get discounted fees. To claim one, bring a current PAAD or SSI card, or proof of Lifeline eligibility, in the owner's name.

Zero-emission vehicle fee

Since July 1, 2024, a vehicle certified as a ZEV under the California Air Resources Board standard for its model year pays an extra annual fee on top of the regular registration. The fee was $250 at the start and rises $10 a year for four years. It's due in full at the first registration and at every renewal. A new ZEV gets a four-year registration, and all four years of the added fee are due up front. The MVC's example is a new ZEV registered between July 1, 2024 and June 30, 2025, which pays the fee for 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2027 at once.

Title and transfer costs

  • Title transfer: $60, or $85 with one lien and $110 with two
  • Registration transfer: $4.50, plus any prorated increase if the weight class changes
  • Duplicate registration: $5 for a one-year registration or the last year of a four-year accelerated registration, and $11 during the first three years of a four-year term

The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center summarizes two purchase charges. Under N.J.S.A. 54:32B-3 and 54:32B-8.55, a ZEV sold on or after July 1, 2025 is taxed at 6.625%, after a reduced rate of 3.3125% from October 2024 through June 2025. Under N.J.S.A. 39:3-8.4, a new passenger vehicle rated under 19 mpg, or sold or leased for $45,000 or more, pays an extra fee of 0.4% of the price. A vehicle over $45,000 rated at 40 mpg or better, or certified as a ZEV, is exempt from that fee.

Moving to New Jersey

The MVC's moving page says new residents must transfer their vehicle title and registration within 60 days of the move, or before the out-of-state registration expires if that comes first. Make a New Title or Registration appointment and bring:

  • Proof of identification, usually your driver's license
  • The original title and your insurance information
  • A completed Universal Title Application (Form OS/SS-UTA) and Vehicle Registration Application (Form BA-49)
  • The title fee and registration fee

If the vehicle is financed or leased, first send an Application for the Release of a Title from Lienholder (Form OS/SS-54). The MVC tells you when the title arrives so you can finish. A vehicle owned by a business needs an Entity Identification Number. Sales tax isn't charged if you qualify for an exemption.

Inspections

A passenger vehicle is inspected every two years, and you can go up to two months before the sticker's expiration date. A new car gets a one-time decal good for five years. When you buy from a New Jersey dealer, the dealer applies it. If you buy or lease a new car out of state, the MVC issues a green temporary card (Form SS-19), and you take the car to a state inspection station within 14 days to get the five-year decal.

The MVC's inspection FAQ says gasoline passenger vehicles from model year 1996 on, up to 8,500 pounds GVWR, take the emissions test every two years once they're five model years old. Diesel passenger vehicles from 1997 on are tested on the same schedule. A 100% electric vehicle is on the exempt list, along with motorcycles and historic vehicles 25 or more years old on historic plates. You can use a state inspection station or a licensed Private Inspection Facility, which the FAQ says performs the same inspection for a fee.
