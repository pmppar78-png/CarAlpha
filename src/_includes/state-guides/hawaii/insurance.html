Hawaii's mandatory coverages

20/40/10 plus PIP: $20,000 bodily injury liability per person, $40,000 per accident, $10,000 property damage liability per occurrence, and $10,000 per person in personal injury protection.

The Insurance Division of the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs sets out these minimums. Your car has to be insured for the whole registration period, and a valid Hawaii insurance identification card must be in the vehicle at all times. You can be ticketed and fined for driving without that card. If you drop coverage, you're required to surrender the registration certificate and plates to the county director of finance, and the car can't be driven after that.

What no-fault means in Hawaii

Hawaii is a no-fault state, but only for injuries. After a crash, your own insurer pays medical and rehabilitative bills for you and your passengers up to the PIP limit, whoever caused the accident. In return, you can't sue or be sued over those injuries unless they're serious.

No-fault doesn't apply to vehicles or other property. Damage to cars, fences and buildings is still paid by the at-fault driver, which is where the $10,000 property damage liability minimum comes in. That limit covers very little if you total a newer car. The $20,000 and $40,000 bodily injury limits pay others for serious injuries you cause that fall outside the no-fault system.

Optional coverages to consider

Beyond the mandatory package, the Insurance Division lists these options:

  • Collision and comprehensive, to pay for damage to your own car
  • Uninsured (UM) and underinsured (UIM) motorist coverage
  • Wage loss benefits
  • Alternative care, including naturopathy, acupuncture and faith healing
  • Death benefits ranging from $25,000 to $100,000
  • A $2,000 funeral benefit
  • A PIP deductible or PIP managed care

Your agent or insurer must explain UM and UIM and your right to decline them in writing. If you buy them, each starts at $20,000 per person. UM pays for serious injury or death when the at-fault driver has no insurance or it's a hit-and-run. UIM pays when the at-fault driver doesn't carry enough insurance.

The Insurance Division's buyer's guide notes that declining UM and UIM in writing lowers your premium. Physical damage coverage raises it considerably, and choosing higher deductibles brings that cost back down. Your vehicle's make, model and how easily it's repaired also affect what you pay for physical damage coverage.

Sample premiums from the state

Each year the Insurance Division publishes a premium comparison guide. The 2026 edition uses rates in effect on January 1, 2026, for a 2024 Honda Accord LX driven for pleasure. It prices a policy with $40,000/$80,000 bodily injury, $20,000 property damage, $10,000 PIP, and stacked UM and UIM at $40,000/$80,000, on Oahu, Maui, Kauai and the Big Island.

The gaps between companies are wide. On Oahu, a driver with a clean record was quoted anywhere from $258 to $1,577 a year for the same coverage, and a single speeding conviction raised most companies' prices. The guide also says that driving to work instead of only for pleasure can add 15% to 25%, and that many companies offer renewal discounts after three to five years. Your own rate will be different, but the guide shows how much shopping around can save.

Where you live matters too. Government Employees Insurance Company's clean-record quote, for example, was $304 on Oahu, $281 on Maui, $233 on Kauai and $288 on the Big Island. For the clean-record driver, 33 of the 37 insurer listings in the guide quoted Kauai lowest of the four counties.

Average costs and uninsured drivers

According to the NAIC's auto insurance database report, Hawaii's 2024 average combined premium for liability, collision and comprehensive was $1,051.00, up from $919.40 in 2020. Average expenditure, based on the coverage people actually bought, was $934.69.

The Insurance Research Council estimates that 9.6% of Hawaii drivers are uninsured, ranking the state 41st. Even so, about one driver in ten on the road may have no coverage, so UM coverage is still worth pricing.

If companies turn you down

Insurers can choose whom they cover, but under the state guide they can't refuse you because of race, creed, ethnic background, age, sex, how long you've been driving, your credit bureau rating, marital status, where you live or a physical handicap. Some companies focus on low-risk drivers, some on high-risk drivers, and some insure only members of a group.

If one company declines you, its sales agent must offer to place you in the Hawaii Joint Underwriting Plan. That's a risk pool every motor vehicle insurer in the state has to join, and it covers drivers who can't get a policy elsewhere or who have several accidents or traffic convictions. You can apply through any agent licensed to sell auto insurance in Hawaii.
