Clean Car Income Tax Credit

New Mexico offers a refundable income tax credit for buying or leasing an electric, plug-in hybrid or fuel cell vehicle from a dealer licensed by the Motor Vehicle Division or a dealer on tribal land in the state. It covers purchases and new leases from May 15, 2024 through December 31, 2029. The Energy, Minerals and Natural Resources Department (EMNRD) certifies eligibility, and the Taxation and Revenue Department pays the credit. Eligibility rules

  • A new vehicle's base MSRP, before options, destination charges and taxes, can't exceed $55,000.
  • A used vehicle must have a market value of $25,000 or less and come with a dealer-provided warranty of at least one year covering parts and labor.
  • Leases must run at least three years. EMNRD doesn't accept subsidized leases.
  • A vehicle can earn the credit twice: once when it's sold new and once when it's sold used.
  • Purchases from a private seller don't qualify. EMNRD says Rivian and Slate aren't currently licensed MVD dealers.
  • The buyer can't be claimed as a dependent on another taxpayer's return.

How to claim it

Apply to EMNRD for a certificate of eligibility within one year of the purchase or lease. Then attach the certificate, Form TRD-41419 and Schedule CR to your New Mexico return, either on paper or through the Taxpayer Access Point. You have to claim the credit within three tax years after the end of the year EMNRD certifies it. Any amount above your tax bill is refunded. You can also sell or transfer the credit, for example to a dealer, if you notify the Taxation and Revenue Department within 10 days on Form TRD-41420. EMNRD says it's a tax credit, so you won't get it as an instant rebate at the dealership.

Charger credits

The same program offers a credit for buying and installing charging equipment through 2029: $400 for a Level 2 or other charging unit, and $25,000 for a DC fast charger or a fuel cell charging unit. Level 3 units have to be installed by a licensed electrician and connected to a utility. The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) also lists a Sustainable Building Tax Credit for EV make-ready wiring in existing buildings: $500 for a residence, or $1,000 for an income-eligible resident, and 50% of the cost up to $1,500 for commercial buildings, or 100% up to $3,000 for affordable housing.

Registration fees for EVs

Starting January 1, 2027, § 66-6-6.4 NMSA 1978 adds an annual fee at registration of $70 for electric vehicles and $35 for plug-in hybrids. It rises to $90 and $45 by 2029.

Utility programs and vehicle standards

Under § 62-8-12, public utilities file transportation electrification plans with the Public Regulation Commission when it asks for them. The plans can include charger incentives, EV charging rates and outreach. AFDC reports that New Mexico has adopted California's Advanced Clean Cars II rules starting with model year 2027, which call for zero-emission vehicles to make up 82% of new passenger vehicle sales by 2032. It has also adopted the Advanced Clean Trucks rule. Neighborhood electric vehicles can be driven on roads with speed limits up to 35 mph.
