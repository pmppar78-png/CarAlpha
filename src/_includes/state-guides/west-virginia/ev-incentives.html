Annual alternative fuel vehicle fees

West Virginia adds an annual registration fee for alternative fuel vehicles under W. Va. Code § 17A-10-3c:

  • $200 for a vehicle that runs exclusively on electricity
  • $100 for a vehicle that runs on a combination of electricity and petrochemical fuels, such as a plug-in hybrid
  • $200 for a vehicle fueled with hydrogen or natural gas

These fees are in addition to every other fee in the registration article. For a passenger car, that means the $200 or $100 fee is paid along with the Class A registration fee, which the Division of Motor Vehicles lists as $51.50 a year. The Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists the fee as enacted on June 16, 2017.

Class A vehicles on the optional biennial staggered registration system pay twice the annual registration fees and other fees in the chapter every two years under § 17A-10-3.

Buying an EV

An electric vehicle is titled the same way as any other vehicle. The DMV lists a $15 title fee, a 6 percent titling tax on a net purchase price over $500, and a $10 lien recording fee if there's a lien. To get trade-in credit, the trade-in has to be titled in West Virginia in your name.

Alternative fuel taxes

Alternative fuels are subject to an excise tax of $0.205 per gasoline gallon equivalent, with a variable component equal to 5 percent of the average wholesale price, according to AFDC. The cited sections are W. Va. Code 11-14C-2, 11-14C-5, 11-14C-6a, 11-15A-13a and 11-15-18b.

Charging

Under W. Va. Code 24-2D-1 through 24-2D-3, as summarized by AFDC, the Public Service Commission doesn't have jurisdiction over the sale of alternative fuels by non-utilities. Anyone using movable generators to sell electricity at temporary EV charging locations isn't a public utility. Temporary locations include fairs, festivals and special events, places where the grid has been hit by an emergency or disaster, and places that help with evacuations.

Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, the West Virginia Department of Transportation had to submit an annually updated EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026. West Virginia also joined Maryland, Virginia and the District of Columbia in the Mid-Atlantic Electrification Partnership to support a regional charging network, though AFDC archived that entry on May 1, 2026.

Other rules

Counties that run part of their school bus fleet on CNG, propane or electricity can get a 10 percent transportation allowance from the Department of Education under W. Va. Code 18-9A-7. They get another 5 percent for buses manufactured in West Virginia.

Low-speed vehicles may operate on roads with speed limits up to 25 miles per hour, must meet the federal safety standards in 49 CFR 571.500, and need a licensed driver under W. Va. Code 17A-3-2.
