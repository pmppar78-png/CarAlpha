Moving to the District

All vehicles kept and driven in the District must be registered here. According to the DMV's registration page, new residents have 60 calendar days from the day they start living in DC. You have to convert your out-of-state license to a DC driver license or non-driver ID first. The DMV suggests starting before your old license or registration is close to expiring. Students, diplomats, active-duty military, part-time residents, members of Congress, and presidential appointees who want to keep their home-state license and plates can apply for a reciprocity permit instead.

Before you go, check that you don't owe the District anything. The DMV won't complete most transactions until child support, unpaid tickets, and bounced checks are paid.

What to bring

  • A completed Certificate of Title/Temporary Tag Application, listing every owner on the title.
  • The title itself. The DMV has to receive it before it can register the car. If a lender holds the title, mail the lienholder the DMV's Out-of-State Title Request form and include a copy of your signed loan contract. You can check online whether the title has arrived.
  • Proof of DC insurance. It must show your name, the coverage dates, the VIN, and your District address, and it must be printed for an in-person visit. Your insurer can also fax it to the DMV.
  • A passed DC inspection. Stickers from other jurisdictions aren't accepted.
  • Payment. Since December 15, 2025, card payments carry a 2.5 percent service fee.

If you're buying out a lease, also bring an odometer statement. You can keep the plates from the lease.

Inspection

New registrations get their first inspection at the DMV Inspection Station, 1001 Half Street SW. Cars are taken in arrival order, and passenger vehicles from 1996 or newer can book an appointment. You can drive there on your current out-of-state tags. If they've expired, bring your title and DC insurance to a service center to get a temporary registration. You pay the inspection fee when you register, not at the station.

A passenger vehicle inspection is good for two years. A brand-new passenger car with a manufacturer's certificate of origin automatically gets a four-year sticker when it's registered, so don't bring it in. A fully electric passenger car gets a sticker that never expires. If you're bringing an EV from another state, you still need to visit the station once to get it. Motorcycles don't need inspection. Late inspection fees start as soon as the sticker expires and increase every 30 days, up to $480. For renewals, residents with a 2005-or-newer passenger car that has been inspected at the station before can use the 24-hour self-service emissions kiosks instead. The DMV then mails the new sticker.

Registration fees (effective March 30, 2026)

The District charges annual registration by weight, according to the DMV fee schedule:

  • Up to 3,499 lb: $70
  • 3,500–4,999 lb: $175
  • 5,000–5,999 lb: $300
  • 6,000 lb and up: $550, plus $75 for every 1,000 lb over 10,000
  • A new battery-electric car under 5,000 lb: $40 for its first two years. A battery-electric car under 5,000 lb can also subtract 1,000 lb from its shipping weight when its class is set. Hybrids don't qualify for either benefit.

The heavier classes went up this spring. A 5,500-pound SUV that cost $250 a year before March 30, 2026 now costs $300. Other fees: $52 for a motorcycle, $12 for a tag transfer, and $100 to reinstate a registration. An insurance lapse costs $150 for the first 30 days, then $7 a day, up to $2,500.

Title fee and excise tax

The title fee is $30, plus $20 for each lien recorded. The District charges a one-time excise tax instead of sales tax, and only when ownership changes. If you're a new resident and the car is already titled in your name, you don't pay it. When you do owe it, the rate depends on the vehicle's weight and its city fuel economy (not MPGe). It's applied to the NADA Eastern Region fair market value, and this schedule took effect on February 17, 2025:

  • Under 3,500 lb: 9% at 20 mpg or less, 5% at 21–25, 3.1% at 26–30, 2.2% at 31–39, 1.5% at 40 or more, and 1% for an EV.
  • 3,500–4,999 lb: 10%, 6%, 4.1%, 3.2%, 2.5%, and 2% for an EV.
  • 5,000 lb or more: 11%, 7%, 5.1%, 4.2%, 3.5%, and 3% for an EV.

Electric vehicles used to be exempt and now pay the lowest rate in their weight band. For example, a $40,000 SUV weighing 4,200 lb and rated 22 mpg city owes $2,400. An electric SUV of the same weight and value owes $800. Residents who received the District's Earned Income Tax Credit for the most recent tax year can choose the older flat rates of 6, 7, or 8 percent by weight if those cost less, with documentation from the Office of Tax and Revenue. The DMV's online estimator shows your exact amount.

Renewals

You can renew online or by mail, and you can replace a registration card online or in person. If you won't be driving the car for a long time, the DMV recommends turning in your DC tags so you don't build up insurance lapse fees or tickets.
