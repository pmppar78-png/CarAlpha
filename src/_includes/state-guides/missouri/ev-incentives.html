EVs in Missouri: fees, exemptions and charging rules

The Alternative Fuels Data Center's list of Missouri laws and incentives doesn't include a state rebate or income tax credit for drivers buying an electric vehicle. Instead, EV owners deal with three state rules: a low registration bracket, an annual special fuel decal that replaces the motor fuel tax, and an exemption from emissions testing. The sources here are the Missouri Department of Revenue, state statutes, and the U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center.

The special fuel decal

Instead of paying motor fuel tax at the pump, the owner of a vehicle that runs on electricity, propane or, in limited cases, natural gas must buy a special fuel decal every year before January 31. Plug-in hybrids from model year 2018 and later need one too. Under RSMo § 142.869, the fees rose each year for five years starting January 1, 2022. The Department's fee chart effective January 1, 2026 lists:

  • Passenger vehicles and vehicles of 18,000 lbs or less: $150 for electric and $75 for plug-in hybrid.
  • School buses: $150 for electric and $75 for plug-in hybrid.
  • Local, commercial and transit buses: $300 for electric and $150 for plug-in hybrid.
  • Replacement decal: $8.50.
  • 15-day temporary decal: $8.

A $9 processing fee is added. For comparison, a passenger EV paid $120 in 2024 and $135 in 2025, each with a $6 processing fee.

Conventional gas-electric hybrids that don't plug in are exempt, as are vehicles with historic registration. Decals run from January 1 through December 31 and are prorated monthly for vehicles that start operating after January 31. The decal stays with the vehicle when it's sold, and the new owner completes the transfer section of Form 2300. It goes on the lower right corner of the windshield. The Department mails Form 3021, a notice of the requirement, in the first week of December. You can buy the decal by mail, at any license office, or by calling 573-751-1957, and you'll need Form 2300 and last year's registration receipt.

Registration and emissions

The Department's fee page puts electric vehicles in the lowest passenger registration bracket, with under-12-horsepower and low-speed vehicles. That's $18.25 for one year or $36.50 for two, plus processing. Under RSMo § 643.315, vehicles powered only by electricity are exempt from the St. Louis-area emissions inspection. A safety inspection still applies on Missouri's usual schedule, unless the vehicle is under ten model years old with fewer than 150,000 miles.

Charging rules

Under RSMo § 386.020, a business that sells, leases, owns, operates or manages EV chargers isn't treated as an electric corporation, unless it produces or sells wholesale electricity. Since August 28, 2024, § 67.288 bars local governments from requiring EV chargers or charging infrastructure in parking lots owned or leased by churches or nonprofit organizations. Property owners can still install chargers voluntarily.

The Missouri Department of Transportation had to submit an updated EV infrastructure deployment plan for each of fiscal years 2022 through 2026 under the federal NEVI Formula Program. The plans are on MoDOT's NEVI website.

State fleets and the EV task force

State agencies with fleets of 15 or more vehicles must make sure at least half of the new vehicles bought over each two-year period can run on an alternative fuel, which includes electricity. In addition, 30% of the fuel their alternative fuel vehicles use each year must be alternative fuel (RSMo §§ 414.400 and 414.410). A 2021 law had the Department of Revenue convene an EV Task Force to study charging, utility impacts and how EV drivers contribute to road funding. Its report was due to the General Assembly and the governor by December 31, 2022.

Federal credits

Since Missouri has no purchase incentive of its own, check the IRS and fueleconomy.gov for the current status of federal clean vehicle credits before you factor one into the price.
