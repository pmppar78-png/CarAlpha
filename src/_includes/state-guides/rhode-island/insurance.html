Rhode Island's minimum liability coverage

Rhode Island's Motor Vehicle Reparations Act, chapter 31-47 of the General Laws, requires financial security for every vehicle that must display registration plates to be driven on public highways. Under § 31-47-2, an owner's liability policy must cover each vehicle for at least:

CoverageMinimum
Bodily injury or death, one person, one accident$25,000
Bodily injury or death, two or more people, one accident$50,000
Damage to others' property, one accident$25,000
Or a combined single limit$75,000

The policy must cover liability arising from the ownership, maintenance, use or operation of the vehicle in Rhode Island and elsewhere in the United States, North America or Canada. Insurers may offer broader coverage than the minimum. Pending issue of a policy, an insurer may give a binder, which counts as coverage.

Other forms of financial security exist. A financial security deposit is $75,000 in cash or eligible securities for each vehicle, and a financial security bond is one executed by the owner and an authorized surety company.

Uninsured motorist coverage

Under § 27-7-2.1, every liability policy for a vehicle registered or principally garaged in Rhode Island must include uninsured motorist coverage, which also covers hit-and-run vehicles and drivers whose insurer is insolvent. The insurer must provide it at the same amount as your bodily injury limits. You can choose a lower limit in writing, though not below the state minimums. If you buy just the minimum required coverage, you can reduce the limit to zero after signing an advisory notice about the risk of uninsured and underinsured drivers.

You can reject the property damage part of uninsured motorist coverage in writing, and if you carry collision coverage, uninsured property damage coverage isn't required unless you choose it. Uninsured motorist property damage from a collision has a $200 deductible per claim unless otherwise agreed. Insurers must remind you of the available coverage and limits whenever the policy is renewed, reinstated or replaced.

Insurance and registration

Section 31-47-3.1 says no vehicle can be registered or renewed unless the applicant signs a statement promising not to drive, or allow anyone to drive, any vehicle that isn't covered by financial security. The statement also summarizes the law and its penalties and warns that it can't prevent an accident with an uninsured driver. Applicants for a driver's license or renewal sign a similar statement. When a dealer registers a vehicle for a buyer or lessee, the buyer or lessee signs the statement.

An operator's policy, which covers a person's own driving of vehicles they don't own, must meet the same minimums as an owner's policy and come from an insurer authorized in Rhode Island. A nonresident's operator policy may come from a nonadmitted insurer that meets the chapter's requirements (§ 31-47-9(d)).

Showing proof of insurance

Under § 31-47-12, a police officer investigating an offense or accident, or stopping a vehicle for probable cause, must ask for proof of financial security. Officers can't stop a vehicle just to check for insurance. You may show proof on a phone, and the officer can't look at any other content on it.

If you can't produce proof, you'll get a citation to the traffic tribunal. A citation issued solely for lacking proof is held for at least one business day, and if you show that you had coverage at the time, it's withdrawn and you don't have to appear.

Penalties

For driving a Rhode Island-registered vehicle without financial security in force on the date of a stop or accident, § 31-47-12(f) provides:

OffenseLicense suspensionFine
FirstUp to 30 days$100 to $250
SecondUp to six months$500
Third or laterUp to one year$1,000 civil violation

Section 31-47-9 adds penalties for an owner who knowingly drives or lets someone drive the vehicle uninsured, and for a driver who knows the owner has no coverage. A first offense brings a license and registration suspension of up to three months and a fine of $100 to $500, a second offense six months and $500, and a third or later offense up to a year and a $1,000 civil fine. A driver who had an operator's policy covering their own driving is excepted.

Before suspended privileges are restored, you must pay a $30 reinstatement fee, which the DMV administrator can raise to as much as $50, and file and maintain proof of financial security.

Choosing a repair shop

Every insurance ID card or other proof of coverage must state in bold print that Rhode Island law gives the consumer the right to choose the repair facility, and that an insurer may not interfere with that choice (§ 31-47-12.1).

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in Rhode Island was $1,849.80 (Table 5 of the Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement). The Insurance Research Council estimated that 12.4% of Rhode Island drivers were uninsured, which ranked the state 25th.
