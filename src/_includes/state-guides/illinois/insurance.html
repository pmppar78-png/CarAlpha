The coverage Illinois requires

25/50/20: $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 bodily injury per accident, and $20,000 property damage per accident, plus uninsured motorist bodily injury coverage of at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident.

Under the Mandatory Insurance Law, 625 ILCS 5/7-601, every owner of a vehicle registered in Illinois must carry liability insurance. The limits come from 625 ILCS 5/7-203. The Illinois Department of Insurance (IDOI) explains in its auto insurance shopping guide that bodily injury liability pays for injury or death to pedestrians or people in another car. It can also cover your passengers if they aren't members of your household. Property damage liability covers another person's car and property such as fences, buildings, utility poles, signs and trees.

The Secretary of State says the requirement applies to every vehicle that must display plates and is driven, including cars, vans, motorcycles, recreational vehicles, trucks and buses. Trailers are the exception. IDOI also cautions that the state minimums may not be enough to protect you from a lawsuit and suggests considering higher limits.

How fault affects a claim

Illinois follows a comparative negligence rule, which IDOI describes in its auto insurance definitions. You can collect from an at-fault driver only if you're less than 50% at fault, and your recovery is reduced by your own share of the blame. IDOI's example: a driver found 20% at fault can recover no more than 80% of their damages from the other driver.

Uninsured, underinsured and UMPD coverage

Uninsured motorist bodily injury (UM) is part of the required package in Illinois. It pays for your injuries when you're hit by a hit-and-run driver or by an at-fault driver who has no liability insurance. You can buy limits above $25,000/$50,000 for an extra premium.

Underinsured motorist bodily injury (UIM) pays the difference between your UIM limits and the at-fault driver's lower liability limits. Under 215 ILCS 5/143a-2, your policy must include UIM once you buy UM limits higher than the minimum.

Uninsured motorist property damage (UMPD) covers damage to your car caused by an identified, at-fault uninsured driver. It's optional, and you can buy it with or without collision coverage. IDOI says insurers must offer it with a deductible of no more than $250.

Physical damage and optional coverages

Collision covers damage from hitting another car or a fixed object, such as a tree. Comprehensive covers most other causes, including theft, fire and hail. Illinois doesn't require either one, but a lender may, and IDOI notes that on an older car the cost may not be worth it. Deductibles typically range from $0 to $1,000.

IDOI's optional add-ons include:

  • Medical payments, which covers medical and funeral costs for you and your passengers no matter who caused the crash
  • Gap coverage, which pays the difference between a totaled car's actual cash value and what you still owe on a loan or lease
  • Rental reimbursement, which pays a set daily amount while your car is being repaired after a covered loss
  • Towing coverage and coverage for custom or non-factory equipment

Electronic verification and suspensions

Since 2020, the Secretary of State has verified each registered vehicle's liability policy at least twice a year through a vendor linked to every insurer that writes auto liability in Illinois. If the first check doesn't find coverage, the vehicle is checked again 30 days later, which allows for cars that were sold or put in storage. If the second check also fails, the owner receives a suspension letter. The owner then has 30 days to show, through an insurance agent, that the vehicle was covered on the date of the first check. If they can't, the registration is suspended.

A first-time offender's plates stay suspended until the owner buys liability insurance and pays a $100 reinstatement fee online at ILIVS.COM, using the reference number on the suspension letter. Repeat offenders serve a four-month suspension before they can insure the vehicle, pay the $100 and get their plates back. While the plates are suspended, no one may drive that vehicle, although you can still drive other insured vehicles.

Tickets for driving uninsured

If you can't show proof of insurance during a traffic stop or after a crash, an officer can write a citation. A conviction suspends your plates and carries a minimum $500 fine. Driving while your plates are suspended for an earlier insurance violation carries a minimum $1,000 fine. The Secretary of State warns that some insurers won't sell coverage to owners who have been driving uninsured. If that happens, ask an agent about the Illinois Automobile Insurance Plan.

To qualify for the plan, IDOI says you must have been turned down by other insurers, have a valid license or be eligible for one, owe no unpaid premium from prior coverage in the past 36 months, and have a vehicle that's safe to drive.

What Illinois drivers pay

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners' auto insurance database report shows a 2024 average combined premium of $1,382.89 in Illinois, with an average expenditure of $1,263.95. The combined average in 2020 was $997.18. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 15.2% of Illinois drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 17th. That's roughly one driver in seven, which is why the required UM coverage matters.

IDOI lists the factors that most affect price: your age, gender and marital status, and your coverage limits. Your driving record and the records of others in your household also count, along with where you live, the vehicle, how far you commute and your credit information. Discounts to ask about include anti-theft devices, auto and home bundles, good student, defensive driving for drivers over 55, low mileage and multiple vehicles. Before you switch insurers, IDOI suggests asking the agent for a non-bound application, so the new company can confirm whether it will accept you and at what price before you cancel your current policy.

Complaints

IDOI investigates complaints about coverage, claims, premiums, cancellations and agents. Raise the issue with the company first and keep notes of each call. If that doesn't solve it, IDOI's Consumer Assistance line at (866) 445-5364 can answer questions, but complaints must be in writing. You can file one online through the IDOI help center, by email to DOI.complaints@illinois.gov, or by mail to 4800 Wabash Avenue, Springfield, IL 62711. Insurers have 21 days to respond, and IDOI asks you to allow four to six weeks for its investigation.
