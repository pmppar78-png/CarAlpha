Alternative-fuel registration fees

Utah charges an extra registration fee for electric, hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles, on top of the regular passenger-vehicle fee. Under Utah Code § 41-1a-1206(1)(g), the fee for an electric vehicle, or for a vehicle fueled only by a source other than gasoline, diesel, natural gas or propane, equals the road usage charge cap in § 72-1-213.1. That cap is $180 for an annual registration in 2026.

The statute sets the hybrid fee at $21.75 and the plug-in hybrid fee at $56.50, both indexed to inflation. A 2026 Utah Legislature staff brief lists the adjusted 2026 amounts as $25 for a hybrid and $64 for a plug-in hybrid. Six-month registrations carry lower amounts.

The cap rises on January 1, 2027, when § 72-1-213.1 sets it at $280 for an annual registration and $216 for a six-month registration. For a commercial electric vehicle of 6,001 pounds or more, the 2027 cap is $500 a year.

Road Usage Charge Program

Instead of the flat fee, the owner or lessee of an electric or other alternative-fuel vehicle can apply to the Utah Department of Transportation's Road Usage Charge Program and pay by the mile. In 2026 the rate is 1.25 cents a mile, and you never pay more than the cap for the registration period. The rate is set to rise to 1.5 cents a mile in 2027 unless the Transportation Commission sets a different rate. UDOT's rules govern enrollment, mileage reporting, payment periods and privacy protections, and § 41-1a-203(6) lets the DMV withhold a registration renewal when it receives a hold request under the program.

Emissions and fuel tax

  • Electric vehicles are on the DMV's emissions exemption lists in each testing county, and they're exempt from the local emissions compliance fee under § 41-1a-1223.
  • The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) reports that electricity and propane used to operate motor vehicles are exempt from Utah's motor fuel taxes, citing Utah Code §§ 59-13-102, 59-13-201 and 59-13-301.
  • The AFDC also reports a 12.5% tax on retail sales of electricity for EV charging under §§ 59-30-101 and 59-30-102. The tax may be based on kilowatt-hours, time or a subscription.

Other state programs and rules

  • Utah's clean fuel HOV lane decal program, which let qualifying electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles use HOV lanes regardless of occupancy, expired on September 30, 2025.
  • The Department of Environmental Quality's Clean Fleet Program reimburses fleets that replace older vehicles and equipment, at up to 45% of the cost for all-electric replacements, including charging equipment and installation.
  • Under Utah Administrative Code R909-2-5, a vehicle powered mainly by an electric battery may exceed state weight limits by the extra weight of its battery system compared with a diesel equivalent, up to 82,000 pounds total.
  • The Public Service Commission is authorized under §§ 54-2-1 and 54-4-41 to set up a utility EV charging program costing up to $50 million.
