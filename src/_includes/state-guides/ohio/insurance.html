Financial responsibility and the minimum limits

Ohio frames its auto insurance requirement as a duty to maintain "proof of financial responsibility." Under Ohio Rev. Code § 4509.101(A)(1), no one may drive, or allow someone to drive, a motor vehicle in Ohio unless proof of financial responsibility is kept continuously throughout the registration period for that vehicle, or for the driver if the driver isn't the owner. A liability policy is the usual way to meet that duty. Section 4509.51 sets the minimum limits for an owner's policy:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in an accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in an accident, and
  • $25,000 for damage to other people's property in an accident.

That's 25/50/25. The policy has to cover you and anyone driving your listed vehicles with your permission, for liability arising anywhere in the United States or Canada.

Other ways to show financial responsibility

Section 4509.101(G) lists the documents that count as proof:

  • A financial responsibility identification card from your insurer
  • A liability policy, its declarations page, or a liability bond that meets the statutory requirements
  • A surety bond, or certification that one has been issued
  • A certificate showing a deposit of money or securities. Under § 4509.62, you can deposit $30,000 with the registrar if you show there are no unsatisfied judgments against you in your home county, or
  • A certificate of self-insurance.

You can also show proof on a phone or other wireless device. Whatever you show must name the insured and the insurer or surety, give the effective and expiration dates, and identify the covered vehicles.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Under § 3937.18(A), an Ohio auto liability policy may include uninsured motorist coverage, underinsured motorist coverage or both, but the law doesn't require it. If a policy does include underinsured coverage, it applies when the at-fault driver's total liability coverage is less than your underinsured limits. Uninsured coverage can apply to a hit-and-run when independent evidence, beyond your own testimony, shows an unidentified driver caused the injury. Because the coverage is optional, check your declarations page to see whether you have it and at what limits.

When you have to show proof

Section 4509.101(A)(3) requires you to verify coverage in two situations:

  • You, or a vehicle you own, are in a crash that requires an accident report under § 4509.06, or
  • You get a ticket saying you didn't produce proof when a peace officer or trooper asked for it.

Everyone who has to file an accident report must include proof of financial responsibility with it. If the registrar finds within 45 days of the report that you didn't have coverage, it orders your license suspended. After a ticket, the registrar sends a written notice. You can present proof, surrender your license, or file a statement that you weren't driving or allowing the vehicle to be driven at the time. If you present proof within 45 days, the suspension order ends. You can request an administrative hearing within 15 days after an order, but asking for one doesn't put the order on hold.

Penalties for driving uninsured

Violations are handled mainly through civil penalties on your driving privileges:

ViolationLicense suspensionReinstatement fee
FirstClass F: until conditions are met$40
Second within one yearClass C: one year$300
Third or later within one yearClass B: two years$600

The suspension periods come from § 4510.02, and the reinstatement fees apply by violation count. Your license is impounded during the suspension. A court can grant limited driving privileges if you show proof of coverage. After a first violation, you also have to be enrolled in a reinstatement fee payment plan. After a second, there are no limited privileges for the first 15 days. After a third, none for the first 30 days. To get your license back, you have to pay the reinstatement fee and a $10 deputy registrar service fee, and file and keep proof of financial responsibility under §§ 4509.44 to 4509.65.

If a court later rules that the insurer on the proof you presented doesn't have to pay a judgment against you, the registrar has to suspend your operating privileges and registration rights.

Rentals and leases

For this rule, an "owner" doesn't include a licensed leasing dealer but does include a rental dealer. A rental company can have the renter agree in the contract to be solely responsible for maintaining proof of financial responsibility during the rental.

Costs and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in Ohio was $1,133.98. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 18.5% of Ohio drivers were uninsured, which ranked the state 12th. With that estimate in mind, you may want to ask your agent about uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage.
