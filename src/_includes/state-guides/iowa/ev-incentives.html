EVs in Iowa: fees, funding and charging rules

Iowa doesn't offer a state rebate or income tax credit to individuals who buy a passenger EV. The Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists Iowa programs that help businesses, fleets and organizations, plus several rules for how public charging is sold and inspected. For a typical driver, the main state cost is an annual supplemental fee.

Supplemental registration fees

Iowa Code § 321.116 adds a yearly fee on top of the regular value-and-weight registration fee:

  • Battery-electric vehicle: $130
  • Plug-in hybrid: $65
  • Electric or plug-in hybrid motorcycle: $9

The statute defines a battery-electric vehicle as one with no internal combustion engine, powered only by electric motors that run on a battery charged from an outlet or charging station. A plug-in hybrid has both an engine and a plug-in battery. The Iowa DOT says the fees apply to electric cars, pickups, SUVs, motorcycles and other vehicles under 10,000 pounds unloaded, and lists the $9 motorcycle amount. Conventional hybrids that can't be plugged in aren't covered by these definitions.

Keep in mind that a new EV's regular annual fee is still 1% of its value plus 40 cents per 100 pounds. Because the base fee depends on both value and weight, check the fee estimator for the specific model you're considering. You'll also pay the one-time fee for new registration of 5% of the purchase price plus $10.

Rules for public charging

  • Charging isn't a utility service. Under Iowa Administrative Code rule 199-20.20(476), an entity providing electricity for EV charging isn't a public utility. Regulated utilities can't prohibit or restrict the sale of electricity at a charging station.
  • Seller licenses. Under Iowa Code § 452A.42, anyone selling or dispensing electricity as vehicle fuel, anywhere other than a residence, needs a license from the Iowa Department of Revenue.
  • Meter inspections. Under § 452A.41, the Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship inspects chargers owned by licensed electric fuel dealers every two years, using the NIST Handbook 44 standards. It reports chargers that fail to the Department of Revenue.

The license requirement doesn't apply to electricity dispensed at a residence, so charging at home doesn't require one.

State funding programs

Iowa's grant programs are aimed at chargers and fleets, not individual car buyers:

  • Charger grants. Iowa DOT funds publicly accessible Level 2 and DC fast charging stations with the state's share of the Volkswagen Environmental Mitigation Trust.
  • Diesel replacement. Iowa DOT also helps government, nonprofit and private fleets replace or convert medium- and heavy-duty diesel vehicles, including school buses, transit buses and trucks, with cleaner models. That can include electric ones.
  • Iowa Energy Center grants. The Iowa Economic Development Authority funds projects that support the Iowa Energy Plan, which can include buying alternative fuel vehicles. Businesses, colleges and nonprofits can apply, and AFDC lists the program as running through July 31, 2029.
  • Research vehicles. Under Iowa Code § 214A.24, the Department of Natural Resources may give demonstration grants for alternative fuel vehicles used for research. The department keeps the title, and the proceeds from an eventual sale go back into research.

Iowa DOT also filed annual EV infrastructure deployment plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026 under the federal NEVI program for highway fast charging.

Converting a vehicle

If you convert a vehicle to run on a different fuel, including electricity, Iowa Code § 321.41 requires you to notify your county treasurer of the new fuel type within 30 days.
