What a Maryland policy has to include

Maryland calls the insurance you need to register a vehicle "required security." Transportation Article § 17-103 sets the floor:

  • $30,000 for bodily injury or death to any one person, and $60,000 for two or more people in one accident, plus interest and costs
  • $15,000 for damage to other people's property in one accident, plus interest and costs
  • Personal injury protection (PIP) under Insurance Article § 19-505, unless it's waived or rejected
  • Uninsured motorist coverage under § 19-509, or enhanced underinsured motorist coverage under § 19-509.1

In almost every case, the security has to be a liability policy from an insurer authorized to write it in Maryland. The MVA may accept another form of security only if it provides the same benefits. Self-insurers pay an annual assessment of up to $750 to fund solvency audits.

Personal injury protection and the waiver

Under Insurance Article § 19-505, every auto liability policy sold in Maryland includes PIP of at least $2,500. It pays reasonable medical, hospital, dental, ambulance and funeral expenses incurred within 3 years of an accident. It also pays 85% of lost income for someone who was working, or the cost of household services for someone who wasn't earning income. PIP covers the first named insured and resident family members, permitted drivers of the insured vehicle, passengers, and pedestrians or cyclists struck by the insured vehicle.

You can give up PIP, but only on purpose. Section 19-506 requires an affirmative written waiver from the first named insured, on a form set by the Insurance Commissioner. Before the waiver, the insurer must give written notice of the coverage's nature, extent and cost. If no waiver is signed, the insurer has to provide PIP. A waiver binds every named insured, every listed driver, and household family members age 16 and older. An insurer can't refuse to cover you because you keep PIP.

Uninsured and enhanced underinsured coverage

Section 19-509 requires uninsured motorist coverage for bodily injury, death and property damage, including loss of use of your vehicle. It has to at least equal the Title 17 minimums. On a private passenger policy, it must match your liability limits unless you waive the higher amount under § 19-510.

For new policies issued on or after July 1, 2024, § 19-509.1 requires insurers to offer enhanced underinsured motorist coverage at purchase. That coverage can pay even when the other driver's liability limits are equal to or higher than your UM coverage. To keep standard UM coverage instead, the first named insured must sign an affirmative written waiver on the Commissioner's form, printed in at least 14-point bold type. Renewal policies issued before that date get the enhanced coverage only if you choose it.

What happens when coverage lapses

Insurers report final cancellations and lapses to the MVA electronically. Under § 17-106, the registration is suspended automatically as of the lapse date. The suspension takes effect no later than 60 days after the MVA is notified, and it lasts until you submit proof of new coverage and pay any uninsured motorist penalty. After the MVA tells you about the suspension, you have 48 hours to surrender the registration. If you don't, the MVA can try to recover it and can suspend your driver's license until it's returned.

The penalty is $200 per vehicle for a lapse of 1 to 30 days, then $7 more for each day starting on the 31st. It's capped at $3,500 per violation in a 12-month period. Until the penalty is paid, the MVA won't reinstate the suspended registration, renew any of your registrations, or issue a new one for a vehicle titled after the violation. Transferring the title to a family member doesn't make the penalty go away.

You can avoid the penalty if you return the plates to the MVA within 10 days of the lapse and one of these applies:

  • the title went to a new owner
  • you moved out of state and mailed the plates back
  • a salvage certificate was issued
  • a licensed dealer took the vehicle and agreed to return the plates

The MVA must check whether the plates were returned within the 10 days before assessing the penalty.

What drivers pay

The NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement puts Maryland's combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,767.57, up from $1,309.95 in 2020. The average expenditure, which reflects the coverage drivers actually bought, was $1,626.11. The Insurance Research Council estimates that 16.9% of Maryland drivers were uninsured in 2023, ranking the state 14th.
