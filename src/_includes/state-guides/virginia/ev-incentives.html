The highway use fee

Virginia charges electric vehicles an annual highway use fee under Va. Code §§ 46.2-770 through 46.2-773, enacted in 2020. Section 46.2-771 says the purpose is more equitable contributions to the Commonwealth Transportation Fund from alternative fuel, electric and fuel-efficient vehicles. The DMV collects the fee at registration, on top of the regular registration fee, and multiplies it by the number of years when you register for more than one.

For an electric motor vehicle, meaning one that runs on electricity alone, § 46.2-772(B) sets the fee at 85% of the state fuel tax a car getting 23.7 miles per gallon would pay over the average annual miles a Virginia passenger vehicle travels. The Commissioner recalculates it by July 1 each year. The DMV Fees chart (DMV 201) lists the electric vehicle highway use fee at $135.63.

The same section charges a smaller fee to other fuel-efficient vehicles, defined as those with a combined fuel economy of 25 miles per gallon or more. Their fee is 85% of the difference between the fuel tax for a 23.7 mpg car and the tax for the vehicle being registered.

The fee doesn't apply to:

  • Autocycles, mopeds and motorcycles
  • Vehicles with a gross weight over 10,000 pounds
  • Vehicles owned by a governmental entity
  • Vehicles registered under the International Registration Plan

A low-speed vehicle pays a flat $25. The DMV must provide a process for contesting the fee and refund any amount collected incorrectly. If you're eligible for a registration refund under § 46.2-688 with six or more months left, the highway use fee is refunded in six-month increments.

The mileage-based alternative

Section 46.2-773 created a voluntary mileage-based user fee program. In any year you're enrolled, you pay a per-mile fee in place of the highway use fee, and the total you pay in 12 months can't exceed the highway use fee you'd otherwise owe. The per-mile rate is the highway use fee divided by the average miles a Virginia passenger vehicle travels.

The DMV has to offer a way to take part without location tracking. Information collected for the program can be used only to administer the fee. It isn't open to the public under the Freedom of Information Act, can't be sold for marketing, and can't be shared except to collect unpaid fees or with an owner contesting a fee.

The authorized EV rebate

Va. Code §§ 45.2-1726 through 45.2-1733 authorize a state rebate for buying a new or used electric vehicle. According to the U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC), the program's rules are:

  • Rebates can be up to $2,500.
  • An additional $2,000 must be available for residents whose household income is no more than 300% of current poverty guidelines.
  • An eligible used vehicle can't cost more than $25,000.
  • The authorization expires January 1, 2027.

The AFDC lists it as an authorization and refers readers to the Virginia Department of Energy's Sustainable Transportation website for details.

Charging and financing programs

The AFDC also lists these Virginia programs:

  • Virginia Department of Energy grants of up to $400,000 to private businesses and public-private partnerships for EV chargers in rural or underserved communities
  • Financing from the Virginia Clean Energy Innovation Bank, including loans and credit enhancements, for alternative fuel vehicle and infrastructure projects by local organizations and businesses
  • Authority for local governments to reduce personal property taxes on alternative fuel vehicles, including electric vehicles, and on low-speed vehicles, under § 58.1-3506

Emissions inspection exemption

According to the AFDC, vehicles powered only by electricity are exempt from Virginia's emissions inspection program, as are those running only on natural gas, propane or hydrogen. Qualifying hybrids rated at least 50 mpg in city driving, or 48 mpg for model years 2008 and 2009, are also exempt unless remote sensing suggests they may not meet current standards. Hybrids that don't qualify pay the $2-a-year emissions fee at renewal.

Other EV rules

  • Under § 46.2-1219.3, a vehicle that isn't actively charging may not park in a designated EV charging space. The penalty is up to $25, and localities can add up to $25 more.
  • Homeowners associations can't prohibit an owner from installing an EV charger for personal use in the owner's designated parking space, though they can set rules for exterior and common-area installations. A condominium association may prohibit one when installation isn't technically feasible or practical because of safety, structural or engineering conditions.
  • Under §§ 56-1.2:1 and 56-232.2:1, a business that provides retail EV charging isn't a public utility if it buys its electricity from an authorized utility, and the State Corporation Commission doesn't set its rates.
  • Low-speed vehicles, with a top speed between 20 and 25 mph, may not be driven on roads with posted limits above 35 mph.
  • According to the AFDC, the Air Pollution Control Board has adopted California's emissions and zero-emission vehicle requirements for passenger cars and light- and medium-duty trucks.
