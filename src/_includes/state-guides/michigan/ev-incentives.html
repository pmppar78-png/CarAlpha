No state purchase rebate

The state laws and incentives listed for Michigan by the U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center include no rebate or tax credit for a person buying or leasing an electric car. The programs on that list fund chargers, set rules for selling electricity, or cover state fleets. What a private owner deals with directly is the annual plug-in fee at registration.

The annual plug-in fee

Since a 2015 change to MCL 257.801, electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles pay a yearly fee on top of the list-price registration tax:

  • Battery electric, 8,000 pounds GVWR or less: $100. Over 8,000 pounds: $200.
  • Plug-in hybrid, 8,000 pounds GVWR or less: $30. Over 8,000 pounds: $100.

The amounts are tied to the fuel tax. For every cent the state motor fuel tax exceeds 19 cents a gallon, the EV fee rises $5 and the plug-in hybrid fee rises $2.50. The Secretary of State's fee calculator shows the current total for a specific vehicle.

Charger funding through EGLE

The Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy's Charge Up Michigan Placement Project funds DC fast charging for public and private organizations. It can pay for the stations, site preparation, networking fees and signage. Applicants must be enrolled in a utility EV program. The grant is the lesser of 33.3% of the project cost or a match of the utility's funding, up to $70,000. This program is for site hosts, so homeowners should check with their electric utility about any home-charger programs it runs.

The Michigan Department of Transportation also had to submit an EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan each year for fiscal years 2022 through 2026. The plans described how it intended to spend federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) formula funds.

Rules for charging businesses

Laws enacted November 14, 2023 settle how charging is regulated. Under MCL 460.10g, a company that provides EV charging isn't treated as a public utility and isn't bound by limits on reselling electricity. Under MCL 460.10a to 10q, however, anyone who sells or dispenses electricity as vehicle fuel anywhere other than a residence needs a license. A commercial electric customer that installs or modifies wiring or outlets for EV charging is exempt from energy optimization charges under Public Act 107 of 2023.

State fleet goals

State agencies must prioritize zero-emission vehicles when they buy new ones, starting with vehicles that travel the most miles or serve underserved communities. The targets are 100% zero-emission light-duty vehicles in the state fleet by 2033, and 100% for medium- and heavy-duty vehicles by 2040. The Department of Technology, Management, and Budget sets the purchasing requirements and exceptions.

Industrial property tax exemption

Under MCL 207.551 and 207.803, industrial property used for high-technology work can qualify for a tax exemption. That includes work on electric, hybrid and alternative fuel vehicles and their components. A facility needs an exemption certificate from the State Tax Commission. This helps manufacturers, but individual drivers don't receive it.

Regional agreements

Michigan signed two multistate agreements on charging. One, with Illinois, Indiana and Wisconsin, aims to build a charging route around the Lake Michigan shoreline for EV tourism. The other, REV Midwest, also includes Minnesota and focuses on medium- and heavy-duty fleets and freight corridors.
