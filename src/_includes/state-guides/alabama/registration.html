Who handles registration in Alabama

The Alabama Department of Revenue's Motor Vehicle Division sets the rules and keeps the title records. The counter work happens locally, at your county's license plate issuing official: the probate judge, license commissioner, or tax collector, depending on the county. ADOR keeps a county office directory. The fees below are state fees. Every county adds its own, so expect the counter total to be higher.

Deadlines

  • New residents: 30 days after becoming an Alabama resident to register a vehicle that is already registered somewhere else. A penalty applies starting the 31st day (ADOR Rule 810-5-1-.245; Ala. Code § 40-12-262).
  • Vehicles you just bought: 20 calendar days from the purchase date. A late registration costs a $15 penalty plus interest.
  • Renewals: staggered by the first letter of the owner's last name (Rule 810-5-1-.484). A and D renew in January. B is February, C and E March, F, G and N April, H and O May, I and M June, L and P July, J, K and R August, Q, S and T September, and U through Z October. Leased and fleet vehicles follow separate October or November schedules.

Title

The state title fee is $15, and the licensing official adds a $3 handling fee. Alabama doesn't title older vehicles: under the Uniform Certificate of Title Act, a vehicle more than 35 model years old is exempt, and it changes hands on a bill of sale and the old registration. For a car coming from another state, the county office collects the title application, the out-of-state title or registration, and payment. Call the county office before you go if a lender holds your title, because the process varies by county.

Registration fees

A standard passenger plate costs $23 a year plus a $1.25 issuance fee under § 40-12-242, and local fees are added on top. Electric and plug-in hybrid owners pay an extra annual fee; the figures are on our Alabama EV page. Specialty and personalized plates have their own fees.

Sales tax and property tax

Two different taxes show up on an Alabama vehicle, and new residents tend to be surprised by the second one.

Sales tax. The state rate on automotive vehicles is 2 percent (§ 40-23-2(4)), half the 4 percent general rate. City and county taxes are added. Alabama taxes only the net difference when you trade in a vehicle, so the trade-in value comes off the taxable price. A dealer collects the tax. In a private sale, you pay it to the county when you register.

Ad valorem (property) tax. This is paid every year with the tag renewal and has to be paid before the plate is issued. ADOR's motor vehicle ad valorem schedule sets the vehicle's market value at 89 percent of average retail as of October 1. That value is assessed at the Class IV ratio of 15 percent and then multiplied by the millage: 6.5 mills for the state plus county, city, and school millage. For a car valued at $20,000, the assessed value is $3,000. At a combined local rate of 40 mills, the tax would be $120. Your county's actual millage sets the real number.

Inspection and emissions

Alabama has no annual safety inspection and no emissions test anywhere in the state. The one check is at the counter: for a vehicle titled out of state, the licensing official verifies the vehicle and its VIN before Alabama issues a title.

What to bring

  • The title or, for a lienholder-held title, the lender's details and the current out-of-state registration.
  • A bill of sale for a private purchase, or a dealer's invoice.
  • Proof of Alabama liability insurance. ADOR checks coverage electronically after you register.
  • Your Alabama driver license or other ID and proof of your county address.
  • Payment for the title, registration, sales tax, and ad valorem tax.
