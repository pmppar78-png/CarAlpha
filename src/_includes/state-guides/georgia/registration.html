Registering after a move to Georgia

The Department of Revenue's new-resident page gives you 30 days from the date you move to register your vehicle with the state. Titling and registration happen at the County Tag Office in the county where you live, not at a state office. The order of steps matters. You have to get a Georgia driver's license or ID card from the Department of Driver Services first, because the tag office won't register a vehicle without it.

Bring these to the tag office:

  • A completed and signed Form MV-1 Title/Tag Application.
  • Your original out-of-state title. If your 1986 or newer vehicle comes from a state or country that doesn't title that kind of vehicle, bring its registration instead.
  • Form T-17, if a lender or leasing company holds your title.
  • Proof of Georgia liability insurance, either filed electronically by your insurer or shown on an insurance binder.
  • A passing emissions inspection, if you live in one of the 13 metro Atlanta counties that require it.

DOR also asks that names and spellings match across every document you hand over, so check your license, title and insurance paperwork against each other before the trip.

Title ad valorem tax instead of sales tax

Georgia doesn't charge sales tax or a yearly car tax on most vehicles bought on or after March 1, 2013. Instead, owners pay a one-time title ad valorem tax (TAVT) when the vehicle is titled. It's due again every time ownership changes, and when a new resident registers a car here for the first time.

  • Standard rate: 7.0% of the vehicle's fair market value.
  • New residents: 3%, under a law in effect since July 1, 2019.
  • Family transfers and inheritance: 0.5% of fair market value when the vehicle is already in the TAVT system and the right affidavit (MV-16 or T-20) is filed.

Fair market value isn't simply your purchase price. According to DOR's TAVT FAQ, a new vehicle is taxed on the greater of the selling price or the value in the state assessment manual, reduced by any dealer trade-in, rebate or cash discount. A used vehicle is taxed on the assessment manual value, which averages current wholesale and retail values. Trading in a car lowers the taxable value only when you buy from a dealer, not in a private sale. When you buy from a dealer, the dealer has to collect the TAVT and file your title application with your county.

Some veterans are fully exempt, including those compensated at 100% disability and Medal of Honor recipients. Vehicles that aren't titled, and older vehicles still in the old system, pay annual ad valorem tax at each renewal instead.

Georgia registration and title fees

DOR's fees, fines and penalties schedule keeps the base charges low, because TAVT does most of the work:

  • Annual plate registration or renewal for a passenger car or light pickup: $20
  • Original title application or change of ownership: $18
  • Replacement title when lost or stolen: $8
  • Replacement plate or decal: $8
  • Expedited in-person title handling: $10 extra

Specialty and prestige plates add manufacturing fees on top of the $20 registration.

Renewing every year

Registration runs on an annual cycle, and DOR mails or emails a renewal notice by the start of your registration month. A missing notice doesn't excuse a late renewal, though. You can renew through DRIVES e-Services in participating counties, at a self-service kiosk, by mail, or in person at the tag office. Online renewal needs your plate number or VIN, your driver's license number or the letter ID from the notice, and a card or e-check.

Renewing after your county's registration period ends adds a penalty of 10% of any ad valorem tax due plus 25% of the plate fees. Separately, you have 30 days after buying a vehicle to apply for a title, and a late application costs $10 more.

Emissions testing in metro Atlanta

Georgia's Clean Air Force runs annual emissions testing in Cherokee, Clayton, Cobb, Coweta, DeKalb, Douglas, Fayette, Forsyth, Fulton, Gwinnett, Henry, Paulding and Rockdale counties. For 2026 registrations, it applies to gasoline cars and light trucks of 8,500 pounds GVWR or less from model years 2002 through 2023. The three newest model years are exempt (2024 and newer for 2026), and so are vehicles 25 model years or older. Diesels and vehicles that run only on electricity, natural gas, propane or hydrogen don't test, but gasoline hybrids do.

A test costs up to $25, and each station posts its price. Test before your registration date, which in Georgia is the owner's birthday; the program suggests four to six weeks ahead. If your car fails, the tag office may give you a single, non-renewable 30-day registration extension while you sort out repairs. Seniors 65 and older may qualify for an exemption, and repair waivers are handled at Clean Air Force customer service centers.

Outside those 13 counties, Georgia vehicles don't need an emissions test to register.
