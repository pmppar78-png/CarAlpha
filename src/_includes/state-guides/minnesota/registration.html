Registering a vehicle in Minnesota

Driver and Vehicle Services (DVS), part of the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, handles titles, registration and plates. Minnesota's registration charge is a tax set by Minn. Stat. § 168.013, and it's based on the vehicle's value, not its weight, for passenger cars. Because of that, a new car usually costs much more to register than an old one.

How the registration tax is calculated

For a passenger car or hearse, the annual tax is $10 plus a percentage of the manufacturer's suggested retail price (MSRP):

  • First registered in Minnesota on or after November 16, 2020: 1.575% of MSRP, without the destination charge.
  • First registered in Minnesota before November 16, 2020: 1.54% of MSRP plus the destination charge.

Separately added accessories and optional equipment aren't counted. DVS takes the MSRP from published list prices, the window sticker, or, when neither is available, the actual sale price.

The percentage then applies to a shrinking share of MSRP as the vehicle ages:

  • Year 1: 100%
  • Year 2: 95%
  • Year 3: 90%
  • Year 4: 80%
  • Year 5: 70%
  • Year 6: 60%
  • Year 7: 50%
  • Year 8: 40%
  • Year 9: 25%
  • Year 10: 10%

From the 11th year on, the value-based part is a flat $20, so the total is $30 before other fees. For a vehicle already registered in Minnesota, the total can't exceed the smallest amount previously paid or due on it, whoever owned it. A vehicle that becomes taxable partway through a registration period pays a prorated tax of 1/12 of the annual amount for each month left.

As an example, a car with a $30,000 MSRP in its first year would owe $10 plus 1.575% of $30,000, which comes to $482.50 before plate and other fees. In its fifth year, the percentage applies to $21,000, so the value-based part is $330.75.

Electric and plug-in hybrid surcharges

All-electric vehicles pay a surcharge on top of the registration tax. It's the greater of 0.5% of MSRP, adjusted by the same age schedule (10% from year 11), or a minimum amount. For registration periods starting January 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027, the minimum is $150, and it drops to $100 for periods starting July 1, 2027 or later. Plug-in hybrids pay 0.25% of MSRP on the same schedule, with a minimum of $75, which falls to $50 on the same date. For an electric car with a $50,000 MSRP in its first year, 0.5% is $250, which is more than the minimum, so the surcharge is $250. By its ninth year, the percentage applies to 25% of MSRP, giving $62.50, so the minimum applies instead. The surcharge money goes to the highway user tax distribution fund.

Title, plate and lien fees

Minn. Stat. § 168A.29 sets these title fees:

  • Original title: $8.25, plus a $2.25 technology surcharge.
  • Each security interest noted on a title: $2.
  • Assignment of a security interest: $1, unless it's noted along with the lien.
  • Duplicate title: $7.25, plus the same $2.25 surcharge.

Plate fees come from § 168.12, subd. 5. Regular, special, collector and replacement personalized plates cost $13.50 for a single plate and $15.50 for a pair, and a duplicate sticker is $1.50. Other filing fees charged at deputy registrar offices are set separately.

Motor vehicle sales tax

When you buy or acquire a vehicle that must be registered in Minnesota, Minn. Stat. § 297B.02 imposes a 6.875% excise tax on the purchase price. It applies whether you bought the vehicle in Minnesota or somewhere else. Certain older passenger cars described in § 297B.025 pay a flat $10 instead.

No emissions test or safety inspection

Minnesota ended the Twin Cities auto emissions inspection program on March 1, 2000, and there's no periodic safety inspection for passenger cars. You won't need an inspection certificate to register or renew.

Proof of insurance

The owner must have the no-fault coverage required by Minn. Stat. § 65B.48 to register a vehicle. Falsely claiming coverage at registration is a crime under § 169.797. See the Minnesota insurance guide for the required limits.
