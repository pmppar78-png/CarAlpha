New Hampshire's lemon law

New Hampshire's lemon law is RSA chapter 357-D, titled "New Motor Vehicle Arbitration," in the New Hampshire Revised Statutes. The legislature's statement of intent in RSA 357-D:1 says manufacturers should provide a "speedy and less costly" resolution of warranty problems. When a manufacturer can't make a new vehicle conform to its warranty, the law calls for a refund, lease payments back, or a replacement the consumer accepts. The chapter also creates a state board that hears these cases. Dealers can't be sued under the chapter.

RSA 357-D:8 adds that a franchised dealer is liable only for written warranties it made apart from the manufacturer's, and can't be named as a defendant in a case under the chapter. The manufacturer also can't charge the dealer back for refunds, replacements or other costs that come from a lemon law case. When a manufacturer delegates warranty repairs to dealers, RSA 357-D:3, II requires it to pay them for that work.

Who and what is covered

Under RSA 357-D:2, a "consumer" is a buyer of a new motor vehicle other than for resale, or a lessee under a written lease of two years or more other than for sublease. Anyone the vehicle is transferred to during the express warranty, and anyone else entitled to enforce the warranty, is also covered. Government entities are excluded.

RSA 357-D:2, X defines the covered vehicles as:

  • Private passenger cars and station wagons up to 11,000 pounds gross weight.
  • Other four-wheel motor vehicles up to 11,000 pounds, except tractors and mopeds.
  • Motorcycles.
  • Off highway recreational vehicles as defined in RSA 215-A:1, VI.
  • Snowmobiles.

A "new motor vehicle" is one still under the manufacturer's express warranty. A "nonconformity" is a defect or condition that substantially impairs use, value or safety. Damage from an accident, abuse, neglect, or modification by someone other than the manufacturer or its authorized service agent doesn't count.

Repairs and repair orders

RSA 357-D:3, III requires the manufacturer to make whatever repairs are needed if you report a nonconformity to the manufacturer, its agent or an authorized dealer during the warranty term, even if the work happens after the warranty ends. Under paragraph IV, the dealer can't refuse to give you a written repair order. Each visit should produce a written summary of your complaint and an itemized statement of all work, including examination, parts and labor. Keep every one, since the presumption described below depends on them.

The presumption: three repairs or 30 business days

RSA 357-D:3, VII presumes a reasonable number of attempts when, during the express warranty term:

  • The same nonconformity, as identified in a written examination or repair order, has been repaired at least 3 times and still exists; or
  • The vehicle has been out of service for repair of one or more nonconformities for a cumulative total of 30 or more business days.

A business day is any day the service departments of the manufacturer's authorized dealers are normally open. A vehicle available to you for a major part of the day isn't counted as out of service. Paragraph VIII limits the presumption to repair attempts made by the same agent or dealer, unless you show good cause for going elsewhere. War, invasion, strikes, fire, floods and other natural disasters extend the warranty term and the 30-day period, and during such an extension the manufacturer must give you free use of a vehicle.

Written notice and choosing a forum

Under RSA 357-D:4, after the third repair attempt or 30 business days out of service, you notify the manufacturer and any lessor in writing that you're electing to proceed under the chapter. The manufacturer has to give you the forms, along with a clear notice of your rights, when the vehicle is delivered. RSA 357-D:9 bars the manufacturer from delegating that duty to the dealer.

In the notice, you choose between the manufacturer's own dispute program and the state's New Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board. That choice is binding; picking one rules out the other. You can't use the chapter if you've stopped your loan or lease payments, unless you stopped because of the manufacturer's breach.

The arbitration process

  • Fees: a consumer filing with the board pays $50, and the manufacturer pays $250.
  • Final repair: within the 40-day period, the manufacturer gets one final chance to repair the defect. If you're satisfied, the case ends without prejudice to asking for arbitration later if the repair fails.
  • Timing: arbitration must be held within 40 days of the notice, with one extension of up to 30 days for good cause. If the manufacturer asks for the extension, it must give you free use of a vehicle. If a manufacturer's own program misses the 40-day deadline without good cause, you're entitled to the relief you asked for.
  • Decision: the board decides within 30 days after the hearing ends.

Under RSA 357-D:5, the board has five members and three alternates appointed by the governor and council. One member is a New Hampshire new car dealer, one is knowledgeable in automobile mechanics, and three represent consumers with no direct ties to the auto industry. The board is administratively attached to the Department of Safety.

Refund or replacement

RSA 357-D:3, V gives you the choice, within 30 days of the board's order, between a comparable new replacement vehicle from the same manufacturer and a refund. A refund includes the full contract price, trade-in and down payment credits, license and registration fees, finance and credit charges, and incidental and consequential damages. The use allowance is based on the miles driven before the first repair attempt. It's the purchase price times those miles divided by 100,000. The divisor is 20,000 for a motorcycle, snowmobile or OHRV of 250 cc or less, and 40,000 for larger ones.

For a leased vehicle, paragraph IX returns your deposit and rental payments, minus a use allowance, and ends the lease with no further charges. The lessor gets its purchase cost less your payments, plus freight, accessories, any fee paid to obtain the lease, and 5% of its purchase cost. Under RSA 357-D:4, VI, the manufacturer has to pay or replace within 30 days of the board's decision or 15 days after final adjudication.

Appeals, enforcement and deadlines

The board's decision is final unless a party proves to the superior court, by clear and convincing evidence, corruption, fraud, partiality, misconduct, or that the board exceeded its powers. Appeals are due within 30 days of the written decision. Under RSA 357-D:7, a manufacturer's failure to comply is an unfair or deceptive act under the Consumer Protection Act, RSA 358-A. RSA 357-D:10 lets a court award you costs and reasonable attorney's fees in a warranty suit.

Under RSA 357-D:11, any proceeding must begin within one year after the later of the warranty's expiration or the manufacturer's final repair attempt. The chapter doesn't limit your other legal remedies. RSA 357-D:12 bars reselling in New Hampshire any vehicle the board finds has a serious safety defect.
