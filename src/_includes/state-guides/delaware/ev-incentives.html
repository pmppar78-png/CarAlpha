Delaware Clean Vehicle Rebate

DNREC's Clean Vehicle Rebate Program pays cash. You don't claim it on a tax return. The current round covers vehicles bought or leased from May 1, 2026 through April 30, 2027, and it ends earlier if the money runs out. Applications for vehicles bought before May 1, 2026 are no longer accepted. The amounts are:

  • $2,500 for a new battery-electric vehicle with a base MSRP under $40,000
  • $1,500 for a new battery-electric vehicle with a base MSRP of $40,000 to $50,000
  • $1,000 for a new plug-in hybrid with a base MSRP under $50,000
  • $2,500 for a used battery-electric vehicle, or $1,000 for a used plug-in hybrid, with a Kelley Blue Book fair purchase price of $40,000 or less.

The cap is based on the base MSRP, meaning the standard trim, according to the program FAQ. If a model's base price is under $50,000, any trim of it qualifies. A used vehicle can't be more than eight model years older than the current calendar year, and only individual residents can get the used-vehicle rebate. Businesses can't. Leases must run at least 36 months. An individual can receive two rebates over the life of the program, and a business, nonprofit, or government agency can receive six. A car bought from an out-of-state dealer still qualifies if you're a Delaware resident and register it here. Active-duty service members stationed in Delaware can use a letter of assignment as proof of residence.

How to claim it

The easiest route is a participating dealer, which deducts the rebate from the price and has to show it on your paperwork. Otherwise, apply yourself at driveelectricdelaware.org within 90 days of the purchase or lease date. First you need a state Supplier ID from the Division of Accounting's eSupplier portal. Applications without one are rejected, and getting the ID can take several days, so start early. You'll also need the sales invoice or lease agreement. Payment usually arrives 8 to 10 weeks after approval. DNREC notes that the rebate may count as taxable income at the federal and state level.

The added registration fee

Since October 1, 2025, Delaware has charged alternative-fuel vehicles a yearly fee on top of the regular $40 registration, under 21 Del. C. § 2151A (House Bill 164 of 2025). For vehicles of 6,000 pounds GVWR or less, the fee is $110 for a battery-electric car, $85 for a plug-in hybrid, and $60 for a conventional hybrid. Electric vehicles between 6,001 and 10,000 pounds pay $150. The DMV says the fee is meant to make up for falling motor fuel tax revenue and the extra road wear from heavier vehicles. An electric motorcycle pays the $15 motorcycle rate.

One benefit: a vehicle that runs only on electricity doesn't need an emissions test, although it still gets the safety inspection when it's due.

Charging at home and at work

Single-family homes. DNREC doesn't fund home chargers. The Sustainable Energy Utility's Energize Delaware program does, for July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. It covers up to 50 percent of the cost of a Level 2 charger and installation, or up to 90 percent for income-eligible households. It also pays half the cost of a needed panel upgrade, up to $2,500. You must be the electric account holder. The charger has to be new and installed by a licensed electrician (do-it-yourself installs and portable cords don't qualify), and it must pass a third-party electrical inspection. Apply within 60 days of installation. Questions go to (888) 735-1510.

Apartments, condos, and workplaces. DNREC's charging equipment rebates have $2 million available for Level 2 chargers installed after May 1, 2026, at buildings with four or more units, workplaces, fleets, and public sites. Multi-family projects can get up to $5,000 per single-port charger or $10,000 per dual-port charger, capped at $75,000 per site. In priority census tracts, the program covers up to 90 percent of costs. You need pre-approval, which DNREC answers within 10 business days, and the project must be finished within a year.

New construction. State law (Title 16, Chapter 80) has required new single-family homes to include at least one EV-capable parking space since January 1, 2024. Since 2025, new multifamily developments must have chargers installed at 5 percent of their spaces, and another 10 percent must be EV-capable. Municipalities with more than 30,000 residents must have a permit process for curbside residential chargers and must decide on applications within 90 days.
