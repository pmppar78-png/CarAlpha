Washington's insurance requirement

RCW 46.30.010 calls driving on Washington highways a privilege granted by the state, and it says the Legislature recognizes the threat uninsured drivers pose to the people of the state. To address that threat, the chapter requires everyone driving a Washington-registered vehicle to meet its financial responsibility rules.

Under RCW 46.30.020, you can't operate a vehicle that's subject to registration under chapter 46.16A RCW unless one of these applies:

  • You're insured under a motor vehicle liability policy with at least the limits in RCW 46.29.090.
  • You're self-insured under RCW 46.29.630.
  • You're covered by a certificate of deposit under RCW 46.29.550.
  • You're covered by a liability bond of at least the RCW 46.29.090 amounts.

Minimum liability limits

RCW 46.29.090 sets the limits, exclusive of interest and costs:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in any one accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in any one accident, subject to the per-person limit
  • $10,000 for injury to or destruction of others' property in any one accident

The policy or bond has to come from an insurer or surety authorized to do business in Washington, with an exception in subsection (2) for some out-of-state policies where the company gives the director of licensing a power of attorney to accept service.

Self-insurance, deposits and bonds

The Department of Licensing (DOL) says you may qualify for self-insurance if you have 26 or more vehicles. A certificate of deposit must be for at least $60,000. You apply with a Financial Responsibility Application and Affidavit, and if you qualify, DOL asks for the assignment form and financial statements within 30 days. A liability bond must also be for at least $60,000 and filed by a surety company authorized in Washington. For each option, DOL lists what the proof you carry must show.

Proof of insurance

RCW 46.30.030 requires an insurer to give you an identification card whenever it issues or renews a policy, and one for each covered vehicle if you ask. The card can be paper or electronic, including an image on a phone. Per DOL, the card shows the insurer's name, the policy number, the effective and expiration dates, and the year, make and model of the insured vehicles or the insured driver's name. The word "fleet" may be used for more than five vehicles owned by the same person or business.

If you show proof on a phone, RCW 46.30.020(1)(e) limits the officer to viewing the proof of insurance and nothing else on the device. You take on liability for any damage to the device when you hand it over.

Penalties

Not showing proof when an officer asks creates a presumption that you're uninsured, and failing to provide proof is a traffic infraction. DOL says driving without the required insurance could bring a fine of $550 or more. If you're at fault in a collision without insurance and don't pay the resulting damages, your license may be suspended. Knowingly giving an officer or court false evidence, including an expired or canceled policy, bond or certificate, is a misdemeanor under RCW 46.30.040.

If you were insured when cited, you can bring written evidence to the court or violations bureau, in person or by mail before your appearance date, and the citation is dismissed. The court may charge $25 in administrative costs.

Exceptions and out-of-state vehicles

Chapter 46.30 doesn't govern vehicles covered by RCW 46.16A.170, common or contract carriers registered with the Utilities and Transportation Commission, motor-driven cycles, mopeds or wheeled all-terrain vehicles. If you drive a vehicle registered in another state that requires insurance, you must show the proof that state requires when an officer asks.

Underinsured motorist coverage

Under RCW 48.22.030, every liability policy on a vehicle registered or principally garaged in Washington must include underinsured motorist coverage. That coverage protects you against underinsured, hit-and-run and phantom vehicles. Bodily injury coverage has to match your liability limits unless you reject all or part of it in writing. A named insured or spouse can reject it in writing, and it won't be added back at renewal unless requested in writing. The policy can carry a deductible of up to $300 for property damage from a hit-and-run or phantom vehicle, and up to $100 in other underinsured property damage cases. A phantom vehicle claim needs corroborating evidence beyond the insured's own testimony and a report to law enforcement within 72 hours.

Personal injury protection

Insurers must offer personal injury protection (PIP) with each new or renewed policy under RCW 48.22.085, and a named insured can reject it in writing. RCW 48.22.095 sets the minimum PIP benefits an insurer must offer for each insured:

  • Medical and hospital benefits of $10,000
  • A $2,000 funeral expense benefit
  • Income continuation of $10,000, up to $200 a week
  • Loss of services of $5,000, up to $200 a week

A written rejection binds all levels of coverage and everyone who would have been covered.
