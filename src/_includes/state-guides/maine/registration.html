Town office first, then registration

In Maine, you pay the annual excise tax at the town office where you live before you register a vehicle. If your town doesn't issue registrations, take the excise receipt to a Bureau of Motor Vehicles branch office. The BMV's passenger vehicle registration page explains the steps for each kind of purchase. You'll need the white and yellow copies of your registration application and excise receipt, plus proof of insurance.

How the excise tax works

According to Maine Revenue Services, the excise tax is based on the vehicle's age and the manufacturer's suggested retail price, not what you paid. The rate drops each January 1:

  • Year 1: 24 mills per dollar of MSRP (2.4%)
  • Year 2: 17.5 mills
  • Year 3: 13.5 mills
  • Year 4: 10 mills
  • Year 5: 6.5 mills
  • Year 6 and later: 4 mills

In the agency's example, a three-year-old vehicle with a $19,500 MSRP owes $263.25. Under 36 M.R.S. § 1482, the minimum tax is $5. For a new vehicle, the tax collector will ask for the window sticker (the Monroney label) or a copy. For used cars, towns look up the MSRP in pricing guides, and many use software that finds it from the VIN. The town keeps the money, and it typically goes toward local roads.

What to bring

  • From a Maine dealer: the dealer's bill of sale or sales agreement, and the blue title application for vehicles 25 model years old or newer.
  • From a private seller or an out-of-state dealer: a signed bill of sale, and the title signed over from the previous owner for vehicles 25 model years old or newer. You'll pay Maine's 5.5% sales tax on the purchase price and the $33 title application fee.
  • New residents: your out-of-state title (for vehicles 25 model years old or newer), out-of-state registration, the name and address of any lienholder, and a Maine use tax form showing you're exempt from sales tax. You'll also pay the $33 title application fee.

The 30-day rule for new residents

Under 29-A M.R.S. § 351, you must register your vehicle in Maine within 30 days of establishing residency. Driving an unregistered vehicle after that is a traffic infraction with a fine of up to $50 for a first offense between 30 and 150 days after you moved, and up to $500 for a second or later offense. After 150 days it becomes a Class E crime. If you're stopped within 30 days after your registration expired, you get a warning instead of a summons. You then have 10 business days to renew, and the renewal keeps the same expiration month and costs a full year's fee.

Registration and plate fees

The BMV's registration fee table lists a yearly fee of $35 for a standard passenger vehicle. Other common amounts:

  • Motorcycles and autocycles: $21
  • Low-speed vehicles: $25
  • Antique autos and street rods: $30
  • Mopeds: $9
  • Rental vehicles and vehicles for hire: $70
  • Trailers up to 2,000 pounds registered weight: $10.50 or $21
  • Specialty plates such as Black Bear, Lobster and Sportsman: $35 or $37

Commercial vehicles, motor homes and farm trucks are charged by weight chart. Several plates for veterans and public agencies carry no fee. Vanity plates cost an extra $25 at BMV branch offices, and the BMV has an online tool for checking whether a plate is available.

Insurance at registration

Under 29-A M.R.S. § 1605(2), every vehicle you register must carry at least the state minimum coverage, which is 50/100/25 plus medical payments and towing coverage. You can show proof at registration electronically, including as an image on a phone. A trailer, camp trailer or mobile home registered to you doesn't need its own proof if your vehicle policy already covers it. Our Maine insurance page explains the required coverage in more detail.

Annual inspection

Under 29-A M.R.S. § 1751, any vehicle required to be registered in Maine must be inspected once a year. The Maine State Police license the dealerships, service stations and garages that do the inspections. Inspectors check the body, brakes, exhaust, glass, horn, lights and other equipment, and a station can charge no more than $12.50.

Vehicles registered in Cumberland County need an annual enhanced inspection, which adds a fuel cap test for 1974 and newer gasoline vehicles and an on-board diagnostics check for 1996 and newer models. The maximum fee is $15.50 for pre-1996 models and $18.50 for 1996 and newer. Owners in other counties can ask for the enhanced inspection too. You pay the fee whether or not the vehicle passes.
