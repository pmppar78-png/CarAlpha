Alabama EV incentives in 2026

Alabama has no state rebate or tax credit for buying or leasing an electric car. The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center list for Alabama shows no consumer purchase incentive. What the state has done is the reverse: it charges EV owners an extra yearly fee and spends part of that money on public charging. For a driver in Alabama, the money that's actually available comes from the electric utility.

Alabama's EV registration fee

Under Ala. Code § 40-12-242, added by the Rebuild Alabama Act in 2019, you pay an extra fee on top of the standard tag each year:

  • Battery-electric vehicle: $203 a year.
  • Plug-in hybrid: $103 a year.

The fee began at $200 and $100. It rises by $3 every four years, starting in 2023, which is why the current amounts end in 3. It's meant to stand in for the gasoline tax an EV driver doesn't pay. A conventional hybrid that can't plug in doesn't pay it.

Some of the money is set aside for chargers. Part of the fee funds the Alabama Department of Transportation's Electric Transportation Infrastructure Grant program, which supports public charging along Alabama roads.

State charging programs

The Alabama Department of Economic and Community Affairs (ADECA) runs the state's charger grants. That includes the Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure grant program and the charging projects funded through Alabama's share of the Volkswagen emissions settlement. These grants go to cities, businesses, and other site hosts. Individual drivers can't apply. They are worth knowing about mostly because they decide where fast chargers show up along the interstates. ADECA lists current rounds on its website.

Utility programs

Alabama Power has the two most useful programs for a homeowner:

  • Home charger rebate. A one-time $500 rebate for a Level 2 charger at a single-family home. You need to own or lease a battery-electric or plug-in hybrid vehicle and install the charger within 90 days of the purchase date. The limit is one rebate per site. Terms are on Alabama Power's electric vehicles page.
  • EV night charging discount. The Plug-In Electric Vehicle rate rider takes 1.7155 cents off each kilowatt-hour you use between 9 p.m. and 5 a.m. It's available to customers on the FD, FD-D, RTA, or RTA-Energy rates, and you have to re-enroll every year. Call 1-800-990-2726 to sign up.

In north Alabama, many homes are served by local power companies that buy from the Tennessee Valley Authority. Huntsville Utilities is one of them. TVA works with these distributors on EV charging programs, and the AFDC lists TVA as an incentive source for Alabama. Programs vary from one distributor to the next, so check your own utility's website. Rural electric cooperatives set their own EV rates and rebates too.
