Registration fees by fuel economy

Oregon DMV sets passenger vehicle registration fees by the combined miles-per-gallon rating assigned to the vehicle's VIN by the manufacturer. You can look up that rating at fueleconomy.gov. Regular passenger registration lasts two years. DMV's fee page lists these two-year fees:

VehicleTwo-year registration
0–19 MPG$126
20–39 MPG$136
40 MPG or higher, not enrolled in OReGO$216
All-electric, not enrolled in OReGO$376
Enrolled in OReGO$86
Motorcycle or moped$88

OReGO is Oregon's road usage charge program, where drivers pay for the miles they drive instead of the per-gallon gas tax. DMV says that starting July 1, 2027, drivers of used EVs will pay an $86 two-year fee at renewal and choose between a flat fee or paying by the mile. That change takes effect January 1, 2028 for new EVs and July 1, 2028 for hybrids and plug-in hybrids.

A new passenger vehicle with a manufacturer's certificate of origin gets a four-year registration, and both the registration fee and any county fees are doubled.

County fees

Some counties add their own registration fee, based on where you live or where the vehicle stays:

  • Multnomah County: $112
  • Washington County or Clackamas County: $60

Title fees

You must title a vehicle in Oregon to register it. Title fees for passenger vehicles and trucks of 26,000 pounds GVWR or less also follow the MPG rating:

VehicleTitle fee
0–19 MPG$101
20–39 MPG$106
40 MPG or higher$116
All-electric (any vehicle type)$192

The same fees apply to original, duplicate and replacement titles, so there's no separate duplicate title charge. Light trailers, travel trailers, motorcycles, mopeds, motor homes, buses, campers, park model RVs and ATVs pay $101. The heavy vehicle title fee is $90, and a salvage title is $27.

When you buy an Oregon-titled vehicle, you have 30 days from the sale to apply for title. A transfer filed 31 to 60 days after the sale costs an extra $25, and one filed after 60 days costs an extra $50.

Plates and other fees

  • New plates at initial registration: $26 for a two-plated vehicle, $13 for a one-plated vehicle.
  • Moving Oregon plates to another vehicle: $30, plus registration and county fees if you don't own the vehicle the plates came from.
  • Replacement plates: $12, in addition to plate fees. Replacement stickers alone are $10.
  • VIN inspection in Oregon: $9.
  • Light vehicle trip permit: $35 for 21 days.

Vehicle privilege and use taxes

The Oregon Department of Revenue collects two taxes of one-half of 1% of the retail price of a taxable vehicle. The Vehicle Privilege Tax is owed by Oregon dealers for the privilege of selling vehicles, and a dealer may pass the amount on to you as an itemized charge. The Vehicle Use Tax applies to vehicles bought from dealers outside Oregon that must be registered and titled here.

A vehicle is taxable if it was bought from a dealer, was driven 7,500 miles or less (or sold with a certificate of origin if it has no odometer), has a GVWR of 26,000 pounds or less, and has never been registered or titled in Oregon, except as a dealer demonstrator. Cash down payments and trade-ins don't reduce the price used to figure the tax.

For an out-of-state dealer purchase, the use tax is due within 30 days. If the dealer didn't collect it, you file online with the Department of Revenue, which emails a certificate with an "L number" that you present to DMV. Late payment brings a 5% penalty, and not filing within 30 days of purchase brings a 20% penalty, plus interest.

How to title and register

DMV's title and registration page says to mail your application to DMV Services, 1905 Lana Ave NE, Salem, OR 97314, or bring it to a DMV office. You'll need:

  • the original title or ownership document, or the certificate of origin for a new vehicle
  • original lien releases from any earlier security interest holders
  • an odometer disclosure, if required, and
  • a $9 VIN inspection if the vehicle has an out-of-state or foreign title. It can be done at your DMV title appointment.

If you have a loan on the vehicle, contact your lienholder, which may submit the application to DMV for you.

Emissions testing

If you register a vehicle in a DEQ area, DMV says the vehicle generally must pass a DEQ emissions test unless it's exempt, newer vehicles are exempt, and the emissions certificate is generally valid for six months. DEQ's Clean Air Stations accept cash, PIN-less debit or credit cards, or money orders, and can collect your DMV renewal fees along with the test fee if you bring your renewal form with the insurance and signature sections completed.

Older diesel trucks

If your home or business address is in Clackamas, Multnomah or Washington County, DMV restricts titles and registrations for certain older diesel trucks unless they have approved retrofit technology certified by DEQ. Since January 1, 2025, DMV hasn't issued titles for medium-duty trucks with a 2009 or older diesel engine or heavy-duty trucks with a 2006 or older diesel engine. Starting January 1, 2029, DMV won't issue or renew registration for medium-duty trucks with a 2009 or older diesel engine.
