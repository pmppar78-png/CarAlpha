Vermont's lemon law

Vermont's lemon law is Chapter 115 of Title 9 of the Vermont Statutes, §§ 4170 through 4181. In § 4170, the Legislature says manufacturers should give consumers a "speedy and less costly" resolution of warranty problems, and should provide a refund or an acceptable replacement whenever they can't make a vehicle conform to its warranty. Disputes go to arbitration, either before the state's Vermont Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board or through the manufacturer's own program, at the consumer's choice. Dealers can't be sued under the chapter.

Who and what is covered

Under § 4171, a "consumer" includes:

  • The purchaser of a new motor vehicle, other than for resale
  • The lessee of a new vehicle under a written lease of two years or more
  • Anyone the vehicle is transferred to while the express warranty is in effect
  • Anyone else entitled to enforce the warranty

Government entities, and businesses that register or lease three or more vehicles, aren't consumers under the chapter.

A "motor vehicle" is a passenger motor vehicle purchased, leased or registered in Vermont. The definition excludes tractors, highway-building equipment, snowmobiles, motorcycles, motor-driven cycles, the living portion of recreation vehicles, and trucks with a gross vehicle weight rating over 12,000 pounds. A vehicle counts as "new" while it's still under the manufacturer's express warranty.

The manufacturer's repair duty and repair orders

If you report a nonconformity to the manufacturer, its agent or an authorized dealer during the warranty term, § 4172(c) requires the manufacturer to make whatever repairs are necessary, even if the work happens after the warranty expires.

Section 4172(d) requires the dealer or agent to give you a written repair order every time. Each time you bring the vehicle in, you should get a written summary of your complaint and a fully itemized statement of the work done, including examination, parts and labor. The shop can't refuse to give you that paperwork, and those records are what the presumption depends on.

When the presumption applies

Section 4172(g) presumes that a reasonable number of repair attempts have been made in either of these situations:

  • The same nonconformity, as identified on a written examination or repair order, has been repaired at least three times by the manufacturer, its agent or an authorized dealer. At least the first attempt has to fall within the express warranty term, and the problem must still exist.
  • The vehicle has been out of service for repair of one or more problems for a cumulative total of 30 or more calendar days during the express warranty term.

Section 4172(h) adds two conditions. Every repair attempt must be documented by a written examination or repair order, and the presumption covers three attempts at the same agent or dealer unless you show good cause for going somewhere else. A vehicle isn't counted as out of service if it's available to you for a major part of the day.

The warranty term and the 30-day period are extended for any time repairs weren't available because of war, invasion, strike, fire, flood or another natural disaster. During such an extension, the manufacturer must give you free use of a vehicle while yours is out of service.

Notifying the manufacturer and choosing a forum

Under § 4173(a), once there's been a reasonable attempt at repair, or the vehicle has been out of service for 30 or more cumulative calendar days, you notify the manufacturer and any lessor in writing. The manufacturer must give you the notice form when the vehicle is delivered. Section 4180 requires it to include a stamped, self-addressed notice in a form the Board approves, and the manufacturer can't hand that duty off to the dealer. Dealers must display notice of your rights under the chapter.

In the notice, you choose between the manufacturer's dispute settlement mechanism and the Vermont Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board. That choice is binding, and picking one forum rules out the other. You can't pursue a remedy under the chapter if you've stopped making your financing or lease payments.

A clause that waives, limits or disclaims your rights under the chapter, or requires you to keep it confidential, is void unless it's part of a settlement agreement or federal law requires otherwise.

The arbitration hearing

Section 4173(c) requires the hearing within 45 days after the manufacturer or the Board receives your notice. Either side can get up to 30 more days by showing good cause, and if the manufacturer asks for the extension, it must give you free use of a vehicle while yours is out of service. If the manufacturer doesn't contest the complaint, no hearing is needed. If you chose the manufacturer's program, the case is contested, and the hearing isn't held within 45 days without good cause, you're entitled to the relief you asked for.

Within the 45 days, and at least five days before the hearing, the manufacturer gets one final chance to repair the defect. If it doesn't finish that repair at least five days before the hearing, the right is waived. If you're satisfied with the repair, the arbitration ends, but you can ask to restart it within one year after the express warranty expires.

At a hearing, you must bring the vehicle to the hearing site so Board members can inspect or test-drive it.

The Vermont Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board

Under § 4174, the Governor appoints five members and three alternates to three-year terms. One member is a Vermont new car dealer, one is an automobile technician, and three have no direct involvement in the design, manufacture, distribution, sale or service of motor vehicles. The Board can subpoena witnesses and documents. It must decide within 30 days after a contested hearing, or within 30 days after the manufacturer's answer in an uncontested case.

Section 4175 says you pay no filing fee or costs to use the Board or the manufacturer's mechanism. If a dealer or its staff are called to testify or produce records, whoever asked for them arranges to compensate the dealer's actual expenses, and if you win, the manufacturer reimburses you for those costs.

Replacement or refund

If the manufacturer can't fix a covered defect that substantially impairs the vehicle's use, market value or safety after a reasonable number of attempts, § 4172(e) lets you choose, within 30 days of the Board's order, between:

  • A new replacement vehicle from the same manufacturer, if available, of comparable worth to the same make and model with all options and accessories, adjusted for model-year differences
  • A refund of the full purchase price, including trade-in and down payment credits, finance and credit charges, registration fees, similar charges, and incidental and consequential damages

The manufacturer can deduct a use allowance only for miles driven before the first repair attempt. The allowance is the purchase price multiplied by those miles over 100,000. The state refunds the matching share of Vermont purchase and use tax if you file a claim with the Commissioner of Motor Vehicles within 90 days of the order's effective date.

Leases follow § 4172(i). The lessee gets back the deposit and rental payments, minus a mileage-based use allowance and allocated tax payments. The lessor gets its purchase cost minus the lessee's payments, plus certain costs and 5% of the purchase cost, and the lease ends with no further charges to the lessee.

The manufacturer must pay within 30 days after receiving the Board's decision, or within 15 days of a final court judgment. If it doesn't complete the transaction by the order's effective date, you get an extra 10% of the total award under § 4173(f).

Under § 4172(f), the manufacturer's defenses are that the problem doesn't substantially impair use, market value or safety, or that it results from abuse, neglect, or unauthorized modifications or alterations by the consumer.

Appeals and enforcement

Under § 4176, the Board's decision is final unless a motion for reconsideration with new evidence is filed within 30 days after the consumer receives the decision. After that, a party can ask the Superior Court to vacate or modify it, within 30 days of delivery of the final decision. The court can do so only on clear and convincing proof of corruption, fraud or undue means, evident partiality or misconduct, the Board exceeding its powers, or a hearing conducted in a way that substantially prejudiced a party. A party who wins confirmation of a decision is awarded attorney's fees and costs.

A manufacturer, dealer or lessor that fails to comply with a Board decision commits an unfair or deceptive act under Vermont's consumer protection law, 9 V.S.A. chapter 63, according to § 4177.

Deadline and other rights

Section 4179 requires any proceeding under the chapter to begin within one year after the express warranty term expires. The chapter doesn't limit rights or remedies you have under other laws.

Returned vehicles are tracked under § 4181. A manufacturer or dealer reselling a vehicle returned under this law, or a similar law in another state, must disclose the return with a clearly visible window sticker, and the return must be printed on the certificate of title. A vehicle found to have a serious safety defect can't be resold in Vermont.
