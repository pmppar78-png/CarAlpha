Purchase incentives

The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists no Oklahoma rebate or income tax credit for buying a battery electric or plug-in hybrid car. Oklahoma's Alternative Fuel Vehicle Tax Credit under 68 O.S. § 2357.22 runs for tax years beginning before December 31, 2028, but the AFDC lists its eligible fuels as natural gas, propane and hydrogen. For those vehicles, the credit is capped by gross vehicle weight rating, starting at $5,500 for 6,000 pounds or less.

Annual EV and plug-in hybrid fees

Under 68 O.S. § 6511, EV and plug-in hybrid owners pay an annual fee in addition to standard registration. The AFDC lists these amounts:

Tax on public charging

Public EV chargers are subject to a tax of $0.03 per kilowatt-hour under 68 O.S. § 6504, and the owner or operator must state the tax separately on receipts. Chargers at private residences aren't taxed. Public chargers with a capacity under 50 kilowatts are exempt. Legacy chargers in operation before November 1, 2021, and public stations that have never charged a fee, are exempt until November 1, 2041.

A fee-charging station over 50 kilowatts that opened after November 1, 2021 must register with the Oklahoma Tax Commission and post its rate at the site, in its mobile app and on its website, if it has one. Rates can be per kilowatt-hour or by time. The Corporation Commission can inspect stations, and violations can bring fines of up to $500 per day per violation.

Grants and rebates for chargers and buses

  • ChargeOK: the Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) awards competitive grants of up to 80% of eligible costs for public DC fast chargers on designated EV corridors, and for DC fast or Level 2 chargers at destinations and community charging hubs. It's funded by Oklahoma's share of the Volkswagen Environmental Mitigation Trust.
  • Alternative Fuel School Bus program: DEQ rebates up to 45% of the cost of repowering or replacing an active diesel school bus with an engine model year of 2009 or older, including with an all-electric bus. Charging infrastructure is eligible, subject to caps. Applications go through Oklahoma's Clean Diesel Program.

Other rules

A vehicle powered wholly or partly by electricity, natural gas or hydrogen may exceed state gross and axle weight limits by up to 2,000 pounds, matching the extra weight of its battery or fuel system over a comparable diesel setup (47 O.S. § 14-109).

An entity that isn't a regulated utility can sell retail EV charging without being treated as a public utility (17 O.S. § 160.33).

Under House Bill 2158 of 2025, a manufacturer can't require a dealer to buy or lease EV chargers at the dealer's expense unless the dealer's franchise includes EV models or the dealer has told the manufacturer it will sell and service its EVs.

Medium-speed EVs, with top speeds over 30 mph but no more than 35 mph, must be registered. They may use roads posted up to 45 mph but not interstate highways.

The Oklahoma Department of Transportation (ODOT) was required to submit an annually updated NEVI deployment plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.
