Required coverage in Kentucky

Kentucky's Motor Vehicle Reparations Act, KRS Subtitle 304.39, dates to 1975. KRS 304.39-110 sets the minimum security a vehicle owner must carry. Liability can be written either way:

  • Split limits of $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, plus $25,000 for property damage
  • A single limit of $60,000 per accident covering both bodily injury and property damage

The coverage must apply throughout the United States, its territories and possessions, and Canada. The policy must also include basic reparation benefits. The Department of Insurance's advisory on its No-Fault Rejection Form adds that uninsured motorist coverage equal to the minimum bodily injury limits is required unless you reject it in a separate writing. Motorcycles need only the liability coverage.

Basic PIP and the no-fault system

Basic personal injury protection, also called basic reparation benefits, provides up to $10,000 per person per accident for medical expenses, lost wages and similar out-of-pocket costs from an injury. According to the Department of Insurance, the insurer of the vehicle you're riding in pays basic PIP, or the insurer of the vehicle that strikes you if you're a pedestrian, regardless of who caused the accident. The department's advisory says lost wages are paid up to $500 a week, and the benefit also covers replacement services and survivors' benefits. You can buy higher benefits and choose deductibles.

The other half of the system limits lawsuits. Everyone who registers, operates, maintains or uses a motor vehicle in Kentucky is deemed to accept limits on the right to sue and be sued. An injured person can't recover medical expenses, lost wages, other expenses or pain and suffering from the at-fault party unless the injury involves one of these:

  • More than $1,000 in medical expenses
  • A broken bone
  • Permanent disfigurement or permanent injury
  • Death

Rejecting the no-fault limits

Each person can reject the limits on their tort rights by filing the department's No-Fault Rejection Form under KRS 304.39-060. The rejection takes effect when the department receives it and stays in effect until you revoke it in writing. A rejection signed for a minor ends when the minor reaches the age of majority.

The department's advisory spells out the trade-offs. If you reject, you can't receive basic PIP benefits. The rejection applies to you in any vehicle, whether you own it or not, and there's no exception for a rejection signed as a condition of employment. To recover damages, you'll have to prove the other party was at fault, and any fault of your own reduces the amount. Your liability premium may be higher, because others can sue you for injuries below the thresholds. If everyone in a household rejects, the policy must include guest PIP for passengers and pedestrians. You can also buy basic PIP coverage back later.

A motorcycle owner or operator can file a rejection that applies only to motorcycles. PIP is optional for motorcycles. The department warns that a rider who doesn't buy it and doesn't file a rejection can't collect basic PIP from any source and can't recover the first $10,000 of a motorcycle injury claim from the at-fault party.

Underinsured motorist coverage

Under KRS 304.39-320, insurers must make underinsured motorist coverage available on request. It pays your uncompensated damages when a judgment against the other driver exceeds that driver's liability limits, up to the UIM limits on your own vehicle. Before you accept a settlement from the other driver's insurer that would leave a UIM claim, you must send written notice by certified or registered mail to every UIM insurer that covers you. Each has 30 days to consent or to preserve its subrogation rights by paying you the amount of the settlement offer.

Penalties for uninsured vehicles

Under KRS 304.99-060, an owner who doesn't keep the required security in force faces:

  • A fine of $500 to $1,000, up to 90 days in jail, or both
  • Revocation of the vehicle's registration and suspension of its plates for one year, or until proof is provided that security is in effect and will stay in effect
  • For a second or later offense within five years, revocation of the operator's license and up to 180 days in jail, a fine of $1,000 to $2,500, or both

A driver who operates a vehicle without security faces the same fine ranges, and an owner who is also the driver can be penalized under both provisions. For a first offense, a judge can reduce the penalty if you produce proof of insurance. For a repeat offense, you also need a receipt showing you paid at least six months of premium, and the court will call you back to show the policy was renewed. Letting that coverage lapse before six months is a class B misdemeanor.

Average costs

NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement puts Kentucky's combined average premium at $1,337.15, compared with $1,059.44 in 2020. The average expenditure was $1,149.83. The Insurance Research Council estimates that 14.1% of Kentucky drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 20th.
