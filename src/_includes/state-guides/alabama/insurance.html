Alabama's minimum auto insurance

Alabama is an at-fault (tort) state. The driver who causes a crash, and that driver's liability insurer, pays for the other side's injuries and property damage, and there is no personal injury protection requirement. The floor is set in Ala. Code § 32-7-6 and enforced through the Mandatory Liability Insurance law, §§ 32-7A-1 and following:

25/50/25: $25,000 bodily injury or death for one person, $50,000 for all injuries or deaths in one accident, and $25,000 property damage.

The Department of Revenue spells out two conditions that out-of-state movers miss: the policy has to be an Alabama policy, and it has to come from an insurer qualified to write auto liability coverage in Alabama. A bond or a cash deposit can substitute for a policy, but almost every private owner uses insurance.

Those limits pay other people, not you. A $25,000 property-damage limit is used up quickly by a newer pickup, and once the policy limit runs out, the rest of the bill is yours. Alabama law also requires insurers to offer uninsured motorist coverage with every liability policy. It stays on the policy unless you reject it in writing (§ 32-7-23). Given how many Alabama drivers carry nothing, that rejection form deserves a second look.

How many Alabama drivers are uninsured

The Insurance Research Council estimated that 16.8 percent of Alabama motorists were uninsured in 2023. That ranks Alabama 15th of 51 jurisdictions, above the 15.4 percent national figure. The Insurance Information Institute republishes the IRC study on its uninsured motorists page. The NAIC puts Alabama's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision, and comprehensive at $1,388.11, in Table 5 of its Auto Insurance Database supplement. That figure is an average across all insured vehicles. Your own quote will differ.

How Alabama checks your coverage

Alabama does not wait for a traffic stop. The Department of Revenue runs an online insurance verification system that matches registrations against insurer records. When it can't confirm coverage, it mails a questionnaire. If the answer doesn't check out or the letter comes back undeliverable, the registration is suspended. Buying a policy after the verification date, or selling the car, does not cancel a suspension that is already required (§ 32-7A-12(a)).

Penalties for driving uninsured

Driving without coverage is a Class C misdemeanor under § 32-7A-16. ADOR's insurance verification brochure lists the penalties:

  • First conviction: fine of up to $500.
  • Second or later conviction: fine of up to $1,000, a driver's license suspension of up to six months, or both. A second offense is a Class B misdemeanor under § 32-7A-12(d).

Separately, the vehicle registration is suspended. To reinstate it you need proof of current insurance and a fee:

  • First violation: $200, with no minimum suspension period.
  • Second or later violation within the preceding two registration years: $400. ADOR's insurance manual adds a mandatory four-month suspension.

You can pay at your county license plate office, any circuit clerk, or the Administrative Office of Courts. If you can show coverage was actually in force on the verification date, the suspension ends and no fee is owed (§ 32-7A-12(e)).

SR-22 in Alabama

The fee isn't the only consequence. After a first violation, ADOR sends the owner's name to the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency. ALEA can then require proof of future financial responsibility, usually an SR-22 certificate filed by your insurer, for one year under §§ 32-7-13 and 32-7-31. After a second violation the period is two registration years (§ 32-7A-12(c)–(d)). ALEA's reinstatement requirements say an SR-22 is needed only when your reinstatement notice calls for one. When it does, the certificate has to show Alabama coverage and stay in force through the date on the notice. A lapse during that period is reported to ALEA and can reopen the suspension. Not every insurer files SR-22s, so ask before you buy.

After a crash

Because Alabama is a fault state, you can claim against the other driver's liability policy, claim on your own collision or uninsured-motorist coverage, or sue. Your insurer and the other driver's will each decide fault, and you have to resolve a fault dispute with the insurers or in court.

Complaints: Alabama Department of Insurance

For a claim delay, a cancellation you think is improper, or an agent problem, first put your complaint to the insurer in writing. If that fails, go to ALDOI's Consumer Services Division, at 334-241-4141, 1-800-433-3966, or ConsumerServices@insurance.alabama.gov. Its mailing address is P.O. Box 303351, Montgomery, AL 36130-3351. File through the online complaint form, and include your policy and claim numbers. ALDOI forwards the complaint, and the company must answer the department. If it finds the company broke the policy terms or the law, it requires a correction.
