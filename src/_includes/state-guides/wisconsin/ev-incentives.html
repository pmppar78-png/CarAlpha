Hybrid and electric registration surcharges

Wisconsin adds surcharges to the regular registration fee for hybrid and electric automobiles, and for motor trucks registered at 8,000 pounds or less, under Wis. Stat. § 341.25(1)(L). A nonhybrid electric vehicle pays a $100 surcharge plus an additional $75, so $175 a year on top of the $85 automobile fee. A hybrid electric vehicle, including a plug-in hybrid, pays $75. The Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists this fee as enacted on September 21, 2017, and amended on June 30, 2023. Both types of vehicle must also display WisDOT decals identifying them as electric vehicles.

Charging tax

Under Wis. Stat. §§ 77.9971 and 77.9972, as summarized by AFDC, anyone who delivers electricity from a charger into an EV's battery pays an excise tax of $0.03 per kilowatt-hour. The tax has to be added to the station operator's selling price. Owners and operators of chargers subject to the tax must register with the Department of Revenue before charging any vehicle, and exemptions and other conditions may apply.

Under § 196.01(5), a business that provides EV charging isn't a public utility and isn't subject to restrictions on reselling electricity.

Government-owned chargers

Under §§ 16.9565 and 66.0442, a state or local agency may own, operate, manage or lease Level 1, Level 2 and DC fast chargers for its fleet. Publicly available chargers run by an agency must be Level 1 or Level 2 and free to use. An agency may also let eligible electric providers own and operate a public charger of any of those levels on government property, subject to further requirements.

Infrastructure programs

Section 85.53 lets WisDOT establish and run an EV infrastructure program that funds charging projects with local, state or federal money, according to AFDC. Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, WisDOT had to submit an annually updated EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.

Property owners can finance EV chargers through Property Assessed Clean Energy (PACE) programs, which cities, villages, towns and counties may set up under § 66.06277. Wisconsin also signed the REV Midwest memorandum of understanding with Illinois, Indiana, Michigan and Minnesota in 2021 to coordinate charger siting along commercial routes and medium- and heavy-duty fleet electrification.

Low-speed vehicles

A municipality may allow low-speed vehicles that meet the federal standards in 49 CFR 571.500 on its roads with speed limits of 35 mph or less, under §§ 349.26 and 340.01(27h). Golf carts aren't low-speed vehicles. A low-speed vehicle's registration is $23 for two years.
