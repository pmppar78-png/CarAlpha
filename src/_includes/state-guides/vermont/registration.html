Who has to register

Vermont's registration rules are in Chapter 7 of Title 23. Under 23 V.S.A. § 301(b), residents register each year any motor vehicle they own or lease for more than 30 days and drive, unless it's already registered in Vermont. A resident is someone living in the state who intends to make it home, either permanently or for an indefinite number of years. Temporary residents, such as students, seasonal workers and people on fixed-term contracts, may register their vehicles here if they use them in Vermont regularly.

If you move to Vermont from another jurisdiction, § 301(d) gives you 60 days after the move to register your vehicle. No one may drive a motor vehicle, or pull a trailer, on a Vermont highway unless the vehicle is registered under the chapter.

Applying

Section 303 says the Commissioner of Motor Vehicles or an authorized agent registers a vehicle when you file an application on the Commissioner's form. The application has to show that the vehicle is properly equipped and in good mechanical condition, and it must come with the registration fee and whatever proof of ownership the Commissioner reasonably requires. Except for state and municipal vehicles, the person registering a vehicle and its titled owner have to be the same.

When a registration is processed electronically, § 305(d) makes a receipt available to view or print. That receipt serves as a temporary registration for 10 days after the transaction, and you can show it to an officer on a phone or other device.

How long a registration lasts

Section 302 sets a standard registration period of 12 months from the date of issue. Owners with several vehicles can choose to have all their registrations expire on the same date, with the fee prorated for any period beyond the annual 12 months, up to 24 months. Under § 305(a), plates become void one year from the first day of the month after the month they were issued, unless a longer first period is authorized or you renew.

Registration fees

For a pleasure car, the statutory fee under § 361(a) is $89 a year or $163 for two years. The DMV's Registration Fees page lists the amounts you actually pay, which include the $2 Clean Air Fund fee and, for plug-in vehicles, the EV infrastructure fee:

  • Gas or diesel car: $91 for one year, $167 for two years
  • Plug-in hybrid: $135.50 for one year, $256 for two years
  • Battery electric: $178 for one year, $341 for two years
  • Other fuel: $159.25 for one year, $292.25 for two years

The same amounts apply to jitneys, motorhomes and trucks up to 6,099 pounds. A motorcycle costs $58 for one year when it runs on gas. The page also lists $15 for a single replacement plate, $29 for a set of two, $20 to replace a registration and $30 to transfer one. A vanity plate adds $58 a year.

The DMV also collects an $8 motor vehicle warranty fee on the registration of each new motor vehicle under 23 V.S.A. § 476. Trailers, tractors, snowmobiles, motorcycles and trucks over 12,000 pounds gross vehicle weight don't pay it.

Title fees

Chapter 21 of Title 23 covers titles, and § 2002 lists the fees:

  • Certificate of title, including a salvage title or a title after a transfer: $42
  • Each security interest noted on a title: $14
  • Duplicate or corrected title: $42
  • Certificate of search of DMV records, per vehicle: $27

A veteran who bought a vehicle with help from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, and whose vehicle is exempt from registration fees under § 378, pays no title fee. If a required title document reaches the Commissioner more than 10 days late, the Commissioner collects a penalty equal to the fee for that transaction.

Purchase and use tax

Vermont collects a purchase and use tax on vehicles under Chapter 219 of Title 32. Section 8903 sets the tax at six percent of the taxable cost of a pleasure car, motorcycle, motor home, or a vehicle up to 10,099 pounds registered as a truck under § 367, other than a farm truck. For other motor vehicles, it's six percent or $2,486, whichever is smaller. The use tax applies when you first register a vehicle or transfer a registration to it, unless you've already paid the purchase tax.

Taxable cost is the purchase price. If you've already paid tax on a vehicle you trade in, sell, or receive an insurance payment for, § 8902 lets you exclude its trade-in allowance from the taxable cost of the replacement. A private-sale price can be excluded up to the vehicle's clean trade-in value if the sale happens within three months of the purchase. Under § 8907, if a vehicle isn't bought in Vermont or the price doesn't reflect its actual value, the Commissioner can set the taxable cost at its clean trade-in value from J.D. Power Values or a comparable publication.

Annual inspection

Under 23 V.S.A. § 1222, every motor vehicle registered in Vermont needs a safety and visual emissions inspection once a year. Vehicles 16 model years old or newer also get an emissions or on-board diagnostic (OBD) systems inspection each year. A vehicle that hasn't been inspected in Vermont must be inspected within 15 days after it's registered here.

Inspections happen at garages and service stations the Commissioner designates. For a passenger vehicle, the safety check is limited to parts and systems relevant to safe operation, and a vehicle fails only if a condition poses or may pose a danger to the driver or other highway users. Each station sets an hourly or flat-rate fee, subject to the Commissioner's approval, and posts it beside its inspection certificate.

You can't drive a vehicle without a valid inspection certificate. If you're cited within 14 days after your sticker expires, the civil penalty is no more than $5. Exhibition vehicles of model year 1940 or earlier are exempt from inspection, but they must still be equipped as originally manufactured and kept in good mechanical condition.
