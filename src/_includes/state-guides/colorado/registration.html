Registration goes through your county

The Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles sets the rules and runs the DRIVES title and registration system. In practice, though, you title and register a vehicle at your county motor vehicle office, run by the county clerk and recorder, or online where your county allows it. Your county also collects the sales tax and the specific ownership tax, which is why two neighbors with the same car can pay different totals if they live in different cities.

Deadlines

  • New residents: 90 days. Under C.R.S. 42-3-103(4)(a), as summarized on DMV form DR 2504, you become a Colorado resident on the first of these dates: you start owning or operating a business here, you get a job here, or you've lived here continuously for 90 days. You then have 90 days to register, even if the car is still registered in another state.
  • Vehicles you buy: 60 days. Title and register a newly purchased vehicle within 60 days. Late fees start on day 61.
  • Renewals. Registration is renewed every year. You get a one-month grace period after it expires.

Late fees are $25 for each month or part of a month, up to $100, or $10 a month for trailers.

Bringing a car from another state

Here's what new residents need, per the DMV's Welcome to Colorado guidance:

  • The out-of-state title, or the current out-of-state registration if a lender holds the title.
  • Proof of insurance that meets Colorado's requirements.
  • Secure and verifiable identification, plus the residency information on DR 2504.
  • A VIN verification on form DR 2698, which is required for vehicles last titled out of state (a brand-new vehicle sold with a manufacturer's statement of origin is the main exception).
  • An emissions test, if you live in the testing area and the vehicle is old enough to need one (see below).

Specific ownership tax: the biggest line on the bill

Colorado doesn't charge personal property tax on vehicles. It charges the specific ownership tax (SOT) instead, every year, whether or not the car is driven. According to the General Assembly's Legislative Council issue brief, a passenger vehicle's taxable value is set once, at 85% of MSRP. It doesn't change when the car is sold. A rate based on the vehicle's year of service is then applied (C.R.S. 42-3-107):

  • Year 1: 2.10%
  • Year 2: 1.50%
  • Year 3: 1.20%
  • Year 4: 0.90%
  • Years 5 through 9: 0.45%
  • Year 10 and later: a flat $3

The Council's own example is a three-year-old car with a $30,000 MSRP. Its taxable value is $25,500, and at 1.2% the SOT is $306. In the first year, the same car would owe about $536, which is why registering a new car in Colorado costs so much more than renewing an old one. SOT money goes to counties, cities, school districts, and special districts. Active-duty service members stationed in Colorado and certain veterans are exempt.

Other fees on the registration

A DMV fact sheet explains the smaller charges you'll see itemized on your receipt:

  • a weight-based base registration fee
  • the FASTER road safety and bridge safety surcharges
  • an age-of-vehicle fee: $12 under seven years old, $10 for seven to nine years, and $7 for ten years or older
  • a $4 clerk hire fee and a county road and bridge fee
  • small emissions-account, emergency medical services, peace officer training, and insurance-database fees.

A title costs $7.20. New plates cost $7.38 for an embossed set or $11.27 for a digital set. Electric vehicles also pay extra fees, covered in the EV guide.

Sales tax is paid to the county when you register. A private seller doesn't collect it. The state rate is 2.9%, and county, city, and special-district taxes are added based on where you live, according to the Department of Revenue's taxation division.

Private sales

In Colorado, plates stay with the seller when a car changes hands, so the seller removes them at the sale (see the DMV's buying and selling guide). The seller signs over the title, completes the odometer disclosure, and gives the buyer a bill of sale. The DMV recommends its secure bill of sale, form DR 2173, and requires it if the price isn't written on the title. The buyer then has 60 days to title and register.

Emissions testing

Gasoline vehicles registered in all or parts of Adams, Arapahoe, Boulder, Broomfield, Denver, Douglas, Jefferson, Larimer, and Weld counties are tested every two years. Vehicles are exempt for their first seven model years, and all-electric vehicles never need a test. If you live outside those areas, no test is required. Colorado has no general safety inspection.
