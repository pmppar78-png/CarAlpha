No state purchase incentive right now

Georgia doesn't currently offer drivers a rebate or income tax credit for buying or leasing an electric car. The state incentives listed for Georgia by the Alternative Fuels Data Center are aimed at businesses, fleets and charging site hosts.

  • Charging station credit: an eligible business can claim an income tax credit of 10% of the cost of buying or leasing and installing a qualified EV charging station in Georgia, up to $2,500, under O.C.G.A. § 48-7-40.16. Unused credit carries forward for up to three years.
  • Conversion credit: businesses can claim 10% of the cost, up to $2,500 per vehicle, to convert a vehicle to run only on electricity, natural gas, propane or hydrogen.
  • Manufacturing job credits: companies that make battery, biofuel and EV components can earn a five-year credit for each new full-time job.

If you're shopping for a car for personal use, none of these lowers your price at the dealership.

Georgia's annual alternative fuel vehicle fee

Georgia charges an extra yearly fee because EV drivers don't pay motor fuel tax. DOR adjusts it each July with a formula tied to average fuel efficiency and the Consumer Price Index. Its July 2026 bulletin sets these amounts for registrations that begin or renew from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027:

  • Non-commercial vehicles: $238.59
  • Commercial vehicles: $357.98
  • Low-speed vehicles: $111.64

For comparison, the non-commercial fee was $234.97 for the year that ended June 30, 2026.

Who pays depends on how the car is powered:

  • Fully electric cars always owe the fee, whatever plate they carry. Swapping to a standard or specialty plate doesn't avoid it.
  • Plug-in hybrids and flex-fuel cars owe it only if the owner chooses an alternative fuel vehicle plate.
  • Conventional hybrids and vehicles running mainly on natural gas or propane never owe it.

The DOR FAQ adds that the fee comes on top of normal registration and has no exemptions, so government and farm vehicles pay it too. An alternative fuel vehicle plate also carries a one-time $25 manufacturing fee and a $35 special tag fee each year, in addition to the $20 registration. Your renewal notice should show the fee automatically.

Put together, a fully electric car on an alternative fuel plate pays $293.59 a year in registration, tag and EV fees before any ad valorem tax, plus the $25 manufacturing fee the first time.

Emissions testing

In the 13 metro Atlanta counties with emissions testing, Georgia's Clean Air Force treats a vehicle that runs only on battery electricity as a dedicated alternative fuel vehicle that never needs a test. Gasoline hybrids and plug-in hybrids still test, so a plug-in owner in Cobb or Gwinnett should plan on the annual inspection like any gas car.

Public charging rules coming in 2027

From January 1, 2027, under O.C.G.A. §§ 10-1-220 to 10-1-228, every public EV charging station in Georgia must be able to measure and display the electricity it delivers per kilowatt-hour. DOR can inspect stations for accuracy, and violators face fines of up to $1,000. In practice, you should be able to see a per-kWh price and amount at public chargers instead of only time-based charges. A separate law says businesses that sell EV charging aren't treated as public utilities for that reason alone.
