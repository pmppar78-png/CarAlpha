State incentives: what's closed and what's still open

California's best-known EV incentive is gone. The Clean Vehicle Rebate Project (CVRP), which gave rebates of a few thousand dollars on new EVs for more than a decade, closed to new applications on November 8, 2023. If a dealer or website still quotes a CVRP rebate, it's out of date.

The carpool-lane perk ended too. The DMV's Clean Air Vehicle decal program ended at midnight on September 30, 2025, when the federal authority behind it under 23 U.S.C. § 166 expired. California changed its own law hoping Congress would extend that authority, but it didn't. The DMV stopped taking decal applications on August 29, 2025. Since October 1, 2025, EV drivers have to meet the posted occupancy rules for HOV lanes and pay full tolls on express lanes.

What's left is mainly aimed at lower-income buyers:

  • Clean Cars 4 All. Run by the California Air Resources Board through local air districts, CC4A helps lower-income residents in priority communities retire an older, high-polluting car. In exchange, they can buy or lease a new or used plug-in hybrid, battery-electric, or fuel-cell vehicle, or a zero-emission motorcycle. Other mobility options are available instead of a car. CARB says it's offered through five participating air districts. Everywhere else, the statewide Driving Clean Assistance Program (DCAP) provides CC4A-style help along with a financing assistance program. Apply through your air district or DCAP before you buy.
  • Bay Area: Clean Cars for All. The Bay Area Air Quality Management District offers income-eligible residents grants of up to $12,000 to replace a model-year 2007 or older car with an EV, plug-in hybrid, hybrid, or fuel-cell vehicle, new or used. The old car must go to an authorized dismantler. EV and PHEV buyers can get up to $2,000 more for a Level 2 home charger (AFDC).
  • El Dorado County. The county air district offers full-time residents up to $599 toward an eligible plug-in vehicle and up to $300 for a Level 2 charger. Its low-income program pays up to $10,000 with a scrapped car, or $7,500 without one, plus a $2,000 charger or public-charging bonus (AFDC).
  • Antelope Valley. The local air district offers up to $500 toward an EV bought or leased from a dealer within its boundaries (AFDC).

Program funding comes and goes, so check the administering agency's own site for current amounts before you count on any of these.

The road improvement fee

California charges zero-emission vehicles a yearly Road Improvement Fee to make up for the gas tax they don't pay (Vehicle Code § 9250.6). It applies to model-year 2020 and newer ZEVs. The DMV's fee schedule currently lists it at $121. The law set it at $100 and adjusts it each year for inflation. The fee isn't charged on the first registration of a new ZEV bought from a licensed dealer, so it starts with your first renewal. Plug-in hybrids don't pay it.

EVs still pay every other registration charge: the base fee, the value-based Transportation Improvement Fee, and the 0.65 percent Vehicle License Fee. Because those last two depend on the car's value, a pricier EV pays more. Details are on our California registration page. One upside: fully electric cars never need a smog check.

Charging rights for renters

California law requires landlords of qualifying residential and commercial properties to approve a tenant's written request to install a charger at the tenant's assigned parking space. Some properties are excluded. The tenant pays for installation, maintenance, removal, and the electricity. (AFDC summary). Homeowners can also finance a charger through a local Property Assessed Clean Energy (PACE) program and repay it on their property tax bill, if their local government offers one.

Utility programs

Utility offers are where most California drivers will still find money. One example is the Sacramento Municipal Utility District:

  • Register your EV and get a 1.5¢ per kWh discount on all electricity used between midnight and 6 a.m. The car has to be registered with the DMV at the same address as the SMUD account.
  • Get up to a $200 rebate on circuit-sharing and energy-management devices, which can save you a panel upgrade.
  • Income-eligible customers can get up to $12,000 toward a new or used EV and up to $2,000 toward charging equipment. Some qualify for a free charger and installation.

The big investor-owned utilities (PG&E, Southern California Edison, and SDG&E) and city utilities like LADWP all sell EV or time-of-use rates, and their programs change often. We haven't listed specific amounts for them because we couldn't confirm current figures on their official pages. Check your utility's EV page directly.
