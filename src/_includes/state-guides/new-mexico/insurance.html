Required liability limits

New Mexico's Mandatory Financial Responsibility Act, NMSA 1978 §§ 66-5-201 to 66-5-239, says no owner may let an uninsured vehicle be driven on the state's streets or highways, and no person may drive one, unless the vehicle or driver is exempt (§ 66-5-205). Section 66-5-208 sets the amounts that count as financial responsibility:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in an accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in an accident, subject to the per-person limit, and
  • $10,000 for damage to or destruction of other people's property in an accident.

That's written as 25/50/10. The law calls for a $60,000 total when the evidence is a surety bond or a cash deposit instead of a policy.

Ways to show financial responsibility

Under § 66-5-218, evidence can be a motor vehicle insurance policy, a surety bond under § 66-5-225, or a certificate of a cash deposit under § 66-5-226. A binder issued while a policy is being prepared also satisfies the requirement, according to § 66-5-205.3(F). The Motor Vehicle Division won't issue or renew a registration for a vehicle without valid coverage or other evidence, and § 66-5-206 directs it to suspend the registration when its records show coverage has lapsed.

What a liability policy must cover

Section 66-5-205.3 requires a policy to name or describe the vehicles it covers and to protect the named insured and anyone driving with permission against liability for damages from owning, maintaining or using the vehicle. It also covers the named insured while driving a vehicle they don't own, with the owner's permission. The policy has to state the insured's name and address, the coverage, the premium, the policy period and the limits.

The statute lists the exclusions an insurer may use, such as vehicles used in the auto business, vehicles furnished for regular use, workers' compensation liability, and damage to property the insured owns or is carrying. A policy may also exclude use while a vehicle is rented out or carrying passengers for a charge, including when the driver is logged on to a transportation network company's app. That exclusion doesn't apply to rides shared on an expense basis.

Under § 66-5-222, a policy can be endorsed to exclude a named driver, and at least one named insured has to sign the endorsement.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Section 66-5-301 requires every auto liability policy delivered in New Mexico for a vehicle registered or garaged in the state to include uninsured motorist coverage, at least at the § 66-5-215 minimums and up to your own liability limits if you want more. The uninsured coverage must include underinsured motorist coverage. It applies when the at-fault driver's total bodily injury limits are lower than your uninsured motorist limits. Property damage under this coverage can carry an exclusion of up to the first $250.

The named insured has the right to reject the coverage. After a rejection, the same insurer doesn't have to add it to a renewal unless you ask for it in writing. Under § 66-5-302, an arbitrator can't demand a fee in advance to hear an uninsured motorist dispute. Under § 66-5-303, either side can ask the district court to confirm the award.

The Insurance Research Council estimates that 24.1% of New Mexico drivers are uninsured, second among the states in its ranking.

Penalties and plate removal

Driving or allowing the operation of an uninsured vehicle is a misdemeanor under § 66-5-205(E). Section 66-8-7 sets the general Motor Vehicle Code misdemeanor penalty at a fine of up to $300, up to 90 days in jail, or both. A driver charged under § 66-5-205 can't be convicted if they show the court evidence of financial responsibility that was valid when the citation was issued.

If you're cited after an accident for lacking coverage, § 66-5-205.1 has the officer issue a temporary operation sticker good for 30 days and take the license plate, or deface it if it can't be removed. To get the plate back, you show proof of compliance and pay a $25 reinstatement fee. If you prove within 15 days that you were covered when the sticker was issued, the plate is returned and the fee is waived.

Insurers report cancellations, terminations and new policies to the department every month. Within 10 days of a cancellation notice, the department asks the owner for proof of coverage. If none arrives within 20 days of the mailed demand, the department suspends the registration.

Average cost

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners' 2024 Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement lists a combined average of $1,421.95 for liability, collision and comprehensive in New Mexico (Table 5). The figure combines all three coverages and doesn't show what a liability-only policy at the state minimums costs.

Getting help

The Office of Superintendent of Insurance licenses insurers, reviews and approves rate filings, and takes enforcement action when insurance laws are broken. Its Consumer Assistance Bureau takes complaints about insurers, agents and adjusters, including disputes over cancellations and claims handling. Its mailing address is P.O. Box 1689, Santa Fe, NM 87504-1689.
