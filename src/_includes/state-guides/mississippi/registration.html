Where and when to register

In Mississippi, the Department of Revenue (DOR) issues titles and sets registration rules. County Tax Collector offices take registrations. A vehicle with a gross vehicle weight (GVW) of 10,000 pounds or less registers with the Tax Collector in the county where it's kept or parked overnight. Heavier vehicles used only in Mississippi also register at the county office. Heavier vehicles that travel interstate register at a DOR office in Biloxi, Clinton, Hattiesburg, Hernando or Meridian.

The deadlines depend on how you got the vehicle:

  • New residents: 30 days after moving, if you have a current registration and valid plate from your previous state.
  • Buyers: 30 business days from the purchase date. If you bought outside your home county, you have 48 hours to take the vehicle home or to your business, and then the 30 business days still apply.

Missing the 30-day deadline brings the standard late penalties plus an extra $250 penalty. A dealer can issue a temporary drive-out tag that's good for 7 days from purchase, so out-of-state buyers can get home. Military members and their dependents who claim another state as home don't have to register here. Neither do full-time students who aren't Mississippi residents.

What a tag costs

DOR says the total depends on the vehicle's type and value and on your city and county. At the Tax Collector you'll pay:

  • Registration fee: $14.00 for a first-time registration or $12.75 to renew.
  • Privilege tax: under Miss. Code Ann. § 27-19-5, the annual highway privilege tax is $15 on each private passenger carrier and $8 on each motorcycle.
  • Ad valorem tax: a local property tax on the vehicle, explained below.
  • Sales or use tax: if it applies.

Electric and hybrid fees are added at both first registration and renewal for those vehicles. DOR notes that those amounts change every July 1.

Ad valorem tax and the Legislative Tag Credit

Owners of vehicles with a GVW of 10,000 pounds or less pay motor vehicle ad valorem tax at registration. The tax is the vehicle's assessed value multiplied by the millage rate set by local government. Assessed value is 30% of the manufacturer's suggested retail price, reduced for depreciation over 10 years, with a $100 minimum for passenger vehicles. Exemptions are listed in Miss. Code Ann. § 27-51-41, and county tax collectors can give you the local millage rate.

Since 1994, the Legislature has given owners a credit against this tax, known as the Legislative Tag Credit. It's funded by sales tax on motor vehicles, and DOR recalculates the credit percentage each year based on what's in the fund. You lose the credit if your late penalty reaches the 25% maximum. New residents also can't get it if their out-of-state plate has expired or the vehicle was never registered in their old state.

Sales tax on vehicle purchases

The general rate is 5% of the net purchase price, after dealer discounts and trade-ins but not counting manufacturer rebates. It applies to cars, vans, buses and trucks under 10,000 pounds GVW. Trucks over 10,000 pounds that carry property pay 3%. Sales tax you paid in another state isn't credited when you register in Mississippi.

Titles

Mississippi has required motor vehicles to be titled since 1969. Titles cost $9.00, whether standard, electronic lien, electronic or replacement. A Fast Track title costs $39.00 and is issued within 72 hours. Standard titles are typically issued within six weeks of a complete application, electronic titles within three to four weeks, and electronic lien titles the next business day. When you sell, complete every part of the assignment except the buyer's printed name and signature, and remove your plate. DOR warns that leaving the buyer's name blank violates state law.

Plates, late renewals and inspections

A Mississippi tag is registered to both the vehicle and the owner, so you can't move it to a new vehicle. If either changes, the tag goes back to the Tax Collector. Registration renews every year, and the renewal notice is only a courtesy. Late penalties start on the 16th day of the month after expiration: 5% for the next 15 days, then another 5% every 30 days, up to 25%. Mississippi doesn't require emissions testing or periodic safety inspections for passenger vehicles.
