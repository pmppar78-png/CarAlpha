Required liability coverage

You need New York State-issued auto liability insurance to register a vehicle in New York. According to the Department of Motor Vehicles, the minimum for most vehicles is:

  • $10,000 for property damage in a single crash
  • $25,000 for bodily injury and $50,000 for death of one person in a crash, and
  • $50,000 for bodily injury and $100,000 for death of two or more people in a crash.

That's usually written as 25/50/10, though the death limits are twice the injury limits. The policy has to come from a company licensed by the Department of Financial Services (DFS) and certified by the DMV. The DMV doesn't accept out-of-state coverage of any kind for a vehicle registered in New York. The policy also has to be in the registrant's exact name and stay in force for as long as the registration is valid, even if you don't drive the car.

No-fault personal injury protection

New York is a no-fault state. Under Insurance Law § 5102(a), basic economic loss covers up to $50,000 per person for injuries from a crash, no matter who caused it:

  • Necessary medical, hospital, surgical, dental, ambulance, prescription, therapy and rehabilitation expenses
  • Lost earnings and the cost of hiring someone to do work you would have done for income, up to $2,000 a month for no more than three years, and
  • Other reasonable and necessary expenses, up to $25 a day for no more than one year.

Under § 5102(b), the payments are reduced by 20% of lost earnings and by amounts paid or payable through Social Security disability, workers' compensation, state disability benefits and certain Medicare benefits. The law also requires insurers to offer an optional $25,000 of extra coverage that can be applied to lost earnings or therapy after the first $50,000 is used up.

The DFS no-fault FAQ says written notice of a claim has to reach the no-fault insurer within 30 days of the accident unless you can justify the delay in writing. Medical bills are due within 45 days and lost-wage claims within 90 days. You file with the insurer of the car you were riding in, or, if you were a pedestrian, the insurer of the car that hit you. If that vehicle is unknown or uninsured, file with the insurer of a relative in your household, or with the Motor Vehicle Accident Indemnification Corporation (MVAIC) if there's no household policy. Motorcycle riders and passengers are excluded from no-fault benefits.

Suing for serious injury

You can sue the driver who caused the crash for pain and suffering only if you have a "serious injury" as defined in § 5102(d). The list includes death, dismemberment, significant disfigurement, a fracture, loss of a fetus, and permanent loss of use of a body organ, member, function or system. It also covers an injury that keeps you from doing substantially all of your usual daily activities for at least 90 of the 180 days after the crash. You can also sue for costs above your policy limits.

Insurance cards and registration

When you buy a policy, the insurer gives you two New York State Insurance ID Cards, or an electronic card, and sends an electronic notice of coverage to the DMV. Your agent or broker can't file that notice for you. The DMV needs both the card and the notice, and you must register within 180 days of the card's effective date. Keep a card in the vehicle.

Lapses and penalties

If coverage ends while the vehicle is registered, the DMV says to surrender the plates and registration right away. Motorcycle owners are the exception and can keep their plates, though they still can't ride without coverage. A lapse can lead to suspension of the registration, and of your driver license if it runs 91 days or more. Reinstating a suspended license costs a $50 termination fee.

For a lapse of 90 days or less, you can pay a civil penalty instead of serving the suspension, but only once every 36 months:

Days without coverageCivil penalty
1–30$8 per day
31–60$10 per day
61–90$12 per day

A full 90-day lapse costs $900. The DMV warns that driving without insurance can bring a traffic court fine of up to $1,500 and impoundment. If an uninsured vehicle is in a crash, the license and registration are revoked for at least a year, and restoring the license takes a $750 civil penalty.

Average cost and uninsured drivers

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners' 2024 Average Premium Supplement lists a combined average of $2,149.87 for liability, collision and comprehensive in New York (Table 5). The Insurance Research Council estimates that 8.6% of New York drivers are uninsured, 44th among the states. The DMV doesn't give out rate information. DFS regulates insurers and handles complaints.
