West Virginia's insurance requirement

West Virginia's required motor vehicle liability security law is Article 2A of Chapter 17D. Under W. Va. Code § 17D-2A-3, every owner or registrant of a vehicle that must be registered in the state has to keep security in effect continuously through the registration period. A recreational vehicle, antique vehicle, motorcycle or other vehicle stored part of the year and used seasonally only needs security for the part of the year it's actually in use.

Security can be provided in either of two ways:

  • An insurance policy delivered or issued for delivery in West Virginia by an insurer authorized to write vehicle liability and property insurance in the state, with limits at least equal to § 17D-4-2
  • Qualification as a self-insurer under § 17D-6-2

Vehicles owned by the state, its political subdivisions or the federal government are exempt from the article. No one may knowingly drive a vehicle that needs security unless the security is in effect.

Minimum liability limits

Section 17D-4-2(b) defines proof of financial responsibility. Since January 1, 2016, the minimum amounts have been:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in any one accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in any one accident, subject to the per-person limit
  • $25,000 for injury to or destruction of others' property in any one accident

The earlier amounts were $20,000, $40,000 and $10,000. A policy in effect on December 31, 2015, at those older limits counted as adequate proof until it expired or was renewed.

Dropping coverage and nonresidents

If you drop the required security during the registration period, § 17D-2A-3(b) requires you to surrender the registration plate to the Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV) right away. Owners of seasonal vehicles may keep the plate, subject to the commissioner's rules. A nonresident whose vehicle has been in West Virginia for more than 30 days during the past 365 days must keep security in effect for as long as the vehicle stays in the state.

Proof of insurance

Under § 17D-2A-4, insurers must give you a certificate of insurance, in duplicate for each policy term and each vehicle except vehicles on a fleet policy. It shows the effective dates, the policyholder and the vehicle owner if different, and confirms coverage at least equal to the § 17D-4-2 minimums. Keep the certificate or other proof in the vehicle, and show it at your safety inspection.

Proof can be the certificate, the policy or a mechanically reproduced copy, a certificate of self-insurance, or an image on a wireless device with the required information from the insurer. Not carrying proof is a misdemeanor with a fine of $200 to $5,000. A second or later conviction can also bring 15 days to a year in jail. You aren't guilty if you show, within seven days of the citation, that the insurance was in effect.

Stops, crashes and court

Under § 17D-2A-6, when officers investigate a motor vehicle offense or crash, or stop a vehicle for reasonable cause, they ask the driver about insurance and may check the online insurance verification program. If coverage isn't in effect, they notify the DMV within five days. Officers can't stop a vehicle solely to ask about the certificate of insurance. A defendant charged with a traffic offense that requires a court appearance must show the court proof of security at the time of the offense.

License suspension and registration revocation

An owner who doesn't have the required security when the vehicle is registered or driven faces penalties under § 17D-2A-7:

  • For a first offense, a 30-day license suspension that lasts until current proof of insurance is presented for all registered vehicles. If you comply and pay a $200 penalty fee before the effective date, the suspension and registration revocation aren't imposed and no reinstatement fees apply.
  • For a second or later offense within five years, a 90-day license suspension, with the registration revoked until you present proof of security.

If the vehicle is titled in more than one name, only one owner's license is suspended. A driver who isn't the owner and is convicted of driving an uninsured vehicle gets the conviction on their record. The DMV sends written notice by certified mail at least 30 days before a suspension or revocation, to you and any lienholder. You have 10 days from receiving it to request a hearing, which is limited to identity and whether insurance was in effect. The commissioner may accept a binder, ID card or declarations page as evidence pending electronic verification.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Under § 33-6-31(b), every liability policy issued in West Virginia must include uninsured motorist coverage with limits at least equal to the § 17D-4-2 minimums. The insurer must offer optional uninsured motorist limits up to $100,000 per person, $300,000 per accident and $50,000 for property damage, at adjusted premiums. The endorsement may exclude the first $300 of property damage caused by an uninsured motorist.

The insurer must also offer optional uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage up to at least the liability limits you buy. An underinsured motor vehicle is one whose liability limits are lower than the underinsured coverage you carry, or have been reduced below it by payments to others hurt in the crash.
