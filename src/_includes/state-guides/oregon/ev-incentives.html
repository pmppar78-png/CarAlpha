Oregon Clean Vehicle Rebate Program

The Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) runs the Oregon Clean Vehicle Rebate Program, a cash rebate for buying or leasing an EV. DEQ describes it as a cash rebate and distinguishes it from a tax credit. For 2026, the program is accepting applications for eligible purchases and leases made from August 25 through November 4, 2026. Vehicles bought or leased before or after that window aren't eligible.

DEQ posted that as of October 17, 2026, available rebate funds were running low. Applications submitted after that date may go on a waiting list to be paid in spring 2027, and the approval email will say if you've been waitlisted. You must apply within six months of the purchase or lease date. DEQ's announcement says the program receives at least $12 million a year, or 45% of the state's Vehicle Privilege Tax.

Standard Rebate

The Standard Rebate is open to Oregon residents, businesses, nonprofits and government agencies, with no income requirement. It covers new battery electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles with a base MSRP under $50,000, and new zero-emission motorcycles:

VehicleRebate
BEV with a battery of 10 kWh or more$2,000
PHEV with a battery of 10 kWh or more$1,500
BEV or PHEV with a battery under 10 kWh$750
Zero-emission motorcycle$375

Charge Ahead Rebate

The Charge Ahead Rebate is for Oregon residents with household income at or below 400% of the federal poverty guideline, and for nonprofits that qualify as low-income service providers. It covers new or used BEVs and PHEVs with an original base MSRP under $50,000:

  • New BEVs: $7,500
  • New PHEVs: $5,000
  • Used BEVs: up to $4,000, or no more than 30% of the total purchase price, with a minimum of $2,500
  • Used PHEVs: $2,500, or no more than the total purchase price

You can prove income before buying with a Charge Ahead Prequalification Application, which gives you a voucher to use at a participating dealer, or apply after the purchase. For a new vehicle you choose either the Standard or the Charge Ahead Rebate; they can't be combined.

Rebate conditions

  • The vehicle must come from a certified dealer or the manufacturer. Person-to-person sales don't qualify.
  • It must be able to travel at 55 mph or more and can't have been declared totaled or carry a salvage title. A BEV must have a range of 75 miles or more on electricity.
  • You must register the vehicle in Oregon and keep it registered for at least 24 months.
  • Individuals can receive a total of two rebates. Businesses, nonprofits and agencies are limited to 10 a calendar year and must report usage to DEQ for three years.

Registration and road usage charges

Oregon DMV charges $376 for a two-year registration of an all-electric passenger vehicle not enrolled in OReGO, and the EV title fee is $192. Enrolling in OReGO, the road usage charge program, brings the two-year registration fee down to $86, and you pay by the mile. The AFDC notes that enrolled EVs and vehicles rated 40 mpg or higher are exempt from the extra registration fees, and that location and daily metered-use records must be destroyed within 30 days after processing.

Charger programs and rules

The AFDC lists these programs and laws for EV charging in Oregon:

  • Community Charging Rebates: ODOT rebates for businesses, multifamily housing and government entities, of $8,000 per Level 2 charger or up to 80% of project costs, awarded first come, first served. Seventy percent of funds are reserved for rural or disadvantaged communities.
  • Zero-Emission Fueling Infrastructure Grants: DEQ grants for chargers and fueling for medium- and heavy-duty zero-emission vehicles.
  • Condominiums and planned communities: an owner may install a charger for personal use in a parking space reserved for that owner. The association must approve a complete application within 60 days or give written reasonable conditions in that time (ORS 94.762 and 100.627).
  • EV rates: regulated electric utilities must offer EV owners a choice of flat or time-of-use rates.

Under ORS 815.300, all-electric vehicles aren't required to have a certified pollution control system.
