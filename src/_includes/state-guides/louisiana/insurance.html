Louisiana's liability requirement

Under R.S. 32:861, every self-propelled vehicle registered in Louisiana must have security in place, and keeping it in place is the registered owner's duty. Most drivers meet the requirement with a liability policy that meets the limits in R.S. 32:900:

  • $15,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in any one accident
  • $30,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in any one accident
  • $25,000 for damage to or destruction of other people's property in any one accident

The policy must cover the named insured and anyone driving with their express or implied permission, anywhere in the United States or Canada. Other ways to meet the requirement include a motor vehicle liability bond with the same limits, a $55,000 deposit of cash or securities with the state treasurer, or a certificate of self-insurance.

The Louisiana Department of Insurance's Consumer's Guide to Auto Insurance (revised March 2026) explains that liability covers damage caused by you, your family members and others driving your car with permission. You and your family are also covered when driving someone else's car, including a rental. A company car or another non-owned car you regularly use may not be covered unless you add an endorsement.

Uninsured motorist options

The LDI guide describes several kinds of uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage:

  • Standard UM: pays for injuries to you and the occupants of your vehicle when the at-fault driver has no insurance or not enough. A hit-and-run driver may be treated as uninsured, and a disinterested witness helps.
  • Economic-only UM: pays only the actual costs of injuries, not pain and suffering.
  • UM property damage: pays for repairs to your vehicle after a crash with an uninsured driver. It has a $250 deductible, and recovery is limited to $25,000 or the vehicle's actual cash value, whichever is less, unless you buy a higher limit.

The guide also covers medical payments coverage, which pays medical and funeral expenses for you, your family and your passengers regardless of fault, and towing and labor coverage.

"No pay, no play"

Under R.S. 32:866, an owner or operator who doesn't have the required security can't recover the first $100,000 of bodily injury damages or the first $100,000 of property damage from a crash, no matter who caused it. The limit doesn't apply if the other driver:

  • Is cited for and convicted of driving while intoxicated, or pleads no contest, as a result of the crash
  • Caused the crash intentionally
  • Fled the scene
  • Was committing a felony at the time

An uninsured owner who sues and is awarded $100,000 or less in bodily injury damages pays all parties' court costs. Passengers can still bring claims, unless the passenger owns the uninsured vehicle. Buying a policy after a crash doesn't restore your right to recover for that crash. The LDI guide, which still gives the older $15,000 and $25,000 figures, adds that buying economic-only UM coverage may waive your right to claim pain and suffering.

Proof in the vehicle

R.S. 32:863.1 requires proof of security in the vehicle. That can be an insurance ID card, a photocopy, or an image of the card on a phone, or the policy or binder itself. The card must show the insurer, the policy number, the vehicle, the effective and expiration dates, and anyone excluded from coverage. Officers check for proof at stops, checkpoints and crash scenes and can verify it electronically. If you can't show proof, the vehicle may be impounded, the officer can remove a Louisiana plate, and you'll get a notice of noncompliance that also explains your right to an administrative hearing.

Lapses and reinstatement

When the Office of Motor Vehicles finds that a vehicle isn't covered or the coverage has lapsed, R.S. 32:863 requires it to revoke the registration, impound the vehicle and cancel the plate until you provide proof of insurance and pay reinstatement fees. The fees depend on how long the vehicle was uninsured:

  • $100 for 1 to 30 days
  • $250 for 31 to 90 days
  • $500 for more than 90 days

There's no reinstatement fee if the lapse was 10 days or less and you surrender the plate within 10 days. Falsely claiming coverage brings sanctions for 12 to 18 months and fees of $250, $500 and $1,000 for first, second and later violations. Total fees are capped at $850 for multiple lapse violations, and at $250 for people 65 or older, if you resolve them within 60 days of the notice. If you stop using a vehicle, surrender the plate within 10 days of canceling the insurance, or notify the state before canceling.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement reports a combined average premium of $2,151.12 for Louisiana, up from $1,684.76 in 2020, and an average expenditure of $1,880.42. The Insurance Research Council estimates that 11.7% of Louisiana drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 32nd. If you can't find coverage in the voluntary market, the LDI guide points to the Louisiana Automobile Insurance Plan at (866) 989-9902. For questions, call the LDI at (800) 259-5300.
