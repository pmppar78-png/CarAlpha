Charge Up New Jersey

The Board of Public Utilities' Charge Up New Jersey program gives New Jersey residents a point-of-sale incentive on the purchase or lease of a new, eligible battery electric vehicle. The amounts on the program site are:

  • $1,500 through Charge Up. The participating dealer takes it off the price and applies for reimbursement, so you have nothing to file afterward.
  • An extra $2,500 through Charge Up+, for a total of $4,000, if you meet the income limits. You have to prequalify before the sale or lease.
  • Up to $250 toward buying and installing a qualifying Level 2 home charger, claimed with proof of purchase through the customer dashboard.

The AFDC's summary of N.J.S.A. 48:25-1 through 48:25-8 says the vehicle's MSRP can't exceed $55,000 and that plug-in hybrids don't qualify. The program site says $245 million has been allocated for EV incentives across fiscal years 2022 through 2027, including $30 million for 2027. Funding is reserved only for applications that are submitted, and the site shows the remaining balance in real time, so check it before you shop. The site also notes that the separate Clean Fleet EV and EV charging program for organizations was fully allocated and paused to new applications as of July 24, 2026.

Sales tax on ZEVs

Under N.J.S.A. 54:32B-3 and 54:32B-8.55, as summarized by the AFDC, a ZEV sold from October 1, 2024 through June 30, 2025 was taxed at 3.3125%, and one sold on or after July 1, 2025 pays the full 6.625%. The Treasury keeps the list of vehicles that qualified as ZEVs.

Annual ZEV fee

N.J.S.A. 39:3-8.5 adds a yearly fee for ZEV owners. It began at $250 on July 1, 2024 and rises $10 a year until 2028. The AFDC says the money goes to a capital reserve subaccount of the Transportation Trust Fund.

Turnpike and Parkway toll discount

The New Jersey Turnpike Authority's Green Pass Discount Plan takes 10% off off-peak tolls on the Turnpike and the Garden State Parkway. The AFDC lists the requirements as a fuel economy rating of at least 45 mpg, California's Super Ultra Low Emission Vehicle standard, and a New Jersey E-ZPass account registered for the plan.

Charging at home and in public

  • Charging spaces. A 2025 law bars stopping, standing or parking in a marked EV charging space unless the vehicle is an EV that's actively charging. The AFDC lists fines of $55 for a first offense, $100 for a second and $200 after that, and towing at the municipality's discretion at the owner's cost.
  • Homeowner associations. Under N.J.S.A. 45:22A-48.4, an association can set reasonable rules but can't prohibit a charger in a homeowner's designated parking space or add restrictions that significantly raise its cost. The homeowner uses a licensed contractor, provides a certificate of insurance and pays for installation, upkeep and any damage.
  • New homes. N.J.S.A. 52:27D-141.11 requires the developer of a single-family home with a designated parking space to offer to install a charger, unless one is already included.
  • Apartments. New or rebuilt multifamily developments with five or more units must make 15% of required off-street spaces ready for chargers, with chargers in 5% before occupancy and more within three and six years.
  • Utility status. N.J.S.A. 48:25-10 says an entity that owns or runs EV charging equipment isn't defined as a public utility.

The AFDC also lists EV programs from Atlantic City Electric, JCP&L, Orange & Rockland and PSE&G. Check your utility's site for current offers.

Clean car rules

New Jersey has adopted California's vehicle emissions standards. The AFDC says the state adopted Advanced Clean Cars II in November 2023, beginning with model year 2027, with a path to 100% ZEV sales of new passenger cars, light-duty trucks and medium-duty vehicles by 2035. A manufacturer that builds only ZEVs and was licensed before January 1, 2014 may sell directly to consumers from up to four locations under N.J.S.A. 56:10-27.1. Federal tax credits follow federal law, so check the IRS site for the current rules.
