Registration is handled by your county

Hawaii doesn't have a single statewide DMV. Each of the four counties registers vehicles, issues titles and collects fees: the City and County of Honolulu on Oahu, plus Maui, Kauai and Hawaii counties. Your vehicle has to be registered in the county where it's driven. A vehicle legally registered in another Hawaii county can still be driven on Oahu with a current safety inspection and insurance, but you should transfer it to Honolulu before that registration expires. The details below come from the Honolulu Department of Customer Services. The neighbor-island counties follow the same state laws but set some of their own fees.

Bringing a car to Oahu

Within 30 days after your vehicle arrives on Oahu, you need either an out-of-state permit or a Hawaii registration. Either way, the process starts at a safety inspection station, which can seem backwards. Because the car has no Honolulu registration yet, it will fail the inspection on that point even if it passes everything else. Any other defects the inspector finds must be fixed before you register.

Next, take the "failed" inspection certificate to a satellite city hall. Most services there need an AlohaQ appointment. Bring:

  • Your current out-of-state registration and the title or lienholder's address
  • The bill of lading or other shipping documents showing the VIN
  • Form CS-L (MVR) 1, the Application for Registration, signed by the registered owner, or Form CS-L (MVR) 27 if you want an out-of-state permit and plan to keep your current plates
  • Form CS-L (MVR) 50, if you're an active-duty service member who isn't a Hawaii resident

The permit costs $5. Once you have Hawaii plates or the permit, go back to the inspection station within 30 days of the failed check to pass and get your safety sticker. Hawaii requires two plates. The one with the emblem space in the upper right goes on the back, and both have to be mounted before that return visit.

What registration costs on Oahu

Registration costs depend mostly on your vehicle's weight, with a state and a county weight tax on top of flat fees. Honolulu itemizes them this way:

  • State registration fee: $46
  • County registration fee: $20
  • State and county weight taxes, based on weight
  • Highway beautification fee: $10
  • Annual emblem: $0.50
  • Initial plate fee: $5, charged only when plates are issued

Honolulu's example is a 3,320-pound vehicle that costs $367 to register. Of that, $232.40 is county weight tax and $58.10 is state weight tax. About 70% of what you pay stays with the county. You can look up the exact amount for a particular vehicle in the city's online fee inquiry tool.

The registration deadline is the last working day of the month your current registration expires. A late renewal adds a flat $16 penalty for passenger-rate vehicles ($8 county and $8 state) and $40 for commercial rates. Honolulu waives its $20 county fee for one vehicle owned by a veteran 65 or older who wasn't dishonorably discharged. Hawaii residents serving on active duty, or in the National Guard or Reserve with a Hawaii unit, are exempt from weight taxes on one vehicle by filing Form CS-L (MVR) 50A.

Renewing

A courtesy reminder is mailed 45 days before your registration expires, but you're responsible for renewing on time even if it never arrives. On Oahu, you can renew at a DMV Now kiosk in certain Safeway and Foodland stores, online, by mail, or at a satellite city hall express window. Kiosk and online renewals work only when your address is current and no extra documents are needed. If your safety inspection certificate has to be submitted, for example, you'll need to go in person. Card payments carry a processing fee, and kiosks also add $4. Registration fees generally can't be refunded once paid, unless the event that qualifies you for a refund happened before the new registration period began.

Buying or selling a car

The seller must send the Notice of Transfer from the top of the title to the county within 10 days. The buyer has 30 days from the seller's transfer date to bring the endorsed title, the last registration certificate and a current safety inspection certificate to a satellite city hall. On Oahu, the transfer fee is $10, and missing the 30-day window adds a $50 penalty. Hawaii County charges $5 for the transfer, with the same $50 late fee. If the registration has expired or expires within 45 days, you'll pay the renewal at the same time. A transfer can't be completed until the vehicle's registration and safety inspection are both current.

Annual safety inspection

Hawaii requires periodic safety inspections for motor vehicles, including motorcycles and mopeds, at stations certified by the state Department of Transportation. You have to show proof of Hawaii insurance to get an inspection. A current sticker and certificate are required to drive on any Hawaii road and to complete any registration transaction. Most cars are inspected every year. A new vehicle inspected when first sold doesn't need another inspection for two years.

Starting July 1, 2025, HDOT raised the inspection fee by 75 cents, to $25.75 for cars and trucks and $17.75 for motorcycles and trailers. It was the first increase since 2017. Replacing a lost or destroyed sticker or certificate costs $7.09. About 500 inspection stations operate across the state, and HDOT's Motor Vehicle Safety Office can be reached at 808-692-7650. Hawaii doesn't require a separate emissions test.
