Two-year registration fees by weight

New York registers most passenger vehicles for two years, and the fee depends on weight. The DMV's Passenger Vehicle Registration Fee Schedule (MV-202) starts at $26 for vehicles up to 1,650 pounds and goes up in steps for each 100 pounds, topping out at $140 for 6,951 pounds and over. Some sample two-year fees:

WeightTwo-year fee
2,951–3,050 lb$48.50
3,451–3,550 lb$56.50
3,951–4,050 lb$69.00
4,951–5,050 lb$93.00
5,951–6,050 lb$117.00

A vehicle with six or more cylinders, or an electric vehicle, pays at least $32.50 for two years. The DMV notes that this is a minimum, so it isn't added on top of the chart amount.

Renewals and motorcycles

The DMV won't renew a registration unless its records show an inspection within the past 12 months. That matters if you're out of state when the sticker expires, so call the number on the DMV's inspection page before renewing. Motorcycles follow a different schedule. They're registered for one year, every motorcycle registration expires on April 30, and owners can keep their plates after canceling insurance over the winter, though riding uninsured stays illegal.

County use tax and the MCTD fee

New York City and many counties charge a vehicle use tax, which the DMV collects at registration and renewal. According to MV-202:

  • In counties such as Albany, Erie, Monroe, Onondaga and Dutchess, it's $10 for two years for a vehicle up to 3,500 pounds and $20 for a heavier one.
  • In Suffolk and Westchester, it's $30 for two years up to 3,500 pounds and $60 above that.
  • In the five New York City boroughs and Nassau, it's $30 for two years at any weight.

Residents of the 12 counties in the Metropolitan Commuter Transportation District (the five boroughs plus Dutchess, Nassau, Orange, Putnam, Rockland, Suffolk and Westchester) also pay a supplemental fee of $50 for two years. If you're exempt from the use tax, file Form UT-11 in New York City or UT-11C elsewhere.

First registration costs

When you register a vehicle for the first time, the DMV adds:

  • A $25 plate fee ($12.50 for a vehicle with one plate), or a $10 fee to transfer plates from another vehicle
  • A $50 title certificate fee, and
  • Sales tax at the rate where you live, from the Department of Taxation and Finance's rate tables.

If you transfer the registration and plates from another vehicle, you don't pay a new plate fee or the MCTD fee, and the registration fee you already paid may carry over.

Insurance before registration

You need New York State-issued liability insurance before you register. The insurer sends an electronic notice to the DMV and gives you an Insurance ID Card, and the registration has to be completed within 180 days of the card's effective date. The names on the registration and the insurance card must match exactly, and the DMV doesn't accept out-of-state policies.

Annual safety and emissions inspection

Vehicles must be inspected every 12 months and whenever ownership changes, and the sticker expires on the last day of the month punched on it. Most inspections also include an onboard diagnostics (OBD II) emissions check. Vehicles are exempt from the emissions part if they're motorcycles, less than two model years old, more than 25 model years old, all-electric, or have historical plates. Most diesels are also exempt, except 1997 and newer models with a GVWR under 8,501 pounds.

The DMV's fee chart (VS-77) sets these maximum charges:

  • Light-vehicle safety inspection: $10.
  • OBD II emissions inspection: $27 in the New York Metropolitan Area and $11 elsewhere.

A station can charge less but not more, and it can charge whether the car passes or fails. You can have repairs done anywhere you choose.

A sticker that expired within the last 60 days can bring a fine of $25 to $50. After 60 days, or with no sticker at all, the fine is $50 to $100, and a mandatory state surcharge of $88 ($93 in town and village courts) is added.

Buying a vehicle

A dealer must have the vehicle inspected within 30 days of the sale date and before delivery. If you buy from someone who isn't a DMV-registered dealer, you get an inspection extension good for 10 days from the registration date, and the previous owner's inspection doesn't carry over.

Moving to New York

If your old state issues inspection stickers, the out-of-state inspection stays valid until it expires or one year after you register in New York, whichever comes first. If your old state doesn't issue stickers, you need a New York inspection right away. Owners who are out of state when an inspection expires can request an extension sticker by mail. It gives them 10 days after returning to get inspected.
