What a passenger car registration costs

An Ohio registration is built from several pieces. For a passenger car, Ohio Rev. Code § 4503.04(B) sets the state license tax at $20. Section 4503.10(C)(1) adds a fee to every registration and renewal, which went from $11 to $16 on January 1, 2026. Together they make the $36 base. On top of that you pay:

  • A deputy registrar service fee: $8 for a one-year registration
  • County and local permissive taxes: these vary by where you live and can't exceed $30 in total, and
  • An alternative-fuel fee, if it applies: under § 4503.10(C)(3), $100 for a hybrid, $150 for a plug-in hybrid and $200 for a battery electric vehicle, charged at every registration and renewal.

Other annual license taxes in § 4503.04 include $14 for a motorcycle, autocycle or motor scooter, $10 for a moped or motorized bicycle, and $10 for a low-speed or utility vehicle or mini-truck. A noncommercial truck rated to carry three-quarters of a ton or less, or a motor home, is $35. One rated for more than three-quarters of a ton up to one ton is $70. Commercial vehicles pay weight-based rates under § 4503.042.

Certificates of title

Titles in Ohio are issued by the county clerk of courts. Under § 4505.09, a certificate of title or duplicate costs $18, or $23 in a county whose commissioners have approved the higher fee. If the vehicle was last registered in another state, § 4505.061 requires a physical inspection certificate with the title application. The inspection checks the make, body type, model, mileage and VIN. It can be done at a deputy registrar's office, at a licensed Ohio dealer, or at a clerk of courts office that offers inspections.

A replacement license plate costs $15 for one plate or $16.25 for a pair.

Renewal timing

Ohio staggers registrations under § 4503.101, and owners who live at the same address can choose any one owner's date of birth as the registration date for all their vehicles. You can renew up to 90 days before your registration expires under § 4503.102, at a deputy registrar or by electronic means. Under § 4503.103, the registrar can let owners register eligible vehicles for up to five years at a time, paying every year's taxes and fees upfront.

Section 4503.11(A) makes it an offense for the owner of a vehicle driven on public roads to skip the yearly registration application or the tax, unless a listed exception such as multi-year registration applies.

If you register a vehicle partway through its registration period, § 4503.11 reduces the state license tax by one-twelfth for each full month that has already passed. You also have to tell the registrar about a change of address within 10 days under § 4503.101(E).

E-Check emissions testing

Ohio's emissions testing program, called E-Check, applies in seven northeast Ohio counties:

  • Cuyahoga
  • Geauga
  • Lake
  • Lorain
  • Medina
  • Portage, and
  • Summit.

In those counties, gasoline and diesel vehicles from 6 to 25 years old, and non-plug-in hybrids from 7 to 25 years old, weighing 10,000 pounds or less are tested every two years. An established Ohio resident gets one free passing test in 365 days. According to the AFDC, vehicles powered solely by electricity, propane or natural gas are exempt after a one-time verification inspection. The registrar records the inspection certificate number in the vehicle's registration record when you renew, as § 4503.10(B)(7) requires.

Moving to Ohio

To title a vehicle you bring from another state, start with the physical inspection described above, then apply to a county clerk of courts for an Ohio title. With the title in hand, register at a deputy registrar. You'll need to show proof of financial responsibility under § 4509.101, such as an insurance ID card, and pay the license tax, fees and any permissive tax for your county. If you live in one of the seven E-Check counties and your vehicle is in the tested age range, plan for the emissions test as well.

Quick cost check

As an example, renewing a gasoline passenger car for one year in a county with the full $30 of permissive taxes would cost $36 in state tax and fees, $8 for the deputy registrar and $30 in permissive taxes, for $74 in total. The same renewal for a battery electric car adds $200, for $274.
