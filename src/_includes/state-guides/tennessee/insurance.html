Tennessee's financial responsibility law

Tennessee's Financial Responsibility Law of 1977, in Tenn. Code Ann. Title 55, Chapter 12, requires drivers to be financially responsible for the damage they cause in a crash. The Department of Revenue says that unless you qualify for an exemption, the easiest way to meet that requirement is an auto liability insurance policy. Under § 55-12-139, the law applies to every vehicle subject to Tennessee's registration and title provisions.

If your vehicle has a regular passenger or motorcycle plate, the Department of Revenue says you need a personal policy that meets the state minimums. A vehicle with a commercial plate must be covered under a commercial auto policy. If you have a car loan or lease, your lender may also require collision and comprehensive coverage.

Minimum liability limits

Section 55-12-102(12)(D) sets the limits that apply when proof is required after December 31, 2022. A split-limit policy must provide at least:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in one accident
  • $25,000 for damage to property in one accident

The statute also accepts a single-limit policy of at least $65,000 per accident, a $65,000 cash deposit with the commissioner, or a $65,000 bond filed with the commissioner. The property damage minimum was $15,000 before 2023. A policy that met the old limits on December 31, 2022 stays compliant until its first renewal after that date.

Uninsured motorist coverage

Under § 56-7-1201, every auto liability policy issued or renewed in Tennessee for a vehicle registered or principally garaged in the state must include uninsured motorist coverage. It protects people insured under the policy who are legally entitled to recover damages for bodily injury or death from the owner or driver of an uninsured vehicle. The limits must equal the policy's bodily injury liability limits.

A named insured may reject the coverage in writing, or choose lower limits that are still at least the state minimums. That signed document binds everyone insured under the policy and carries over to renewals and replacements with the same insurer unless you later ask for the coverage in writing.

When you buy uninsured motorist bodily injury coverage, the insurer must give you the chance to add uninsured motorist property damage coverage. It applies to losses over $200. The $200 deductible is waived when the same insurer covers your vehicle for both collision and uninsured motorist property damage and the other driver is positively identified and solely at fault.

If the driver who hit you is unknown, you can recover under the uninsured motorist coverage when all three of these are true:

  • there was actual physical contact with the unknown vehicle, or its existence is proven by clear and convincing evidence from someone other than the occupants of your vehicle
  • the crash was reported to law enforcement within a reasonable time
  • you weren't negligent in failing to identify the other vehicle and driver

An insurer can't raise your rate or cancel your coverage solely because it paid an uninsured motorist claim. If the other driver hasn't filed the required financial responsibility forms with the Department of Safety within 90 days of the crash, the law presumes, subject to rebuttal, that the driver was uninsured.

Showing proof to an officer

When an officer charges a driver with a traffic violation, or responds to a crash that has to be reported under § 55-10-106, the officer must ask for evidence of financial responsibility. At a reportable crash, every driver involved is asked, regardless of fault. Accepted evidence includes:

  • a declarations page, binder or insurance card from an insurer authorized in Tennessee, on paper or on a phone or other device
  • a one-year certificate from the commissioner of revenue showing a cash deposit, a bond or self-insurer status
  • proof the vehicle belongs to a regulated common carrier or a government and was driven with the owner's consent

If you show proof on a phone, the statute says you aren't consenting to a search of anything else on the device. Officers can also check coverage through the state's vehicle insurance verification program.

Penalties for driving without proof

Failing to provide evidence of financial responsibility is a Class C misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $300. It becomes a Class A misdemeanor if you were uninsured and at fault, through criminal negligence, in a crash that caused bodily injury or death, or if you showed proof of insurance you knew wasn't valid. An officer may tow the vehicle if the officer's agency has a written policy on doing so.

If you had coverage at the time of the stop but couldn't show it, you can bring proof to court on or before your court date. For a first violation, the court must dismiss the charge, with no costs and no litigation tax. For a later violation, dismissal is at the court's discretion. If you had no coverage when you were charged, the charge can't be dismissed this way.

Electronic insurance verification

The James Lee Atwood Jr. Law, in Part 2 of Chapter 12, created an online program that matches registered vehicles with insurance records. The Department of Revenue says uninsured drivers have faced fines and the loss of their registration under the program since 2017.

Under § 55-12-210, if the records show your vehicle as uninsured, the Department of Revenue sends a notice giving you 30 days to provide proof of coverage, proof of an exemption, proof you no longer have the vehicle, or a statement under penalty of perjury that it isn't driven on public roads. If you don't respond, you owe a $25 coverage failure fee and get a second 30-day notice. Ignoring that notice adds a $100 continued coverage failure fee, and the department suspends or revokes the vehicle's registration. Giving the department a false statement is a Class B misdemeanor.

You can check a vehicle's insurance status, or respond to a notice, through the Tennessee Insurance Verification application on the Drive Insured TN page, using your plate number and VIN or PIN.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

Table 5 of the NAIC's 2024 Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement puts Tennessee's combined average for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,353.62. That figure blends all three coverages. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 21.3% of Tennessee drivers were uninsured, ranking the state fifth, which is worth weighing before you sign a rejection of uninsured motorist coverage.
