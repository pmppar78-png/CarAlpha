Alaska EV incentives in 2026

The State of Alaska doesn't offer an EV purchase rebate, a tax credit, or a sales-tax break. There's no state sales tax to waive. The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center listing for Alaska shows no consumer incentive. It also shows no extra registration fee for electric cars, so an EV registers for the same $100 every two years as a gasoline car, plus any local motor vehicle registration tax.

Public charging: the Railbelt corridor

The state's EV spending has gone into public fast charging along the road system. Since 2021, the Alaska Energy Authority has used Volkswagen Settlement Trust money and federal State Energy Program funds to build a fast-charging corridor from Seward and Homer north to Healy. AEA committed almost $900,000 of VW Trust funds plus $90,000 of SEP money. That paid for 15 fast chargers and eight Level 2 chargers at nine stations, each less than 100 miles from the next. AEA doesn't own or run the stations; the site hosts do. AEA lists the sites on its EV fast-charging network page.

The federal NEVI program carries the network on to Fairbanks. AEA and the Department of Transportation and Public Facilities announced Round 1 NEVI awards in September 2023 for sites from Anchorage and Wasilla through Trapper Creek, Denali State Park, Cantwell, Healy, Nenana, and Ester to Fairbanks. The FY26 Alaska NEVI plan aims to finish the Anchorage–Fairbanks corridor first and expand after that. Opening dates depend on the site, so check a charging app before a Parks Highway trip in winter.

In 2021 the Regulatory Commission of Alaska ruled, in Order U-21-022(2), that businesses selling EV charging aren't public utilities. That lets hotels, stores, and other private site hosts charge drivers for electricity.

What utilities offer

For Alaska drivers, the real money comes from electric cooperatives. The programs differ a lot depending on whose territory you live in.

  • Chugach Electric (Anchorage area): a $200 bill credit for each Level 2 charger at a home. Chugach also accepts a 240-volt outlet paired with a mobile Level 2 connector. There's a limit of two credits per account location, so a two-EV household with two chargers can claim $400. Chugach says plainly why it pays: it wants to know where home charging is happening on its grid. Details are on the residential EV charging program page.
  • Matanuska Electric Association (Mat-Su and Eagle River): an off-peak EV rate that cuts the price in half for charging between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m. Option 1 covers up to 500 kWh a month at the discount on a free time-of-use meter. Option 2 gives unlimited discounted off-peak charging on a second, sub-metered service that you pay an electrician to install. MEA warns that this can cost several thousand dollars, and that some homes need a transformer upgrade. As of October 2025, new applicants go on a waitlist while MEA works on meter supply. See the MEA program page. MEA also gives free Level 2 chargers to member businesses that agree to host public charging.

Other utilities, including Golden Valley Electric in Fairbanks, the Juneau and Ketchikan utilities, and rural co-ops, set their own rules. Ask yours before you buy a charger. Some will want to know about the extra load ahead of time.
