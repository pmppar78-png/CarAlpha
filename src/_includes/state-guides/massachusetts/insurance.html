Higher minimums since July 1, 2025

Massachusetts raised its required auto insurance limits under Chapter 275 of the Acts of 2024, which amended M.G.L. c. 90, § 34A. The Division of Insurance's January 2025 filing notice told insurers that the new limits apply to policies written or renewed on or after July 1, 2025. Older guides that list 20/40/5 are out of date.

Under the Division's Basics of Auto Insurance guide, you need four compulsory coverages to register a car:

  • Bodily injury to others: at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident. It covers you, or someone you let drive, for injuring or killing someone else in Massachusetts. It doesn't cover your own passengers.
  • Personal injury protection (PIP): up to $8,000 for medical expenses, up to 75% of lost wages, and replacement services.
  • Bodily injury caused by an uninsured auto: at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident. It protects you, permitted drivers, household members and passengers against uninsured and hit-and-run drivers.
  • Damage to someone else's property: at least $30,000.

How PIP works

The Division describes Massachusetts as a no-fault state. Whoever causes the accident, your PIP pays its benefits to you, anyone you let drive your car, household members, passengers in your car, and pedestrians. PIP stayed at $8,000 per person when the other limits went up.

Optional coverage every company offers

According to the Division, all insurers offer these:

  • Higher bodily injury limits of $40,000 per person and $90,000 per accident. This optional tier also covers passengers in your car and accidents outside Massachusetts.
  • Underinsured motorist coverage. Whatever you collect from the at-fault driver is deducted from it. In the Division's example, a $50,000 underinsured limit minus $25,000 recovered from the other driver leaves $25,000.
  • Collision and comprehensive, with a $500 deductible unless you have an excessive claims history or choose a different amount.
  • Medical payments coverage of at least $5,000.

An insurer may require a used private passenger car to be inspected before it provides comprehensive coverage. The inspection can be put off for ten calendar days, not counting Sundays and legal holidays. If the car isn't inspected in time, those coverages are suspended automatically. A lender may also require collision and comprehensive.

What insurers can and can't use

Massachusetts insurers can't use sex, marital status, race, creed, national origin, religion, age, occupation, income, education, home ownership or credit information to underwrite or rate a policy. They may use where the car is principally garaged to set the rate, but not to decide whether to insure you. Some discounts are required by law, including for passive restraint systems and for drivers 65 or older.

Each company's merit rating plan, approved by the Commissioner, sets how your record affects your premium. A plan can't count an accident or violation more than 6 years old, or raise your premium for one for more than 5 years. Insurers report at-fault accidents to the RMV's Merit Rating Board. If you think you were not more than 50% at fault, you can appeal to the Board of Appeal within 30 days of the notice, for a $50 fee. You must keep paying any increased premium while the appeal is pending. If you win, you get a refund or credit.

Driving without coverage

Under c. 90, § 34J, operating an uninsured vehicle, or letting it be driven or left on a public or private way, carries a fine of $500 to $5,000, up to a year in a house of correction, or both. A conviction or guilty plea also makes you liable to the state's residual market plan for the greater of $500 or one year's premium at the highest rated territory and class. The registrar suspends your license for 60 days, or for one year after a second conviction within six years. In court, a registrar's certificate showing no policy on record is enough to presume the car was uninsured unless you prove otherwise.

Switching or canceling

You can cancel at any time, but you may get only a partial refund. No refund is issued until the insurer gets a receipt showing your plates were canceled, or notice that coverage moved to another company. At renewal, you have 30 days after receiving an invoice with your new premium to change insurers without a penalty. The Division also asks you to tell your insurer within 60 days when a child in your household gets a license.

Costs and contacts

The NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement puts the Massachusetts combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,610.84, up from $1,248.44 in 2020. The average expenditure was $1,503.52. The NAIC notes that Massachusetts figures reflect the Safe Driver Plan. The Insurance Research Council estimates that 7.9% of Massachusetts drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 45th.
