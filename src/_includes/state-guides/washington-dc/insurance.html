Required coverage in the District

25/50/10 plus uninsured motorist 25/50/5: $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 bodily injury per accident, and $10,000 property damage, plus uninsured motorist coverage of $25,000/$50,000 for bodily injury and $5,000 for property damage (with a $200 deductible).

These amounts come from D.C. Code § 31-2406, part of the Compulsory/No-Fault Motor Vehicle Insurance Act. The DC DMV requires valid DC insurance to register a vehicle or get a reciprocity sticker. You need to bring a printed copy to any registration or renewal. Uninsured motorist coverage isn't optional here. Every policy on a car registered or principally garaged in the District must include it. You can raise it to as much as $100,000/$300,000 for injuries and $25,000 for property damage, and insurers must also offer underinsured motorist coverage. A policy may prohibit "stacking" uninsured motorist limits across several vehicles.

Optional no-fault PIP and the 60-day election

Insurers must offer personal injury protection (PIP), but buying it is your choice. You can take any one or any combination of its three parts (§ 31-2404). PIP pays regardless of who caused the crash, with at least:

  • $50,000 per person in medical and rehabilitation costs, with a $100,000 option
  • $12,000 in work loss, covering lost income and replacement services for up to three years, with a $24,000 option
  • actual funeral costs up to $4,000.

Having PIP doesn't automatically limit your right to sue. Instead, after a crash you have 60 days to choose whether to take PIP benefits (§ 31-2405). If you take them, you can sue the at-fault driver only if you have substantial permanent scarring or impairment, if you can't carry out your usual daily activities for more than 180 days in a row, or if your medical costs or lost wages exceed your PIP limits. Your insurer has to notify you of the deadline in writing. The deadline can be extended by written agreement, and a relative can choose for you if you're incapacitated. If you don't choose, you keep your claim against the other driver's liability coverage. Either way, families of people killed in crashes can still sue. Claims under the required coverage or PIP can be decided by binding arbitration before the Board of Consumer Claims Arbitration if both sides agree.

Coverage must stay in force while you're registered

The DMV is clear: don't cancel your insurance until you've returned your tags. Insurers report every cancellation. The DMV then sends a verification notice, and you or your new insurer have to show proof of coverage at DCIVS.org. If you don't, your registration or reciprocity sticker is suspended. Under § 31-2413, a lapse costs $150 per vehicle for 1–30 days, then $7 a day, up to $2,500 per violation. The DMV can waive some or all of that if you prove the car wasn't driven during the lapse. You won't be fined if you believed in good faith that you had coverage with a company that later went out of business.

Traffic-stop penalties

Police can ask for your license, registration, and proof of insurance at any stop. Driving without insurance carries a $500 civil fine, a license suspension of up to 30 days, or both. Repeat offenses raise the fine by 50 percent and allow a suspension of up to 60 days. Not being able to show an insurance card costs $30 and creates a presumption that you were uninsured, which you can rebut by mail or in person with your policy or other proof. A digital ID card on your phone counts, and showing it doesn't give the officer permission to look at anything else on the device. After a revocation, the DMV can require an SR-22 filing before reinstating your license or registration.

Prices and uninsured drivers

In the NAIC's 2024 figures, the District's combined average premium was $1,986.50, up from $1,541.98 in 2020 and about $374 above the national average. The average expenditure was $1,840.36. Uninsured drivers are a real risk here. The Insurance Research Council puts the District's rate at 23.1 percent, third-highest in the country (Insurance Information Institute). That's a strong reason to raise your uninsured motorist limits above the minimum.

Where to get help

Department of Insurance, Securities and Banking (DISB), 1050 First Street NE, Suite 801, Washington, DC 20002; (202) 727-8000, weekdays from 8:15 a.m. to 4:45 p.m. You can request help online or mail in the complaint form. After a non-injury crash, notify your insurer and use the DMV's non-injury checklist. Don't report crashes to the DMV. If anyone is hurt, call 911.
