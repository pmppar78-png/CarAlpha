Titling and registering a vehicle in Kansas

Original titles and registrations are handled at the county treasurer's motor vehicle office in the county where the vehicle will be kept. You can't take an assigned title to the state office in Topeka and pick up a new one there, according to the Department of Revenue's titling FAQ. After you register, the owner is responsible for renewing each year before the registration expires.

After buying a vehicle

The Division of Vehicles says you must apply for title and registration within 60 days of the purchase date to avoid a penalty. Kansas generally doesn't require notarization of title assignments, title applications or bills of sale completed in the state. It does require notarization of a lien release, a Kansas lienholder's consent, and the lienholder's part of form TR-42.

The state holds every title application in its computer system for 35 days from the purchase date. That gives lenders time to file a Notice of Security Interest, which is due within 30 days of purchase. A title with no lien usually arrives 10 to 40 days after you apply. When a lien is recorded, the title stays electronic until the loan is paid off. If you lose a title with no lien on it, you can get a duplicate with form TR-720B and the $10 fee at any county treasurer's office or by mail to the Titles and Registrations Bureau.

Moving to Kansas

According to the DOR, once a vehicle has been located in Kansas for 90 days or more, including time with frequent short absences, you must apply for a Kansas title and registration. Johnson County's new-resident page also counts 90 days from when you establish a Kansas domicile or residence. It lists these documents:

  • The original out-of-state title, because copies aren't accepted
  • A Kansas Highway Patrol VIN inspection
  • Proof of insurance
  • Your registration from the previous state

If your lender or leasing company holds the title, the county can ask it to send the original directly. Johnson County holds titles it receives for 30 days and returns them after that, so finish the inspection quickly.

VIN inspections

The Kansas Highway Patrol requires a VIN inspection before the state will issue a title or plate in these cases:

  • The vehicle has a title from another state or a foreign country
  • The vehicle was assembled, reconstructed or restored from one or more vehicles
  • The correct identification number is in doubt

The DOR adds vehicles bought out of state on a bill of sale, 1950 or newer antiques bought in Kansas on a bill of sale, and rebuilt salvage or formerly non-highway vehicles returning to the road. The inspection costs $20. When it's done at a KHP station, the county treasurer collects the fee with your title work. A designee location, such as a sheriff's office, collects it on the spot. Bring the vehicle, the original signed title, the purchase documents and a government-issued ID. You'll get an MVE-1 receipt, and the pink copy goes to the county treasurer. Active-duty military members and full-time students out of state may qualify for an out-of-state VIN verification instead. The DOR states that Kansas has no safety or emissions inspection program.

Registration fees

Annual fees for passenger vehicles are set in K.S.A. 8-143:

  • $30 for a vehicle with a gross weight of 4,500 pounds or less
  • $40 for one heavier than 4,500 pounds
  • $70 for an electric hybrid, $100 for a plug-in hybrid and $165 for an all-electric vehicle
  • $16 for a motorcycle, $30 for an all-electric motorcycle and $11 for a motorized bicycle
  • $40 for a truck registered at 12,000 pounds or less, $125 if it's a hybrid or plug-in hybrid and $200 if it's all-electric

Surcharges and other charges

Johnson County's fee page lists what's added at registration: a $2 Highway Patrol and training surcharge, a $1.25 Law Enforcement Training Center surcharge, a $4 DMV modernization surcharge and a $5 county service fee. Other charges include:

  • $10 for a title
  • $1.50 to record a lienholder
  • $0.50 for a reflectorized plate
  • $5 per transaction
  • $25 for a rebuilt salvage inspection
  • $45.50 for a personalized plate

Personal property tax is due each time you register or renew, and the DOR has a calculator to estimate it by vehicle. Sales tax may also be due the first time you register a vehicle bought from an individual or an out-of-state dealer. If you bought from a licensed Kansas dealer, bring the sales tax receipt. A vehicle bought within the past six months needs both the receipt and the bill of sale.
