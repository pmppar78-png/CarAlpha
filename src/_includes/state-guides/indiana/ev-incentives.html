EV incentives and costs in Indiana

Indiana doesn't have a state rebate or tax credit for buying a passenger EV. None of the Indiana entries in the U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) offers money to individual buyers. What the state does have is an extra annual fee for EVs and hybrids, plus several policies that shape where chargers get built.

The supplemental registration fee

Under Indiana Code § 9-18.1-5-12, EV and hybrid owners pay a supplemental fee on top of normal registration and excise tax. The BMV's fee chart, revised January 1, 2026, lists:

  • Electric vehicle supplemental fee: $242
  • Hybrid vehicle supplemental fee: $81

AFDC explains that the fees change every year based on the special fuel tax index factor. It also says the hybrid fee covers plug-in hybrids as well as conventional hybrids. AFDC's entry still shows the older amounts of $230 and $77, so check the current BMV chart before you budget. A new EV still pays the full excise tax, which is based on the manufacturer's retail price. At $42,500 or more, that's $532 in the first year.

Emissions testing

Indiana requires emissions tests for vehicles registered in Lake and Porter counties. Under 326 IAC 13-1.1-4, AFDC notes that dedicated alternative fuel vehicles running only on natural gas, propane, ethanol, hydrogen or methanol are exempt from inspection and maintenance. If you have a plug-in hybrid with a gasoline engine registered in those counties, ask Clean Air Car Check at (888) 240-1684 whether it needs a test.

Charging policy

A few state laws that AFDC lists affect public charging:

  • Charger owners aren't utilities. Under Indiana Code § 8-1-2-1.3, a person or joint agency that owns, operates or leases public chargers isn't treated as a public utility.
  • Utility pilot programs. Under § 8-1-43-8, electric utilities can ask the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission (IURC) to approve pilots for chargers serving public-use EVs, such as school buses, transit buses and delivery vehicles. Pilots can include customer rebates for vehicles and chargers, and they must plan for chargers in underserved and diverse communities.
  • Utility resource planning. In Docket No. 45816, decided in December 2023, the IURC set out its authority to name EV issues that each utility must address in their next integrated resource plans.
  • Highway fast charging. The Indiana Department of Transportation (INDOT) filed annual EV infrastructure deployment plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026 under the federal NEVI formula program. INDOT's EV charging site has details.

Regional agreements

In 2021, Indiana joined Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota and Wisconsin in the REV Midwest agreement. Its focus is electrifying medium- and heavy-duty fleets, planning chargers along commercial routes, and making regulations consistent across state lines. In 2022, Indiana, Michigan, Illinois and Wisconsin also signed the Lake Michigan EV Circuit agreement, which aims to build a charging route around the lake's shoreline.

Indiana also has an EV Commission, created under Indiana Code 9-13-3. It studies EV production capacity and the workforce, and it reports to the Indiana Economic Development Corporation by September 30 each year. For businesses, the 21st Century Research and Technology Fund offers grants and loans that can go to fuel-efficient vehicle development and production.

Before you buy

Add the supplemental fee to the registration fee and excise tax when you compare an EV with a gas car. Then check your electric utility directly for any home-charging rates or rebates, because AFDC doesn't list a statewide program for individual drivers.
