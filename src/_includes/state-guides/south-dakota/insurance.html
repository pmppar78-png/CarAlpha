South Dakota's insurance requirement

Under SDCL 32-35-113, every driver or owner of a motor vehicle must keep financial responsibility in force on the vehicle at all times, using one of four methods:

  • An owner's liability insurance policy meeting SDCL 32-35-70
  • A bond under SDCL 32-35-83
  • A certificate of deposit of money or securities, in the amount of $50,000
  • A certificate of self-insurance, with the self-insurer agreeing to pay what an insurer would have owed under an owner's policy

Failing to maintain financial responsibility is a Class 2 misdemeanor.

Minimum liability limits

SDCL 32-35-70 sets the limits for an owner's policy. It must cover the named insured and anyone driving with their express or implied permission, for liability arising from the vehicle's ownership, maintenance or use in the United States or Canada:

CoverageMinimum per vehicle
Bodily injury or death, one person, one accident$25,000
Bodily injury or death, two or more people, one accident$50,000
Damage to others' property, one accident$25,000

A policy may exclude liability coverage if the policyholder certifies in writing that the vehicle won't be driven during the policy period. It may also exclude a relative living in the household. By written agreement, a policy can exclude a named person or reduce limits when a named person drives, but any liability coverage it does give that person can't fall below the chapter 32-35 minimums (SDCL 58-11-9.3).

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

SDCL 58-11-9 requires every liability policy for a vehicle registered or principally garaged in South Dakota, other than a snowmobile, to include uninsured and hit-and-run motorist coverage for bodily injury and death. The limits must equal the policy's bodily injury limits, though the requirement stops at $100,000 per person and $300,000 per accident unless you ask for more. A vehicle whose insurer can't pay because it's insolvent counts as uninsured.

Under SDCL 58-11-9.4, policies must also include underinsured motorist coverage at a face amount equal to your bodily injury limits, subject to the same $100,000 and $300,000 ceiling unless you request more. Under SDCL 58-11-9.5, it pays your uncompensated damages when a judgment against the other driver exceeds that driver's policy limits. The payment is capped at your underinsured limits minus what the other driver's insurer paid. Policies on government-owned vehicles don't have to carry either coverage.

Proof of insurance

When an officer detains you for a suspected violation of Title 32 or another offense, you must show evidence of financial responsibility if asked, as a secondary action (SDCL 32-35-114).

If you're cited for a Title 32 violation or are in a reportable accident, you must give the officer written evidence of financial responsibility on request. An electronic copy counts if the insurer issued it as an official electronic document. If you can't produce it, the officer may cite you under SDCL 32-35-113 (SDCL 32-35-116).

Written evidence can be a copy of the policy or bond, an ID card showing the policy number, insurer, effective date and expiration date, or written confirmation from the insurer that you were covered at the time of the citation (SDCL 32-35-119). If you're cited, you can show that evidence to the magistrate named on the citation in person or by mail. Once the magistrate accepts it, the case is dismissed (SDCL 32-35-117).

If you're driving someone else's vehicle with permission and can't show proof, SDCL 32-35-118 says you can't be cited. The officer passes the information to the state's attorney for possible prosecution of the owner, by summons instead of an arrest warrant. This doesn't apply to dealers letting customers drive inventory vehicles.

Penalties

A Class 2 misdemeanor carries up to 30 days in county jail, a $500 fine, or both (SDCL 22-6-2). Giving an officer or magistrate false evidence of insurance, including an expired policy, is a Class 1 misdemeanor, punishable by up to a year in county jail, a $2,000 fine, or both (SDCL 32-35-120).

A conviction under either section also brings a license suspension of 30 days to one year (SDCL 32-35-121). During the suspension, the judge must restrict your driving to purposes such as work, child care drop-off or pickup, health appointments, school, court, probation, 24/7 sobriety testing, counseling and treatment. Before driving under the restriction, you must establish financial responsibility and keep it for the whole period (SDCL 32-35-122).

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in South Dakota was $1,290.69 (Table 5 of the Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement). The Insurance Research Council estimated that 9.4% of South Dakota drivers were uninsured, which ranked the state 43rd.
