Iowa's required liability limits

20/40/15: $20,000 for injury to or death of one person, $40,000 for injury to or death of two or more people in one accident, and $15,000 for damage to property in one accident.

Iowa's limits come from Iowa Code § 321A.21, the financial responsibility chapter's definition of a motor vehicle liability policy. Under § 321.20B, you can't drive on Iowa highways unless financial liability coverage is in effect for the vehicle and you have the insurer's proof-of-coverage card with you. If the vehicle is registered in another state, other evidence of coverage is acceptable.

The card can be on paper or electronic. The statute specifically allows showing it on a phone or other portable device with a touchscreen or small keyboard. Insurers must issue a card for each insured vehicle, listing the registration or VIN and the coverage expiration date. When a policy ends, you're supposed to destroy the card.

A $15,000 property damage limit won't cover much in a crash involving a newer vehicle. Anything above your limits can become your personal responsibility, and you're free to buy higher limits.

Uninsured, underinsured and hit-and-run coverage

Under § 516A.1, an auto liability policy for a vehicle registered or principally garaged in Iowa must include coverage for injuries caused by uninsured, underinsured and hit-and-run drivers. The limits for bodily injury or death must be at least equal to the state's financial responsibility minimums. The Commissioner of Insurance approves the form of this coverage.

The named insured can turn down all of it, or just the uninsured and hit-and-run part, or just the underinsured part, by signing a written rejection. If the insurer or agent supplies the rejection form, it must be on a separate sheet that contains only the rejection and directly related information. Once you've rejected coverage on a policy, the same insurer doesn't have to add it back to a renewal. Ask your agent which coverages your current policy actually has, especially if you signed paperwork years ago.

Stacking more than one policy

Section 516A.2 lets policies include anti-stacking terms, and the legislature abrogated Hernandez v. Farmers Insurance Company (1990) to the extent that the decision allowed stacking against the policy language. When more than one of your policies provides this coverage, the stated intent is that you can recover up to the highest single limit under any one of them, paid according to the policies' priority rules. The statute also directed the Commissioner of Insurance to adopt rules making policies or endorsements that do allow stacking available in Iowa. If you own several vehicles, ask whether your policy stacks before you rely on the combined limits.

What happens at a traffic stop

If an officer stops a vehicle registered in Iowa and the driver can't show proof of coverage, § 321.20B gives the officer four options:

  • Issue a warning memorandum
  • Issue a citation
  • Issue a citation and remove the plates and registration receipt
  • Issue a citation, remove the plates and registration receipt, and impound the vehicle

If your plates are removed, you may drive the vehicle for up to 48 hours only to get it off the road, and you must keep the citation in the vehicle while you do. An impounded vehicle can be claimed with proof of coverage and payment of any fine and the towing and storage costs. If no one claims it within 30 days, it may be treated as abandoned. A lienholder must be notified within 72 hours of an impoundment.

Fines and fees

If you were actually insured and show proof to the clerk of court before your court date, you get a receipt. You then pay the county treasurer a $15 administrative fee for new plates and registration. If you weren't insured, you can either sign an admission and pay the scheduled fine or ask for a court appearance. Section 805.8A sets the scheduled fine at $325. If the violation is connected to a motor vehicle accident, it's $645. Getting new plates afterward requires proof of payment, proof of coverage and the $15 fee.

Average premiums in Iowa

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners put Iowa's 2024 average combined premium for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,132.96, and its average expenditure at $973.17. In 2020 the combined average was $822.17. These figures average many different drivers and policy choices, so use them as rough context. Your own quote will depend on your record, vehicle and coverage.

The Insurance Research Council estimated that 11.4% of Iowa drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 33rd. About one driver in nine may have no liability coverage to pay for injuries they cause, which is what uninsured motorist coverage is for.

Collision and comprehensive coverage aren't required by state law, but a lender or leasing company may require them in the financing contract.

Questions and complaints

The Iowa Insurance Division regulates the insurers and agents that sell auto coverage in the state. It's at 1963 Bell Avenue, Suite 100, Des Moines, IA 50315, and its phone number is (515) 654-6600. For plate, registration or impoundment questions, start with your county treasurer.
