Annual EV fees

Washington charges plug-in vehicles extra fees at each annual registration renewal. They apply to a vehicle that can be recharged from an external source of electricity, can travel at least 30 miles on battery power alone, and is designed to go faster than 35 miles per hour:

  • A $100 fee under RCW 46.17.323(1), which is used for highway purposes and to study a shift from fuel taxes to a road user assessment
  • A $50 fee under RCW 46.17.323(4), with the first $1,000,000 going to the multimodal transportation account
  • A $75 transportation electrification fee under RCW 46.17.324

On the Department of Licensing (DOL) fee list, those come to $225 a year on top of standard fees. They're collected at renewal. Under RCW 46.17.323(7), the fees in that section stop once legislation imposing a vehicle-miles-traveled fee or tax takes effect.

Other electric and hybrid vehicles pay smaller fees:

  • An electric motorcycle pays $30 at renewal under RCW 46.17.323(5).
  • A hybrid or alternative fuel vehicle that doesn't owe the $100 and $50 fees pays a $75 hybrid vehicle transportation electrification fee under RCW 46.17.324(2).

RCW 46.17.324 states its purpose as supporting EV adoption, charging infrastructure, greener transit options and clean alternative fuel infrastructure. Its fees went to the electric vehicle account until July 1, 2025, when they began going to the motor vehicle fund.

The sales and use tax exemption

The Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) describes Washington's retail sales and use tax exemption under RCW 82.08.9999 for passenger vehicles, light-duty trucks and medium-duty passenger vehicles powered by natural gas, propane, hydrogen or electricity. New vehicles couldn't be valued above $45,000 and used vehicles above $30,000. From August 1, 2023, to July 31, 2025, up to $15,000 of a new vehicle's price and $16,000 of a used one's could be exempted.

The exemption for sales of new and used vehicles expired on July 31, 2025. Leases that qualified on or before that date can keep claiming the exemption on lease payments due through July 31, 2028.

Instant rebates

The Department of Commerce ran the Washington EV Instant Rebate Program for households with income below 300 percent of the federal poverty level. AFDC lists rebates of up to $9,000 for a new EV and $2,500 for a pre-owned one, with one rebate per applicant and no more than three per address. Commerce reports an average rebate of $7,360, applied at the point of sale by more than 300 participating dealers.

In October 2026, Commerce opened a request for proposals for a firm to develop and run a $5 million point-of-sale EV rebate program, with proposals due by 4 p.m. Pacific on November 20, 2026. The project period runs from January to June 2027 with an option to extend through June 2029, and at least $4,200,000 of the contract is for incentives. The earliest a contract may be signed is January 22, 2027.

Emissions checks

Washington's emission-check program ended on January 1, 2020. AFDC lists RCW 46.16A.060 as exempting vehicles powered exclusively by electricity, natural gas, liquefied petroleum gas or propane, along with plug-in hybrids rated at least 50 miles per gallon in city driving.

Charging rules

Under RCW 46.08.185, public EV charging spaces need signs identifying them as reserved for EV charging, and parking in one without connecting to the charger brings a $124 fine. Chargers must show all charges and fees. Department of Agriculture rules require EV service providers to meet interoperability standards and offer multiple payment methods, though chargers installed before January 1, 2024, are exempt until January 1, 2034. AFDC lists annual registration fees for charger ports of $20 per Level 2 port and $40 per DC fast charging port.

Under Senate Bill 5129 (2025), a unit owners' association can't prohibit or restrict installing or using a charger in a personal, noncommercial parking space. It can set reasonable restrictions for chargers on association property or common areas. As the charger owner, you pay for installation, maintenance and electricity and register the charger with the association.

State goals and standards

RCW 43.392.020 sets a goal that all light-duty vehicles sold, purchased or registered in Washington be electric for model year 2030 and later. Washington has also adopted California's Advanced Clean Cars II standards, starting with model year 2026, which require all new passenger vehicles sold in the state to be zero-emission vehicles by 2035.
