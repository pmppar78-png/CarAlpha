Deadline for new residents

RSA 261:45 gives someone who establishes bona fide residency in New Hampshire a maximum of 60 days to register their vehicles in the state. A business that sets up a place of business in New Hampshire and keeps vehicles garaged there for intrastate work has the same 60 days. Until then, RSA 261:44 lets a nonresident's current out-of-state registration be used to the extent the home state grants New Hampshire residents the same privilege.

Two steps: town, then state

The NH Division of Motor Vehicles describes registration as a two-part process. Every vehicle except a moped starts at the town or city clerk where the owner lives. The clerk confirms your residency and collects the municipal permit fee. The state portion comes second. Many clerks act as municipal agents of the state and can complete it for an extra fee; otherwise you finish at a DMV office. The DMV can't process the state registration until the town fees are paid.

What to bring depends on how you got the vehicle:

  • New Hampshire dealer: the title or the blue copies of the title application, plus proof of residency.
  • Out-of-state dealer: the title from the dealer or the New Hampshire title application from the lienholder. A brand-new vehicle needs the Manufacturer's Statement of Origin.
  • Private sale: a properly assigned title.
  • Title-exempt vehicle: a bill of sale under RSA 261:148 and proof of the VIN, such as a title copy, an earlier New Hampshire registration, or a VIN verification form.

If you have an out-of-state loan or lease, the DMV points you to its 60-day temporary plate process.

The municipal permit fee

RSA 261:153 sets the town or city permit fee as a share of the maker's list price, which drops each year:

  • 18 mills per dollar (1.8%) for a current model year vehicle
  • 15 mills for the first preceding model year
  • 12 mills for the second
  • 9 mills for the third
  • 6 mills for the fourth
  • 3 mills for the fifth preceding model year and older

The list price is rounded to the nearest $100, and the fee to the nearest dollar, with a $5 minimum. On a vehicle with a $40,000 list price, that's $720 the first year and $120 from the sixth year on. Under the straight-line method in paragraph IV, each rate applies for 12 months of registration, so you pay a full year at each step.

Towns can add a reclamation trust fee of $3 for automobiles and light trucks. They can also vote, at town meeting or by city council, to add up to $5 for a municipal transportation improvement fund, which can pay for roads, bridges, transit and EV charging stations.

State registration fee

RSA 261:141 bases the state fee on gross weight for most motor vehicles:

  • Up to 3,000 pounds: $42 a year ($3.50 a month)
  • 3,001 to 5,000 pounds: $48 ($4 a month)
  • 5,001 to 8,000 pounds: $66 ($5.50 a month)
  • Over 8,000 pounds: $1.06 per hundred pounds

Motorcycles pay $30 and mopeds $14. Registrations expire at the end of the owner's birth month, so a first registration is prorated by the month. Electronic processing adds $5 under RSA 261:141-b. Electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles pay the surcharge in RSA 261:141-c, covered on our EV page.

Other state fees include $4 per plate under RSA 261:75, which the DMV lists as an $8 plate fee for first-time plates, plus $10 to transfer a registration to another vehicle, $20 for a duplicate registration and $25 to restore a suspended one. Some disabled veterans and former prisoners of war pay no state registration fee.

Title fee and sales tax

Since January 1, 2026, RSA 261:20 has set the fee for a first certificate of title, a title after transfer or a duplicate title at $35, up from $25. The town permit fee is the part of the bill that follows the vehicle's list price; the state fee follows its weight.

Renewals and transfers

Renewals also start with the town. If you're temporarily out of state, the DMV lets you mail your renewal notice and payment to your town within four months before expiration. A plate transfer credit applies only when the primary owner's name stays the same on both registrations, and the old vehicle can't be driven once its plates move.

The DMV's guidance dated February 13, 2026 says the state inspection program is suspended until further notice, so no inspection sticker is needed while that stands.
