Oregon's insurance requirement

Under ORS 806.010, you commit the offense of driving uninsured if you operate a motor vehicle on a highway or premises open to the public in Oregon without one of two things: insurance under a motor vehicle liability policy that meets ORS 806.080, or other proof of financial responsibility that satisfies the Department of Transportation. ORS 806.070 sets the minimum liability amounts:

CoverageMinimum
Bodily injury or death of one person in one accident$25,000
Bodily injury or death of two or more people in one accident$50,000
Damage to others' property in one accident$20,000

That's written 25/50/20. On top of liability, an Oregon policy on a private passenger vehicle must include personal injury protection and uninsured motorist coverage, described below.

Personal injury protection

ORS 742.520 requires every motor vehicle liability policy issued for delivery in Oregon on a private passenger vehicle to provide personal injury protection (PIP) benefits. PIP covers the insured, family members in the same household, children being raised in the household, passengers in the insured vehicle and pedestrians it strikes. ORS 742.524 sets the minimum benefits per person:

  • Medical expenses: reasonable and necessary medical, hospital, dental, surgical, ambulance and prosthetic expenses incurred within two years of the injury, up to $15,000.
  • Lost income: if you work and are disabled for at least 14 days, 70% of lost income, up to $3,000 a month for up to 52 weeks.
  • Essential services: if you don't work for pay and are disabled for at least 14 days, the cost of hiring someone outside your household to do the work you'd have done, up to $30 a day for up to 52 weeks.
  • Funeral expenses: up to $5,000, incurred within one year.
  • Child care: for a parent of a minor child who is hospitalized at least 24 hours, $25 a day after the first 24 hours, up to $750.

Insurers may offer deductibles of up to $250 on the medical, income and essential services benefits for you and household members. Medical expenses are presumed reasonable and necessary unless the insurer notifies the provider of a denial within 60 days of receiving notice of the claim.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

ORS 742.502 requires uninsured motorist (UM) coverage in every liability policy issued for delivery in Oregon, or for a vehicle principally used or garaged here. The UM limits must match your bodily injury liability limits unless a named insured chooses lower limits in writing, and you can't go below the ORS 806.070 minimums.

UM coverage must include underinsurance coverage. It pays when the at-fault driver's liability insurance is less than the damages you're legally entitled to recover, up to your UM limits.

If you choose lower limits, you must sign a statement within 60 days acknowledging that you were offered UM coverage equal to your liability limits. The statement includes a summary of what UM and underinsured coverage provide and the price of each option.

Under ORS 742.510, insurers must also offer uninsured motorist property damage coverage for your own vehicle, at least equal to the $20,000 property damage minimum. It doesn't pay the first $300 of damage after a hit-and-run or phantom vehicle accident, or the first $200 in other cases, and it doesn't cover loss of use.

Carrying proof

ORS 806.011 requires proof of insurance, or other current proof of compliance approved by DMV rule, in every non-exempt vehicle operating in Oregon. You can show it on an electronic device, and doing so doesn't give an officer consent to look at anything else on the device. You may black out the addresses on the card, but nothing else.

If you can't show proof when an officer asks, that gives the officer reasonable grounds to believe you're driving uninsured. Not carrying proof is itself an offense under ORS 806.012.

Penalties

Driving uninsured is a Class B traffic violation under ORS 806.010(5). Under ORS 153.018 and 153.019, a Class B violation carries a maximum fine of $1,000 and a presumptive fine of $265. Other consequences under ORS 806.010:

  • If you're in an accident while uninsured, your driving privileges are subject to suspension under ORS 809.417.
  • After a conviction, you must file proof of financial responsibility with DMV and keep it on file for one year. Failure to do so is subject to ORS 809.415.

ORS 806.020 exempts some vehicles, including antique vehicles with permanent registration, farm trailers and tractors, implements of husbandry and certain collector vehicles used for exhibitions and parades.

What coverage costs

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in Oregon was $1,374.74 (Table 5 of the Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement). The Insurance Research Council estimated that 14.7% of Oregon drivers were uninsured, which ranked the state 18th.
