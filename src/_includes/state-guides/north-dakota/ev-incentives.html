Purchase incentives

The Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC), run by the U.S. Department of Energy, lists no North Dakota rebate or tax credit for individuals who buy or lease an EV. The state programs it lists are grants for fleets and energy projects, plus a few rules for charging and road use.

Annual road use fees

Under N.D.C.C. § 39-04-19.2, North Dakota collects a road use fee each registration year on top of regular registration fees:

VehicleAnnual fee
Electric vehicle$120
Plug-in hybrid$50
Electric motorcycle$20

An electric vehicle is defined as one powered by an electric motor running on a battery or other onboard electric device, with no engine burning gasoline, diesel, propane or natural gas. A plug-in hybrid draws energy from an engine, an energy storage device and a plug that accepts grid electricity. The money goes to the highway tax distribution fund.

Fleet and project grants

  • Clean diesel grants: The Department of Environmental Quality (NDDEQ) awards grants to cut diesel emissions from on-road and non-road fleets. AFDC says eligible projects can include school bus replacements and zero-emission, hybrid or alternative-fuel options, and that funding and focus change from cycle to cycle.
  • Volkswagen settlement grants: NDDEQ's repower and replacement program for medium- and heavy-duty vehicles can cover up to 38% of non-government and 50% of government project costs, with all-electric vehicles eligible. AFDC reports that no funding is available at present.
  • Clean Sustainable Energy Authority: The Industrial Commission's authority gives grants to businesses, cooperatives and associations for low-emission technology projects in the state.

Charging rules

Section 24-02-45.4 lets the Department of Transportation (NDDOT) accept federal or other non-state funds to run reimbursable EV charging grants and set award criteria, including for operation and maintenance. NDDOT also prepared the state's plans for the federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) formula program for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.

Under § 49-03-01.5, a person or business that resells utility electricity at an EV charging station isn't regulated as a public utility.

A parking space for EV charging has to carry NDDOT-approved signs consistent with the federal Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices. Under §§ 39-13-06 and 39-10-50.1, you can't stop, stand or park in one unless your vehicle is connected and charging, and a non-EV parked there faces a $50 fee.

Low-speed vehicles

Chapter 39-29.1 defines a low-speed vehicle as a four-wheeled vehicle with a top speed between 20 and 25 mph and a fully loaded weight of 3,000 pounds or less. It may not use roads posted above 35 mph except to cross them, and it needs a $20 registration good for two years.
