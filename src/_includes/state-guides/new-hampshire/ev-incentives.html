EV rules and programs in New Hampshire

The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center lists 16 New Hampshire laws and incentives, and none of them is a purchase rebate or tax credit for an individual EV buyer. The entry that applies to every EV owner is a fee: New Hampshire charges an annual surcharge on electric and plug-in hybrid registrations. The rest cover charging station rules, fleet grants, planning and legislative studies.

The EV registration surcharge

RSA 261:141-c, in effect since July 1, 2023, adds a surcharge to each annual registration:

  • $100 for a battery electric vehicle
  • $50 for a plug-in hybrid

The money goes to the highway fund, and the DMV and Department of Transportation report the revenue each year to the transportation council and legislative leaders. The surcharge is on top of the weight-based state fee and the town permit fee.

A 2026 amendment replaces that schedule on January 1, 2027 with one based on gross vehicle weight rating:

  • BEV up to 1,000 pounds: $0
  • BEV from 1,001 to 3,000 pounds: $50
  • BEV from 3,001 to 8,000 pounds: $100
  • BEV over 8,000 pounds: $150
  • PHEV of any weight: $50

The DMV will classify vehicles from manufacturer specifications and registration records, and it may adopt rules on exemptions. A 2023 legislative commission is also studying alternatives to the road toll for electric, hybrid and fuel-efficient vehicles, with reports due by December 1, 2026.

Public charging rules

RSA 236:133 and 236:134 set the rules for public chargers:

  • Chargers paid for with settlement money, competitive grants or the Volkswagen Mitigation Trust must support universal access as defined by NHDOT.
  • A paid station must accept multiple payment options and can't require a subscription or membership.
  • Owners may set time limits on charging, and they must report each station's location, voltage and timing restrictions to the AFDC.
  • Only EVs may park in a space with a public charger.
  • Owning charging equipment doesn't make someone a public utility.

NHDOT also has to keep EV charging signs on federal highways uniform and develop signs for state roads that follow the federal Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices.

Under RSA 374-F and 374-G, the Public Utilities Commission and Department of Energy may set standards and rate mechanisms for time-based rates, demand response and utility EV charging programs. Towns that adopt the optional transportation fee under RSA 261:153, VI can spend it on EV charging stations.

Grants and fleet programs

The Department of Environmental Services distributes federal Diesel Emissions Reduction Act funds, covering up to 100% of eligible costs for projects that cut diesel emissions. Businesses, schools, municipalities, transit companies and agencies can apply, and electricity is an eligible fuel for replacement vehicles. Executive Order 2016-03 directs the state to pursue EV purchases for its own fleet and to install charging for state agencies.

Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, NHDOT submitted annual EV infrastructure deployment plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026, posted on its EV charging infrastructure website.

Neighborhood electric vehicles

Under RSA 259:66-b, 265:158 and 266:114, a neighborhood electric vehicle is a four-wheel EV with a top speed of 20 to 25 mph that meets the federal standard in 49 C.F.R. 571.500. It may be driven on roads posted at 35 mph or less and can cross faster roads.

Federal tax credits depend on current federal law, so check IRS guidance before counting on one.
