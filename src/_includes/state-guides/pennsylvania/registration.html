Pennsylvania registration fees

PennDOT's Bureau of Motor Vehicles publishes its fees on Form MV-70S, the Schedule of Fees (revision 4-26). The schedule lists one-year and two-year fees, and the two-year fee is double the one-year fee:

Passenger vehicle$48$96
Motorcycle$25$50
Motor-driven cycle or motorized pedalcycle$11$22
Motor home, 8,000 lbs. or less$88$176
Truck, 5,000 lbs. or less$82$164
Truck, 5,001 to 7,000 lbs.$111$222

The registration fee includes a standard plate the first time one is issued. Specialty plates cost extra. Heavier trucks are charged by registered gross weight on a separate table, and apportioned and fleet vehicles can't take a two-year registration. Trailers of 10,000 lbs. or less can be registered for one year or five, at $7 or $35 for 3,000 lbs. or less.

Some counties also charge a $5 Fee for Local Use each year the registration is valid. PennDOT keeps a fact sheet listing the participating counties.

Reduced and free registration

A retired owner whose total income doesn't exceed $30,778 a year may qualify for a $12 processing fee in place of the registration fee, using Form MV-371 or MV-140. The reduced rate applies to one passenger car or truck with a registered weight of 9,000 pounds or less. Registration is free for vehicles of totally disabled veterans with a service-connected disability, veterans who lost a limb or eye or became partially paralyzed in service, former prisoners of war, and recipients of the Medal of Honor or the Purple Heart.

Title and other fees

  • Certificate of title: $72, and the same for a duplicate title
  • Recording a lien: $36 per lien, on top of the title fee
  • Transfer of a registration plate: $11
  • Duplicate registration card: $2 at the time of a registration transaction, $7 at other times, and free online
  • Replacement of a defaced, lost or stolen standard plate: $14; replacement of an illegible plate is free
  • Personalizing a standard plate: $103
  • Motor vehicle registration restoration: $119
  • Civil penalty in place of suspension: $500

Sales tax

When ownership changes, the buyer completes Form MV-4ST, the Vehicle Sales and Use Tax Return/Application for Registration, which is available from PennDOT's authorized agents. The Department of Revenue lists the Commonwealth sales tax at 6 percent. It also notes that Act 21 of 2026 requires vendors to collect Philadelphia's 2 percent and Allegheny County's 1 percent local sales taxes on taxable sales to customers in those counties.

Buying a vehicle and renewing

To transfer ownership of a Pennsylvania vehicle you need the Pennsylvania title, a Pennsylvania driver's license or photo ID, a copy of your current insurance card if a registration will be issued, and Form MV-4ST. You can't renew a registration without insurance; trailers are the exception. Renewals can be done online, by mail, at a messenger service or at a Driver and Vehicle Services center. Online renewal isn't available if more than six months remain on the current registration. Report a new address to PennDOT within 15 days of moving.

New residents

New residents must apply to title and register their vehicles within 20 days of establishing residency, and have 60 days to get a Pennsylvania driver's license. The out-of-state title must be surrendered with Form MV-1, the Application for Certificate of Title, which isn't available online and must be completed by an authorized agent, such as a notary, dealer or messenger service. Bring:

  • a valid title in your name from your previous state for each vehicle
  • proof of Pennsylvania insurance showing effective and expiration dates
  • a tracing of the VIN, or verification of the VIN by an inspection mechanic or a notary at a dealership or issuing agent, and
  • the title, lien and registration fees and any sales tax due.

If your lender holds the out-of-state title, it may require you to refinance through another lender so the title can be released.

Safety and emissions inspection

Inspection stations pay PennDOT $12 for an annual safety inspection sticker and $10 for a semi-annual one. Emission inspection stickers carry no state fee. Under 67 Pa. Code § 177.51, gasoline vehicles from model year 1975 and newer with a GVWR of 9,000 pounds or less that are registered in a designated I/M county or region must pass an emission inspection each year along with the safety inspection. The safety sticker can't be put on until the vehicle passes, or gets an exemption or a waiver. Current model year vehicles and vehicles driven less than 5,000 miles a year are exempt.

Electric vehicles

Since April 1, 2025, Pennsylvania has charged EV owners a yearly Road User Charge under Act 85 of 2024. For 2026 the flat fee is $250 for one year or $500 for two years for an EV, and $63 or $126 for a plug-in hybrid. The registration period has to match the Road User Charge period. The AFDC notes that EVs paying the fee are exempt from the Alternative Fuels Tax.
