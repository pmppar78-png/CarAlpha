Indiana's required liability limits

25/50/25: $25,000 for injury to or death of one person, $50,000 for injury to or death of two or more people in one accident, and $25,000 for property damage in one accident.

According to the Indiana Driver's Manual, you may not operate a motor vehicle in Indiana unless financial responsibility is in effect for it, or you're otherwise insured to drive it. A liability policy at the state minimum, commonly called 25/50/25, is the usual way to meet that rule. Bodily injury liability pays, up to your limits, what you owe people you injure through your own negligence. Property damage liability does the same for other people's property.

Those are minimums. When a crash costs more than your policy limits, you can be personally responsible for the difference.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

The Indiana Department of Insurance (IDOI) says every newly written auto liability policy in Indiana must include uninsured (UM) and underinsured (UIM) motorist coverage unless you reject it in writing. These coverages let you collect from your own insurer when the at-fault driver has no insurance or not enough. IDOI lists these minimum limits:

  • UM bodily injury: $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident
  • UM property damage: $25,000 for policies issued on or after July 1, 2017
  • UIM bodily injury: $50,000

Under Indiana Code § 27-7-5-2, insurers must provide UM and UIM in limits at least equal to your bodily injury liability limits, unless you reject them in writing. Insurers may not sell UIM below $50,000. You can reject both coverages, or just one if the insurer sells them separately. A named insured's rejection covers everyone on the policy, and the insurer doesn't have to offer the coverage again on a renewal or replacement policy unless you ask for it in writing. You also can't carry UM property damage coverage unless you carry UM bodily injury coverage too.

When the BMV asks for proof

The Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) asks for proof of insurance after any of these:

  • An accident for which it receives a crash report
  • A pointable moving violation within one year of two other pointable moving violations
  • A serious traffic violation, such as a misdemeanor or felony
  • Any pointable violation by a driver previously suspended for failing to prove financial responsibility

Your insurer answers by electronically filing a Certificate of Compliance (COC) showing that you and the vehicle were insured on the date in question. The police officer won't hand you a form. The BMV must receive and process the COC within 90 days of mailing its request, or your driving privileges will be suspended. IDOI warns not to send a COC to the Department of Insurance, since it won't reach the BMV. If you were driving a company or rental vehicle, the employer or rental company completes State Form 55434 instead.

Suspensions, SR22 filings and fees

If you're suspended after a court conviction for driving without financial responsibility, or for not filing insurance with the BMV, your insurer has to file an SR22, which is proof of future financial responsibility. An SR22 policy can't be canceled without notice to the BMV, and if an SR26 cancellation notice arrives, the suspension comes back.

Once a no-insurance suspension ends, the BMV charges a reinstatement fee on top of any SR22 requirement. For suspensions after January 1, 2015, the fee is $250 for a first suspension, $500 for a second, and $1,000 for a third or later one. You can avoid the fee by having your insurer file an SR22 and keeping it in force for 180 consecutive days. Fees can be paid at myBMV.com, at a BMV Connect kiosk, by mail, or by phone at (888) 692-6841.

Cancellation and rates

IDOI says an insurer may cancel a new policy within its first 60 days. After that, it can cancel only for specific reasons, such as unpaid premiums or losing your license. At the end of a policy term, it can decline to renew if it gives you 20 days' notice. IDOI doesn't set rates, but insurers must file rates and forms with the department before using them, and those filings are open for public inspection.

IDOI lists the factors insurers may use in pricing: age, sex, marital status, driving record, how the car is used, where you live, limits, deductibles, the type of car, driver training, claims history and credit scores. When you compare quotes, make sure each one uses the same coverage. If no insurer will take you, any Indiana agent can apply for you through the Indiana Auto Insurance Plan.

Average premiums in Indiana

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners reported an Indiana average combined premium of $1,142.11 for 2024, covering liability, collision and comprehensive, and an average expenditure of $1,019.29. In 2020 the combined figure was $863.29. Averages blend very different drivers and coverage choices, so your quote may differ a lot.

The Insurance Research Council estimated that 14.0% of Indiana drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 22nd. That's roughly one driver in seven, which is a good reason to think twice before rejecting UM and UIM coverage.

Questions and complaints

IDOI's Consumer Services Department handles questions and complaints about auto insurers and agents. You can reach it at (800) 622-4461 or consumerservices@idoi.in.gov, or write to 311 W. Washington Street, Suite 103, Indianapolis, IN 46204-2787. You can also file a complaint online. For COC, SR22 and suspension questions, contact the BMV.
