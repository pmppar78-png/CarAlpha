Registration fees

South Carolina registers vehicles every two years. The SCDMV's fee schedule lists these biennial amounts:

These amounts come from § 56-3-620, which also sets $46 for a property-carrying vehicle of 6,000 pounds or less owned by someone 65 or older. Sixteen dollars of each biennial fee goes to the Infrastructure Maintenance Trust Fund. Owners claiming the disability rate must submit a doctor's statement that the disability is permanent.

The gross vehicle weight fee runs from $30 for up to 4,000 pounds to $110 for 10,001 to 11,000 pounds, in $10 steps above 4,000 pounds except for a $20 jump from the 4,001–5,000 class to the 5,001–6,000 class. The SCDMV's Schedule of License Fees covers heavier trucks and trailers.

Road use fees for electric and hybrid vehicles

Section 56-3-645 adds a biennial road use fee on top of registration: $120 for a vehicle powered exclusively by electricity, hydrogen or another fuel not subject to the state motor fuel user fee, and $60 for a vehicle that combines taxed motor fuel with one of those power sources. The SCDMV collects it at titling or registration, and the money goes to the Infrastructure Maintenance Trust Fund.

Title and the infrastructure maintenance fee

The title fee is $15 under § 56-19-420, which also covers a title transfer, a duplicate title and changes to transfer-on-death beneficiaries. An expedited title, available in person, is $35.

When you first title or register a vehicle in South Carolina, you pay the infrastructure maintenance fee (§ 56-3-627). For a vehicle bought or leased from a dealer, it's 5% of the sales price, up to $500, and a dealer that offers to title or register the vehicle collects it. For a private purchase, it's 5% of fair market value, up to $500. Exclusions include transfers to immediate family members and to heirs. A lessee who buys the leased vehicle doesn't pay it again if the registrant stays the same.

To title a vehicle, the SCDMV asks for the Title Application (Form 400), the title signed over to you with the price and odometer reading, a bill of sale when needed, the title fee and any IMF due.

Moving to South Carolina

Before transferring a title and registration, new residents must pay property tax on the vehicle. You give the county auditor the VIN and your latest registration card, pay the resulting bill to the county treasurer and bring the original paid receipt to the SCDMV. You'll also need Form 400, your out-of-state title or registration, identification, and information on your insurer, which must be licensed in South Carolina.

New residents pay a one-time $250 IMF for each vehicle. The SCDMV says a regular passenger vehicle usually costs $305 to bring in: the $250 IMF, the $15 title fee and the $40 registration fee, plus county property tax. Your new title won't be mailed until the out-of-state title clears the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System, but you'll get your registration before then.

Plates and temporary tags

A registered vehicle has a plate, a color-coded decal in the plate's lower right corner and a registration card. The SCDMV can't issue a registration until a title has been issued, and you must pay county property taxes first. For a temporary plate, bring the title, bill of sale or lease contract plus the Application to Issue or Replace 60-Day Temporary Plates (Form 60-A) to a branch.
