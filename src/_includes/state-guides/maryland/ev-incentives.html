The excise tax credit, up to $3,000

Maryland's main EV purchase incentive is a credit against the 6.5% vehicle excise tax. It was created by the Clean Cars Act of 2022 and is codified at Transportation Article § 13-815. The MVA runs it. The credit is $3,000 for a zero-emission plug-in electric or fuel cell vehicle, $2,000 for a three-wheeled electric motorcycle or autocycle, and $1,000 for a two-wheeled electric motorcycle.

To qualify, the vehicle must:

  • be bought new and titled for the first time on or after July 1, 2023, and before July 1, 2027
  • have a base price of no more than $50,000 (the price before options, shipping, taxes, fees or trade-in, as shown on the window sticker)
  • have a battery of at least 5.0 kilowatt-hours (4.0 for a motorcycle or autocycle), plus a top speed of at least 55 mph
  • be unmodified from the manufacturer's specifications, be acquired for your own use or lease (resale doesn't qualify), and be registered in Maryland

Each individual can get the credit on one vehicle, and each business on up to 10. The statute says the credit is allowed "subject to available funding." Under the program, the Maryland Energy Administration transfers up to $8.25 million each fiscal year to the Transportation Trust Fund to pay for it.

Is there money right now?

Funding has run out before. An MVA dealer bulletin dated August 12, 2025, said the fiscal 2026 money was gone and that applicants were being placed on a waiting list for fiscal 2027. Dealers had to warn customers before a sale that they might not receive the credit. The MVA's October 1, 2026 bulletin reports that fiscal 2027 funding is available, with $2.2 million in credits left.

To apply, you need form VR-334, a copy of the window (Monroney) sticker, the bill of sale and the Manufacturer's Certificate of Origin. The MVA prefers that dealers submit these through the dealer portal, and you don't need to apply separately if the dealer does. You can also send them yourself by email to mvaelectricrefunds@mdot.maryland.gov or by mail, but that may slow things down. The MVA says refund checks arrive about 4 to 6 weeks after it has all the documents.

Charger rebates

According to the Alternative Fuels Data Center, the Maryland Energy Administration offers a rebate on the cost of buying and installing qualified chargers. It covers up to 50% of costs, capped at $700 per charger for residential applicants and $5,000 for businesses, nonprofits, workplaces, multifamily housing and state or local governments. Rebates are first-come, first-served.

Annual surcharges and other rules

EV owners pay a yearly surcharge on top of regular registration: $125 for a zero-emission vehicle and $100 for a plug-in hybrid (§ 13-956). All-electric vehicles are exempt from VEIP emissions testing, but hybrids must be tested. Parking in a marked EV charging space without a plugged-in EV can mean a $100 fine. Since January 1, 2026, chargers used in commercial transactions must be registered with the Maryland Department of Agriculture's Weights and Measures program, at $150 per charger. Maryland has also adopted California's Advanced Clean Cars II standards, which require manufacturers to meet ZEV sales requirements.
