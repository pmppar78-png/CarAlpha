Who handles registration in Idaho

The Idaho Transportation Department's Division of Motor Vehicles sets the rules, but you do the paperwork at a county DMV office. Those offices are run by each county's sheriff or assessor. Generally, only Idaho residents and businesses can title and register vehicles here. Residents who are temporarily out of state, such as students and military personnel, can apply for titles and registrations by mail. A vehicle has to be titled in Idaho before it can be registered, and you can apply for registration as soon as the title application is submitted.

Moving to Idaho

ITD's new-to-Idaho guide gives you 30 days after moving to title and register your vehicle, whether or not your old registration has expired. That's the same deadline as for getting an Idaho driver's license. Bring the vehicle itself to the DMV, because an employee will inspect its vehicle identification number (VIN) there. You'll also need:

  • Your current out-of-state title and registration, or the lienholder's name and address if the lender holds the title
  • A bill of sale if the price isn't written on the title
  • Personal identification

ITD also asks you to tell your insurer about the move so your liability policy is with a carrier admitted in Idaho, since the DMV checks coverage electronically.

If a lender holds your out-of-state title, Ada County, for example, asks you to send the lienholder a lien letter so the title can be released directly to the county office. Once it arrives, the county tells you by mail to bring the vehicle in. If your title has been lost, you'll need a duplicate from the state that issued it before Idaho can title the car.

Titling a purchase

The ITD titles page covers each kind of sale. An Idaho dealer prepares and files the title application for you within 30 days of delivery. For a purchase from an out-of-state dealer or a private seller, the dealer or a financing lender may file for you. If they don't, you have to file with a county office within 30 days of the transfer or pay a $20 late-filing penalty. The documents are:

  • The existing title signed over by the previous owner and released by any lienholder, or the manufacturer's certificate of origin for a new vehicle
  • An odometer disclosure for vehicles newer than model year 2010 that weigh under 16,000 pounds
  • A VIN inspection by a law enforcement officer, DMV employee, military police officer or licensed Idaho dealer if the vehicle comes from another state or has never been titled in Idaho

Sellers should file a release of liability, which costs $3.50 and is due within five days of delivery.

Title fees and sales tax

The state title fee is $14, and each county adds its own title administrative fee. On ITD's county table, the add-on ranges from nothing (in Bannock, Boise, Camas, Caribou, Clark and Power counties) to $18.75 in Blaine County. That puts the total title fee between $14 and $32.75. Ada and Canyon both charge $21. Optional rush processing costs another $26, and a duplicate title is $14.

Idaho charges 6% sales or use tax on the purchase price, less any trade-in on a dealer sale, unless an exemption applies. Local option taxes may also apply. According to Ada County, a new resident doesn't owe the tax again with proof of paying at least 6% in the state of purchase, or proof that the vehicle was bought at least three months before the move.

Registration fees by vehicle age

Under Idaho Code § 49-402, the annual state fee for cars, pickups and other vehicles up to 8,000 pounds depends on age:

  • $69 for a vehicle one or two years old
  • $57 for a vehicle three to six years old
  • $45 for a vehicle seven years old or older

Electric vehicles pay $140 more each year, and plug-in hybrids pay $75 more. Counties add a registration administrative fee, which ranges from $3.00 in Lewis County to $14.00 in Kootenai County on ITD's table. Motorcycles pay a $19 state fee, and registration fees aren't refundable.

Staggered renewals

Idaho uses 12 staggered registration periods, one starting each month. Your registration runs for 12 months and expires at midnight on the last day of the month shown on your registration card. When you renew, you stay in the same month. The card is the only proof of the expiration date. A first registration can be prorated by the month for a shorter or longer period so the vehicle fits into the monthly system.

Counties offer several ways to renew. In Ada County, for instance, you can renew at one of five offices, through an online virtual service, or by phone at (208) 577-3135 with a Visa or Mastercard.

Plates and the end of stickers

ITD's plates page says that as of July 1, 2026, the DMV stopped issuing registration stickers, and plates are no longer replaced every ten years. Any plate stays valid as long as it's undamaged, readable and has active registration. The standard red, white and blue scenic plate costs $3.75 per plate and includes a county designator in the number. Plates are made to order and mailed by the U.S. Postal Service, so allow about six weeks for them to arrive.

Personalized plates add a $25 initial fee plus $15 for each registration year. Vehicles over 26,000 pounds gross vehicle weight can't have them, and the combination is reviewed before it's approved. If a personalized registration lapses, the combination can be issued to someone else right away. Idaho also offers more than 40 special plate designs whose fees support causes such as education, farm research, recreation and conservation.

Inspections

A VIN inspection is required whenever a vehicle is titled in Idaho for the first time, and also when a vehicle previously titled here is presented without an Idaho title. The state repealed its emissions testing requirement for the Treasure Valley effective July 1, 2023, so no county requires an emissions test to register or renew.
