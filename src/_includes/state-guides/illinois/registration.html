Titles and plates through the Secretary of State

In Illinois, the Secretary of State's Vehicle Services Department issues titles and license plates, and most Secretary of State DMV facilities handle applications in person. Every vehicle driven on Illinois public roads must be titled and registered. Off-road motorcycles and ATVs are titled but not registered. For a vehicle that will never be driven on public roads, you can file the Application for Vehicle Transaction(s), form VSD-190, marked "title only."

New residents: the 30-day rule

The Secretary of State's new-resident page gives you 30 days after establishing residence to apply for an Illinois title and registration, provided the vehicle is still properly registered in another jurisdiction. On the VSD-190 you'll provide:

  • The owner's name and Illinois address
  • The odometer reading without tenths, marked as actual mileage, not actual mileage, or beyond the odometer's mechanical limits
  • The year, make, model, VIN, body type, purchase date, and whether the vehicle was new or used
  • Any lienholders, in order of priority
  • Original signatures of all owners

Include the out-of-state title. If an out-of-state lender holds it, send a copy of the title, certificate of origin, out-of-state registration or bill of sale along with a copy of the loan agreement. Leased vehicles need extra paperwork: the lease agreement, the out-of-state registration and title, the manufacturer's certificate of origin, and a bill of sale naming the lessor. Some plate types also require proof of insurance, an inspection certificate, or proof of disability, veteran or military status.

Illinois vehicle tax forms

Every title application needs the right Department of Revenue tax form. For a vehicle bought from a private individual, including one bought from or given by a relative, use form RUT-50. For a vehicle bought from an out-of-state dealer, use RUT-25 with the bill of sale. If you're moving here with a vehicle you recently bought that hasn't been titled or registered yet, include any tax due with your application.

A vehicle that was titled and registered in another state at least three months before you moved to Illinois is exempt from Illinois tax, but you still have to file the RUT-50 or RUT-25 showing the exemption. That exemption doesn't cover lessors, lessees, relocating businesses, or military members whose home of record is Illinois, although they can claim depreciation on the RUT-25. For tax amounts, the Department of Revenue can be reached at (800) 732-8866 or (217) 782-3336.

Fees

The Secretary of State's fee schedule lists these standard amounts:

  • Original title: $165
  • Duplicate or corrected title: $50
  • Passenger plates and B-truck plates (up to 8,000 pounds): $151 a year
  • Personalized passenger plates: $158 a year, or $164 for vanity plates
  • Motorcycle: $41 a year
  • Electric vehicle: $251 a year

A newly bought passenger car pays $316 for title and plates together ($165 plus $151). Replacement fees are $20 for a sticker, $6 for one plate, $9 for two plates, $29 for two plates with a sticker, and $3 for a registration ID card. Changing your address on a registration is free unless you want a corrected ID card.

Getting the title

To apply, you need the title application, the $165 fee, the tax form, and the previous title or certificate of origin signed over to you. The Secretary of State says titles take two to six weeks depending on volume, and expedited service is available. You can't pick up a title in Springfield, because the law requires it to be mailed to the first lienholder or, if there isn't one, to the owner. When you sell to another individual, you must sign over the title in the assignment section. Federal and state law require it to show the names and signatures of all sellers and buyers and to certify the odometer reading.

Renewals and sticker timing

All Illinois registrations expire at the end of a month. The Secretary of State sends renewal notices by mail or email as a courtesy, but you're responsible for keeping your sticker current even if no notice arrives. You can renew online, by phone, by mail or in person. Allow at least 30 days for a mailed renewal. Renewals are processed in order of expiration, and stickers for a given month usually go out by the 22nd. If yours hasn't arrived by the last week before it expires, call (800) 252-8980.

When you register a vehicle that already has an Illinois title within six months of buying it, your expiration month is set from the sale date, and you pay from that date. If you register six months or more after buying it, the expiration is based on the month you register.

Emissions testing in the Chicago and Metro East areas

The Illinois Vehicle Emissions Inspection Law of 2005 (625 ILCS 5/13C) requires testing in Cook, DuPage and Lake counties and in parts of Kane, Kendall, McHenry, Will, Madison, Monroe and St. Clair counties, excluding ZIP codes listed in § 13C-5. The Illinois EPA says most 1996 and newer gasoline passenger vehicles are tested once they're four years old, in the month their plates expire. Even model years are generally tested in even years and odd model years in odd years. The on-board diagnostic test is free, and no appointment is needed. A notice arrives up to four months before your plates expire. The Secretary of State won't renew plates for a vehicle that hasn't complied.
