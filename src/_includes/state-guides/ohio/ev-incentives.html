Purchase incentives

The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists no Ohio rebate or tax credit for buying or leasing a personal EV. Ohio's entries on that list are grants aimed at fleets and buses, an emissions-testing exemption, a weight allowance and several rules on fees and charging.

Annual registration fees for electrified vehicles

Under Ohio Rev. Code § 4503.10(C)(3), the registrar and deputy registrars collect an extra fee with each registration and renewal:

Vehicle typeAdditional annual fee
Battery electric$200
Plug-in hybrid$150
Hybrid$100

These fees are on top of the $36 base license tax and fee, the deputy registrar's service fee and any county or local permissive taxes. Ohio charges the $100 fee on conventional hybrids too, even though they don't plug in.

E-Check exemption

AFDC reports that vehicles powered solely by electricity, propane or natural gas are exempt from Ohio's E-Check emissions inspections after a one-time verification inspection, citing Ohio Administrative Code 3745-26-1 and 3745-26-12. E-Check applies in Cuyahoga, Geauga, Lake, Lorain, Medina, Portage and Summit counties. Under the E-Check rules described on the registration page, non-plug-in hybrids from 7 to 25 years old in those counties are still tested every two years.

Fleet grants

  • Volkswagen settlement grants: The Ohio Environmental Protection Agency (Ohio EPA) funds replacement or repower of eligible Class 4–8 trucks, school, shuttle and transit buses, and off-road equipment such as airport ground support equipment, forklifts and port cargo handlers. Alternative fuel infrastructure can qualify if the applicant does a site assessment.
  • Diesel Emissions Reduction Grants: Ohio EPA's DERG program, funded through the federal Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality Improvement program, helps retire and replace diesel public transit buses. Projects need at least a 20% match from non-state, non-federal sources.

Other rules that affect EV owners

Under § 5577.044, natural gas vehicles and EVs may exceed the usual gross vehicle weight limits by 2,000 pounds, except on the interstate system or on a road or bridge with its own posted weight restriction.

The Public Utilities Commission decided in Docket 20-434-EL-COI that an entity providing EV charging to the public isn't considered a public utility.

Section 3704.20, added by House Bill 201 in 2023, bars state agencies, counties, townships and other political subdivisions from adopting or enforcing a policy that restricts or bans the use, sale or lease of a motor vehicle based on its power source.

The U.S. Department of Transportation's National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) formula program required Ohio to submit an annually updated EV infrastructure deployment plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.
