Missouri's financial responsibility law

Missouri requires every owner and driver to carry motor vehicle liability coverage. Under Mo. Rev. Stat. § 303.025, an owner can't drive, register, or keep a vehicle registered, or let anyone else drive it, without the required financial responsibility. You also can't knowingly drive someone else's uninsured vehicle unless your own coverage applies. Nonresidents must carry what their home state requires. The Missouri Department of Revenue enforces the law and says that each year thousands of Missourians are in crashes with drivers who didn't keep the required insurance.

Required coverage

The Department of Revenue lists these minimums:

  • $25,000 per person for bodily injury
  • $50,000 per accident for bodily injury
  • $25,000 per accident for property damage
  • uninsured motorist coverage of $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury

The liability limits are usually written as 25/50/25. Missouri is an at-fault state, so the driver who causes a crash pays for the harm through liability coverage. The Department's list doesn't include collision, comprehensive or underinsured motorist coverage. A lender can still require collision and comprehensive on a financed car.

Three ways to comply

Under § 303.160, Missouri residents can meet the requirement with any of these:

  • a liability policy meeting the 25/50/25 limits
  • proof of financial responsibility filed with the Department of Revenue
  • a certificate of self-insurance from the Department, available to companies and religious organizations

Each option comes with an insurance identification card that must stay in the vehicle.

Showing proof

You need proof of insurance to register a vehicle and to renew plates, and you must keep proof in the vehicle at all times. If an officer asks and you can't show it, both the driver and the owner can be ticketed. The Department is notified when an uninsured vehicle is involved in a crash or stop, and it can ask an owner for proof at any time during the registration period. An owner who can't provide it faces a license suspension. Insurers report the drivers and vehicles they cover to the Missouri Insurance Verification System (MOIVS).

Penalties

Driving without coverage is a misdemeanor under § 303.025. A first violation is a class D misdemeanor. A second or later violation can bring up to 15 days in county jail and a fine of $200 to $500. You won't be found guilty if you show the court you were covered when the ticket was written. On conviction the court must notify the Department and choose one of these:

  • suspend your driving privilege
  • send the conviction for four points on your record
  • order supervision instead of points

The Department notes that eight points within 18 months is enough to lose your driving privilege.

Suspensions for failing to maintain insurance, under §§ 303.042 to 303.044, work like this:

  • First suspension: no waiting period. Reinstatement requires proof of insurance and a $20 fee.
  • Second within two years: 90 days, then proof of insurance and a $200 fee.
  • Third or later: one year, then proof of insurance and a $400 fee.

After any of these, you must keep proof of insurance on file with the Department for three years, or the suspension comes back. If a crash was involved, you need an SR-22 filing, and an ID card won't do.

Stored or inoperable vehicles

You don't violate the law by not insuring a vehicle that's inoperable or in storage. The Department has a process for voluntarily suspending its registration. Driving it during that period is a class B misdemeanor, and you have to notify the Department and show proof of insurance before it goes back on the road.

Crashes with uninsured drivers

The Department's crash checklist starts with stopping, since leaving the scene is a crime. Next, help anyone who's hurt and call the police. Move your vehicle only if it could cause another crash, unless an officer tells you otherwise. Exchange names, addresses, license numbers, vehicle and plate details, insurers and policy numbers, and stay until an officer says you can leave.

You must report a crash to the Driver License Bureau if it happened in Missouri less than a year ago, involved an uninsured motorist, and caused more than $500 in property damage or any injury or death. Use Motor Vehicle Accident Report Form 1140, available from the Department's website, your insurer or a license office.

What Missouri drivers pay

The NAIC's 2024 Auto Insurance Database supplement lists Missouri's combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,462.03, compared with $1,042.81 in 2020. The average expenditure was $1,274.15. The Insurance Research Council estimated 20.7% of Missouri drivers were uninsured, ranking the state sixth.
