Owner's or operator's security

Utah Code § 41-12a-301 requires every resident owner of a motor vehicle to keep "owner's or operator's security" in effect whenever the vehicle is driven on a highway, or on a quasi-public road or parking area, in Utah. A nonresident whose vehicle has been in Utah for 90 or fewer days in the past year needs the coverage their home state requires. After more than 90 days, the owner must keep Utah coverage continuously while the vehicle stays in the state.

The requirement doesn't apply to registered off-highway vehicles operated where the law allows, off-highway implements of husbandry, electric-assisted bicycles, motor-assisted scooters or electric personal assistive mobility devices.

What a Utah policy has to include

Under § 31A-22-302, a policy bought to meet the requirement must include four parts:

  • Liability coverage at or above the § 31A-22-304 minimums
  • Uninsured motorist coverage, unless you waive it
  • Underinsured motorist coverage, unless you waive it
  • Personal injury protection (PIP)

For a policy issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2025, § 31A-22-304(2) sets the liability minimums at:

  • $30,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in an accident
  • $65,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in an accident
  • $25,000 for damage to other people's property in an accident

A single combined limit of at least $90,000 per accident also satisfies the law. Policies issued or renewed through December 31, 2024 had lower minimums of 25/65/15, or $80,000 combined, and those lower limits still apply to self-insured private rental fleets.

Motorcycles, off-highway vehicles, street-legal ATVs, trailers and semitrailers don't need PIP, and their owners and operators aren't covered by PIP for injuries while operating those vehicles.

Personal injury protection

Section 31A-22-307 sets the minimum PIP benefits for each injured person:

  • At least $3,000 for the reasonable value of necessary medical, surgical, X-ray, dental, rehabilitation, ambulance, hospital and nursing services
  • Lost income of the lesser of $250 a week or 85% of gross income, for up to 52 consecutive weeks
  • Up to $20 a day, for up to 365 days, for household services you'd otherwise have performed
  • Up to $1,500 for funeral, burial or cremation
  • A $3,000 death benefit paid to the person's heirs

The income and household benefits don't have to cover the first three days unless the disability lasts longer than two weeks. Medical charges are measured against a relative value study that the insurance commissioner updates every other year.

Utah pairs PIP with a limit on lawsuits. Under § 31A-22-309, a person covered by PIP can't sue for general damages from a car accident unless the injury involves death, dismemberment, permanent disability or impairment based on objective findings, permanent disfigurement, a bone fracture, or medical expenses over $3,000. That limit doesn't apply to an uninsured motorist claim. PIP benefits are paid monthly as expenses come in, and they're overdue 30 days after the insurer gets reasonable proof. Overdue amounts earn 1.5% interest a month, and if you have to sue to collect them, the insurer also pays a reasonable attorney's fee.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Under §§ 31A-22-305 and 31A-22-305.3, uninsured (UM) and underinsured (UIM) motorist coverage must match the lesser of your liability limits or the maximum UM or UIM limits your insurer makes available. To reject either one or buy lower limits, you sign an acknowledgment form that's filed with the Insurance Department and shows the extra premium for full limits. Your choice stays in effect with that insurer until you ask in writing to change it.

For UM purposes, "uninsured motor vehicle" includes:

  • A vehicle with no liability coverage
  • A vehicle insured below the state minimums, to the extent of the shortfall
  • An unidentified hit-and-run vehicle
  • A vehicle whose insurer disputes coverage for more than 60 days
  • A vehicle whose insurer is declared insolvent, to the extent a guaranty fund doesn't pay

UIM covers you when the at-fault driver has insurance, but too little to pay your full damages.

Proof of insurance and the database

Section 41-12a-302 lists what counts as evidence of coverage, including a copy of your policy, declarations page, binder, renewal notice or insurance card. Information from the Uninsured Motorist Identification Database Program showing the vehicle or driver is insured also counts. You may show proof on a phone, and the officer may not look at anything else on the device. An officer may not cite you for failing to show proof if the database shows you're insured, and insurance cards may not display your address.

The database compares insurance records with vehicle registrations. Under § 41-12a-804, if a vehicle shows as uninsured for three consecutive months, the owner gets a notice with 15 days to provide proof of coverage or an exemption, then a second 15-day notice. If the owner still doesn't respond, the Motor Vehicle Division revokes the registration.

Penalties for driving uninsured

Driving without required coverage, letting someone else drive your uninsured vehicle, or failing to show proof to an officer is a class C misdemeanor under § 41-12a-302. A conviction carries a minimum fine of $400. The court may waive up to $300 of it if you show before sentencing that you got coverage after the violation. A second offense within three years carries a minimum fine of $1,000.

After a conviction, the Department of Public Safety suspends your license and won't renew or reissue it until you file proof of coverage, which you must keep on file for three years. If you had coverage but couldn't show it, that's an affirmative defense, and the court must let you submit the proof electronically or by mail.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement, Table 5, lists a combined average of $1,402.65 in Utah for liability, collision and comprehensive. The Insurance Research Council's estimate puts Utah's share of uninsured drivers at 6.2%, 50th in the nation.
