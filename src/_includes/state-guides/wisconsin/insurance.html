Wisconsin's insurance requirement

Under Wis. Stat. § 344.62(1), no one may drive a motor vehicle on a Wisconsin highway unless the owner or operator has a motor vehicle liability policy in effect for that vehicle. Section 344.61 defines that policy as one issued by an insurer authorized to write motor vehicle liability insurance in Wisconsin. If the vehicle wasn't registered in Wisconsin when the policy took effect, an insurer from the state where it was registered or the owner lived also qualifies. The policy must meet the requirements of § 344.33(2) and (3) on the date the vehicle is driven. For this subchapter, trailers, semitrailers, ATVs, utility terrain vehicles and limited use off-highway motorcycles aren't motor vehicles.

Minimum liability limits

Chapter 344 sets these minimum limits, in § 344.15(1) and the definition of proof of financial responsibility:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in any one accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in any one accident, subject to the per-person limit
  • $10,000 for injury to or destruction of others' property in any one accident

Exceptions

Section 344.63 lists situations where § 344.62 doesn't apply:

  • The owner or operator has a bond meeting § 344.36(1), filed with the secretary of transportation, and the vehicle is driven with the filer's permission.
  • The vehicle is insured as required by §§ 121.53, 194.41 or 194.42.
  • The vehicle belongs to a self-insurer with a valid certificate under § 344.16.
  • The owner or operator has deposited cash or securities under § 344.37(1), which requires $60,000 in cash or securities of that market value.
  • The vehicle is a rented or leased vehicle covered by §§ 344.51, 344.52 or 344.55.
  • The vehicle is owned by or leased to the federal government, a state, or a county or municipality.

Proof of insurance

Under § 344.62(2), you must carry proof of insurance while driving and show it when a traffic officer asks. The proof can be printed or electronic, including an image on a phone. If you show it on a device, the officer can look at the proof itself, and showing it isn't consent to look at anything else on the device.

Penalties

Under § 344.65, driving without the required insurance can bring a forfeiture of up to $500. Failing to carry proof carries a $10 forfeiture, but you can't be convicted if you later show you were insured when cited, either in court or at the issuing officer's office. Forging or falsifying proof of insurance, or representing invalid proof as valid, violates § 344.64 and can bring a forfeiture of up to $5,000.

A traffic officer may not stop or inspect a vehicle solely to check for insurance, and can't take you into custody solely for that violation. Officers can still cite you for it when they've stopped you for another reason.

Uninsured motorist and medical payments coverage

Under § 632.32(4), every policy covering a vehicle registered or principally garaged in Wisconsin must include two coverages:

  • Uninsured motorist coverage of at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident, except in policies written by town mutuals
  • Medical payments coverage of at least $1,000 per person, which may be excess over other sources of reimbursement

The named insured may reject medical payments coverage. After a rejection, the insurer doesn't have to include it at renewal unless a named insured asks for it in writing. Insurers that pay under these coverages are subrogated to the insured's rights unless they waive that right.

Underinsured motorist coverage

Under § 632.32(4m), an insurer must give one named insured written notice of the availability of underinsured motorist coverage, with a brief description, for each policy taking effect after November 1, 2011, that doesn't include it. The notice is given once, with delivery of the policy. Accepting or rejecting doesn't have to be in writing, and not paying a premium for the coverage is conclusive proof of rejection. If you accept underinsured motorist coverage, the limits must be at least $50,000 per person and $100,000 per accident.

A rejection by the person who received the notice applies to everyone insured under the policy, including on renewal. The same insurer doesn't have to offer the coverage again on a renewed policy unless an insured later requests it in writing.

Commercial policies whose vehicle coverage is limited to non-owned vehicles, along with umbrella and excess policies, are outside both subsections. If those policies do provide the coverages, they must meet the same minimum limits.
