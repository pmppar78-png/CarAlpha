Title transfer: 15 days, $15, 6% tax

The Michigan Department of State's title transfer and registration page lists these charges when you buy a vehicle:

  • a $15 title transfer fee
  • 6% sales tax on the full purchase price or the fair market value, whichever is greater
  • $10 to $15 to move an existing plate to the vehicle, or the registration fee for a new plate
  • an optional Recreation Passport for state parks: $15 with a one-year registration, $29 with a two-year registration, or $7 with a motorcycle

You have 15 days from the sale to transfer the title. A transfer after that costs an extra $15. Under MCL 257.234, a vehicle that isn't transferred in time is considered unregistered, and the Secretary of State may repossess the plates. A $20 option produces a title the same day, while the regular $15 title arrives by mail in about 14 days. If there's a loan, the title is held electronically until the lender releases the lien.

Buying from a dealer or a private seller

A Michigan dealer handles the title and plate transfer for you. If you buy from an individual, the department recommends going to a Secretary of State office together. If the seller can't come, bring your license or ID, the completed title, Michigan no-fault insurance, and payment for the tax, the $15 title fee and any registration fees. Tax is due when you apply for the title, unless the transfer is exempt.

Some private sales can be done online. Both parties need a Michigan driver's license or ID and an online account, and the sale must be from one individual to one individual. It also needs a paper Michigan title (not an electronic one), a car, minivan, SUV, motorcycle, pickup or van, and no loan by the buyer. The buyer uploads both sides of the completed title, including the odometer statement and any lien release, and approval can take up to 3 days.

Before you buy from a private seller, the department suggests these steps:

  • Have a mechanic inspect the car and check the odometer reading.
  • Match the VIN on the title to the one visible through the windshield.
  • Confirm the person signing is the owner named on the front of the title.
  • Make sure any lien has been released.
  • Never take the vehicle without a title signed by every owner.

Moving your plate to the new car

To transfer a plate after a private purchase, bring your current registration or plate number to a Secretary of State office. You'll also need the title to the new vehicle, or a copy of the title application if you just applied. The charge is a $10 transfer fee plus a $5 transaction fee, with more due if the plate is moving to a different type of vehicle.

How the registration fee is figured

For a passenger vehicle from model year 1984 or later, the annual registration tax under MCL 257.801 is based on the list price, not on what you paid:

  • $36 for a list price of $6,000 or less
  • rising $6 for each additional $1,000 of list price up to $30,000
  • $178 plus $6 for each $1,000, or fraction, over $30,000

The list price is the base price published by the Secretary of State, or the window-label price when no published figure exists. The tax drops as the vehicle ages. The second registration is 90% of the first, the third is 90% of the second, and later years stay at the third-year amount. The department's online registration fee calculator applies this schedule to a specific vehicle. A replacement plate or registration tab is $5, and renewing after expiration adds a $7 late fee.

Electric and plug-in hybrid fees

MCL 257.801 adds a yearly fee on top of the registration tax for vehicles that plug in. According to the Alternative Fuels Data Center's summary of that section:

  • An electric vehicle of 8,000 pounds or less pays $100, and a heavier one $200.
  • A plug-in hybrid of 8,000 pounds or less pays $30, and a heavier one $100.

Each fee rises when the state motor fuel tax goes above 19 cents a gallon. Electric fees go up $5, and plug-in hybrid fees $2.50, for every cent above that level, so the amount on your renewal reflects the current fuel tax.

Insurance and inspections

You must show proof of a valid Michigan no-fault policy to register. The department's title and registration requirements don't include a safety or emissions inspection. Its checklist covers the title, odometer reading, ID and insurance.
