Arizona EV incentives in 2026

Arizona has no state rebate or income-tax credit for buying an electric car. Its best-known EV perk has also ended for new buyers. For years, alternative-fuel vehicles paid a much lower vehicle license tax: $4 per $100 of an assessed value set at just 20 percent of MSRP and reduced 15 percent a year, with a $5 minimum. According to the Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center, that reduced AFV license tax under A.R.S. § 28-5805 doesn't apply to vehicles bought on or after December 31, 2022. An EV bought today pays the same VLT as a gasoline car of the same price. In its first year, that's $2.80 per $100 of 60 percent of the base retail price.

Arizona also doesn't charge EV owners an extra registration fee, which many other states now do. What it offers instead are smaller perks tied to the AFV plate.

Perks for EV drivers

  • No emissions test. Vehicles that run only on electricity are exempt from emissions testing in the Phoenix and Tucson areas (A.R.S. §§ 49-541 and 49-542). That saves the $16.15 OBD test fee and the trip to the station.
  • Alternative fuel vehicle plate. ADOT has to issue a special AFV plate or sticker to vehicles that run only on alternative fuel, including battery-electric cars, and there's no cap on how many it can issue (A.R.S. § 28-2416, amended by House Bill 2887 in 2025). Plug-in hybrids don't qualify, because they can also run on gasoline.
  • Carpool-area parking. A dedicated AFV that's using alternative fuel can park without penalty in spaces reserved for carpools (A.R.S. § 28-877).
  • Protected charging spaces. Parking in a designated EV charging space without being an EV with an AFV plate or sticker can bring a civil penalty of at least $350 (A.R.S. § 28-876).

Utility programs

The real money for Arizona drivers is at the utility level, and it depends on who serves your address.

  • Tucson Electric Power: a rebate of 75 percent of the purchase price of a networked Level 2 charger, up to $300, paid as a credit on your TEP account. You have to apply within 60 days of buying the charger, be on a time-of-use rate, and stay on it for at least two years. Used and refurbished chargers don't qualify, and the limit is two incentives per home. Details are on TEP's electric vehicles page.
  • Salt River Project (SRP): a smart-charger rebate for permanent SRP residential customers who buy an eligible new Level 2 charger. You have to apply within 90 days of purchase, and the limit is two chargers per account in five years. Portable chargers, chargers bought through auction sites, and chargers a dealer gave you free don't qualify. Check the rebate amount in effect on your purchase date, because SRP applies the rules that were in force then. See SRP's EV charger rebate page.
  • Arizona Public Service (APS): APS customers can charge on time-of-use plans with cheaper overnight rates. Plans and eligibility change, so check aps.com before you enroll.

The Arizona Corporation Commission oversees utility EV programs under its 2019 EV policy statement (Decision No. 77044).

Public charging

ADOT runs Arizona's federal NEVI program, which funds fast chargers along the interstates and the state's alternative fuel corridors. ADOT updates its EV infrastructure deployment plan every year as required by the program. In that plan, ADOT sets which corridors get chargers next.
