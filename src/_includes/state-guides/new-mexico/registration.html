Passenger vehicle registration fees

New Mexico bases passenger car registration on the vehicle's gross factory shipping weight and on how long it has been registered. Section 66-6-2 NMSA 1978, last amended by Chapter 4 of the 2026 laws, sets these fees for each 12-month period:

Shipping weightFirst five yearsAfter five years
2,000 lb or less$33.75$26.25
2,001 to 3,000 lb$48.75$38.75
Over 3,000 lb$70.00$56.25

The five years count from the date the vehicle was first registered in New Mexico or any other state. Each registration also carries a $1.50 tire recycling fee. Motorcycles, trucks, buses and trailers have their own schedules in §§ 66-6-1, 66-6-3 and 66-6-4.

One-year and two-year terms

Registrations are staggered, so they run 12 months from the month you register. Under § 66-3-20.1, a vehicle with a declared gross weight of 26,000 pounds or less can be registered for two years instead, at twice the one-year fee. If you sell the vehicle in the first year of a two-year registration and move the registration number to another vehicle, you can apply for a refund of half the two-year fee.

Section 66-6-13(E) reduces the registration fee by 5% when you renew online or by phone after the first registration. When you register a vehicle that hasn't been driven on New Mexico highways earlier in the year, the same section prorates the fee: three-quarters before April 1, half before July 1 and a quarter before October 1.

Electric and plug-in hybrid fees

Chapter 4 of the 2026 laws added § 66-6-6.4, an annual fee on top of the regular registration fee for electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles of 26,000 pounds or less. It's due at the first registration and each renewal:

  • Electric vehicles: $70 in 2027, $80 in 2028 and $90 from January 1, 2029.
  • Plug-in hybrids: $35 in 2027, $40 in 2028 and $45 from January 1, 2029.

The division decides which category applies from the fuel type or powertrain shown when it decodes the vehicle identification number.

Title, excise tax and transaction fees

A vehicle has to be titled before it can be registered for the first time. The Motor Vehicle Division's application for a duplicate certificate of title lists a $5 vehicle title fee, with or without adding or removing a lien. Section 66-6-19 adds a $3 transaction fee for issuing, transferring or revoking a title or registration, which covers everything done for one vehicle at one visit.

The motor vehicle excise tax in §§ 7-14-3 and 7-14-4 is 4% of the price paid, and you pay it when you apply for the title. The MVD fees manual says the value of a trade-in is deducted, and that a used vehicle is taxed on the higher of the stated price or 80% of its average trade-in value, unless a licensed dealer's invoice is presented. A resident who doesn't apply for a title within 90 days of a transfer owes a penalty of half the tax, which brings the rate to 6%. A gift supported by a notarized affidavit owes no excise tax, and neither does a vehicle you bought out of state for personal use at least 30 days before moving to New Mexico.

Buying, selling and plates

A buyer must apply for a new title and registration within 30 days of the transfer under § 66-3-103, and missing that deadline is a penalty assessment misdemeanor. New Mexico plates stay with the owner. Under § 66-3-104, when you sell or trade a vehicle you remove the plate and, within 30 days, either have the number assigned to another vehicle of the same class or turn the plate in to be destroyed.

New residents

Under § 66-3-301, a nonresident may drive a vehicle with current out-of-state plates in New Mexico for up to 180 days. Anyone working in the state for 30 days or more within a 60-day period is presumed to be a resident. Full-time out-of-state students with a school sticker and workers who commute daily from another state that offers New Mexicans the same privilege are exempt. Under § 66-3-4(B), a vehicle that has been registered in another state but never in New Mexico has to have its identification number inspected by the division or its agent before it's registered or titled.

Emissions testing

Section 66-3-7.1 bars the division from registering a vehicle that a county or city ordinance requires to pass an emissions test until a valid certificate is shown. It's a misdemeanor to register a vehicle somewhere else to dodge a testing program where you live. The program covers vehicles registered in, or commuting into, Bernalillo County. Vehicles from model year 1991 and later weighing up to 10,000 pounds are tested every two years and at a change of ownership. New vehicles are exempt for four years after their first registration, and all-electric vehicles aren't tested.
