Electric vehicles in Mississippi

The Alternative Fuels Data Center's list of Mississippi laws and incentives doesn't include a state rebate, tax credit or other purchase incentive for drivers buying an electric or hybrid vehicle. Instead, the state's EV laws focus on annual fees for EV and hybrid owners, rules for who can sell charging, and programs aimed at public fleets, highway charging and battery manufacturing. The summary below draws on the U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center and the Mississippi Department of Revenue.

Annual electric and hybrid fees

Under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 27-19-21 and 27-19-23, owners pay these fees on top of standard registration:

  • All-electric vehicles: an annual fee that started at $150.
  • Plug-in hybrids and conventional hybrids: an annual fee that started at $75.

Since July 1, 2021, the Department of Revenue has adjusted both fees every year by the prior year's increase in the Consumer Price Index for urban consumers. DOR says the fees are due at first registration and at every renewal, and that the amounts change each July 1. Your county Tax Collector can tell you the current figure. Hybrids that don't plug in pay this fee too, which matters if you're comparing a hybrid with a gasoline-only model.

Selling electricity for charging

Under Miss. Code Ann. § 77-3-3, as amended in 2023, a business that buys electricity at metered retail rates from a utility and resells some of it to the public for EV charging is an end-use customer, and it isn't treated as a public utility. That lets businesses run charging stations without being regulated as utilities.

Also in 2023, the Legislature authorized the Mississippi Transportation Commission to partner with private companies to design, finance, build, operate or maintain charging stations, under §§ 65-1-181 and 65-1-181.01. Stations built this way must meet the Commission's minimum standards, and the Commission can authorize periodic inspections.

Highway charging plans

Under the federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) Formula Program, the Mississippi Department of Transportation had to submit an updated EV infrastructure deployment plan each year for fiscal years 2022 through 2026. Those plans describe how the state planned to distribute NEVI funds and handle physical and cybersecurity. MDOT's Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure website has the plans.

Public fleets and school districts

Since 2022, local school boards may buy, own and operate EVs to take children to and from public schools, and may use district transportation funds to buy and maintain them (§ 37-41-81). The Mississippi Development Authority's Alternative Fuel School Bus and Municipal Motor Vehicle Revolving Loan Program offers zero-interest loans to school districts and cities. Its eligible fuels are propane and natural gas, so it doesn't fund electric vehicles. For state agencies, the State Bureau of Fleet Management encourages fuel-efficient and hybrid vehicles. At least 75% of the vehicles titled under the Bureau must have an EPA highway rating of at least 40 miles per gallon (§ 25-1-77).

Battery manufacturing

A 2024 law directs the State Treasury to create a fund for grants or loans to EV battery manufacturing and assembly projects. The Mississippi Development Authority manages the fund and must publish an annual report on how it's used.

Federal credits

Because there's no Mississippi purchase incentive, any tax break for a new or used EV would come from federal law. Check the IRS and fueleconomy.gov for the current status of federal clean vehicle credits before you count on one.
