Alaska's Motor Vehicle Warranties Act

Alaska's lemon law is the Motor Vehicle Warranties Act, AS 45.45.300 through 45.45.360. The Department of Law's lemon law page explains it in plain terms. Two things make Alaska's version stand out. Its deadlines are among the tightest in the country. And it takes Alaska's distances seriously: it requires manufacturers to keep warranty repair shops inside the state and to pay for shipping a lemon to them.

The law covers the purchaser of a new motor vehicle, other than a buyer for resale, and anyone the car is later transferred to under Alaska's title law, as long as the deadlines haven't passed. "Motor vehicle" here means a land vehicle with four or more wheels, normally used for personal, family, or household purposes and required to be registered under AS 28.10. Tractors, farm vehicles, and vehicles built mainly for off-road use are excluded (AS 45.45.360(6)). Used vehicles aren't covered. Boats and ATVs have their own, separate protection in AS 45.27.190–.220.

The one-year window

Everything in Alaska turns on one period: the term of the express warranty or one year from delivery to the original owner, whichever ends first. There is no mileage limit. Because nearly every new-car warranty lasts longer than a year, in practice this means the first year of ownership.

A defect counts only if it substantially impairs the car's use or its market value. The statute defines both narrowly (AS 45.45.360(11)–(12)). "Use" is impaired when the defect keeps the car from being driven or makes it unsafe to drive. "Market value" is impaired when the defect substantially lowers what the car is worth compared with the same car without it. That leaves out squeaks and trim problems. A no-start in January, a failing heater, or a transmission that drops out of gear is the kind of problem the law has in mind.

When Alaska presumes the car is a lemon

Under AS 45.45.320, the manufacturer is presumed to have had a reasonable number of attempts if, within that one-year window:

  1. the same defect was repaired three or more times by the manufacturer, distributor, dealer, or authorized repair shop and it still exists; or
  2. the car was out of service for repairs for a total of 30 or more business days.

Alaska counts business days, not calendar days, so 30 days in the shop works out to about six weeks. Time when repairs can't be done for reasons the manufacturer or shop can't control, such as a strike or a natural disaster, doesn't count. A part sitting on a barge to Kodiak is a gray area. Ask the dealer to write the reason for any delay on the repair order.

To head off parts delays, AS 45.45.325 requires the manufacturer to send any warranty part a dealer doesn't have in stock as soon as possible, with no added freight or handling charge. The Department of Law says that usually means air freight.

The demand letter and its 60-day deadline

This is where most Alaska claims are won or lost. To claim a refund or replacement, you must mail written notice by certified mail to the manufacturer and to its dealer or repairing agent. You have to do it before 60 days have passed after the warranty or the one-year period ends, whichever ends first (AS 45.45.310). For most owners, that means the letter has to go out by roughly month 14.

The statute lists what the letter must say. The Department of Law adds a few practical items:

  • A statement that the vehicle has a nonconformity, with a reasonable description of it.
  • A statement that the manufacturer or its dealer has made a reasonable number of attempts to fix it.
  • Your demand for a refund or replacement, delivered on the 60th day after you mail the letter.
  • The year, make, model, and VIN.
  • Your name, address, and a daytime phone number.

Once the manufacturer receives the letter, it has 30 days to make one final repair attempt. If that attempt fails, the refund or replacement is due.

Arbitration in Alaska

Alaska has no state-run lemon-law arbitration board. AS 45.45.355 lets a manufacturer require you to try its own process first, but only if two conditions are met. The process must be either an informal dispute program that substantially complies with the FTC's 16 CFR Part 703 rules, or a written offer, made after your notice arrives, to arbitrate or mediate. And the Alaska Attorney General must have approved it. The decision binds the manufacturer but not you. If the program isn't AG-approved, you can choose to use it or go straight to court.

Refund, replacement, and shipping costs

You choose the remedy (AS 45.45.305): a new, comparable vehicle, or a refund of the full purchase price. "Full purchase price" includes original registration fees, transportation, dealer prep, and dealer-installed options (AS 45.45.360(4)). Any lienholder is paid first from the refund.

The manufacturer can subtract a "reasonable allowance" for use. Alaska caps it in an unusual way: it can't exceed straight-line depreciation over seven years for the time you had the car, plus any loss in value from your neglect or abuse or from body damage the defect didn't cause (AS 45.45.360(9)). For a $49,000 truck kept 13 months, seven-year straight-line depreciation is about $7,580 ($49,000 ÷ 84 months × 13). The Department of Law notes that a refund doesn't include finance charges you've already paid.

Under AS 45.45.350, the manufacturer must also repay reasonable costs of shipping the car to and from the nearest authorized repair facility. In rural Alaska that can mean barge or air freight, so keep every receipt. And AS 45.45.345 requires every manufacturer that sells here to keep authorized dealers in Alaska that can do the warranty work.

The manufacturer can defend by showing the defect doesn't substantially impair use or value, or that it came from unauthorized alterations, abuse, or neglect (AS 45.45.315). The dealer isn't a new defendant under this law (AS 45.45.340). Your other legal rights, including federal warranty law, remain.

Resold buybacks

A car returned under the act can't be resold in Alaska unless the buyer is fully told why it was returned before the sale closes (AS 45.45.335).
