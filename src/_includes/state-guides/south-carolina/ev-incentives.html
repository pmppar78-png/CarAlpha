EV programs in South Carolina

The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists no state rebate or tax credit for buying a personal electric vehicle in South Carolina. The programs it lists are aimed at public agencies, nonprofits, businesses and infrastructure.

Grants and loans

  • Alternative Fuel Project Grants: The Office of Regulatory Staff's Energy Office offers grants of up to $50,000 for alternative fuel demonstration projects, including EVs and plug-in hybrids. State agencies, local governments, public colleges and universities, K-12 public schools and nonprofits can apply through its Mini-Grant Program.
  • Revolving loans for public entities: The South Carolina Energy Office provides low-interest loans for energy efficiency improvements, including alternative fuel vehicle conversions and the added cost of a new alternative fuel vehicle. Loans can cover up to 100% of project costs, from $25,000 to $500,000 per state fiscal year.
  • Revolving loans for private entities: The South Carolina Business Development Corporation offers similar loans to businesses and industries, from $50,000 to $1 million and up to 100% of project costs.

Road use fee

Instead of paying motor fuel user fees at the pump, owners of vehicles powered exclusively by electricity or hydrogen pay a $120 biennial road use fee on top of registration under § 56-3-645. Vehicles that combine taxed motor fuel with electricity pay $60. The SCDMV fee schedule lists these as $120 for electric vehicles and $60 for hybrids.

Charging rules and planning

  • A business that operates an EV charger isn't treated as a public utility. It must buy electricity from an authorized utility, and its chargers must be immobile. Utilities offering DC fast charging must give all operators fair and nondiscriminatory rates.
  • Public electric utilities may seek to recover the costs of EV charging programs in rate cases before the Public Service Commission.
  • Executive Order 2022-31 directed the Department of Transportation to convene an Interagency EV Working Group to plan charging deployment, with priority on the interstate system and rural areas. The same order created the Department of Commerce's EV Economic Development Initiative.
  • Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, the Department of Transportation submitted annual EV infrastructure deployment plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.

Other rules

  • State agencies buying vehicles must give preference to hybrid, plug-in hybrid, fuel cell and other alternative fuel vehicles when performance, quality and expected life-cycle costs are comparable.
  • A low-speed vehicle is a four-wheeled vehicle, other than an ATV, that can reach at least 20 mph and no more than 25 mph and weighs under 3,000 pounds gross. It may run on secondary highways posted at 35 mph or less, may cross faster highways at intersections and must be registered and licensed.
