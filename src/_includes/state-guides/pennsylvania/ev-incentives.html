Alternative Fuel Vehicle Rebate: closed

Pennsylvania's consumer EV rebate is the Department of Environmental Protection's Alternative Fuel Vehicle (AFV) Rebate Program, set up under Act 178 of 2004. DEP's program page now says the Alternative Fuel Vehicle Rebate Program is closed. The 2025–26 round, announced in the Pennsylvania Bulletin in July 2025, was set to run through the end of June 2026, or until at least 500 rebates were awarded or the money ran out.

For reference, that round was limited to Pennsylvania residents with household income at or below 300% of the federal poverty level, and the final purchase price could be no more than $45,000. It paid:

VehicleRebate
Battery electric vehicle$3,000
Plug-in hybrid$1,500
Natural gas or propane vehicle$500
Electric motorcycle$500

Applicants with household income below 150% of the federal poverty guideline got an extra $1,000. Applications were due within six months of purchase. Check DEP's page before you buy in case a new round opens.

Road User Charge for EVs

Since April 1, 2025, Pennsylvania has charged EV owners a yearly Road User Charge under Act 85 of 2024 to pay for road and bridge upkeep. PennDOT's 2026 schedule sets the flat fee at:

  • Electric vehicle: $250 for a one-year registration, $500 for two years
  • Plug-in hybrid: $63 for one year, $126 for two years

The fee is on top of the regular $48 passenger registration, and the registration period must match the Road User Charge period. The AFDC notes that the plug-in hybrid fee is set at 25% of the EV fee, and that EVs subject to the charge are exempt from the state's Alternative Fuels Tax.

Emissions testing

Pennsylvania's emission inspection rules in 67 Pa. Code § 177.51 apply to gasoline-powered vehicles, so an all-electric car doesn't need an emission test. It still needs the annual safety inspection.

Grants for businesses and fleets

The AFDC lists these state programs for organizations:

  • Alternative Fuels Incentive Grant (AFIG): funding for school districts, municipal authorities, nonprofits and Pennsylvania businesses to cover the extra cost of buying or converting alternative fuel vehicles, fleet or home fueling equipment, and research and training.
  • Medium- and heavy-duty vehicle rebates: DEP rebates, funded by the state's share of the Volkswagen settlement trust, for replacing or repowering Class 4–8 freight and drayage trucks and school, transit and shuttle buses. The old vehicles must be scrapped.
  • Small Business Advantage Grants: matching grants of up to 80% of project costs, up to $12,000, for energy-efficient or pollution-prevention equipment.

Other EV rules

  • An EV may exceed state gross weight limits by the difference in weight between its battery and a comparable diesel fuel system, up to 2,000 pounds.
  • Owning or running a public EV charging station doesn't make a business a public utility.
  • Neighborhood electric vehicles, with a top speed above 20 mph and no more than 25 mph, must carry insurance like any registered vehicle.
  • Under the Pennsylvania Clean Vehicles Program, new cars and light trucks must meet California emission standards, except the zero-emission vehicle sales requirement.
