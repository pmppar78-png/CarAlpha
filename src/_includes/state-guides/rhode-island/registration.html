Rhode Island registration fees

Rhode Island charges registration by vehicle weight. Passenger registrations renew every two years, and commercial registrations renew each year. The Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV) weight table lists these annual fees, each with a $20 DOT surcharge per year:

On top of the weight fee, each transaction includes a $3.50 technology surcharge. New registrations are prorated by date. Card payments carry a third-party service fee of at least $1.55, or 2.40% for transactions of $65 or more.

2026 surcharge and hybrid fees

The DMV announced fee changes effective January 1, 2026. Under R.I. Gen. Laws § 39-18.1-4, the DOT surcharge rose from $30 to $40 for a two-year passenger registration and from $15 to $20 for a one-year registration. Plate types such as antique, farm, motorcycle, dealer, school bus and trailer have their own surcharge amounts in that law. The DMV fee page lists an $18 DOT surcharge on a new motorcycle registration, and a trailer's surcharge equals half its registration fee.

Amendments to § 31-6-1 in the FY 2026 budget added yearly fees for electrified vehicles, charged in addition to the standard registration fee and surcharge:

  • Battery electric vehicle: $200 per year
  • Plug-in hybrid: $100 per year
  • Hybrid that doesn't plug in, including mild hybrids: $50 per year

The new fees apply to renewals expiring in January 2026 or later and to new registrations processed on or after January 1, 2026. The DMV system applies them automatically.

Sales tax

Rhode Island's sales tax on vehicles is 7%, paid at the DMV when you register or title, or earlier at the Division of Taxation. It's due by the 20th of the month after the month of the sale, even if the vehicle isn't registered yet, and interest and penalties are added after that.

  • From a dealer, new or used: 7% of the purchase price, minus trade-in and other allowances. The trade-in allowance applies to passenger vehicles, motorcycles and motor homes.
  • Private sale of a vehicle seven years old or newer: 7% of the NADA Clean Retail book value or the sale price, whichever is greater.
  • Private sale of a vehicle 8 to 29 years old: 7% of the sale price, unless it's on the Cars of Particular Interest list.
  • Gifts: no sales tax between immediate family members with a signed letter of gift. Other gifts need a notarized letter and gift affidavit.

Rhode Island's motor vehicle excise tax, a local property tax on cars and trailers, has been phased out. The House Fiscal Advisory Staff's 2023 local aid report says the phase-out was restarted and completed with the FY 2024 budget, with the state reimbursing cities and towns.

Moving to Rhode Island

New residents must get a Rhode Island title and registration within 30 days of moving. You need the Application for Registration (TR-1), a sales or use tax exemption certificate and the sales tax exemption affidavit. Insurance is mandatory, with liability coverage at the minimum from a company licensed in Rhode Island. A title is required unless the vehicle is older than model year 2001 and comes from a state that doesn't title it. A vehicle titled or registered in another state needs a VIN check, done at a police station, before registration. If two owners are on the title, both must be present or the absent owner's signature must be notarized.

Safety and emissions inspection

Newly registered vehicles, other than new ones, must have a valid Rhode Island sticker or pass inspection within five days of registration, or the registration may be suspended. New vehicles are exempt for two years from purchase or until they reach 24,000 miles, whichever comes first. Out-of-state stickers aren't honored.

Fully electric light-duty vehicles need the safety inspection every 24 months but are exempt from emissions testing. A used vehicle from a licensed Rhode Island dealer must have a new sticker from an inspection no more than 90 days before the sale, with no more than 500 miles added since. Vehicles registered exclusively with antique plates are exempt, as long as they're kept for exhibitions, club activities, parades and other functions of public interest.
