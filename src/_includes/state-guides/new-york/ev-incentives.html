Drive Clean Rebate

The New York State Energy Research and Development Authority (NYSERDA) runs the Drive Clean Rebate, which takes $500 to $2,000 off the purchase or lease of an eligible new electric vehicle. NYSERDA lists more than 60 qualifying battery-electric and plug-in hybrid models. The amount depends on the model's MSRP and its all-electric range. New York residents, businesses and government entities can all qualify.

The rebate is applied at the point of sale. A participating dealer applies for it on your behalf and lowers the price by the full amount, so you don't file anything yourself. NYSERDA's website has a dealer locator, and it says more than 200,000 New Yorkers have used the rebate.

Toll discounts

NYSERDA points EV drivers to the Green Pass programs of the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey and the New York State Thruway, which offer a 10% discount on certain tolls.

Charging incentives

  • Tax credit: The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists a state credit under Tax Law § 187-b for 50% of the cost of alternative fueling infrastructure installed in New York, including EV charging equipment, up to $5,000.
  • Charge Ready NY: AFDC describes NYSERDA rebates of $3,000 per port for Level 2 chargers at public parking facilities, workplaces and multifamily buildings, plus $1,000 more per charger in disadvantaged communities.
  • EVolve NY: The New York Power Authority has allocated up to $250 million to fast charging and other projects that fill gaps in the charging network.
  • Utility programs: The Public Service Commission has authorized utility programs for EV chargers.

Rights for homeowners and condo owners

Under Real Property Law § 343, a homeowners' association can't prohibit or restrict installing or using an EV charger in a homeowner's designated parking space. It may set reasonable rules for chargers on common property. Condominium boards face a similar rule, as long as their restrictions don't significantly raise the cost or block installation. In both cases the owner pays for installation and upkeep and has to follow safety codes. Condo owners also have to use a licensed contractor and provide proof of insurance.

EV charging spaces

Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1202 bars any vehicle that isn't an EV from stopping, standing or parking in a space marked for EV charging. A vehicle that isn't actively charging may not park there either. An EV parked at a charger without being plugged in for more than 30 minutes is presumed not to be charging.

Registration and inspection

An electric vehicle pays at least $32.50 for the two-year registration under the DMV's MV-202 schedule. That's the same floor that applies to a gas vehicle with six or more cylinders. All-electric vehicles skip the emissions part of the annual inspection but still need the safety inspection.

Sales requirements

AFDC reports that state law requires all new light-duty passenger vehicles sold or leased in New York to be zero-emission by 2035, and new medium- and heavy-duty vehicles by 2045. New York has also adopted California's Advanced Clean Trucks rule.
