MOR-EV rebates

The Department of Energy Resources funds the Massachusetts Offers Rebates for Electric Vehicles (MOR-EV) program. It has four parts, and they can be combined:

  • MOR-EV Standard: $3,500 for an eligible new vehicle with a total MSRP of $55,000 or less. Any resident, business or non-profit can apply, without an income test.
  • MOR-EV Used: $3,500 for an eligible used vehicle with a final purchase price of $40,000 or less, bought from a licensed dealer. Private sales don't qualify, and you must meet an income test.
  • MOR-EV+: an extra $1,500 on top of a Standard or Used rebate for people enrolled in a listed assistance program. It must be requested at the same time as the main rebate.
  • MOR-EV Trade-In: an extra $1,000 for trading in a qualifying gas or diesel vehicle when you buy or lease an eligible EV.

The vehicle must appear on the program's eligible vehicle list. The price limits don't include registration fees, documentation fees or sales tax.

Income qualification

For the Used rebate, you can qualify with a tax transcript showing modified adjusted gross income below $150,000 for joint filers or a surviving spouse, $112,500 for heads of household, or $75,000 for other filers. You can also qualify by showing enrollment in a listed program, such as SNAP, MassHealth, LIHEAP, WIC, a Mass Save income-eligible program or a Section 8 voucher. MOR-EV+ requires enrollment in one of the listed programs and has no income test.

How to claim it

You can get the Standard rebate at a participating dealer, who subtracts it from the price. For the Used rebate and MOR-EV+, you first apply online for a prequalification voucher and bring it to the dealer. Vouchers expire 6 months after approval. Otherwise, you can apply online within 90 days of the purchase or lease and get a check by mail. Applications must also come in before the rebate funds run out. A vehicle bought outside Massachusetts qualifies if it's registered here within 90 days.

For a post-purchase claim, you'll need the Massachusetts registration certificate, the final sales or lease contract itemizing credits and incentives, and proof of residency.

Ownership rules

You must keep the vehicle for at least 36 consecutive months, and reselling it for gain inside that window is prohibited. A purchased vehicle must stay registered with the RMV for those 36 months, and a lease must run at least 36 months. You also can't modify the emissions controls or sell emission reduction credits generated by the car.

Trade-in requirements

To earn the $1,000 trade-in rebate, the vehicle you trade in must meet every one of these conditions:

  • It's a gas or diesel vehicle (hybrids and plug-in hybrids don't count) at least 12 model years old.
  • It was traded in on or after August 8, 2023, at a licensed Massachusetts dealer, with a trade-in value shown on the contract.
  • It's rated 8,500 pounds GVWR or less and was up to date on inspections.
  • It was registered in Massachusetts to you or your immediate family for at least 2 years.

This rebate isn't available at the dealer. You apply online within 90 days, including a Massachusetts Vehicle Check inspection report and a signed attestation.

Charging and other state rules

The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center lists these Massachusetts programs and rules:

  • MassEVIP pays 60% of the cost of Level 1 or Level 2 chargers at workplaces, fleets, multi-family housing and educational campuses, up to $50,000 per street address.
  • Its Public Access Charging grants cover 80% of Level 2 hardware and installation (100% on government property), up to $50,000 per address. Chargers must be open to the public at least 12 hours a day.
  • Customers of participating municipal light plants may get a free or discounted Level 2 home charger through the NextZero program.
  • Vehicles powered only by electricity are exempt from emissions inspection under 310 CMR 60.02. They still need the annual safety inspection.
  • Cities and towns may reserve parking for zero emission vehicles, with a penalty of up to $50 for violators.
