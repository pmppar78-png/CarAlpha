Titling and registering in Missouri

The Missouri Department of Revenue handles titles and license plates through license offices. Missouri bases passenger-car registration fees on taxable horsepower, not on value or weight. Sales tax, inspections and, for new residents, proof that county personal property taxes aren't owed are all part of the process. The Department notes that its information follows current statutes and may change.

Deadlines and late penalties

You have 30 days from the purchase date to title a vehicle and pay sales tax. Starting on the 31st day, there's a $25 title penalty, and it grows by $25 for every additional 30 days late, up to $200. New residents also have 30 days from becoming Missouri residents to title their vehicles. A 30-day temporary permit costs $5, plus a $9 processing fee if it's issued at a license office.

Titling a vehicle you bought

A Missouri resident titling a newly purchased vehicle submits:

  • the certificate of title properly signed over to you, or the Manufacturer's Statement of Origin for a new vehicle
  • a signed Application for Missouri Title and License (Form 108)
  • an identification number and odometer (ID/OD) inspection if the vehicle came to you on a title from another state or country, which a safety inspection no more than 60 days old satisfies
  • an Odometer Disclosure Statement (Form 3019) and a notarized lien release (Form 4809), if they apply

You'll pay the $8.50 title fee and a $9 processing fee. Sales tax is the state's 4.225% plus your local sales tax on the purchase price, minus any trade-in allowance. The Department's online calculator can estimate the total.

New residents

To title and register a vehicle already titled in another state, you'll need these documents:

  • the original title or other proof of ownership
  • Form 108
  • an ID/OD inspection
  • proof of insurance
  • a statement of non-assessment from your county assessor, or the City of St. Louis assessor, showing you don't owe personal property taxes
  • a safety inspection no more than 60 days old
  • an emissions inspection no more than 60 days old if you live in St. Louis City or in Jefferson, St. Charles or St. Louis County

If your lienholder holds your old title and won't release it, Form 5834 and certain lender documents let you get a one-year registration. It can't be renewed until the Department receives the out-of-state title.

If you've owned and driven the vehicle in another state for at least 90 days before titling it here, no more sales tax is due. If you've owned it for less than 90 days and paid less sales tax elsewhere than Missouri's state and local rates would charge, you pay the difference. An electronic transmission fee of $2 may also apply.

Registration fees by horsepower

Your vehicle's taxable horsepower appears on the title or on your Form 108. The Department's fee page lists these amounts for passenger vehicles. Each one-year registration also carries a $9 processing fee, and each two-year registration an $18 processing fee:

  • Under 12 HP, electric vehicle, or low-speed vehicle: $18.25 for one year or $36.50 for two.
  • 12 to 23 HP: $21.25 or $42.50.
  • 24 to 35 HP: $24.25 or $48.50.
  • 36 to 47 HP: $33.25 or $66.50.
  • 48 to 59 HP: $39.25 or $78.50.
  • 60 to 71 HP: $45.25 or $90.50.
  • 72 HP and up: $51.25 or $102.50.

So a one-year renewal for a vehicle in the top bracket comes to $60.25 with processing. Pickups and other vehicles registered as trucks pay by gross weight plus load, and the rate depends on whether you drive locally, within 50 miles of home, or farther. For example, a local 6,000-pound truck pays $15.75 for one year and a beyond-local 12,000-pound truck pays $38.25. Recreational vehicles pay $32.25 a year.

Safety and emissions inspections

Missouri's safety inspection rule under RSMo § 307.350 applies to passenger vehicles at registration and renewal. Even model years are inspected in even calendar years and odd model years in odd calendar years. Since August 28, 2019, vehicles in their first ten years after the model year with fewer than 150,000 miles are exempt. The Department's example is a 2023 vehicle that stays under 150,000 miles, which is exempt through 2033. A safety inspection is also needed when ownership changes, and the certificate is generally good for 60 days.

The Gateway Vehicle Inspection Program covers gasoline vehicles from model year 1996 and diesel vehicles from model year 1997, at or under 8,500 pounds, registered in St. Louis City or St. Louis, St. Charles or Jefferson County. Franklin County has been exempt since July 1, 2022, and Kansas City isn't in the program. Under RSMo § 643.315, vehicles powered only by electricity are exempt from emissions inspection.

Proof of insurance

You must show proof of insurance to register a vehicle or renew its plates. A current insurance identification card works, whether it's the original, a copy, or a legible electronic version. See the Missouri insurance guide for the required coverage and the penalties for driving uninsured.
