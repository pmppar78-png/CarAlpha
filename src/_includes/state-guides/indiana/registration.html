Titling and registering in Indiana

The Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) issues titles, plates and registrations in Indiana. You can renew online through myBMV, but a first-time Indiana title for an out-of-state vehicle has to be done at a branch. What you pay each year is a flat registration fee plus an excise tax that depends on the car, so two neighbors renewing on the same day can owe very different amounts.

After you buy a vehicle

According to the BMV's buying and selling page, you must apply for a title within 45 days of buying or otherwise acquiring a vehicle, or you'll owe an administrative penalty. Before you go to the branch, make sure the seller has fully completed both the seller and purchaser sections of the title. The seller keeps the license plate. As the buyer, you're responsible for getting the new title, registration and plate. Indiana doesn't require sold vehicles' plates to be returned, and sellers can move them to another vehicle they own.

Sales tax is due on the purchase price. An Indiana dealer collects it and gives you an ST108 (State Form 48842) as proof. In a private sale, you pay it at the branch when you apply for the title. If you bought from a dealer in another state, you get credit for sales tax paid there, but you owe Indiana the difference if the other state's tax was lower.

Moving to Indiana

The BMV's new-resident page gives you 60 days after moving to apply for an Indiana title, or to file a Request for Title (State Form 1014), for each vehicle with an out-of-state title. Miss that deadline and you'll be charged a penalty. Bring these to the branch:

  • The certificate of title
  • The vehicle, for a VIN inspection, or a Physical Inspection of a Vehicle or Watercraft (State Form 39530) completed by a law enforcement officer
  • Proof of address dated within the last 60 days, which can be your license if the address on it is current
  • Proof of your Social Security number or federal ID number, if required
  • Proof of insurance, which you need to buy a plate and registration

The VIN inspection is free at a branch or a BMV-certified service provider. A law enforcement officer may charge up to $5 for it. If a lender in another state holds your title, the branch mails Form 1014 to the lender and contacts you when the title arrives so you can finish the application. New residents don't pay sales tax when moving a title from their prior state.

Registration fees and excise tax

The BMV's fee chart (revised January 1, 2026) lists these amounts:

  • Passenger vehicle registration: $21.35
  • Truck of 11,000 pounds or less: $30.35
  • Motorcycle: $26.35
  • Title (new, duplicate or replacement): $15
  • Speed title: $25
  • Late title penalty: $30
  • Late registration penalty: $15
  • Transfer, duplicate or replacement plate, decal or registration: $9.50
  • Personalized plate message: $45
  • Temporary permit: $18
  • Transportation Infrastructure Improvement Fee: $15
  • Supplemental fee: $242 for an EV, $81 for a hybrid

The excise tax is usually the bigger part of the bill. The BMV's excise tax page assigns each passenger vehicle to one of 17 classes based on its original manufacturer's retail price. The tax then drops as the vehicle ages. Age is the registration year minus the model year. For a vehicle priced at $42,500 or more, the tax starts at $532 in year zero and falls to $50 at age 9 or older. A car priced between $25,000 and $29,999 starts at $350. A 1980 or older passenger vehicle pays a flat $12. Some counties and cities add their own vehicle excise or wheel tax, and the BMV's Quick Quote tool estimates the total.

Emissions testing in Lake and Porter counties

Indiana's emissions testing applies to vehicles registered in Lake and Porter counties. The BMV's emissions page says vehicles built after 1975 with a gross vehicle weight rating of 9,000 pounds or less need an emissions test and tampering inspection every two years. Vehicles from odd model years are tested in odd years, and even model years in even years. The four newest model years and antique vehicles are exempt.

Bring your renewal notice or current registration to a Clean Air Car Check station. If you renew online, myBMV asks for the emissions test number printed at the bottom right of the notice. You can test as early as October of the year before you're due, which the BMV recommends if the car is with a college student or out of state for part of the year. Program questions go to (888) 240-1684.
