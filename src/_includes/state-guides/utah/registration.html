When you have to register

Under Utah Code § 41-1a-202(3), you have 60 days after establishing residency to register a motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer, classic vehicle or restored-modified vehicle in Utah. The statute counts you as a resident if you've set up a domicile in Utah, stay in the state for six months or more in a calendar year, work in Utah other than seasonally without commuting from another state, or declare Utah residency to get a driver license, vehicle registration, or in-state school benefits. Military members temporarily stationed in Utah and out-of-state students carrying at least seven quarter hours aren't counted as residents.

The Tax Commission can use insurance address data from the uninsured motorist database to find vehicles owned by Utah residents that aren't registered here. If it finds you out of compliance, it can impose a $150 penalty and give you 60 days to fix the problem. After that, the penalty can rise to $500 or a tax-underpayment penalty, whichever is greater.

What has to happen before registration

Section 41-1a-203 lists the steps before a vehicle can be registered:

  • A vehicle identification number inspection under § 41-1a-204
  • An emissions certificate, if your county and model year require one this year
  • Payment of property taxes or the uniform fee in lieu of property tax, or a property tax clearance
  • The automobile driver education tax
  • The registration fee, the uninsured motorist identification fee and any local emissions compliance fee
  • Any sales and use tax that's due

If the vehicle hasn't been registered before, or is still registered to a previous owner, you must apply for a Utah certificate of title in your name first.

Registration fees

Section 41-1a-1206 sets a $44 base fee for a motor vehicle of 14,000 pounds or less, and $46 for a motorcycle. Since January 1, 2024, another $7 is added at registration. The Tax Commission adjusts these fees each January 1 for inflation, rounded up to the nearest 25 cents, so the amount you pay is higher than the number written in the statute. A Utah Legislature staff brief from 2026 lists the adjusted calendar-year 2026 fee as $58.25 for a passenger vehicle and $61.50 for a motorcycle.

Other charges are added on top:

  • An age-based uniform fee in lieu of property tax under § 59-2-405.1. The DMV's 2026 table lists $150 for model years 2024–2026, $110 for 2021–2023, $80 for 2018–2020, $50 for 2015–2017, and $10 for 2014 and older.
  • A $1 uninsured motorist identification fee under § 41-1a-1218, or 75 cents for a six-month registration
  • A local emissions compliance fee of up to $3 in counties that impose one under § 41-1a-1223. Electric vehicles are exempt.
  • Alternative-fuel registration fees for electric, hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles, covered in the EV guide
  • A license plate fee for new plates under § 41-1a-1211, with extra application fees for personalized and special group plates

The DMV's Summary of Common Fees lists $6 for a new title, a duplicate title, or a title correction. Its Fee Estimator calculates the full total for a specific vehicle.

Registration periods

Registration is generally annual. A six-month option is available for passenger vehicles and motorcycles, at the reduced amounts in § 41-1a-1206(2). The DMV says a 24-month option is available for trailers, off-highway vehicles, street-legal ATVs and electric vehicles, and § 41-1a-1206(10) doubles the 12-month fees for a 24-month registration. The DMV must send a renewal notice at least 30 days before expiration, by mail or by email if you choose.

Emissions inspections

Emissions inspections are a registration prerequisite in Cache, Davis, Salt Lake, Utah and Weber counties. The Tax Commission's Publication 9 says an inspection certificate is valid for two months from the date it's issued. Under the DMV's 2026 tables for Salt Lake, Davis and Utah counties, model years 2026, 2025, 2023 and 2021 don't need a test this year. Model years 2024, 2022 and 1996 through 2020 do, and 1995 and older are exempt.

That pattern comes from the rule for vehicles under six years old: they test every other year, with even model years testing in even calendar years. Publication 9 adds that Cache County doesn't test vehicles under six years old and tests older ones every other year. The DMV's Utah County table also requires tests for diesel vehicles of model years 1998 through 2021. Electric vehicles, motorcycles, off-highway vehicles and new vehicles with a manufacturer's statement of origin are on the DMV's exemption lists. Farm trucks can apply to their county health department for an exemption.

Safety inspections

Under § 41-1a-205, a safety inspection is still required when a vehicle is first registered as a street-legal ATV, a street-legal novel vehicle, a salvage vehicle or a roadable aircraft. A current safety inspection certificate must also be displayed on registered commercial vehicles, certain motor vehicles with three or more axles or pulling trailers, combination units, buses or vans for hire, taxicabs and ground transportation vehicles.
