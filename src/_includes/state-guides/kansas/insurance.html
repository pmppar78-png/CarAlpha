What a Kansas auto policy must include

Under the Kansas Automobile Injury Reparations Act, every vehicle owner must carry motor vehicle liability insurance on each vehicle they own. A small set of exceptions, such as approved self-insurance, is listed in K.S.A. 40-3104. The required contents of each policy are set out in K.S.A. 40-3107. Liability limits must be at least:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in any one accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in any one accident
  • $25,000 for damage to or destruction of other people's property in any one accident

Coverage must apply anywhere in the United States or Canada and must protect anyone driving the insured vehicle with the named insured's express or implied permission.

Personal injury protection

Liability coverage alone doesn't satisfy Kansas law. Every policy must also include personal injury protection (PIP). PIP pays benefits after an accident to the named insured, relatives living in the same household, anyone driving the insured vehicle, its passengers, and pedestrians it strikes. The minimum amounts, defined in K.S.A. 40-3103, are:

  • Medical benefits of at least $4,500 per person
  • Disability benefits for lost earnings, up to at least $900 a month for no more than one year
  • Rehabilitation benefits of at least $4,500
  • Substitution benefits for household services you can't perform, up to $25 a day for no more than 365 days
  • Funeral, burial or cremation benefits of up to $2,000
  • Survivors' benefits for lost earnings of at least $900 a month, for no more than one year after death minus any months of disability benefits already paid

A motorcycle owner who is the named insured may reject PIP in writing for injuries suffered while operating or riding on the motorcycle.

Limits on injury lawsuits

PIP comes with a threshold for lawsuits. Under K.S.A. 40-3117, an injured person can sue another driver for pain, suffering, mental anguish and other non-economic losses only if one of these applies:

  • Medical treatment for the injury has a reasonable value of $2,000 or more
  • The injury involves permanent disfigurement, permanent injury or a permanent loss of bodily function
  • There is a fracture to a weight-bearing bone, or a compound, comminuted, displaced or compressed fracture
  • The injured person loses a body member or dies

The reasonable value of the treatment counts toward the $2,000 figure, not just what was billed. Nursing-type care provided by a relative or household member can count as well.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

K.S.A. 40-284 requires every primary auto liability policy for a vehicle registered or garaged in Kansas to include uninsured motorist coverage. The limits must match the bodily injury liability limits you buy. That coverage must also include an underinsured motorist provision, which pays when the at-fault driver's bodily injury coverage is lower than yours.

You can reject in writing only the part of this coverage above the 25/50 minimums in 40-3107, so every policy carries at least the basic amount. A rejection applies to everyone insured under the policy and carries over to renewals with the same insurer unless you ask in writing to add the coverage back. The statute also bars stacking. No matter how many policies or vehicles are involved, the total available can't exceed the highest limits of any single policy that applies.

Proof at a traffic stop

Drivers must show evidence of financial security when a law enforcement officer asks for it. Kansas allows that proof on a phone or other electronic device. The officer may look only at the insurance information, not anything else stored on the device. A driver who can't show proof gets a citation. When proof is later produced in court, the state can check it with the insurer. Under 40-3104(e), the charge must be dismissed if no request for a trial setting is made within 60 days of the date the proof was produced.

Penalties for driving uninsured

An owner can't let an uninsured vehicle be driven on a highway or on property open to public use, and no one may knowingly drive one there. Under 40-3104(g):

  • A first violation is a class B misdemeanor with a fine of $300 to $1,000, up to six months in county jail, or both
  • A second conviction within three years is a class A misdemeanor with a fine of $800 to $2,500

After an accident involving an uninsured vehicle, the Division of Vehicles can also suspend the licenses of the drivers and the owner and revoke the registration of all vehicles the owner has. To reinstate, you need to file proof of financial security, pay a $100 reinstatement fee, and resolve your liability for the accident. The fee is $300 if a registration was revoked again within a year of a prior revocation.

What Kansas drivers pay

NAIC's 2024 Average Premium Supplement lists a combined average of $1,295.40 for liability, collision and comprehensive in Kansas, up from $966.92 in 2020. The average expenditure, which reflects what drivers actually bought, was $1,063.82 in 2024. The Insurance Research Council estimates that 12.0% of Kansas drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 27th.
