Title fee and the 6.5% excise tax

Titling a vehicle in Maryland costs $200 under Transportation Article § 13-802. Rental vehicles pay $100, off-highway recreational vehicles $70, and mopeds and motor scooters $40. No title fee is charged when a surviving spouse takes over a vehicle from a joint owner who has died, or when a vehicle moves into or out of a trust under the Estates and Trusts Article.

You'll also pay the titling (excise) tax. Since July 1, 2025, it has been 6.5% for most vehicles and 3.5% for rentals. According to the MVA's excise tax page, the tax is based on the total purchase price or on a used-car valuation guide the MVA has adopted, adjusted for mileage. If the price you report is $500 or more below that valuation, the MVA may ask for documentation. Vehicles seven years old or older are taxed on the purchase price or a $640 minimum value, whichever is greater, so the minimum tax is $41.60.

Moving to Maryland

The excise tax works differently if you moved to Maryland within the last 60 days:

  • Vehicle 6 model years old or newer: if your previous state's tax rate was 6.5% or higher, you pay $100. If it was lower, you pay the difference. The MVA's example is someone from Virginia, where the rate is 4%, who pays 2.5% of the vehicle's retail value.
  • Vehicle 7 model years old or older: you pay $41.60 ($20.80 for a trailer).
  • From a state with no sales tax: you pay the full 6.5% on a vehicle 6 model years old or newer, and $41.60 on an older one.

For these calculations, the MVA uses the "clean retail value" from the National Automobile Dealers Association.

Safety inspection before you title a used vehicle

Under § 23-107, the MVA won't title and register a used vehicle (other than a Class L historic vehicle) without a valid inspection certificate. The MVA's safety inspection page notes that this is a one-time inspection when the vehicle is first titled, and it doesn't recur. The certificate is sent to the MVA electronically and is good for 90 days. For a used vehicle in a licensed dealer's inventory, it's good for 6 months or until 1,000 more miles are driven, whichever comes first. The MVA can issue a temporary registration while it waits for the certificate, and the vehicle must then be inspected within 60 days.

Some transfers don't need an inspection. Under § 23-106 and the MVA's regulation, these include transfers between spouses, between a parent and child, or between co-owners when one name comes off the title. Also exempt: transfers to a licensed dealer, vehicles that won't be both titled and registered in Maryland, and a leased vehicle bought by the lessee at the end of the lease.

Registration fees

The MVA's fee page lists one-year amounts. They include the $40 yearly surcharge for the Emergency Medical Services system. You can register for one, two or three years.

  • Passenger cars and multipurpose vehicles, 3,500 lbs. shipping weight or less: $120.50
  • Passenger cars and multipurpose vehicles, 3,500 to 3,700 lbs.: $125.50
  • Passenger cars and multipurpose vehicles, over 3,700 lbs.: $191.50
  • Class E trucks: $133.75 up to 3,500 lbs. GVW, $138.75 up to 5,000 lbs., and $178.75 up to 7,000 lbs.
  • Motorcycles: $105
  • Historic vehicles (model year 1999 or older): $55.50

Zero-emission vehicles pay an added $125 a year, and plug-in hybrids $100. Vanity plates cost $50, and personalization adds $50 a year. Other fees include $15 for a tag transfer, $40 for a replacement tag, $50 for a temporary registration and $40 to file a security interest. You can get a refund for returned tags with more than 12 months left on the registration. Since January 2025, some one-year registrations can be paid in two installments, with the second due six months into the registration period, at no extra cost.

Emissions testing every two years

The Vehicle Emissions Inspection Program (VEIP) covers vehicles registered in Anne Arundel, Baltimore, Calvert, Carroll, Cecil, Charles, Frederick, Harford, Howard, Montgomery, Prince George's, Queen Anne's and Washington counties and Baltimore City. Most vehicles test every two years, when the MVA sends a notice. A new vehicle (2019 or newer, original owner, first titled in Maryland) gets its first test 72 months after its model year. If you buy a used vehicle, you'll get a test notice four months after you register it, since the safety inspection doesn't check emissions.

The test costs $30, or $26 at a self-serve kiosk. A late fee of $30 is added the day after the due date and again every 28 days after that. Exempt vehicles include:

  • 1995 and older vehicles under 8,500 lbs. GVW
  • vehicles over 26,000 lbs.
  • vehicles powered solely by diesel or electricity
  • motorcycles
  • historic vehicles

Hybrids do have to be tested.
