Annual registration fees

North Carolina registers vehicles one year at a time. Under N.C.G.S. § 20-66(a), each registration has to be renewed annually. The base fees for private vehicles are set in § 20-87:

Trucks and other weight-based vehicles pay under separate schedules. A vehicle given by the federal government to a disabled war veteran is registered for $1.55 while the original recipient or another eligible veteran owns it.

Title fees and highway use tax

Section 20-85 sets the charges for title and registration paperwork:

  • Certificate of title: $66.75
  • One-day title service: $126
  • Duplicate or corrected title: $25
  • Transfer of registration: $25
  • Replacement plates: $25
  • Duplicate registration card: $25, and
  • Recording a supplementary lien, or renewing or removing a lien: $25 each.

Titling a vehicle also triggers the highway use tax under § 105-187.3. The rate is 3% of the vehicle's retail value. For a dealer sale, that's the sales price including accessories, minus any trade-in allowance. For a private sale, it's market value minus any trade-in. A vehicle titled for another reason, such as a move into the state, is taxed on market value, which is presumed to be the figure in the Commissioner's schedule. A $2,000 cap applies to commercial Class A and Class B vehicles and recreational vehicles. A separately stated service contract isn't taxed.

Expiration and grace period

Under § 20-66(g), a registration renewed with a sticker expires at midnight on the last day of the month shown on it. You may still drive the vehicle until midnight on the 15th day of the next month. NCDMV can stagger expiration dates so roughly equal numbers expire each month, and it can send renewal notices by email if you agree to that in writing. A registration renewed by issuing a new plate expires at the end of the year the plate was issued, with driving allowed through February 15 of the next year.

Renewals can be blocked by unpaid civil penalties, including lapse-of-insurance penalties under § 20-311 and emissions penalties under § 20-183.8A.

Safety and emissions inspections

Section 20-183.2(a) makes every vehicle subject to registration also subject to a safety inspection, unless it's inspected under the federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations, is a trailer under 4,000 pounds or a house trailer, or is a historic vehicle or a school bus covered by its own inspection rules. An emissions inspection applies to vehicles registered in an emissions county that meet the model-year rules in § 20-183.2(b). Motorcycles, plug-in electric vehicles and fuel cell electric vehicles are exempt from it.

Emissions testing currently covers 19 counties. Session Law 2023-134 and Session Law 2026-41 end that program once the EPA approves, and the statewide safety inspection stays in place. Until then, § 20-183.2(b)(3) limits emissions testing to vehicles within 20 model years of the current year. It skips the three newest model years unless the vehicle has 70,000 miles or more. After the change takes effect, subdivision (3a) limits it to vehicles within 20 model years and earlier than model year 2017.

The inspection fee is owed even if the vehicle fails. A vehicle that fails can be reinspected at the same station within 60 days without paying another inspection fee.

When to get inspected

Section 20-183.4C sets the schedule. A vehicle that's already been inspected here must be inspected again by the last day of the month its registration expires, and it can be done as early as 90 days before that. A vehicle bought from a North Carolina dealer or in a private sale is due when its current registration expires unless it passed within the previous 12 months. Dealers have to inspect new vehicles before retail delivery and used vehicles before offering them for sale, and they give the buyer a receipt showing compliance.

Under § 20-183.8A, NCDMV assesses a $50 civil penalty if a vehicle isn't inspected within four months after it was due. Tampering with an emission control device, or giving a false county of registration to avoid an emissions test, carries a $250 penalty. Service members who were continuously out of state on active duty can get the $50 penalty waived if nobody drove the vehicle and it's inspected within 30 days of their return.

Vehicles from out of state

A vehicle a new resident brings in, and a vehicle a North Carolina resident buys out of state, must pass inspection before NCDMV registers it, unless the Commissioner allows a single extension of up to 12 months from the first registration date. You'll need North Carolina liability insurance from a licensed insurer before registering. Expect to pay the title fee, the 3% highway use tax and the first year's registration fee.
