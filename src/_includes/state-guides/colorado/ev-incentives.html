Colorado's EV tax credit has shrunk

For years, Colorado's main EV incentive was a state income tax credit, and it's still there, but much smaller. Under C.R.S. 39-22-516.7 and 516.8, summarized by the Alternative Fuels Data Center and explained on the Colorado Energy Office's EV tax credits page, a new light-duty electric or hydrogen fuel-cell vehicle titled and registered in Colorado earns:

  • $750 for purchases or leases in calendar year 2026, down from $3,500 in 2025
  • an additional $2,500 if the vehicle's MSRP is under $35,000 (this applies to vehicles bought or leased from January 1, 2024, until January 1, 2029)
  • nothing unless the MSRP is $80,000 or less, which is the Energy Office's cap.

Amounts for 2027 and 2028 haven't been set yet. Bought vehicles have to be new. Leases qualify only if the term is two years or longer. You can assign the credit to the dealer or finance company at signing using the Department of Revenue's form DR 0618. The dealer then pays you the credit right away, and you can't also claim it on your state return. The dealer can keep an administrative fee of up to $250, and that fee has to be listed separately in the contract.

Medium-duty and heavy-duty electric trucks get much larger credits: $4,000 and $8,000 per year from 2026 through 2028. Those are capped at the price difference from a comparable gas or diesel truck.

Extra fees EV owners pay

Colorado charges EV owners extra at registration because they don't pay gas tax. The DMV's taxes and fees page lists two:

  • Plug-in electric vehicle fee: $63.05 a year. It's indexed to inflation under C.R.S. 42-3-304(25)(a), so it changes from one fiscal year to the next.
  • Road usage fee. From July 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027, it's $26 for a battery-electric vehicle and $13 for a plug-in hybrid. It rises each July, to $36 and $16 for 2027–28, then $51 and $19, then $66 and $21. By 2031–32 it reaches $96 and $27, and annual inflation adjustments start in fiscal year 2032–33.

So a battery-electric car registered this year pays about $89 in EV-specific fees on top of the usual specific ownership tax and registration fees. Colorado also offers an EV license plate under C.R.S. 42-3-259. It costs the same as a standard plate.

No emissions test

Cars that run only on electricity are exempt from the emissions inspections required in the Denver and North Front Range areas (AFDC summary). Plug-in hybrids have gas engines, so they're still subject to the usual testing schedule in those counties.

Charging rights at home

Renters. Under C.R.S. 38-12-601 (AFDC summary), a tenant can install a Level 1 or Level 2 charger at their own expense. The landlord can charge a reasonable access fee or ask to be reimbursed for the electricity and installation costs. The landlord can also require you to meet safety, registration, and appearance rules.

HOAs and condos. A housing association can't ban a Level 1 or Level 2 charger for personal use in your own parking space. It also can't charge you any fee for having one, beyond the cost of the electricity. You have to follow its design rules, hire a licensed electrical contractor, pay for installation and maintenance, and carry insurance on the charger.

New homes. Builders have to offer buyers of a new home a charger, charger-ready wiring, or an outlet that can support a charger in the parking area.

Since July 1, 2026, home chargers sold in Colorado must be ENERGY STAR certified (C.R.S. 6-7.5-105).

Public charging rules

It's illegal to park in a designated EV charging space when you're not charging. An EV parked at a charger but unplugged for more than 30 minutes is presumed not to be charging, and the fine is $182 (C.R.S. 42-4-1213). There are exceptions for lodging and airport chargers and for 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. Charging stations are exempt from state property tax until January 1, 2030.

Free help

The Energy Office's ReCharge Colorado program gives individual drivers, workplaces, and multifamily properties free coaching on EV savings and grants. Its Charge Ahead Colorado grants cover 80% of the cost of chargers for local governments, businesses, nonprofits, multifamily buildings, and HOAs. That's up to $7,000 per Level 2 port, or up to 90% for income-qualified applicants.
