Idaho's required liability limits

25/50/15: $25,000 for injury or death of one person, $50,000 for injury or death of two or more people in one accident, and $15,000 for damage to other people's property.

The Idaho Department of Insurance (DOI) says every resident must keep bodily injury liability and property damage liability on each personal-use vehicle registered in the state. Bodily injury liability pays for other drivers' and passengers' injuries when you cause a crash, usually their medical bills. Property damage liability pays for what you damage, whether that's another car, a fence or a telephone pole. Your limits are the most your insurer has to pay on your behalf, and DOI points out that you can buy higher limits for more protection.

The Idaho Transportation Department (ITD) adds that the policy must come from a company licensed to do business in Idaho. If you're moving here, ITD asks you to tell your insurer about the move so the policy is written with an admitted Idaho carrier.

A $15,000 property damage limit doesn't go far when a late-model car or pickup is totaled, and anything above your limits can become your personal debt. Owners who'd rather not buy a policy can post an indemnity bond with the Director of Insurance under Idaho Code § 49-1229. The bond must guarantee at least $50,000 per accident, of which $15,000 is for property damage, and the required amount tops out at $120,000 for five or more vehicles.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Under Idaho Code § 41-2502, an auto liability policy for a vehicle registered or principally garaged in Idaho must include uninsured motorist (UM) and underinsured motorist (UIM) bodily injury coverage. A named insured may reject either one or both, in writing or in an authorized electronic record. That rejection applies to everyone on the policy, and the same insurer or an affiliate doesn't have to add the coverage back at renewal. Before a new policy or its first renewal, your insurer must give you a standard disclosure form approved by the Director that explains both coverages and the kinds of UIM offered in Idaho.

DOI says UM/UIM limits generally match your bodily injury liability limits, although insurers differ. Idaho insurers sell UIM in two forms, and the form you have changes what you'd collect:

  • Excess. Your UIM limit is added above whatever the at-fault driver's insurer pays. With $25,000 of UIM and an at-fault driver carrying $25,000, up to $50,000 is available. With $100,000 of UIM, the total is $125,000.
  • Difference in limits (offset). Your UIM limit is reduced by what the other driver's insurer pays. With $25,000 of UIM against a $25,000 at-fault policy, you get nothing extra. With $100,000 of UIM, you could get up to $75,000 more.

Read the disclosure form before you sign it, because a rejection stays on the policy at renewal unless you change it.

How the DMV checks for coverage

A 2019 law requires registered owners to keep insurance or have the vehicle's registration suspended. ITD's online verification system compares registrations with insurer reports. If a vehicle shows no coverage for two consecutive months, the owner receives a warning and has 30 days to provide proof of insurance or obtain an exemption. If the owner doesn't, the registration is suspended. Getting it back takes proof of insurance and a $75 reinstatement fee, which you can pay by phone, by mail or online.

If you get a notice, you can report your insurance or update your situation with ITD's Drive Insured self-reporting tool. ITD notes that county DMV offices aren't part of that system, so you can't fix a coverage notice in person at the county office. If you've sold or gotten rid of a vehicle, ITD asks you to file a release of liability, for which you'll need the VIN and title number.

Penalties for driving uninsured

Under § 49-1428, operating a vehicle on a highway without valid liability insurance at the required limits is an infraction carrying a $75 fine for a first violation. A second or later conviction within five years under that section or the related owner and proof sections (§§ 49-1229 and 49-1232) is a misdemeanor. The penalty is a fine of up to $1,000, up to six months in county jail, or both. The department must warn anyone convicted about the tougher penalties for repeat violations.

Average premiums in Idaho

In the National Association of Insurance Commissioners' auto insurance database report, Idaho's 2024 average combined premium for liability, collision and comprehensive was $1,086.21, and the average expenditure was $955.98. The 2020 combined figure was $832.94. A single average blends a wide range of drivers and coverage levels, so your own quote can be far from it.

According to the Insurance Research Council, about 6.4% of Idaho drivers were uninsured, which put the state 49th. That leaves roughly one driver in sixteen without coverage, and an uninsured driver who hurts you may have little to collect from.

Collision and comprehensive aren't required by Idaho law. If the vehicle is financed or leased, though, your lender or leasing company may require them as a condition of the contract.

Questions and complaints

DOI's Consumer Affairs section is a free resource. It answers general insurance questions and helps people resolve disputes with insurers and agents. You can file a complaint online or call (208) 334-4250. For registration suspensions and proof-of-insurance notices, contact ITD's Division of Motor Vehicles instead.
