Owning an EV in Kansas

The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center lists Kansas laws on registration fees, charging station rules and infrastructure planning for plug-in vehicles. It doesn't list a state purchase rebate or an individual tax credit for buying an electric car.

Annual registration fees

House Bill 2122 (2025) amended K.S.A. 8-143. Starting January 1, 2026, the annual fees are:

  • $165 for an all-electric passenger vehicle
  • $100 for a plug-in electric hybrid
  • $70 for an electric hybrid
  • $30 for an all-electric motorcycle

These amounts take the place of the weight-based $30 or $40 charge for gas-powered passenger vehicles. They aren't an extra fee on top of it. Trucks and truck tractors registered at 12,000 pounds or less pay $200 if all-electric and $125 if hybrid or plug-in hybrid. The usual surcharges, county fees and personal property tax still apply.

In dollar terms, an all-electric car pays $125 more a year than a gas car at 4,500 pounds or less, or $135 more than one above that weight. A plug-in hybrid pays $60 or $70 more, and a conventional hybrid $40 or $30 more. The AFDC shows the EV and hybrid fee schedule was first enacted in April 2019 and most recently amended in April 2025.

Charging station rules

A business or person that resells electricity from a public utility to power EV chargers isn't regulated as a public utility under K.S.A. 66-104.

Since 2025, the Kansas Department of Agriculture has had authority to inspect and test EV charging stations under its weights and measures law. Stations must meet the standards for dispensing electricity, and violators can be fined up to $5,000. If a charger's displayed amount doesn't match what you were billed, this is the agency that oversees the measurement.

In 2019, the Legislature also ordered a study of Kansas utilities' retail electricity rates, including EV charger rate design and service deregulation. The study went to the Kansas Corporation Commission in two parts in 2020.

Federal charging funds

The federal NEVI Formula Program required the Kansas Department of Transportation to submit an updated EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan each year for fiscal years 2022 through 2026. Each plan describes how the state intends to distribute NEVI money and how it will handle physical and cybersecurity, and includes a community engagement report. KDOT publishes its plans and program updates on its Charge Up Kansas website.

Tax credits that don't cover electric cars

Kansas has an alternative fuel vehicle tax credit and an alternative fueling infrastructure credit under K.S.A. 79-32,201. Neither covers EVs. Both are available only to corporations with Kansas corporate income tax liability. They apply to fuels made from grain starch, oil seed, animal fat, other biomass or biogas, and the AFDC lists the technologies as biodiesel, ethanol, natural gas and propane. Unused credits can be carried forward up to three years.

Federal credits

The federal clean vehicle credits under Internal Revenue Code sections 30D, 25E and 45W ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. Check with the IRS before counting on any federal credit.
