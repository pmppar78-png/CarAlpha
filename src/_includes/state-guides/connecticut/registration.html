Plan on an appointment

You can't register a car in Connecticut just by walking in. The DMV handles new registrations by appointment at a hub or branch office, or through a participating dealership. Before you book, use the DMV's online compliance check. Unpaid local property taxes, unpaid parking tickets, an insurance-lapse fine, or a suspended registration will each stop the transaction until they're resolved.

Moving here: 90 days

Once you're a Connecticut resident, you have 90 days to transfer your out-of-state registration. Transfers are done in person, by appointment, at a DMV hub office or the Enfield branch. The process has three steps:

  1. Get an emissions test or a VIN verification at an emissions test station. Vehicles older than four model years need the emissions test. Newer vehicles are exempt but still need the VIN checked. Either way, you leave with a vehicle inspection report (VIR).
  2. Bring your documents: the original out-of-state title (a photocopy won't work), ID, proof of Connecticut insurance, a completed Form H-13B, the VIR, and payment.
  3. Book and keep your appointment. You can transfer your driver's license during the same visit.

If a lender holds your title, the lienholder must mail the original directly to the DMV Title Section, Room 305, 60 State Street, Wethersfield, CT 06161. Wait five business days after it's delivered before your appointment. The DMV holds the title for up to 90 days and then returns it, and you'd have to start over. If the lender will only send a front-and-back photocopy, you can get a courtesy registration valid for up to six months. It costs 25% of the base registration fee plus the usual first-time fees, and you can't renew until the original title arrives. If you lease, you'll need a power of attorney from the leasing company.

Fees for a regular passenger car

The DMV's fee schedule lists the following for a regular passenger car, SUV, or van. The $120 base fee covers a three-year registration.

  • $120 registration fee
  • $5 plate fee
  • $25 title fee
  • $10 administrative fee
  • $15 Clean Air Act fee
  • $10 lien fee, if you have a loan
  • Passport to the Parks fee, $24 since July 1, 2025
  • $40 emissions exemption fee for vehicles four model years old or newer
  • $15 greenhouse gas fee for a new car sold with a certificate of origin

The Passport to the Parks charge gives every Connecticut-plated car free parking at state parks and beaches. A 10-day temporary registration to drive the car to its inspection costs $21, and sales tax is collected when it's issued.

Sales and use tax

You pay tax when you first register. According to the DMV's sales tax guidance, the rate is 6.35%, or 7.75% if the vehicle costs more than $50,000. For a private sale, the tax is based on the higher of the bill-of-sale price or the NADA average trade-in value. If you really paid less than book value, you can ask the Department of Revenue Services for a partial refund on form CERT-106 within three years. The DMV also has an online sales tax calculator.

Local property tax on cars

Connecticut cities and towns charge property tax on registered vehicles, and the DMV checks for unpaid property tax. If you fall behind on the car tax, the DMV won't renew your registration or register another vehicle in your name. When you move away, cancel your Connecticut registration and keep the receipt. That stops further tax bills from building up and stops insurers from reporting you for a lapse.

Buying or selling privately

A seller can keep the plates. Either cancel the registration or move the plates to your next car, and get a DMV receipt either way. Otherwise the old insurer's cancellation can trigger a lapse notice and the $200 fine. The buyer needs a signed-over title and a bill of sale (Form H-31 is the DMV's version), and then registers the car with an appointment like any other first-time registration. That's also when the buyer pays use tax on the higher of the price or the NADA value.

Renewals and emissions

Renewal notices go out about 45 days before expiration, and you can renew online if there are no compliance holds. The emissions schedule continues after your first test. Cars older than four model years have to keep testing on the schedule the DMV mails to you, and an overdue test blocks renewal.

Equipment rules that catch newcomers

  • Window tint is legal only if each tinted window has a valid compliance sticker or the factory safety certification label.
  • Aftermarket side-pipe exhausts are illegal. Factory side pipes are fine.
  • The car must have two outside rear-view mirrors.

Check these before you buy a car from out of state.
