DRIVE EV rebates

Rhode Island's DRIVE EV program, run by the Office of Energy Resources, offers rebates on electric vehicles bought or leased from a licensed Rhode Island dealer. Amounts were increased for purchases made on or after January 5, 2026:

VehicleNewUsed
Battery electric or fuel cell$3,000$2,500
Plug-in hybrid$2,000$1,750

Price caps apply: $75,000 for a new vehicle and $55,000 for a used one. You have 120 days from purchase to apply, and you can receive one rebate every 36 months.

DRIVE+

DRIVE+ adds to the standard rebate for income-qualified residents, up to $1,500 more. For plug-in hybrids the extra amount is $1,000 new and $750 used. You can qualify through enrollment in an income-qualified program or by self-attesting your income.

DRIVE EV Fleet

Businesses, nonprofits and public entities can apply through DRIVE EV Fleet, which pays $3,000 for a new vehicle and $2,500 for a used one. Fleets in Central Falls, Cranston, East Providence, Pawtucket and Providence get an extra $500 per vehicle. A fleet can receive rebates for up to five vehicles every 24 months.

PowerUpRI home charger incentives

PowerUpRI helps pay for home charging equipment. The standard incentive covers 100% of costs up to $800, or 50% up to $1,000 when an electrical upgrade is needed. Income-qualified households can get 75% of costs, up to $1,500. The program page was last updated May 15, 2026.

State EV laws

The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center lists these Rhode Island rules:

  • No one may stop, stand or park in a signed EV charging space without being connected to the charger. The fine is $100.
  • The DMV may issue special license plates for electric drive vehicles, including hybrids, plug-in hybrids and all-electric vehicles. They aren't required, but the DMV encourages them for the safety of first responders.
  • Since January 1, 2024, new parking lots, and existing lots adding 50% or more spaces, must designate EV spaces, starting at one space for lots of 10 to 25.
  • The Office of Energy Resources sets cost-recovery fees for chargers on state property. The proposed 2025 fees were $0.28 per kWh for Level 2 and $0.39 per kWh for DC fast charging.
  • Since January 1, 2023, EV chargers sold, leased or rented in the state must be ENERGY STAR certified.
  • Rhode Island has adopted California's vehicle emissions standards, including its zero-emission vehicle sales requirements.
  • From July 1, 2024, a low-speed vehicle is a four-wheeled electric vehicle with a top speed above 20 mph and no more than 25 mph and a gross vehicle weight rating under 3,000 pounds.

Registration fees for electrified vehicles

Amendments to § 31-6-1 in the FY 2026 budget added yearly fees on electrified vehicles, on top of the standard registration fee and surcharge: $200 for a battery electric vehicle, $100 for a plug-in hybrid and $50 for a hybrid. Fully electric light-duty vehicles still need the safety inspection every 24 months but are exempt from emissions testing.
