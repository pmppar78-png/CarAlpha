Deadlines to register

Arkansas registration goes through the DFA's Office of Motor Vehicle at county revenue offices. The DFA sets two main deadlines:

  • New residents: 60 days after moving to Arkansas.
  • New or used vehicle purchases: 60 days from the purchase date.

Miss the window and a penalty of $3 for every 10 days starts to build, up to the amount of the yearly registration fee. Sales tax filed late carries a separate 10 percent penalty. If the car truly wasn't driven, you can file an Affidavit of Non-Use (form 10-300) to avoid the late-registration penalty.

What you need at the revenue office

Arkansas ties registration to county property tax, which surprises a lot of people moving in. Before you can register or renew, you need:

  • Proof of assessment from your county assessor, showing the vehicle is on the county's personal property rolls.
  • Proof that last year's personal property taxes are paid.
  • Proof of liability insurance on the vehicle.
  • For an out-of-state title branded salvage, rebuilt, or similar, a VIN verification is required. A certified city or county officer, the Arkansas State Police, or a State Police designee can do it.
  • The manufacturer's certificate of origin for a new car, or the previous owner's title signed over to you for a used one.
  • A bill of sale or invoice showing the full price. If you traded a car in, it has to list the trade-in's VIN and allowance.
  • A completed odometer statement signed by buyer and seller, for vehicles under 20 years old.
  • A signed copy of the lien contract, if you financed.

Every owner has to sign the title application when ownership is joined by "AND," or the application will be rejected. The order matters: the assessor's office comes first, then the revenue office.

Registration and title fees

Passenger vehicles in Arkansas are registered by weight. The DFA lists these yearly fees:

  • 3,000 pounds or less: $17
  • 3,001 to 4,500 pounds: $25
  • 4,501 pounds and over: $30

The title fee is $10. These low base fees are a big reason Arkansas registration feels cheap at renewal. The heavier costs are the county property tax and, on a purchase, the sales tax. Electric and hybrid vehicles also pay an extra yearly fee, covered on our Arkansas EV page.

Sales tax on vehicles

State sales tax on a vehicle is 6.5 percent, or 7 percent in Texarkana, and you pay it when you register, not at the dealer. The rules depend on the price and whether the car is new or used:

  • New vehicles priced at $4,000 or more pay the full rate.
  • Used vehicles: under Act 1013, for sales on or after January 1, 2022, a used vehicle priced under $4,000 owes no state sales tax. One priced from $4,000 to $9,999 pays a reduced 3.5 percent. At $10,000 and up, the full rate applies.
  • Trade-ins are credited against the taxable price.
  • If you sell a vehicle yourself within 60 days before or after buying the replacement, you can take a credit for that sale price.
  • Local sales tax on the vehicle is capped at $25 for each 1 percent of local rate.
  • Extended warranties and service contracts are taxable.

Since the tax is due at registration, a dealer won't usually collect it at closing. Budget for it separately, and keep the bill of sale showing the price and any trade-in.

Renewing

Arkansas registrations renew every year. You can renew online through the DFA, by phone at 1-800-941-2580, by mail to the Office of Motor Vehicle, P.O. Box 3153, Little Rock, AR 72203-3153, or in person at a revenue office. The system checks your assessment, property taxes, and insurance automatically. If any of them shows "NO," phone and online renewal are blocked, and you'll have to bring paper proof by mail or in person. The DFA's assistance line is 1-888-389-8336.

Insurance holds on registration

Registration and insurance are linked in Arkansas. If the state's AOIVS database can't confirm coverage on your vehicle and you don't respond to the notice, the registration is suspended, and reinstating it costs $100. A court conviction for driving uninsured leads to a separate suspension with a $20 reinstatement fee. Details are on our Arkansas insurance page.
