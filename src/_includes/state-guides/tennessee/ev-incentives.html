What Tennessee offers EV drivers

The Tennessee listings in the Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) don't show a state rebate or tax credit for buying or leasing an electric vehicle. What they do list includes grant funding for charging stations and cleaner fleet vehicles, a weight allowance for heavy EVs, EV rate plans from several local utilities, and an annual registration fee for electric and hybrid vehicles.

Annual electric and hybrid fees

Tenn. Code Ann. § 55-4-116 charges an extra fee each time an electric or hybrid vehicle's registration is renewed:

  • All-electric vehicles pay $200 for renewals through the end of 2026 and $274 during 2027. From January 1, 2028, the $274 is adjusted each year for inflation, capped at 3% a year.
  • Hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles pay $100 through 2027, with the same annual adjustment from 2028.

The fee isn't charged at initial registration. Low-speed and medium-speed vehicles are exempt, and the county clerk guide says electric motorcycles are too. Active-duty service members stationed outside Tennessee have been exempt from the all-electric fee since July 1, 2026, under Public Chapter 969.

Charging and fleet grants

The Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation (TDEC) funds projects that repower or replace Class 4-8 school, shuttle and transit buses, Class 4-7 local freight trucks, and Class 8 local freight and port drayage trucks with alternative fuel or all-electric models. TDEC also funds light-duty EV charging infrastructure. Private, public and nonprofit organizations, including state, local and tribal governments, can apply. Part of the money comes from Tennessee's share of the Volkswagen Environmental Mitigation Trust.

With the Tennessee Valley Authority, TDEC also supports the Fast Charge TN Network of DC fast chargers along travel corridors, where TVA-served local power companies are the eligible applicants. Check TDEC's project solicitations page for current funding rounds.

Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, the Tennessee Department of Transportation submitted annual EV Infrastructure Deployment Plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.

Utility programs

The AFDC lists these EV offerings from utilities serving Tennessee:

  • Middle Tennessee Electric: a residential EV time-of-use rate and rebates for buying and installing a Level 2 home charger
  • Memphis Light, Gas and Water: a residential EV time-of-use rate
  • Nashville Electric Service and Knoxville Utilities Board: EV time-of-use rates for non-residential customers
  • TVA: rebates and pilot programs for non-residential DC fast charging and charger deployment

Within TVA's service area, EV chargers that provide electricity exclusively for EVs aren't treated as public utilities, the AFDC says.

Weight limits and low-speed vehicles

Under § 55-7-203, EVs and natural gas vehicles may exceed state gross and axle weight limits by up to 2,000 pounds, to a maximum of 82,000 pounds, including on interstate highways.

Tennessee treats low-speed vehicles (four-wheeled electric vehicles, other than golf carts, with a top speed of 20 to 25 mph) and medium-speed vehicles (more than 30 and up to 35 mph) as utility terrain vehicles. They must meet federal safety standard 49 CFR 571.500 and can't be driven on interstates or on roads posted above 45 mph.

Federal tax credits are governed by federal law, so check the IRS website for what applies to your purchase.
