Nevada's liability insurance requirement

NRS 485.185 requires every owner of a motor vehicle that is registered, or required to be registered, in Nevada to keep liability insurance in force continuously while the vehicle is in the state or registered there. The policy has to come from an insurer licensed by the Nevada Division of Insurance and approved to do business in the state. Mopeds are the one exception named in that section. The Nevada DMV's insurance page puts it bluntly: there are no grace periods.

Minimum limits: 25/50/20

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in any one crash
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in any one crash
  • $20,000 for injury to or destruction of other people's property in any one crash

NRS 485.3091 repeats those limits for the policy itself. It also says coverage applies to you and anyone driving the listed vehicles with your express or implied permission, anywhere in the United States or Canada. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, collision and comprehensive aren't part of that list. Ask your agent to price higher limits and the optional coverages before settling on the minimum.

What the DMV checks

The DMV lists several conditions on its insurance page:

  • The registered owner has to be a named insured on the policy.
  • An insurer authorized in Nevada must validate the coverage electronically.
  • Out-of-state insurance isn't accepted; the policy has to be written for Nevada.
  • The names and VIN on the registration and the insurance must match.
  • You must carry Nevada Evidence of Insurance in the vehicle, on paper or on a phone.

Under NRS 485.317, the DMV has to verify coverage for every vehicle registered in the state. If it can't, it mails or emails a verification request, and you have 15 days to answer. If no answer arrives or the coverage really did lapse, a certified notice follows, and the registration is suspended 10 days after that notice is mailed. With a suspended registration you can't drive on public streets, and the DMV says officers must take the plates and may impound the vehicle.

Operator's policies

NRS 485.186 lets a natural person meet the requirement with an operator's policy, which covers you as a driver instead of covering a specific car. It's available when your household owns more vehicles than it has licensed drivers and every licensed driver in the household carries such a policy. The policy won't cover anyone else driving your vehicle, and you can't knowingly let an uninsured person drive it. Businesses, fleets, dealers and motor carriers can't use this option.

Penalties

NRS 485.187 makes it a misdemeanor to drive without the required insurance, to drive without evidence of insurance in the vehicle, or to refuse to show it to a peace officer or DMV representative. The fine is $600 to $1,000 per violation. For a first violation, the court must cut that to $100 if you buy a liability policy by sentencing, unless the vehicle is fleet-registered or self-insured. A court can't convict you of the evidence or no-insurance counts if you prove the coverage was in force when the officer asked.

The DMV's lapse penalties are separate from the court case. They're tiered by how long the lapse lasted and how many lapses you've had in five years:

  • First lapse: $250 for 1 to 30 days, rising to $1,250 at 181 days or more, with an SR-22 for 3 years once the lapse passes 90 days.
  • Second lapse within five years: $500 to $1,500.
  • Third lapse within five years: $750 to $1,750, plus 3 years of SR-22 and a driver's license suspension of at least 30 days.

A license suspended under NRS 485.326 stays suspended until you show proof of financial responsibility, which you then keep for 3 years. Reinstating the license costs $75 plus the license fee and any tests. The DMV may reduce the registration fee for a dormant vehicle or documented extenuating circumstances, but it says financial hardship doesn't qualify.

Cancel before you drop coverage

If you're storing a car or selling it, cancel the registration or surrender the plates before ending the policy. NRS 485.320 sets out the process for dormant vehicles, and the DMV warns that even a one-day lapse can trigger suspension and fines.

What drivers pay

In the National Association of Insurance Commissioners' 2024 Auto Insurance Database supplement, Nevada's average combined premium for liability, collision and comprehensive was $1,865.83, up from $1,365.05 in 2020, and the average expenditure was $1,703.58. The Insurance Research Council put Nevada's uninsured driver share at 11.1%, 35th among the states. Quotes from several Nevada-licensed carriers will show how much your record, ZIP code and vehicle change those numbers.
