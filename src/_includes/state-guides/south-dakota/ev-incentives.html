Incentives for EV buyers

South Dakota's entries in the Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) don't include a state purchase rebate or tax credit for people who buy or lease an electric vehicle. The incentives listed there include several for ethanol and biodiesel, plus grant programs for diesel emission reductions.

Annual electric vehicle fee

Owners of electric vehicles pay an extra annual fee at registration under SDCL 32-5-188. The statute, as amended by the 2026 Legislature, sets that fee at $100. The AFDC's listing still shows the earlier $50 amount. The fee applies to a noncommercial vehicle whose sole source of propulsion is an electric motor running on a battery that can be recharged from an outside source. The vehicle must be legal on interstate highways, built mainly for use on public roads and meet federal motor vehicle safety standards. Electric motorcycles that pay the motorcycle license fee are exempt, and the revenue goes to the state highway fund.

The EV fee is in addition to the regular license fee, which for a car, pickup or van depends on shipping weight and age.

Charging stations

A business that owns or runs a facility supplying electricity to the public exclusively to charge EVs isn't treated as an electric utility, as long as it buys its power from the retail utility serving that area. Those charging stations also aren't subject to the civil fine for overcharging under state utility rate law (SDCL 49-34-14 and 49-34A-116).

Under the federal NEVI Formula Program, the South Dakota Department of Transportation was required to submit an annually updated EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026, describing how it intended to distribute NEVI funds.

Grants for cleaner vehicles

  • Volkswagen settlement funds: The Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources (DANR) may use South Dakota's share of the VW Environmental Mitigation Trust for grants that reduce nitrogen oxide emissions from mobile sources. The money goes into a Clean Air Act Settlement fund (SDCL 34A-1-63 and 34A-1-64).
  • Clean Diesel Grant Program: DANR runs this program for bus diesel emission reduction projects, funded by the VW trust and the EPA's Diesel Emission Reduction Act program.

Low-speed vehicles

DOR defines a low-speed vehicle as a four-wheeled motor vehicle that can reach more than 20 mph but no more than 25 mph within one mile on a paved level surface. It can't be driven on a highway with a speed limit above 35 mph. To register one, you file a Low-Speed Vehicle Affidavit, and the vehicle needs equipment that includes headlamps, turn signals, tail and stop lamps, reflectors, mirrors, a parking brake, a windshield meeting federal glazing standards, a conforming VIN and seat belts. Fees follow the noncommercial vehicle fee schedule.
