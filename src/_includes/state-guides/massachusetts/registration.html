What you pay the RMV

The RMV's dealer-purchase instructions list three charges: the registration fee, the title fee and 6.25% sales tax. Registration fees vary by plate type. According to the RMV schedule of fees, a standard passenger registration is $60 and runs for two years. A certificate of title is $75. Your plates and the rear-plate decal showing the expiration year come with the registration.

Buying from a Massachusetts dealer

The process starts with paperwork from the dealer and your insurance agent:

  1. The dealer completes and signs the Registration and Title Application (RTA).
  2. A licensed Massachusetts insurance agent stamps and signs it to show an active policy. If you have a loan, make sure the lienholder information is accurate.
  3. The application goes in with the bill of sale and either the manufacturer's certificate of origin (for a new car) or the previous owner's title. A vehicle that's exempt from title because of its age uses its previous registration instead. The sale date, odometer reading and purchase price must be filled in on the back, and both parties must sign.
  4. Dealers and agents can file the transaction for you electronically or through the RMV's business-to-business service. You can also make an appointment at an RMV Service Center, which accepts walk-ins but serves scheduled customers first.

You'll get a registration certificate, plates and a decal when the transaction is done. The title is mailed in about 6 to 8 weeks. If there's a lien, it goes to the lender. You must also meet the RMV's identity requirements.

Sales tax on dealer and private sales

For a sale by a dealer registered with the Department of Revenue, the 6.25% tax is based on the purchase price. For a private sale, or a dealer that isn't registered with DOR, it's 6.25% of the purchase price or the NADA clean trade-in value, whichever is higher. If no clean trade-in value exists, the RMV and DOR use an agreed default value. A leased vehicle needs a leasing company registered with DOR, or the application is rejected. The lessee needs a power of attorney to sign for the leasing company.

Buying from an out-of-state dealer

There are three ways to get the vehicle home. The dealer or you can register it in Massachusetts first. You can use the dealer's temporary out-of-state registration to drive it back. Or you can tow or ship it. Massachusetts honors a temporary registration only for that trip home, and only if it's displayed properly. The vehicle must also carry liability and property damage insurance at least at the Massachusetts minimums, with proof kept in the vehicle.

Once the car is here, Chapter 90D, Section 4, gives you 10 days to apply for a title. Sales or use tax is due by the 20th of the month after the purchase, depending on Massachusetts' reciprocity agreement with the other state. The temporary registration doesn't extend either deadline.

Inspection within 7 days, then every year

According to the RMV's inspection page, every Massachusetts-registered vehicle must pass a yearly inspection, and a newly purchased vehicle must be inspected within 7 days of registration. The fee is $35 for most vehicles, and inspection stations may charge more for a commercial inspection. A motorcycle inspection is $15 at a licensed Class M station. The Massachusetts Vehicle Check program handles both the safety and emissions tests at licensed stations statewide.

A vehicle with onboard diagnostics that's less than 15 years old must also pass an annual emissions test, though a vehicle that runs only on electricity is exempt. A failed vehicle gets a "reject" sticker and a printed report. Since November 1, 2022, a passing sticker shows the month the last sticker expired. If the old one expired the year before, you get a January sticker for the current year. Driving without a valid sticker is a traffic violation that may bring a fine and affect your insurance rate. You can file a complaint about a station within 30 days for a safety issue, or 2 days for an emissions issue.

The annual excise tax

Massachusetts also charges a yearly motor vehicle excise under M.G.L. c. 60A, § 1 for the privilege of registration. The rate is $25 per $1,000 of value. The value is capped at a falling share of the manufacturer's list price:

  • 50% in the year before the model year
  • 90% in the model year
  • 60% in the second year and 40% in the third
  • 25% in the fourth, and 10% from the fifth year on

The RMV generates the bill, but you pay the city or town where the vehicle is garaged within 30 days of the issue date. You owe it even if the bill never arrives, and interest and penalties build on unpaid bills. An unpaid excise can block renewing your license or registration. If you move within Massachusetts, update your address with the RMV and your local assessor. If you move away, sell the car or take it off the road, cancel the plates and registration to stop new bills.
