Registering a vehicle in Iowa

In Iowa, the county treasurer handles titles and registrations, while the Iowa Department of Transportation (Iowa DOT) sets the rules and runs the statewide system. You renew in your county of residence. According to the county treasurers' Iowa Tax and Tags site, you can transfer or replace a title, or note a lien, at any county treasurer. If you do that outside your home county, your plate will show the county that issued the title.

Moving to Iowa

The Iowa DOT says you must title and register your vehicle with a county treasurer within 30 days of establishing residency, even if your out-of-state plates haven't expired. You'll surrender your current certificate of title and a completed application for an Iowa title and registration, signed by every owner and listing any lien. Some transactions require ID, such as a driver's license, state ID or Social Security card.

Moving within Iowa has its own rule. If you move from one county to another, you must report the new address to the treasurer in your former county within 10 days so your registration record can be sent to your new county.

After buying a vehicle

Under Iowa Code § 321.46, a new owner must apply for a title within 30 calendar days of the purchase or transfer, and penalties apply after that. The title application fee is $30 under § 321.20.

When a vehicle is first registered to you, Iowa charges a one-time "fee for new registration" under § 321.105A. It's $10 plus 5% of the purchase price, and the Department of Revenue administers it as closely as possible to the state use tax. On a $30,000 car, that's $1,510. These are among the exemptions:

  • A vehicle owned by someone who moved to Iowa intending to become a resident, if it was bought for use in the state or country they left and not for use in Iowa
  • A vehicle on which you already paid another state's sales, use or similar tax. If that tax was less than Iowa's fee, you owe the difference.
  • A vehicle previously registered in Iowa that returns after being registered in another state, if the fee or use tax was paid when it was first registered here

The treasurer can tell you which form or affidavit applies.

How the annual fee is calculated

For most passenger cars, SUVs and light trucks, § 321.109 sets the annual fee at 1% of the vehicle's value, as set by the department, plus 40 cents for each 100 pounds of weight. The weight includes the battery, heater, bumpers, spare tire and wheel. Section 321.113 then lowers the value-based part as the vehicle ages:

  • More than 7 model years old: value portion at 75% of the new rate, with a $50 minimum total
  • More than 9 model years old: value portion at 50%, with a $50 minimum total
  • 12 model years or older: a flat $50 a year

There's an exception for renewals by owners who registered the same vehicle before January 1, 2009. Their fee can't rise above the previous year's. Iowa DOT has an online fee estimator, found under Tools, then Dealer Inquiry, then Fee Estimator, and a credit calculator for the credit carried over from a vehicle you're replacing. The DOT notes the estimator doesn't work on mobile devices.

Other fees in the Code

  • Replacement plate or pair of plates: $5 (§ 321.42), or $40 for a special plate
  • Personalized plates: $25 to issue, plus $5 a year on top of the regular fee (§ 321.34)
  • Temporary 30-day permit for an unregistered or delinquent vehicle: $25 (§ 321.109)
  • Supplemental fee for a battery-electric vehicle: $130 a year, or $65 for a plug-in hybrid (§ 321.116)

Renewal timing and late penalties

Under § 321.40, you may renew from the first day of the month before your registration expires through the last day of the month after it expires. The treasurer sends a statement of fees due ahead of time. Starting on the first day of the second month of the registration year, § 321.134 adds a penalty of 5% of the annual fee, with a $5 minimum, and another 5% each month until you pay. If the last day of a month falls on a weekend or holiday, in-person payments to the treasurer are due the next business day.

If you'll be keeping a vehicle off the road, you can avoid those penalties by surrendering the plates to your county treasurer before they become delinquent. Later you can register again by paying that year's fee without penalty.

Proof of insurance

Iowa requires you to carry a proof-of-coverage card in the vehicle. If an officer removes your plates over a coverage violation, getting new ones from the treasurer costs a $15 administrative fee. See our Iowa insurance guide for details.
