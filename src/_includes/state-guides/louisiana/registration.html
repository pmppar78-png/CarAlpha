Who handles it

Titles and plates in Louisiana are issued by the Office of Motor Vehicles (OMV), part of the Department of Public Safety and Corrections. You can apply at an OMV office or a Public Tag Agent, or mail the paperwork to OMV headquarters at P.O. Box 64886, Baton Rouge, LA 70896. The OMV's general number is (225) 925-6146.

Titling a new vehicle

According to the OMV's new title and registration page, every new vehicle has to be titled with the OMV. You'll need:

  • A completed Vehicle Application, form DPSMV 1799
  • The Manufacturer's Statement of Origin, assigned from the dealer to you
  • The dealer's original itemized invoice, showing the vehicle's make, year, model and VIN, any trade-in, and the dealer's current permit number
  • A completed and signed odometer disclosure statement
  • A UCC-1 financing statement or other security agreement, original or copy, if a lien is being recorded

To get a plate at the same time, add a copy of each owner's current photo ID and proof of liability insurance. The insurance proof isn't needed when a licensed dealer submits the transfer.

Titling a used vehicle

For a used vehicle, the OMV's used-vehicle page asks for the current title, signed over to you before a notary, with any lien properly released. You'll also need an original notarized bill of sale or invoice showing the actual price and a full description of the vehicle. If the price is already written in the title assignment, you don't need a separate bill of sale. Other rules:

  • If the title has no odometer disclosure on the back and the vehicle isn't exempt, submit a separate odometer statement.
  • An authorized agent of a federally insured financial institution can witness the assignment instead of a notary, but only if the seller is present, a lien is recorded at the time of transfer, and the institution documents who its authorized officers are.
  • An Act of Donation must be notarized.
  • When the sale happens in a state that requires notarization, the invoice must be the notarized original. A certified true copy is accepted if the original is lost. A carbon copy is accepted from a state with no notary requirement.

Title and handling fees

The OMV's fee schedule lists:

  • Title: $68.50 (a title correction costs the same)
  • Recording a lien: $15 with a UCC-1 financing statement, or $10 with another kind of security agreement
  • Handling fee: $8
  • Plate transfer: $3
  • Local fee: up to $6, where it applies

Sales or use tax is charged according to where the vehicle is domiciled. It's based on the sale price minus any qualifying trade-in and rebate, and the OMV publishes a parish tax table with the local rates.

What a car plate costs

A passenger car plate is priced at 0.1% of the vehicle's value per year, with a minimum value of $10,000. Plates are sold two years at a time, so the smallest charge is $20. The OMV's table rises $2 for each $1,000 band of value: a car worth $25,000 pays $50 for two years, and one worth $30,000 pays $60. The published table stops at the $40,500–$41,499 band ($82). For higher values, work from the 0.1% rate the page states. Other common plates from the same schedule:

  • Motorcycle: $12 for four years
  • Motor home: $50 for two years
  • Trucks up to 6,000 pounds GVW: $40 for four years
  • Trucks from 6,001 to 10,000 pounds: $112 for four years
  • Utility trailer up to 500 pounds: $12 for four years
  • Boat trailer up to 1,500 pounds: $25 for four years

Specialty and personalized plate fees vary by plate. The OMV's Specialty Plates Viewer shows the options and the fees, and the Specialized Vehicle Unit answers questions at (225) 925-6278.

Renewing and pausing a registration

You can renew online with the Renewal Identification Number on your mailed notice, or with the details on your current registration certificate, and pay by Visa, MasterCard, Discover or American Express. If your notice has no renewal ID, you can't renew online and should call the OMV. Every vehicle you register must stay insured at the required limits.

If a car will sit unused and you want to drop its insurance, file a Statement of Non-Use before the policy ends. You'll need the plate number, registration expiration date, VIN and model year, and you can file online, by mail or in person. You keep the plate while the statement is in effect, and you won't get an insurance cancellation notice during that time. The statement can't be used to clear a lapse that has already happened.

Inspection stickers and emissions

Act 701 of 2026 (HB 1085) bars officers from writing citations for a missing inspection certificate from June 30, 2026 to January 1, 2027. After that, the inspection requirement ends for personal non-commercial vehicles in most parishes. In parishes covered by the federal inspection-and-maintenance program, the Act takes effect only if and when EPA approves the state's proposed plan amendments. Commercial vehicles and student transportation vehicles are still inspected.

According to the Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality, the emissions program covers Ascension, East Baton Rouge, Iberville, Livingston and West Baton Rouge parishes. It applies to gasoline vehicles with a GVWR of 10,000 pounds or less. Inspectors check for emissions tampering and test the gas cap, and model year 1996 and newer vehicles also get an OBD II check. The inspection fee in these parishes is $18, and it covers the combined safety and emissions inspection. LDEQ says a retest is free at the original station within 30 days of the temporary sticker; after that, or at a different station, the full $18 fee applies again, and a completed Repair Form is required first. A vehicle that fails gets an orange temporary sticker and must be repaired and pass a retest within 30 days.
