What Delaware requires

25/50/10 plus PIP 15/30: $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 bodily injury per accident, $10,000 property damage, and personal injury protection of $15,000 per person and $30,000 per accident.

The Department of Insurance's Instant Insurance Guide lists three coverages every registered vehicle in Delaware must carry. The first two are standard liability coverage for injuries and property damage you cause to others. The third, personal injury protection (PIP), pays medical bills and lost wages for you and your passengers whether or not you caused the crash, and it includes up to $5,000 for funeral expenses.

The DMV adds that only policies from companies authorized to write insurance in Delaware count. If a vehicle has a valid registration, liability coverage has to stay in force at all times, even if the car is parked and off the road.

Watch the PIP deductible

PIP pays first, but the deductible you choose can quietly cancel it out. In its motorcycle section, the Department warns that agents may quote the minimum $15,000 PIP limit with a $15,000 deductible, which leaves you essentially no PIP coverage unless you deliberately choose otherwise and pay more. Ask whether a quote's PIP is "restricted" or "unrestricted," and what the deductible is, before you compare prices. The Department also points out that cheaper motorcycle policies may pay medical and wage benefits only for crashes on a highway that involve actual contact with another vehicle.

Optional coverage worth understanding

  • Collision and comprehensive. Neither is required by law, but your lender will likely require both. The Department suggests collision may not be worth it on a car worth less than about $2,000, because the premiums can exceed anything you'd ever collect. Comprehensive covers fire, weather, vandalism, flood, theft, glass, and hitting a deer.
  • Uninsured motorist. This covers losses caused by an uninsured or hit-and-run driver. Property damage claims carry an automatic $250 deductible, regardless of your other deductibles. You must have made a police report to claim under it.
  • Underinsured motorist. This covers your injuries, not your car, when the at-fault driver's limits are too low.
  • Towing and rental reimbursement. These are cheap add-ons. If you belong to an auto club, you may already have towing.

How Delaware limits rating

A 2018 reform, House Substitute 1 for House Bill 80, restricts what insurers can use to price your policy. Credit scores can set the rate on a new policy, but they can't be used at renewal unless you ask, and then only if it lowers your rate. An insurer can't raise your rate just because you turn 75, or because your marital status changed when your spouse died. If every driver on a vehicle completes an approved defensive driving course, you get 10 percent off part of your premium for three years. A refresher course taken just before that period ends earns 15 percent.

After a crash, the final choice of repair shop is yours. If your shop's estimate is higher than the insurer's, though, you may owe the difference unless everyone agrees the extra cost is justified.

Penalties and the plate-surrender rule

According to the DMV's insurance FAQ, a conviction for driving without insurance carries a fine of at least $1,500 for a first offense and $3,000 for each repeat offense within three years. Your license or driving privileges are also suspended for six months.

Delaware also polices insurance lapses that never come up during a traffic stop. Insurers notify the DMV when a policy is cancelled, and registered vehicles are randomly checked for coverage. Before you cancel insurance on a vehicle with an active registration, you have to surrender the plates at a DMV office or by mail. If you skip that step and an audit finds you were uninsured, the penalty is $100 per vehicle for 1–30 days, plus $5 a day after that. If you ignore the audit, the DMV suspends the registration and every owner's license, and reinstatement costs $50 per registration on top of the lapse penalties. To answer an audit, ask your insurer to send Form FR-19 to deum@delaware.gov or fax it to 302-739-4750. Notices go to the address on file, so report a move promptly.

Average cost and uninsured drivers

In the NAIC average premium supplement, Delaware's 2024 combined average premium was $1,745.95, up from $1,346.83 in 2020 and above the $1,612.81 countrywide average. Its average expenditure per insured vehicle was $1,619.81. The Insurance Research Council's most recent estimate, published by the Insurance Information Institute, puts Delaware's uninsured rate at 17.6 percent, the 13th highest in the nation.

Getting help

Delaware Department of Insurance: 302-674-7300, online complaint form. If your auto policy is being cancelled or not renewed, you may be able to appeal to the Department under 18 Del. C. § 3906(a). If informal attempts don't resolve a dispute, the Department offers arbitration before a three-person panel (an attorney and two adjusters with no stake in the case) for a $50 filing fee. To report suspected insurance fraud, call (800) 632-5154 toll-free in Delaware or (302) 674-7350.
