EV rebates in Maine

Efficiency Maine, the state's energy efficiency trust, runs Maine's EV purchase rebates. Its rebates for low- and moderate-income Mainers are tied to an off-peak home charger. You have to buy the charger before the car, and the charger is meant to keep you from charging during weekday evening peak hours.

Rebate amounts

Efficiency Maine's current page lists these standard rebates:

  • Low-income households: $7,000 for a new EV, $3,000 for a used one
  • Moderate-income households: $5,000 new, $2,000 used

The Alternative Fuels Data Center's Maine listing also shows rebates for businesses ($3,000 new, $2,000 used) and for governments, Tribal governments and nonprofits (the same amounts). A March 31, 2026 Efficiency Maine announcement extended a temporary $1,000 boost through September 30, 2026, raising the low-income amounts to $8,000 new and $4,000 used. That promotion has now ended.

For a dealer purchase, the rebate comes off the price at the time of sale. If you buy directly from Polestar, Slate, Rivian or Tesla, you submit a claim form within 60 days of buying the car, and processing can take up to six weeks. AFDC notes that from 2026 to 2028, Efficiency Maine must direct at least half of its EV rebate funding to low- and moderate-income residents.

Who qualifies

  • Low income: a household member takes part in MaineCare, HEAP, SNAP or TANF.
  • Moderate income: adjusted gross income under $70,000 for an individual filer or $100,000 for joint filers.
  • You must be a Maine resident, at least 18, with a valid Maine driver's license (a temporary license works). You also need an income verification letter from Efficiency Maine before you buy, which takes about five business days.
  • You must be able to install a Wi-Fi-connected off-peak charger where the car is regularly parked, so street parking alone won't qualify. Efficiency Maine puts the average installation cost at $1,000, not counting the charger itself.
  • The limit is one rebate per person, and two per household, every 36 months.

Which vehicles count

Only all-electric vehicles qualify. Hybrids, including plug-in hybrids, don't. The car must be bought, not leased, from a participating dealer or one of the four direct-sale manufacturers, and titled in the rebate recipient's name. A new vehicle must be on the eligible list, with a sticker price before destination charges of no more than $80,000 for pickups and commercial vans or $55,000 for everything else. A used EV must cost no more than $50,000, be no more than six years old (model year 2020 or newer in 2026) and have no more than 72,000 miles.

After you buy

Within 90 days of the purchase, install the charger at home and connect it to the internet. Avoid charging from 5 to 9 p.m. on weekdays, and keep the car owned and registered in Maine for at least 36 months. According to AFDC, Efficiency Maine's Emporia off-peak chargers come with a $200 point-of-sale discount plus another $200 once set-up is done. They pause charging automatically during peak hours. You can override the pause one day at a time, but you can't turn it off for good.

Other Maine EV laws

  • Condos and associations: Since January 1, 2026, under 33 M.R.S. § 576-A, condominium and residential associations can't ban or restrict EV chargers in designated parking spaces. Unit owners pay installation and electricity costs, carry insurance, maintain the charger, and must disclose it to buyers.
  • Charging sellers: Selling electricity only to charge EVs doesn't make a business a regulated electricity provider. A station operator can bill a submetered user only for kilowatt-hours used.
  • Utility rates: A 2021 law requires electric utilities to design rates that encourage the use of EV chargers and to file proposals with the Public Utilities Commission.
  • Vehicle standards: Maine has adopted California's motor vehicle emissions standards, including the zero-emission vehicle sales requirements for manufacturers, under DEP rules Chapter 127.

The Maine Department of Transportation filed yearly NEVI charging plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026, and Efficiency Maine's Public EV Charging Initiatives page tracks that work.

Federal credits

Federal clean vehicle credits for new, used and commercial EVs don't apply to vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. The IRS has the official details.
