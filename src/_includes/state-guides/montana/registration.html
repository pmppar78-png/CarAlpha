Who registers and where

Montana vehicles are registered through the Motor Vehicle Division of the Department of Justice, and renewals are handled by the County Treasurer's office in the county where you live. New residents have 60 days after establishing residency to title and register their vehicles in Montana. A nonresident who takes paid work in Montana must show the treasurer the vehicle's current out-of-state registration and pay the Montana fees and taxes. That person can register by calendar quarter, and the vehicle displays plates or decals from both states.

Your renewal month is set when the vehicle is first titled and registered in Montana, and it's shown on your plates. If you move to another county, you can keep using your plates until the next renewal and update your address then through MVD Online Services.

Renewal options

Owners of light vehicles, meaning passenger cars, pickups rated at one ton or less, vans and SUVs, can choose among three options:

  • 12 months: one year of the registration rate, county option tax, other fees and any special plate fees.
  • 24 months: the registration rate, county option tax, other fees and any special plate fees for the full two-year period.
  • Permanent: for vehicles 11 or more years old. Under § 61-3-562 you pay an $87.50 registration fee, five times the county option tax or flat fee, and other applicable fees, and you don't renew while you own the vehicle. Permanent registration ends when the vehicle is sold. National guard, reserve armed forces and amateur radio plates can't be used for it.

The registration rate

Under § 61-3-321(2), the annual rate for a light vehicle depends on its age, found by subtracting the model year from the year the fee is due:

  • 0 to 4 years old: $217
  • 5 to 10 years old: $87
  • 11 or more years old: $28

The MVD's example is a 2011 car registered in 2018. It's 7 years old, so the rate is $87, plus a 3% administrative fee. A light vehicle 10 years old or newer with a manufacturer's suggested retail price over $150,000 pays an extra $825 a year.

County option tax

Counties may add a local option motor vehicle tax of up to 0.7% of the vehicle's depreciated MSRP, or a flat fee, if voters approve it (§ 61-3-537). Depreciation follows a table in § 61-3-503 that differs by body type. A new-model-year vehicle registered the year before its model year is valued at 100%. A two-year-old automobile is valued at 69% of MSRP, compared with 86% for a truck, 78% for a van and 90% for an SUV. For a two-year-old car with a $40,000 MSRP, the value is $27,600, and the tax at the full 0.7% rate would be $193.20. The tax is split between the county and its cities and towns.

Other fees on a renewal

  • State parks and fishing access: $9 per light vehicle. You can opt out if you certify that you won't use the vehicle to visit state parks or fishing access sites.
  • Additional fee: $10 per vehicle under § 61-3-321(20).
  • Optional $5: you can choose to pay an extra $5, which goes to the Department of Transportation.
  • Donations: $1 goes to the traumatic brain injury program unless you opt out. You can also give to organ donor or cancer screening funds.
  • Plates: $12 for a new standard set, or $16 more to keep a plate number from before 2010 when the plates must be replaced.

The MVD Online Services site has a fee estimator. Credit isn't given for taxes or fees already paid, and only U.S. funds are accepted.

Title

Under § 61-3-203, the certificate of title fee is $12 for a light vehicle or a truck or bus of one ton or less, and $10 for other vehicles. On July 1, 2028, those fees drop to $7 and $5.

Electric and plug-in hybrid fees

Electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles pay an extra annual fee on top of these charges under § 61-3-572. For a vehicle under 6,000 pounds unladen, that's $130 for an EV and $70 for a plug-in hybrid. A permanently registered class 1 vehicle pays $260 or $140 once. The EV guide for Montana covers the weight classes in more detail.

Tax exemptions

Vehicles owned by governments, qualifying disabled veterans, Purple Heart recipients, former prisoners of war, tribal members who live on their reservation, nonresident active military members and some organizations may be exempt from vehicle taxes. Charities, schools, hospitals and other organizations need a determination letter from the Department of Revenue, and their applications are due March 1 each year. Bring the documents to the County Treasurer when you title, register or renew.
