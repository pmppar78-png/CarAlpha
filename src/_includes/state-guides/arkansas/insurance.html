Required coverage in Arkansas

Arkansas requires every vehicle on its roads to carry liability insurance or a certificate of self-insurance (Ark. Code Ann. § 27-22-104). The minimums are:

25/50/25: $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 bodily injury per accident, and $25,000 property damage.

Arkansas is an at-fault state, so the driver who causes a crash pays through their liability coverage. It isn't a no-fault state, but every private-passenger policy sold here includes some no-fault first-party benefits unless you turn them down in writing (§ 23-89-203). Medical bills from a crash you didn't cause go to the other driver's insurer first.

PIP and uninsured motorist coverage

Personal injury protection. Under § 23-89-202, Arkansas policies include no-fault benefits for you, family members in your household, your passengers, and pedestrians your car hits:

  • medical and hospital expenses incurred within 24 months of the crash, up to $5,000 per person;
  • 70 percent of lost income, starting eight days after the crash, for up to 52 weeks and capped at $140 a week. A person without income gets up to $70 a week for replacement services; and
  • a $5,000 accidental death benefit if the crash causes death within a year.

Uninsured motorist coverage. Every Arkansas liability policy has to include uninsured motorist bodily-injury coverage unless a named insured rejects it in writing. That rejection stays in effect until you withdraw it in writing (§ 23-89-403). If you buy higher liability limits, the agent has to offer UM up to those limits, and turning that offer down also takes a written rejection. Once you've rejected UM, your insurer doesn't have to remind you at renewal, so check your declarations page.

How Arkansas checks for coverage

Insurers report policies to the Arkansas Online Insurance Verification System, AOIVS, and the DFA checks registered vehicles against it. If AOIVS can't confirm coverage, you get one notice. No follow-up or clearance letter is sent. Common reasons for a false alarm are a policy written for another state, a fleet policy the insurer didn't report, a VIN that doesn't match between the registration and the policy, or a brand-new policy that hasn't been reported yet. If you have coverage, your agent or insurer can send it to AOIVS electronically using the reference number on your letter. If the car was sold, stored, or inoperable, you can report that yourself on the site.

Ignore the notice past its due date and the registration is suspended, and you'll owe $100 to reinstate it. Turning in the plate doesn't avoid the fee unless you can show continuous coverage from the letter date. For help, call the Motor Vehicle help desk at 1-888-682-0005 or email MV.OnlineInsurance@dfa.arkansas.gov.

Penalties for driving uninsured

Under § 27-22-103, driving without coverage brings a mandatory fine:

  • First offense: $100 to $250.
  • Second offense: $250 to $500. The minimum is mandatory.
  • Third or later offense: $500 to $1,000, up to a year in jail, or both.

If you can show the judge that coverage was in effect when you were stopped, the charge can be dismissed. If you can't show coverage by the time the case is decided, the court sends an order to the Office of Motor Vehicle within five business days, suspending the vehicle's registration until you prove coverage. Reinstating after a court suspension costs $20, on top of the fine.

After an accident: Safety Responsibility and SR-22

Under the Motor Vehicle Safety Responsibility Act, you have to file an SR-1 accident report with the DFA's Office of Driver Services within 30 days of any crash that injures or kills someone, or damages one person's property by more than $1,000, no matter who was at fault. If you can't show insurance for that crash, the department can suspend your license and registration until you do one of the following:

  • deposit security in an amount the department sets;
  • file a written release of liability signed by the other party; or
  • win a hearing showing there's no reasonable possibility of a judgment against you. You have to request the hearing in writing within 20 days of receiving the notice.

If a court judgment from a crash goes unpaid, the suspension lasts until it's satisfied or stayed. You'll also have to file proof of future financial responsibility, the SR-22, through your insurer (Ark. Code § 27-19-707). Office of Driver Services: 501-682-7100.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 average combined premium for Arkansas is $1,465.02. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 12.1 percent of Arkansas drivers were uninsured in 2023, ranking the state 26th of 51 jurisdictions. That's roughly one driver in eight, so uninsured motorist coverage is worth pricing.

Complaints

The Arkansas Insurance Department's Consumer Services Division handles complaints about insurers, agents, and adjusters. It investigates each one with the company and the consumer. Call 501-371-2640 or 1-800-852-5494, or file online.
