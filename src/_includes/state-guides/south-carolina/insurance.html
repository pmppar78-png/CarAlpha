South Carolina's minimum liability coverage

Section 38-77-140 of the South Carolina Code says an auto policy can't be issued for a vehicle principally garaged or used in the state unless it covers liability arising from the vehicle's ownership, maintenance or use in the United States or Canada, with these limits for each vehicle:

CoverageMinimum
Bodily injury, one person, one accident$25,000
Bodily injury, two or more people, one accident$50,000
Damage to others' property, one accident$25,000

Insurers may sell liability coverage above these amounts.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Every South Carolina auto policy must include uninsured motorist coverage (§ 38-77-150). It pays what you're legally entitled to recover from the owner or driver of an uninsured vehicle, at limits no lower than the liability minimums above. It must also cover at least $25,000 of damage to your own property in one accident, though the policy may exclude the first $200 of the loss.

Under § 38-77-160, insurers must also offer you, at your option, extra uninsured motorist coverage and underinsured motorist coverage, each up to your liability limits. Underinsured coverage applies when your damages exceed the at-fault driver's liability limits or a damages cap set by law. If you carry more than the basic limits, you're protected to the extent of the coverage on the vehicle involved in the accident. If none of your vehicles is involved, coverage is available up to the amount on any one vehicle that carries it.

Insurance and your license and registration

When you apply for or renew a South Carolina driver's license, you must certify that you're covered by an auto liability policy from a company licensed in the state, according to the SCDMV. If you don't own a vehicle and none is in your household, you mark that on the application form.

You must give the name of your insurer the first time you register a vehicle in South Carolina and at every renewal. The state checks coverage electronically through the Automobile Liability Insurance Reporting (ALIR) system, with insurers sending policy changes to the SCDMV. If ALIR can't verify your insurance, your registration and license can be suspended. New residents with a national insurer should update their address with the company before registering, and the insurer must be licensed in South Carolina.

The option to register a vehicle without insurance by paying an uninsured motor vehicle fee is gone. Section 56-10-510 was repealed and marked "Reserved" by 2023 Act No. 51, effective July 1, 2024.

If your coverage lapses

Under § 56-10-240, if a vehicle becomes uninsured during its registration period, the owner must get new insurance right away or surrender the plate and registration. When the SCDMV learns a policy was canceled, it suspends the owner's driving privileges, plate and registration. An owner who had coverage during that time can submit documents showing it, and the suspension is voided.

The SCDMV says you'll first get a letter giving you 20 business days to have your insurer verify coverage electronically. If verification doesn't arrive, your license, plate and registration for the vehicles on the policy are suspended, and reinstatement can cost up to $400. Turning in your plate and registration at an SCDMV branch before canceling insurance avoids the penalty.

Proof of insurance during a stop

According to the SCDMV, if you can't prove you have insurance when stopped by an officer, you may get a ticket, pay a fine or be jailed. To avoid a license suspension, you must show within 30 days of the ticket that the vehicle you were driving was insured. A suspended license carries a $100 reinstatement fee.

Penalties for driving uninsured

Section 56-10-520 makes it a misdemeanor for an owner to drive, or allow someone to drive, an uninsured vehicle, and for anyone to drive a vehicle they know is uninsured:

ConvictionPenalty
First$100 to $200 fine or 30 days in jail
Second$200 fine or 30 days in jail, or both
Third or later45 days to six months in jail

Prior convictions count if they fall within five years. The SCDMV suspends an owner's license, registrations and plates until a reinstatement fee is paid. The statute sets that fee at $600, adjusted each year by order of the Department of Insurance, and the SCDMV's page lists a $700 uninsured motorist fee. An owner must also have the insurer file an SR-22 certificate for three years from the date of suspension. A driver who doesn't own the vehicle has their license suspended for 30 days and, per the SCDMV, pays a $100 reinstatement fee.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in South Carolina was $1,664.69 (Table 5 of the Auto Insurance Database Average Premium Supplement). The Insurance Research Council estimated that 10.3% of South Carolina drivers were uninsured, which ranked the state 39th.
