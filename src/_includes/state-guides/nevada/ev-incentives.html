EV incentives and rules in Nevada

None of the Nevada entries in the U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center list is a rebate or tax credit for an individual buying an electric car. The list does include an emissions testing exemption, a weight allowance, parking enforcement at chargers, state grant programs, and the clean car standards that shape what dealers stock. This page summarizes those entries.

Emissions testing exemption

Under NRS 445B.770, 445B.815 and 445B.825, as summarized by the AFDC, alternative fuel vehicles are exempt from Nevada's emissions testing requirements. That covers battery EVs. A new hybrid is exempt for its first five model years and then has to be tested every year. In Clark and Washoe counties, where testing applies, this means an all-electric car skips the annual smog check that a comparable gasoline car needs from its fourth registration.

Charging spaces

NRS 484B.468 bars parking in a space designated for EV charging unless the vehicle is an EV that is actively charging. The AFDC lists fines of up to $750. Under NRS 704.021, someone who owns or runs a facility that sells electricity for EV charging isn't treated as a public utility, so businesses and property owners can host chargers without utility regulation.

Clean Cars Nevada

Nevada adopted California's vehicle emissions rules in Title 13 of the California Code of Regulations. Starting with model year 2025, manufacturers have to meet the greenhouse gas standard and zero-emission vehicle production and sales requirements for passenger cars, light-duty trucks and medium-duty vehicles. The AFDC cites NAC 445B.2 through 445B.36 and regulation R093-20 for this program. Nevada also signed the multi-state memorandum on medium- and heavy-duty zero-emission vehicles, whose task force released an action plan in March 2022.

State grant programs

  • Nevada Clean Energy Fund. The Office of Energy administers the fund under NRS 701B.930 to 701B.995 for clean energy projects, including alternative fuel vehicles and their infrastructure. Projects that burn fossil fuels aren't eligible.
  • Clean Trucks and Buses Incentive Program. Under NRS 445B.900 to 445B.936, the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection offers incentives for zero-emission medium- and heavy-duty trucks and buses. Eligible applicants include local governments, state fleets, school districts, nonprofits, commercial fleets and independent truck operators. Amounts are based on gross vehicle weight rating, with higher amounts for underserved communities.
  • Diesel Emission Mitigation Fund. NDEP uses Nevada's share of the Volkswagen settlement to help public and private fleets replace or repower model year 2009 and older medium- and heavy-duty diesels. Amounts vary by vehicle, applicant and fuel.

Charging network planning

Under the federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Formula Program, NDOT submitted annual EV infrastructure deployment plans for fiscal years 2022 through 2026. NDOT's Alternative Fueling Infrastructure Plan website has the current plan.

Other rules that touch EVs

NRS 484D.635 lets EVs and natural gas vehicles exceed the maximum gross weight limit for comparable conventional vehicles by up to 2,000 pounds. NRS 482.36349 lets a manufacturer sell directly to buyers, without franchised dealers, if it builds only all-electric passenger cars, sells only cars it makes, and was selling in Nevada by January 1, 2016.

The DMV's fee page lists the $33 passenger car registration fee and the Governmental Services Tax without a separate EV charge. Federal tax credits change with federal law, so check the IRS website for the current rules before relying on one.
