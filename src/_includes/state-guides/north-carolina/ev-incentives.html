Purchase incentives

The U.S. Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) lists no North Carolina rebate or tax credit for buying or leasing a passenger EV. The state incentives it does list are aimed at charging equipment, fleets and inspections, and they're covered below.

Charging station grants

The Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) runs Level 2 and DC fast charging infrastructure programs funded by North Carolina's share of the Volkswagen Environmental Mitigation Trust. According to the AFDC, they help pay to buy and install public and private Level 2 stations and public fast chargers. Funding amounts and availability are posted on DEQ's Volkswagen Settlement website. DEQ's Diesel Bus and Vehicle Programs separately fund heavy-duty replacements and repowers, including alternative fuel vehicles, for public and private fleet owners.

Under N.C.G.S. § 62-3.23, a person who resells electricity through charging equipment to fuel EVs isn't treated as a public utility, subject to conditions. Section 136-18.02 lets NCDOT install and operate public charging stations at highway rest areas if it has a way to charge users for the electricity and operating costs.

Emissions inspection exemption

Plug-in electric vehicles and fuel cell electric vehicles are exempt from the emissions inspection under § 20-183.2(b). They still need the annual safety inspection that applies statewide. State law defines a plug-in EV as one that can't run on gasoline, draws on a battery of at least 4 kilowatt-hours that can be charged from an outside source, keeps its original powertrain, weighs 8,500 pounds GVWR or less, reaches at least 65 mph and meets federal safety standards.

Annual EV fees

Section 20-87 adds a charge at every initial registration and renewal: $214.50 for a plug-in vehicle with no nonelectric power source (low-speed vehicles excluded) and $107.25 for a plug-in hybrid. These are on top of the regular $46.25 passenger fee.

Dealer rules

Section 20-305 bars manufacturers from requiring or coercing dealers to buy or lease charging stations unless the dealer sells that manufacturer's EVs. Dealers don't have to offer public charging. The AFDC also notes that manufacturers can't prevent rural and other franchised dealers from obtaining, selling or leasing electric or hydrogen vehicles.

State goals and plans

Executive Order 246 (2022) set goals of at least 1,250,000 registered zero-emission vehicles by 2030 and a 50% ZEV share of new passenger vehicle sales by 2030. It also targets greenhouse gas emissions at least 50% below 2005 levels by 2030 and net zero by 2050. NCDOT was directed to write a Clean Transportation Plan and a ZEV infrastructure needs assessment. It reports EV and hybrid registrations on its performance dashboard. State agencies have to prioritize ZEVs when they buy or lease new vehicles.

NCDOT also prepared the state's plans for the federal National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) formula program for fiscal years 2022 through 2026.
