The 10-day deadline

Florida gives you less time than you might expect. According to FLHSMV's new-resident page, you need Florida insurance, a Florida title and Florida registration within 10 days of establishing residency. You also need a Florida driver license within 30 days. The state considers you a resident once any of these is true:

  • You've started a job or begun working in a trade or profession in Florida.
  • You've enrolled your children in a Florida public school.
  • You've registered to vote in Florida or filed for a homestead exemption.
  • You've lived in the state for more than six consecutive months.

The job and school triggers apply even if you consider yourself a nonresident. In either case, the car must be registered with a Florida plate and a Florida insurance policy within 10 days.

Where to go

County tax collectors handle registration and plates, along with motor vehicle service centers. Your first Florida registration must be done in person when you title the vehicle. Many tax collector offices also charge a $6.25 service fee on driver license transactions, so expect local add-ons beyond the state fees.

What to bring

If you own the car outright, bring:

  • Proof of identity for every owner.
  • Proof of Florida insurance (PIP and property damage liability).
  • Your original out-of-state title.
  • A completed Application for Certificate of Title With/Without Registration (HSMV 82040), signed by all owners.
  • A VIN verification, recorded on form 82040 or on form HSMV 82042.

The VIN check is a physical inspection of the vehicle identification number. It isn't a safety or emissions test, and Florida doesn't require either of those. A law enforcement officer, a military police officer, a licensed Florida dealer, or a tax collector employee or state compliance examiner at a service center can do it.

If you still owe money on the car

Ask your out-of-state lienholder to transfer the title to Florida. If it agrees, complete form 82040 at a service center. If it refuses, get the refusal in writing on the lender's letterhead and bring it with you. Either way, you still have to register the car. Florida doesn't let you skip registration because a lender is holding the title.

What it costs

The figures below come from FLHSMV's fee schedule. The registration amounts are the base vehicle tax only. Other statutory fees are added, so your actual bill will be higher.

  • Initial registration fee: $225. You pay this when you don't already have a Florida plate, or a record of one, that you can transfer from a car you previously owned.
  • Base annual tax for cars by weight: $14.50 under 2,500 pounds, $22.50 from 2,500 to 3,499 pounds, and $32.50 at 3,500 pounds or more. Pickups up to 5,000 pounds follow a similar scale, and heavier trucks start at $60.75.
  • Plate: $28 for an original or replacement.
  • Title: $77.25 for a new vehicle, $85.25 for a used vehicle, and $75.25 for a transfer or duplicate. These are electronic titles. A paper title costs $2.50 more, and recording a lien adds $2. Fast title service costs $10, and a late title fee is $20.
  • Leased cars are taxed as for-hire vehicles: $17 plus $1.50 per 100 pounds. That makes them more expensive to register and renew.

Use tax on a car you bring with you

According to the Department of Revenue's new-resident guide, Florida's 6% use tax, plus any county surtax, applies to a vehicle brought into the state within six months of purchase. If you paid at least 6% sales tax in another state or territory, you owe nothing more. If you paid less, you owe the difference. For example, if you paid 4% elsewhere, you'd owe 2%. A vehicle used in another state for six months or longer is presumed not to have been bought for Florida, but you must document that period. If you import a vehicle from another country, you owe the full tax on its current value, and tax paid abroad doesn't count.

Renewals and late fees

Florida registrations expire at midnight on the first owner's birthday, unless a business owns the vehicle. You can renew for one or two years, up to three months early, through the MyDMV Portal ($2 processing fee) or the MyFlorida app ($4 by card or $3.75 by checking account, for up to five vehicles at once). If the state can't verify your insurance electronically, it won't issue a renewal. Mailed registrations usually arrive in 7 to 10 business days, and the emailed receipt works as proof until then.

A delinquent fee applies starting on the 11th day of the month after your renewal was due. It's tied to your license tax: $5 if the tax is up to $25, $10 up to $50, $15 up to $100, $50 up to $400, $100 up to $600, and $250 above that.

Displaying your registration

A complete registration includes the metal plate, which must match the registration and decal, the registration certificate, which must be in the car, and the validation decal, which goes in the upper right corner of the plate. Before you cancel your insurance or move away, turn your plate in at a tax collector's office or service center. Otherwise, you can face a suspension.
