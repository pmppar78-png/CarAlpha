Titling and registering in West Virginia

The Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV) says every motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer, pole trailer and recreational vehicle driven or moved on a West Virginia highway must be titled and registered. ATVs titled since July 1, 1989, and UTVs since December 28, 2010, must be titled but don't have to be registered.

Under W. Va. Code § 17A-3-1, driving an unregistered or untitled vehicle, or one whose fees haven't been paid, is a misdemeanor with a fine of up to $500. A second or later conviction can also bring up to six months in jail. If you buy a vehicle from someone other than a registered dealer, you can drive it under the previous owner's registration for up to ten days, as long as you carry and show their written consent.

New residents

New residents must title and register their vehicles within 30 days of establishing residency, according to the DMV. The fees are a $15 title fee, a $10 lien fee if there's a lien, and the $51.50 regular registration fee.

The DMV says the 6 percent titling tax doesn't apply to a new resident's vehicle already titled in their name in another state, as long as all of these are true:

  • You weren't a West Virginia resident when you bought or acquired the vehicle.
  • You can show it was titled in your previous state of residence, typically with the out-of-state title in your name at an out-of-state address.
  • You can show bona fide West Virginia residency, such as a new West Virginia driver's license.
  • You apply and pay all fees within 30 days of establishing residency.

The exemption doesn't cover leased vehicles. You'll need form DMV-1-TR, an odometer disclosure, and insurance from a company licensed to sell in West Virginia. If a lienholder holds your title, include a copy of it and a letter from the lienholder.

Registration fees

Section 17A-10-1 sorts vehicles into classes. Class A covers passenger vehicles and trucks with a gross weight of 10,000 pounds or less, and Class G covers motorcycles. Under § 17A-10-3, the base Class A fee is $50 a year and the Class G fee is $8. The DMV may adjust the Class A fee every five years on September 1 based on the Consumer Price Index, with no increase above 10 percent in a single year. The DMV lists the regular registration fee as $51.50. Class A vehicles on the optional biennial staggered system pay twice the annual fees every two years.

Heavier trucks in Class B pay by declared gross weight:

  • 10,001 to 16,000 pounds: $28 plus $5 per 1,000 pounds over 10,000
  • Over 16,000 and under 55,000 pounds: $78.50 plus $10 per 1,000 pounds over 16,000
  • 55,000 pounds or more: $737.50 plus $15.75 per 1,000 pounds over 55,000

Class C trailers pay $50, and the registration lasts for as long as you own the trailer. Moving a registration from one vehicle to another of the same class costs $10 under § 17A-10-10.

Title fees and the titling tax

The DMV lists the following standard fees for a new vehicle:

  • A $15 title fee
  • A 6 percent titling tax when the net purchase price is over $500, or a flat $30 under $500
  • A $10 lien recording fee, if there's a lien
  • Registration or transfer fees

A duplicate title is also $15. Under W. Va. Code § 11-15-3c, the motor vehicle sales tax rate rose to 6 percent for purchases on or after July 1, 2017.

When you trade in a vehicle, the tax is calculated on the net price after the trade-in. To qualify, the trade-in has to be titled in West Virginia in your name. Manufacturer rebates are taxable. In-state dealers collect and send the title taxes and fees to the DMV. For a private sale below 50 percent of the NADA Clean Loan value, you need a notarized bill of sale signed by both parties, or the tax is charged on the full book value. A vehicle two years old or newer bought from someone other than a dealer is taxed on its book value regardless of the price. Gifts between parent and child or between spouses of a vehicle titled in West Virginia don't need a gift affidavit.

Safety inspection

Under § 17C-16-4, the State Police superintendent requires every motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer and pole trailer registered in West Virginia to be inspected once every two years. Commercial vehicles subject to federal motor carrier rules are inspected at least once a year. Under § 17C-16-6, a station may charge up to $19 for the inspection, any needed headlight adjustment and the certificate, not including replacement parts. The State Police charge stations $6 per sticker. Driving without a current certificate, or not producing one on demand, is a misdemeanor with a fine of up to $100 under § 17C-16-9. You aren't guilty if you get the vehicle inspected and show the receipt to the court within five days of the citation, unless the certificate had been expired for more than three months.

The DMV may suspend the registration of a vehicle the superintendent finds unsafe, or one whose owner hasn't gotten the required certificate.
