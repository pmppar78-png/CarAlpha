When you have to register

Texas Transportation Code § 502.040 gives you 30 days after buying a vehicle or becoming a Texas resident to apply for registration. You apply through the tax assessor-collector of the county where you live, or through any county assessor-collector willing to accept the application, and you bring personal identification as set by Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV) rules.

Title comes with it. Under § 501.022, the owner of a vehicle registered in Texas must apply for a title and may not drive on a public highway until the title and registration applications are filed. When you buy a used vehicle, § 501.145 gives you until the later of 30 days after the assignment date or the sales-tax deadline under Tax Code § 152.069 to file the title documents with the county. Members of the armed forces and of the National Guard or reserves serving on federal active duty get 60 days. When a licensed dealer sells you a car, § 501.0234 makes the dealer responsible for applying for title and registration in your name and remitting the sales tax.

What the county needs

  • A completed registration application, filed as § 502.043 provides
  • Personal identification under TxDMV rules
  • Evidence of financial responsibility under § 502.046, such as an insurance card, a photocopy or an electronic submission
  • The title or other evidence of ownership for a title transfer
  • A passing emissions inspection if your county is an emissions county and your vehicle is subject to testing

The county can't register the vehicle without proof of insurance. It examines the document and hands it back unless you gave a photocopy or an electronic submission.

Registration fees

Section 502.252 sets the annual registration fee at $50.75 for a vehicle with a gross weight of 6,000 pounds or less, unless another provision of Chapter 502 sets a different fee. Several other charges are added at registration:

  • A $1 financial responsibility program fee under § 502.357, part of which funds TexasSure
  • A registration processing and handling fee set by TxDMV board rule under § 502.1911
  • An optional county road and bridge fee of up to $10 under § 502.401
  • An optional county child safety fee of up to $1.50 under § 502.403
  • In certain border counties and counties coterminous with a regional mobility authority, an optional transportation project fee of up to $10, or up to $20 with voter approval in some counties, under § 502.402

Section 502.190 requires TxDMV to post a complete fee schedule online, so check it, or your county tax office, for the total in your county.

Inspections and the replacement fee

House Bill 3297 ended safety inspections for non-commercial vehicles on January 1, 2025. In their place, § 548.510 charges an inspection program replacement fee at initial registration and each renewal:

  • $7.50 a year for a motor vehicle, trailer, semitrailer, pole trailer or mobile home
  • $16.75 once for a new passenger car or light truck registered for an initial two-year period, with no $7.50 fee due the next year
  • $22.25 once for a passenger car or light truck registered under § 502.0026, with no fee due for the next two registration years

The Department of Public Safety says commercial vehicles still need a passing safety inspection in every county and don't pay the replacement fee.

Emissions testing still applies in the counties DPS lists: Brazoria, Collin, Dallas, Denton, El Paso, Ellis, Fort Bend, Galveston, Harris, Johnson, Kaufman, Montgomery, Parker, Rockwall, Tarrant, Travis and Williamson. Bexar County joins them on November 1, 2026. Gasoline vehicles from 2 through 24 years old registered there get an on-board diagnostic (OBDII) test, and electric-only vehicles are exempt. A TxDMV bulletin sets the Bexar test at $18.50 paid to the station plus $2.75 paid to the state at registration, and says the test must fall within the 90-day window before the registration expires.

Section 548.3011 adds a resale rule: a vehicle last titled or registered in a county without an emissions program, then sold at retail, must pass an emissions test within 90 days before it can be titled or registered in an emissions county. A 1996 or newer model with fewer than 50,000 miles is exempt from that rule.

Title fee and motor vehicle tax

Under § 501.138, the title fee is $33 if you live in a county inside a federal Clean Air Act nonattainment area or an affected county under Health and Safety Code § 386.001, and $28 in any other county.

Tax Code § 152.021 imposes a 6.25% motor vehicle sales tax on the total consideration for a retail sale in Texas, and § 152.022 applies the same 6.25% use tax to a vehicle a Texas resident buys out of state. Under § 152.002, the value of a trade-in isn't part of the taxable total. A new resident who brings in a vehicle already registered in their name in another state pays a $90 use tax under § 152.023 instead.

Renewal and penalties

Registration runs for 12 consecutive months under § 502.044. A new passenger car or light truck of the current or preceding model year that's sold in Texas and has never been registered gets a 24-month period instead. You can renew online when you're otherwise eligible, under § 502.0435, and the registration sticker goes on the inside of the windshield in the lower left corner under § 502.059.

Driving with an expired registration is an offense under § 502.407 once more than five working days have passed since it expired. A court can dismiss the charge if you fix it by the 20th working day after the offense or before your first court date, whichever is later, and can charge a reimbursement fee of up to $20. If you're cited for a registration violation and then register, § 502.045 adds a charge of 20% of the prescribed fee.
