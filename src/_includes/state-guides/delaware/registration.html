Moving to Delaware: the 60-day deadline

According to the DMV's Guidelines for New Residents (revised February 2026), you have 60 days after becoming a resident to title and register your vehicles. The same 60-day deadline applies to getting a Delaware driver license. Address changes have to be reported to the DMV within 30 days. Active-duty military members and their dependents don't need a Delaware license or registration until 60 days after they leave the service.

Delaware has four full-service DMV offices: Greater Wilmington (2230 Hessler Blvd, New Castle), Delaware City (2101 Mid County Dr, New Castle), Dover (303 Transportation Circle), and Georgetown (23737 DuPont Blvd). They're open 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday, and 11 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. on Wednesday. The DMV recommends arriving at least an hour before closing.

Three steps: insurance, inspection, title

1. Proof of insurance. Bring an insurance ID card that shows the coverage period and your insurer's NAIC code. A digital card in the insurer's app is accepted, but photocopies, photos, and printouts are not. You can also use a policy, a binder on the insurer's letterhead within 30 days of issuance, or a fax sent directly by the insurer. The card has to be valid on the day you visit.

2. Inspection. Every vehicle with an out-of-state title goes through a DMV inspection lane. Depending on its model year and weight, it gets either a full inspection or a VIN check, and every vehicle gets a safety inspection. Common reasons for failing include burned-out or misaimed lights, tires with less than 2/32 inch of tread, windshield cracks that block your view, any window crack longer than 5 inches, exhaust leaks, a missing catalytic converter, tinted front side windows or windshield without a waiver, and a bumper lower than 22 inches. If you fail, you get a report listing what to fix, and the retest is free.

3. Title application. The inspection associate gives you Form MV-212 (Application for Certificate of Title) and an MV-210 inspection report. Every owner must sign the MV-212. A power of attorney has to be an original and notarized. Surrender your out-of-state title. If a lender holds the title, the DMV gives you Form MV-35 to send to the lienholder. Applicants under 18 need a parent or guardian to sign.

Cars bought out of state follow the same inspection-lane process. For a new car, the dealer can verify the VIN and give you the MV-212. Bring the certificate of origin or the title with an odometer disclosure, plus the dealer's bill of sale. Check whether the state where you bought the car requires notarized title documents. If the out-of-state registration has expired, the DMV can issue a $20 five-day temporary tag so you can drive the car into Delaware.

What it costs

  • Registration: $40 a year for a passenger vehicle. A new current-model-year vehicle of 10,000 pounds or less can be registered for up to seven years at once.
  • Title: $35, or $55 if there's a lien.
  • Document fee: 5.25 percent of the price or value, with an $8 minimum.
  • Alternative-fuel surcharge (since October 1, 2025, for vehicles of 6,000 lb GVWR or less): $110 a year for a battery-electric vehicle, $85 for a plug-in hybrid, $60 for a conventional hybrid, and $110 for hydrogen or natural gas. Heavier vehicles pay more.
  • Late renewal: $20. A late title transfer is $35.
  • Duplicates: $10 for a plate with a sticker, $50 for a title, and $10 for a registration card.
  • Vanity plate: $40 a year.

Delaware doesn't have a general sales tax. The one-time document fee on a vehicle is the closest thing to one. For a new resident, it's based on the vehicle's value, using the NADA guide. It's waived if you can show you paid sales tax or a similar levy to your previous state and titled and registered the car there within the 90 days before registering in Delaware. On a used car without a recorded price, the DMV also uses the NADA trade-in value, but you can submit an appraisal from a licensed Delaware dealer instead.

Renewals and inspections

The DMV sends renewal reminders 90, 60, and 30 days before your registration expires if it has your email address or phone number on file. You can renew up to 90 days early without losing any time. According to the registration page, vehicles in the six newest model years that weigh less than 10,000 pounds can renew by mail without an inspection. A six-year-old vehicle gets a one-year renewal without inspection. Older vehicles get two-year renewals after passing the safety and emissions inspection.

The inspection program FAQ says the DMV runs 21 test lanes at its four offices, and inspections are free for Delaware residents. Emissions testing covers cars and trucks with a gross vehicle weight rating of 8,500 pounds or less. Motorcycles, vehicles built before 1968, and diesel vehicles built before 1997 are exempt. If a car keeps failing the exhaust test after repairs, the DMV may grant a waiver once repair costs pass a set limit, which is $200 for 1981-and-newer vehicles in Sussex County. Waivers aren't available for failed fuel-system or safety checks.

There's no grace period. Your registration expires at midnight on the date shown on the sticker, which goes on the lower right corner of the rear plate. Keep the registration card and proof of insurance in the car whenever you drive it.
