Where Kentucky vehicles are titled and registered

Every motor vehicle driven on Kentucky roads must be titled and registered. Kentucky residents apply at the county clerk's office in their county of residence, or in the county where the vehicle is mainly operated, according to the Transportation Cabinet's titling page. A nonresident registers in the county where the vehicle is principally operated. Don't use correction fluid, cross out or erase anything on a title. If information needs to change, attach a signed, notarized affidavit.

Buying from another Kentuckian

When a vehicle is sold from one Kentucky resident or business to another, the title must be transferred to the new owner within 15 days. For an in-state vehicle, the buyer brings proof of Kentucky insurance issued within the last 45 days, a Kentucky driver's license, ID card or Social Security card, and the signed-over Kentucky title with notarized signatures. If the name on the title doesn't match your ID, bring an affidavit and supporting proof, such as a marriage license or divorce decree.

Bringing a vehicle from another state

Under KRS 186.020, anyone who brings a motor vehicle into Kentucky must apply for registration within 15 days in their county of residence. For an out-of-state vehicle, the clerk needs:

  • Proof of Kentucky insurance issued within the last 45 days
  • A Kentucky driver's license, ID card or Social Security card
  • A completed form TC 96-182 and the signed-over title, or the registration or bill of sale if your former state issues only those, with notarized signatures
  • Proof that the vehicle passed a sheriff's inspection

If a lender holds your title, the clerk may request it from the lien holder. Logan County's clerk lists a $5 sheriff inspection fee, but fees are set locally, so check with your county.

The Transportation Cabinet's insurance page lists some exceptions. Students with a current ID from a Kentucky college, university or technical college may keep their home-state registration and insurance under KRS 186.430. Active-duty service members may use out-of-state insurance to title and register a vehicle in their name.

The 6% usage tax

Kentucky charges a motor vehicle usage tax of 6%, collected by the county clerk when ownership changes or a vehicle is registered in Kentucky for the first time. The Department of Revenue explains how it's calculated:

  • New vehicles: the selling price shown on a notarized Affidavit of Total Consideration (Form 71A100) or form TC 96-182, with a trade-in allowance since July 1, 2014. Without an affidavit, the tax is based on 90% of the MSRP, including equipment and transportation charges.
  • Used vehicles: the affidavit price, as long as it's at least 50% of the difference between the trade-in values of the vehicle being registered and any vehicle traded in. Without an affidavit, the NADA retail value is used.
  • New residents: a credit for tax paid to your former state, if that state levies a substantially identical tax and grants similar credit for Kentucky tax. Proof can be your prior registration or title, or a purchase contract that shows the tax paid. Finance contracts aren't accepted.

Logan County's clerk notes that the taxable value for a new resident's vehicle is the average NADA trade-in value. A false statement of the price paid is a Class D felony.

Fees

Under KRS 186.050, the state registration fee is $11.50 a year for cars, pickups and passenger vans and $9 for a motorcycle. Logan County's clerk lists $21 for a yearly renewal, a $9 title fee and a $2 notary fee. A duplicate title costs $6 plus notary costs, and a speed title, mailed the next business day, is $25. A personalized plate costs $25 more than the regular registration, and moving one to another vehicle costs $11. Plug-in vehicles also pay the electric vehicle ownership fee under KRS 138.475. Bring money for fees and any applicable taxes when you register. Online renewal isn't available if you owe property tax on another vehicle.

Renewing and reinstating

You can renew at your county clerk's office with a Kentucky photo ID, your registration certificate, and original proof of Kentucky insurance dated within 45 days. Online renewal is available for many plates if your registration expired less than a year ago and your vehicle has been insured with the same company for at least 45 days. Leased vehicles can't be renewed online. Online renewals take five to seven working days and include a service fee plus $2 per vehicle for mailing. Your Kentucky plate stays with you when you sell or transfer a vehicle.

The state checks insurance records against registrations each month, and owners without coverage get notices that their registrations will be canceled. Since 2024, KRS 186.040 has required a $40 fee to reinstate a canceled registration. If you're taking a seasonal vehicle off the road, turn in the plate to the clerk before you cancel the insurance.
