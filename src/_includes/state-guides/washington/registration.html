Who has to register

RCW 46.16A.030 makes it unlawful to drive a vehicle on a Washington public highway without a current registration and license plates. The Department of Licensing (DOL) won't issue plates or a registration certificate until you've applied for a certificate of title or shown that one was already issued.

Under RCW 46.16A.140, a resident for registration purposes is someone who shows an intent to live or be located in Washington on more than a temporary basis. Registering to vote, receiving state public assistance, or declaring residency for a state license or resident tuition are all evidence of residency. You're presumed a resident if at least two of these apply:

  • You maintain a residence in Washington for personal use.
  • You hold a Washington driver's license or resident hunting or fishing license.
  • You use a Washington address for federal income tax or state tax purposes.
  • You previously kept a Washington residence and haven't set up a permanent one elsewhere, such as a retiree living in a motor home.
  • You claim Washington residency to hold public office or for judicial actions.
  • You're a custodial parent with a child in a Washington public school.

New residents have 30 days from becoming residents to get Washington registration, and that period can't be combined with any other reciprocity period.

Basic fees

DOL says every renewal starts with a basic fee of $43.25, and vehicle weight, location and taxes determine the final amount. The statutes behind that figure are:

  • A $30 vehicle license fee for a passenger car, sport utility vehicle, motorcycle or motor home under RCW 46.17.350
  • A $6 filing fee for registration under RCW 46.17.005, or $6.50 for a title
  • An $11 service fee for a registration renewal under RCW 46.17.040, or $18 for title changes
  • A 75-cent license service fee under RCW 46.17.025

DOL also applies a $10 additional weight fee to all vehicles subject to motor vehicle weight fees, including passenger vehicles, and to trucks with a declared gross weight of 12,000 pounds or less. Trucks registered by gross weight pay the RCW 46.17.355 schedule in place of the $30 fee. That schedule starts at $53 for 4,000 pounds and rises 2 percent each year starting July 1, 2026. Vehicles over 10,000 pounds also pay a freight project fee of 15 percent of the license fee.

Title fees

DOL lists these title fees:

  • $15 for an application for a certificate of title, or $40 for a dealership sale
  • $6.50 title filing fee and $18 title service fee, or $12.50 and $29 for title with registration
  • $50 for a quick title, plus titling and service fees
  • $39.50 to replace a lost title
  • A late transfer fee of $50 on the 16th day, then $2 a day, up to $125

RCW 46.12.555 defines a quick title as a certificate printed at the time of application. It can't be used for the first title on a vehicle previously designated salvage.

License plates

RCW 46.17.200 sets the plate fees DOL charges:

  • $50 per plate for an original issue, or $20 for a motorcycle
  • A $2 reflectivity fee per plate
  • $30 per plate for a replacement
  • $20 to keep your current plate number when the plates are replaced
  • $10 to move standard plates from one vehicle to another

Replacing lost tabs costs $18.50.

Regional Transit Authority tax

If you live in the Sound Transit District in King, Pierce or Snohomish County, you pay the Regional Transit Authority (RTA) tax when you buy a vehicle or renew tabs. DOL lists the current voter-approved rate as 1.1 percent. It's a motor vehicle excise tax on the depreciated value, figured from the manufacturer's suggested retail price and a state depreciation schedule. A vehicle in its first year of service is taxed on 100 percent of MSRP, 74 percent in year five, and 10 percent from year 13 on. Transportation benefit district fees vary by district and may show as "TBD" at renewal.

VIN inspections

Under RCW 46.12.560, you need a Washington State Patrol or other authorized inspection before titling a vehicle that was declared a total loss or salvage, or a rebuilt vehicle in some cases. The same applies to assembled, glider kit, homemade, kit, street rod and custom vehicles titled for the first time. You bring original parts invoices, and the patrol marks the door latch pillar of a rebuilt salvage vehicle. A vehicle with an altered or missing VIN gets a new number assigned by the patrol. DOL lists the inspection fee as $65. Out-of-state vehicles pay a $50 out-of-state service fee, which DOL says is used to check whether the vehicle is stolen.

Penalties

Driving before making the initial registration is a traffic infraction with a $529 fine that can't be suspended or reduced, plus delinquent taxes and fees. Driving with an expired registration is also an infraction, and a car parked on a public street with a registration expired for more than 45 days may be impounded.

If you're a resident and register a vehicle in another state to evade Washington taxes or fees, that's a gross misdemeanor. A first offense carries up to 364 days in jail, the $529 fine, an extra $1,000 fine and the delinquent taxes and fees. For a second or later offense, the extra fine is $5,000.
