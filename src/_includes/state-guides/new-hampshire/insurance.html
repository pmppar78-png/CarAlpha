Insurance is optional, with conditions

New Hampshire's approach to auto insurance runs through the financial responsibility law in RSA chapter 264, which requires security and proof of insurance after certain crashes and convictions. Drivers without a policy remain personally responsible for the damage they cause, and the consequences after an uninsured crash can include losing your license, registration and plates until you pay or post security.

The 25/50/25 benchmark

RSA 264:20 defines proof of financial responsibility as the ability to pay damages from crashes in New Hampshire in these amounts:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury or death of one person
  • $50,000 for bodily injury or death of two or more people in one accident
  • $25,000 for injury to or destruction of property in one accident

Proof is required at those amounts for each vehicle registered in the person's name. Under RSA 264:21, it can be shown with an insurer's or surety's certificate filed with the department, or with a deposit of money or securities under RSA 264:22.

What a New Hampshire policy must include

If you do buy a liability policy on a vehicle registered or principally garaged in the state, RSA chapter 264 sets several required coverages.

  • Uninsured motorist coverage. RSA 264:15 requires uninsured and hit-and-run coverage at least equal to the bodily injury minimums. If you buy liability limits above the minimum, your uninsured motorist limits automatically match them. Umbrella and excess policies have to carry matching uninsured motorist limits unless the named insured rejects that in writing.
  • Insolvent insurers. If the at-fault driver's insurer becomes insolvent and no other insurance applies, uninsured motorist coverage also has to pay at least $25,000 for property damage.
  • Medical payments. RSA 264:16 requires at least $1,000 per person in medical payments coverage for the driver and passengers of the insured vehicle, for reasonable costs incurred within 3 years of the injury. You can claim under it, your health insurance, or both, but you can't be paid twice for the same expense. Commercial policies covering more than 4 vehicles are excluded.
  • Release warning. Any document that settles a bodily injury claim covered by uninsured motorist insurance has to carry a printed warning that signing may forfeit those benefits. The releasing party reads and signs it.

After a crash

RSA 264:25 requires any driver involved in an accident causing death, injury or property damage to stop and give their name, address, license number, insurance information if any, registration number and the names and addresses of the occupants. Failing to stop or report is a misdemeanor for property damage. It becomes a class B felony when someone is killed or injured, or when the information given is knowingly false.

Under RSA 264:3, after a reported crash the director suspends the license, registration and plates of the driver and owner involved. They stay suspended until the person posts security to satisfy any judgment from the crash and then keeps proof of financial responsibility. Notice goes out at least 10 days before the suspension starts. The suspension doesn't apply if a liability policy or bond was in effect for the vehicle or driver at the time of the accident, or if the director finds the liability is covered another way. RSA 264:6 lists further exceptions, including when the director is satisfied that neither person caused or contributed to the crash.

RSA 264:7 keeps the suspension in place until the person gets a release, wins the lawsuit or pays any judgment, and then maintains proof of financial responsibility. Under RSA 264:9, a court order allowing the judgment to be paid in installments restores the license and registration as long as no installment is in default. RSA 264:24-b blocks transferring a suspended registration to someone else's name unless the director is satisfied the transfer is in good faith.

Security deposited under RSA 264:5 can be used only for a judgment in a suit filed within 2 years of the accident. After that, it's returned unless the department has been told a suit was filed.

Convictions that trigger proof

RSA 264:2 lets the director suspend a license and registrations, and take the plates, until the driver files proof of financial responsibility after a conviction for:

  • Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
  • Leaving the scene of an accident without stopping and reporting.
  • Homicide or assault arising out of driving.
  • A second excessive-speed or second reckless-driving conviction, or other violations the director names.

Convictions in another state count too.

Premiums and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 Auto Insurance Database supplement lists New Hampshire's average combined premium for liability, collision and comprehensive at $1,103.33, against $879.28 in 2020. The average expenditure was $1,073.18. In the Insurance Research Council's estimate, 10.0% of New Hampshire drivers were uninsured, ranking 40th. Because RSA 264 ties the proof requirement to crashes and convictions, uninsured motorist coverage on your own policy is the part that pays when an uninsured driver injures you.
