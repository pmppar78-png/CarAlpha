Required coverage

North Dakota requires two kinds of protection on a registered vehicle: liability insurance and no-fault benefits. Under N.D.C.C. § 26.1-41-02, the owner of a vehicle required to be registered in the state has to keep security in place continuously for basic no-fault benefits and for liability. The liability minimums come from § 39-16.1-11:

  • $25,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in an accident
  • $50,000 for bodily injury to or death of two or more people in an accident, and
  • $25,000 for damage to the property of others in an accident.

That's written as 25/50/25. The policy must come from an insurer authorized to do business in North Dakota, or the owner can self-insure with the commissioner's approval. A vehicle registered in another state may be covered by an insurer authorized in either state.

No-fault benefits

North Dakota is a no-fault state. Section 26.1-41-01 caps basic no-fault benefits at $30,000 per person per accident for economic loss, regardless of the number of insurers involved. Economic loss covers:

  • Medical and rehabilitation expenses
  • Work loss and replacement services
  • Survivors' income loss and replacement services, and
  • Funeral, cremation and burial costs.

Within that cap, work loss and survivors' income loss are limited to $150 a week per person, and funeral, cremation and burial expenses to $3,500. Medical charges are paid at usual and customary rates, which exclude private-room costs beyond semiprivate rates unless intensive care is needed, nonprescription drugs, and experimental or medically unproven treatment.

Insurers also have to offer optional excess no-fault benefits under § 26.1-41-04. That coverage begins when the basic benefits run out and goes up to a total of $80,000 per person per accident. It can cover you or a relative injured in a vehicle you don't own or as a pedestrian.

When you can sue

Under § 26.1-41-08, a secured person who causes a crash is exempt from paying damages for noneconomic loss, such as pain and suffering, unless the injury is a "serious injury." They're also exempt from paying economic loss to the extent no-fault benefits cover it. Section 26.1-41-01 defines a serious injury as one that results in:

  • Death or dismemberment
  • Serious and permanent disfigurement
  • Disability beyond 60 days, or
  • Medical expenses over $2,500.

Disability here means being unable to do substantially all of your usual daily activities. If you receive medical services free or at a discount, a court can still find a serious injury if their usual and customary value tops $2,500.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Section 26.1-40-15.2 requires uninsured motorist coverage, at the same 25/50 limits as liability, in every policy on a vehicle registered and principally garaged in North Dakota. It pays the damages you're legally entitled to collect for bodily injury or death caused by an uninsured driver. On request, insurers must offer higher limits up to the lesser of your liability limits or $100,000 per person and $300,000 per accident. Under § 26.1-40-15.3, insurers must also provide underinsured motorist coverage at the same limits as the uninsured coverage.

Proof and penalties

Section 39-08-20 bars anyone from driving, and any owner from knowingly letting someone drive, a vehicle in North Dakota without a valid liability policy. If an officer stops you, you have to show written or electronic proof of insurance. A charge can be cleared if you later give the prosecutor's office proof that a valid policy was in force at the time. Coverage bought after the stop doesn't count.

A violation brings these added requirements:

  • You must keep a certificate of insurance on file with the Department of Transportation for one year, or your driving privileges are suspended.
  • You must surrender your license and buy a $50 duplicate noting the requirement. Removing the notation costs another $50.
  • On a second or later violation, the court impounds the plates of the vehicle you owned and drove until you provide proof of insurance and pay a $20 fee. Failing to turn the plates in is a class B misdemeanor.

If an uninsured driver damages another person or their property, § 39-08-20.1 requires the court at minimum to order the driver to pay the victim's insurance deductible. Under § 26.1-41-02, an owner who knowingly drives or allows driving without security can have the registration revoked or suspended until security is provided. An owner without security at the time of a crash is personally liable for the no-fault benefits.

Suspending coverage on a stored vehicle

If you tell your insurer that a vehicle won't be driven on public roads for at least 30 consecutive days, § 26.1-41-03 requires the insurer to suspend the coverage pro rata, or give a similar credit, until you ask to reinstate it. You don't have to turn in the plates. If the vehicle is driven during the suspension, the security requirements apply again. This option isn't available if you're required to file proof of financial responsibility.

Costs and uninsured drivers

The NAIC's 2024 combined average premium for liability, collision and comprehensive coverage in North Dakota was $1,077.51. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 10.6% of North Dakota drivers were uninsured, ranking the state 37th.
