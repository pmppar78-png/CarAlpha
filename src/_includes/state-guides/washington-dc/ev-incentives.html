No purchase rebate, but lower fees

The District doesn't offer a cash rebate or tax credit for buying an electric car. Its incentives come through the fees you pay at the DMV, a charger tax credit, and building rules. The Department of Energy and Environment (DOEE) keeps the official list.

Registration discount

A new battery-electric passenger car under 5,000 pounds pays a $40 registration fee for its first two years (D.C. Code § 50-1501.03). After that it moves into the regular weight classes, but it can subtract 1,000 pounds from its shipping weight when its class is set. For a 4,300-pound EV, that means it's treated as 3,300 pounds and pays the $70 lightest-class fee instead of $175. Plug-in hybrids and hybrids don't get either break.

Excise tax: no longer exempt

Fully electric vehicles were exempt from the District's one-time titling excise tax until February 17, 2025. They now pay the lowest rates on the schedule: 1% of fair market value under 3,500 pounds, 2% from 3,500 to 4,999 pounds, and 3% at 5,000 pounds or more. A comparable gas car can pay up to 11%. Hybrids and other cars rated at 40 mpg city or better also pay reduced rates of 1.5% to 3.5%. DOEE notes that the reduced excise rates apply only to newly registered vehicles, not leases.

A permanent inspection sticker

A fully electric passenger car gets an inspection sticker that never expires, because it has no tailpipe emissions to test. If a dealer handles your registration, the sticker comes in the registration package. If you're bringing an EV from another state, take it to the inspection station at 1001 Half Street SW once to get the sticker.

Home charger tax credit

The Alternative Fuel Infrastructure Credit covers 50% of the cost to buy and install a charger at your home, up to $1,000 per charging station, against District income tax. Under § 47-1806.12, the credit can't be refunded and can't be more than the tax you owe that year, but unused credit carries forward for two years. Land and building costs don't count. Claim it with Form D-40, the Office of Tax and Revenue's AFV credit schedule, your paid invoices, and the electrical permit. The Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Support Act of 2026 extended the credit through tax years ending December 31, 2036, and DOEE lists that end date. The online D.C. Code text hasn't been updated yet and still shows 2026. DOEE lists December 31, 2026 as the end of a separate credit for converting a vehicle to run on an alternative fuel (50%, up to $19,000).

Right to charge and curbside charging

  • Condominiums can't ban or restrict chargers in an owner's assigned parking space (§ 6-1451.03c). They can set reasonable rules, as long as the rules don't significantly increase the cost. The owner hires a licensed installer and pays for installation, power, upkeep, and any damage. DOEE describes the 2024 law as giving all multifamily residents a right to install charging, subject to safety rules and building-owner approval procedures.
  • No driveway? Residents who own or lease an EV and have no off-street parking can charge at the curb without a public space permit. They must follow DDOT's guidance for running a cord across the sidewalk, or the equipment can be confiscated.
  • Multifamily rebates. DOEE's Multifamily EV Charging Assistance Program, which opened September 16 and runs through September 2027, covers up to 80% of installation costs for chargers at apartment and condo buildings and includes free technical help.
  • Time-of-use rate. Pepco offers a whole-house time-of-use rate to DC residents who have a charger and a District-registered vehicle that can go at least 30 miles on electricity alone. Off-peak power costs less, and peak power costs slightly more.

New buildings

New or substantially renovated single-family homes with off-street parking must include at least one EV-ready space. Starting January 1, 2027, new or substantially improved buildings with six or more parking spaces must meet higher requirements. Commercial buildings need chargers installed at 15% of spaces and EV-ready wiring at 25%. Multifamily buildings need 25% EV-ready, with stricter rules phasing in from 2031 and 2034. DOEE notes that full enforcement of the 2024 law depends on funding in an approved budget.
