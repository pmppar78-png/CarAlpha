The 10-day rule for newcomers

Alaska's deadline is short. The Division of Motor Vehicles says that if you're working in Alaska or establishing residency, you have to register your vehicle within 10 days of entering the state or starting work. Visitors who aren't residents can drive on a valid out-of-state registration for up to 60 days.

You can register at any DMV office or partner office, or by mail. You'll need a completed Vehicle Transaction Application (Form V1), signed in ink, your title, and the fees. If you buy from an Alaska dealer, the dealer usually handles the paperwork. If there's no lienholder, the title has to be transferred into your name as part of registering, and a vehicle coming from outside Alaska gets an Alaska title.

Titling rules worth knowing

Alaska law gives you 30 days from purchase to apply for a title in your name, according to the DMV's new vehicle page. The seller has to release the title and assign it to you, and fill in the odometer reading for any vehicle under 12,000 pounds and less than 20 years old. If that reading is left blank, the DMV may record the odometer as "unknown," which hurts resale value.

If a lender outside Alaska holds the title to a car that's already in your name, and the car is physically in Alaska, the DMV issues a registration only. In that case the $15 title fee and $15 lien fee are waived. If you want an Alaska title, ask the lender to mail the title to the DMV with a title-state-change letter, a V1, and the fees. The DMV won't request the title for you. A vehicle manufactured after 1981 also has to carry the federal safety-standards label, or come with a letter from the manufacturer, before Alaska will title it (2 AAC 92.020). That matters mostly for gray-market imports.

State fees

The DMV's fee and tax chart lists these state fees for a non-commercial vehicle:

  • Registration: $100 for two years. Registration in Alaska is biennial for passenger cars, motorhomes, pickups, and cargo vans. Motorcycles are $60 and trailers $30.
  • Title: $15.
  • Lien recording: $15 more if the vehicle is financed.
  • In-person renewal: a $10 convenience fee if you renew at a DMV counter instead of online or by mail.
  • Duplicate plate or tab: $5. Duplicate paper registration: $2.

Most vehicles keep the same expiration month even after a sale. If a registration has been expired for less than a year, you pay the full two-year fee from the original expiration month, even if you just bought the car. If it's been expired longer than a year, the two-year period starts over.

Motor vehicle registration tax (MVRT)

Alaska has no state sales tax, and the state doesn't charge sales tax on a vehicle purchase. Some boroughs and cities charge their own sales tax, so ask before you buy. Many local governments also collect a motor vehicle registration tax through the DMV at each renewal. It's based on your residence address and the vehicle's model year. The tax drops as the car ages. A few examples from the DMV's chart for a non-commercial passenger car, for each two-year registration:

  • Anchorage and the Mat-Su Borough: $150 for the newest model years, stepping down to $70 for a vehicle eight or more years old.
  • Kenai Peninsula Borough: $140 for the newest model years, down to $30.
  • Juneau: a flat $70 regardless of age.
  • Kodiak: $300 for the newest model years, down to $140.

If your address isn't in a listed taxable location, you pay no MVRT. The DMV's online cost estimator adds up the registration fee and tax for a specific address and vehicle.

Inspection and emissions

Alaska doesn't require a safety inspection. It no longer requires emissions testing anywhere, either. According to the Department of Environmental Conservation, the Fairbanks inspection and maintenance program ended January 1, 2010, and Anchorage's ended March 1, 2012. You may still see old "I/M certificate" references in older guides. They no longer apply.

Insurance at registration

Alaska's minimum liability coverage is 50/100/25. The DMV can suspend your license if you don't keep it. Our Alaska insurance page covers the details.
