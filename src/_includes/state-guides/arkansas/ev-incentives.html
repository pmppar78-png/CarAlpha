State purchase incentives

Arkansas has no state rebate, tax credit, or sales-tax break for buying or leasing an electric vehicle right now. You'll pay the same 6.5 percent state sales tax on an EV as on a gas car, collected when you register it. That could change. In December 2022, the governor's Arkansas Council on Future Mobility (created by Executive Order 22-06) published recommendations that included considering tax credits to grow the state's DC fast-charging network. It also recommended raising the EV registration fee. None of those ideas has turned into a buyer incentive, so be wary of any site that lists an "Arkansas EV credit."

Annual EV and hybrid fees

What Arkansas does have is an extra yearly fee, added on top of normal registration under Ark. Code § 27-14-614:

  • Battery-electric vehicles: $200 a year
  • Plug-in hybrids: $100 a year
  • Conventional hybrids: $50 a year

The fee started in 2019 to make up for the fuel tax that EV drivers don't pay at the pump. The money first went only to the State Highway and Transportation Department Fund. Since July 1, 2024, it's also shared with the County Aid Fund and the Municipal Aid Fund, so some of it now pays for local roads. Vehicles with a military service or veterans special plate and permanent decal under § 27-24-201 are exempt. The base registration itself is still the low weight-based fee described on our Arkansas registration page. Most electric cars weigh more than comparable gas models, so check which bracket yours falls in before you budget.

Charging rules in Arkansas

Two state decisions shape who can build and sell charging here. First, Arkansas law says a business that sells EV charging isn't a public utility just for doing so (Ark. Code § 23-1-101(9)). Hotels, stores, and independent networks can charge drivers for electricity without being regulated like a power company. Second, the Arkansas Public Service Commission has allowed the state's investor-owned electric utilities to own and operate DC fast chargers (Docket 22-026-TF, Order No. 8). That means your utility may also be one of the companies installing public chargers.

Highway fast charging is being planned by the Arkansas Department of Transportation under the federal NEVI formula program. ARDOT had to file a yearly EV Infrastructure Deployment Plan for fiscal years 2022 through 2026, covering where NEVI money would go and how stations would be secured.

Utility programs

Your utility is the main place to look for money toward a home charger:

  • SWEPCO (Arkansas customers): a $250 rebate for an ENERGY STAR certified, networked Level 2 charger. Applications are due within 90 days of installation and are paid first come, first served while funds last. Apply at SWEPCOar.clearesult.com or call 888-266-3130.
  • OG&E (western Arkansas): no charger rebate, but its SmartHours plans, including an EV-specific SmartHours Overnight rate, price power at nearly half price overnight and all weekend. That fits home charging well.
  • Entergy Arkansas and electric co-ops: check your own utility directly. We couldn't confirm a current charger rebate amount on an official page, so we haven't listed one.
