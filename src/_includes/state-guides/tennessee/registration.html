Where you title and register

In Tennessee, titles and registrations are handled by the county clerk's office in the county where you live, acting for the Department of Revenue. The department notes that applications for registration are processed through your local county clerk.

For a new vehicle, the Department of Revenue says to bring the manufacturer's statement of origin and the new vehicle invoice. For a used vehicle, bring the certificate of title properly assigned to you, with an odometer disclosure statement if one applies. In either case, bring a copy of your current registration if you're moving your plate from another vehicle. You'll also need proof of identification and proof of residency.

Starting January 1, 2027, Tenn. Code Ann. § 55-4-144 adds a requirement for initial registrations of certain vehicles: documentation that you're a U.S. citizen, a lawful permanent resident, or someone whose presence in the country has been authorized by the federal government for a specific purpose and period. The Department of Revenue publishes a list of acceptable documents.

Moving to Tennessee

The Department of Revenue says a person who moves to Tennessee from another state should register within 30 days of the move, citing § 55-4-101(b)(2). It notes limited exceptions, such as students from other states attending school in Tennessee.

New residents apply for a Tennessee title and registration through the county clerk. Bring proof of identification and residency, your latest out-of-state registration, and either the name and address of the lienholder you're making payments to or, if there's no lien, your out-of-state title.

Registration fees

The Department of Revenue lists the standard plate fee for a passenger vehicle at $26.50 and for a motorcycle at $19.50. It also says to check with your county clerk for additional fees, since counties can add their own charges, including a wheel tax. A personalized standard passenger plate costs $35 on top of the regular registration fee, and the department offers more than 100 specialty plates.

Under § 55-6-104, the county clerk is entitled to these fees for its work:

  • $2.50 for issuing a registration and plates, transferring plates to another vehicle or accepting surrendered plates
  • $3 for a replacement registration certificate
  • $8.50 for receiving and forwarding each title application
  • $5.50 for a duplicate certificate of title
  • $1 for each transaction where the clerk collects sales tax

Title fees

Section 55-6-101 sets the state's charge for issuing a certificate of title at $5.50, in addition to the registration fee. The state charges $11 to note a new lien, or to transfer a lien to the lienholder's assignee when there's no change of ownership, and another $11 to note the extension of a mortgage on the title. A duplicate title to replace one that's lost or destroyed costs $5.50 in state fees. A new plate and number for a vehicle whose identification number has been removed or destroyed costs $10.

The county clerk's $8.50 fee for receiving and forwarding a title application rose from $5.50 on July 1, 2022, under Public Chapter 1003, according to Department of Revenue Notice 22-11.

Sales tax on vehicle purchases

The Department of Revenue says buyers of new and used vehicles pay state sales tax at 7% and the state single-article tax at 2.75%. Each county may also have a wheel tax and a local tax. The Wilson County Clerk explains that when you buy from a Tennessee dealer, you pay the sales tax to the dealer, and otherwise you pay it to the county clerk's office.

The three layers work like this, according to the department's single-article guidance:

  • The 7% state tax applies to the full sales price.
  • The local option tax, which can't exceed 2.75%, applies to the first $1,600 of the price in 93 of the 95 counties. Hamblen County uses a $300 base (Morristown stays at $1,600) and Hancock County uses $375.
  • The 2.75% state single-article tax applies to the part of the price from $1,600.01 through $3,200, so it never exceeds $44 per vehicle.

Parts and accessories installed at the factory, or by the dealer before or at the time of sale, count as part of the vehicle for these limits.

Electric and hybrid vehicle fees

Section 55-4-116 adds a fee for all-electric, hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles. It's charged when the registration is renewed, on top of the standard fees, and is no longer collected at initial registration. The schedule is:

  • all-electric vehicles: $200 for renewals from January 1, 2024 through 2026, then $274 in 2027, with an annual inflation adjustment from January 1, 2028
  • hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles: $100 for renewals from January 1, 2024 through 2027, with an annual inflation adjustment from January 1, 2028

The county clerk guide says low-speed and medium-speed electric vehicles, such as golf carts, and electric motorcycles don't pay the fee. Starting July 1, 2026, Public Chapter 969 exempts active-duty service members stationed outside Tennessee from the all-electric fee.

Renewals

You can renew online at tncountyclerk.com by choosing your county, by mailing payment and your renewal notice to the county clerk, or in person at the clerk's office with your renewal notice or current registration. Renewing online also lets you opt out of postcard reminders and get email or text reminders instead.

Keep your insurance current at renewal time. Under the Drive Insured TN program, the Department of Revenue can suspend a registration for a vehicle that shows up as uninsured.

Emissions testing

Tennessee's vehicle emissions testing has ended. EPA approved removing the inspection and maintenance program for the Middle Tennessee and Hamilton County areas from the state plan in August 2021, and Nashville's Metropolitan Council voted to end Davidson County's program. Metro Public Health says that since February 5, 2022, residents titling, registering or renewing a vehicle don't need an emissions test.
