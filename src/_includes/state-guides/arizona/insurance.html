Arizona's minimum coverage

Arizona is an at-fault state. The driver who causes a crash is responsible for the damage, and there's no no-fault system or personal injury protection requirement. The minimum limits are in A.R.S. § 28-4009. They went up for every policy issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2020:

25/50/15: $25,000 bodily injury or death per person, $50,000 for two or more people in one accident, and $15,000 property damage.

Before July 2020, the minimums were 15/30/10. Older guides still list those numbers. The lower figures now apply only to drivers who hold a certificate of self-insurance or partial self-insurance. Arizona's $15,000 property-damage minimum is low by national standards. Many new cars cost more than twice that to replace, and anything above your limit comes out of your own pocket.

The policy can be from one or more insurers, as long as the combined coverage meets the limits. A binder issued while the policy is pending counts as coverage (§ 28-4009(E)–(F)). Your liability coverage applies anywhere in the United States and Canada. A personal auto policy generally doesn't cover you while you're logged in to a ride-hailing app unless the policy expressly says so (§ 28-4009(C)(4)(e)). Rideshare drivers need a separate endorsement or the company's coverage.

Proof in the car

Under A.R.S. § 28-4135(B), you have to keep evidence of current coverage in the vehicle. Showing it on your phone counts, and showing your phone to an officer doesn't give consent to search anything else on it. If you're cited for not having proof but actually had coverage, the citation is dismissed when you show the court that the car, or you as a driver, was insured at the time of the stop (§ 28-4135(D)).

Penalties for driving uninsured

Driving uninsured in Arizona is a civil violation. The penalties climb steeply, though, and they look back over 36 months (§ 28-4135(E)):

  • First violation: a minimum civil penalty of $500, and a three-month suspension of your driving privileges. ADOT can issue a restricted license instead, but only after it confirms you're insured.
  • Second violation within 36 months: a minimum of $750, and a six-month suspension of your license and of the registration and plates of the vehicle.
  • Third or later within 36 months: a minimum of $1,000, and a one-year suspension of license, registration, and plates.

Reinstating a suspended license requires proof of insurance and a $10 fee. Reinstating a suspended registration and plates is $25 (§ 28-4141).

SR-22 in Arizona

After an insurance-law suspension, ADOT's Motor Vehicle Division requires an SR-22 certificate of future financial responsibility. It has to stay on file for three years, counted from the date you become eligible for reinstatement, according to ADOT's future financial responsibility page. A third violation within 36 months requires it by statute.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is optional in Arizona. Insurers do have to offer both in writing, on a form approved by DIFI, with limits up to your bodily-injury liability limits, and add them if you ask (A.R.S. § 20-259.01). Read that form before you sign it. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 10.6 percent of Arizona drivers were uninsured in 2023, which ranks the state 37th. UM coverage matters more in Arizona because of the low property-damage minimum. Even an insured at-fault driver may carry only $15,000.

How fault is shared after a crash

Arizona uses pure comparative fault (A.R.S. § 12-2505). If you were partly to blame, your damages are reduced by your percentage of fault, but you can still recover even if you were mostly at fault. A driver found 30 percent responsible for a $20,000 loss can recover $14,000 from the other driver's insurer. The deadline to sue for personal injury is generally two years (§ 12-542). Don't let a slow claim run past it.

Average premium

The NAIC's 2024 average combined premium for Arizona was $1,647.25. That's an average across all insured vehicles and coverages. A minimum-limits policy usually costs less.

Complaints

The Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions (DIFI) handles complaints against insurers and agents. Call its Consumer Services line at 602-364-2499, or write to 100 N. 15th Ave., Suite 261, Phoenix, AZ 85007-2630. Complaints can be filed through DIFI's filing a complaint page. For questions about a license suspension or an SR-22, contact ADOT MVD.
