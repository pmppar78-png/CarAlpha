When newcomers have to register

Arizona doesn't give new residents a grace period. ADOT's Motor Vehicle Division tells newcomers to register their vehicles as soon as they become residents. Under A.R.S. § 28-2153, a vehicle can be driven on its home-state plates only while its owner is a resident of that state. If you need more time to get paperwork together, MVD sells a 90-day resident permit for $15 (§ 28-2003).

You can register at an MVD office or an authorized third-party provider, and many renewals can be done online at AZ MVD Now. For an out-of-state vehicle, bring the out-of-state title (or the lender's information if the title is held by a lienholder), a signed Title and Registration Application (Form 96-0236), and proof of Arizona insurance. In the Phoenix and Tucson areas, you'll also need an emissions compliance form. ADOT lists the documents on its out-of-state vehicles page.

Fees

Arizona's flat fees are small. They're set in A.R.S. § 28-2003:

  • Title: $4.
  • Registration: $8 a year. Motorcycles are $9.
  • Air quality research fee: $1.50.
  • Duplicate registration card: $4.
  • Temporary general use registration: $15. It's useful if you buy a car and need to drive it before the paperwork is complete.
  • License plate: $5 for a standard plate.

Those flat fees are a small part of what you'll pay. The big number is the vehicle license tax.

Vehicle license tax (VLT)

Arizona has no annual property tax on cars. Instead, it charges a vehicle license tax with each registration, under the state constitution and A.R.S. § 28-5801. Here's how it works:

  • In the vehicle's first 12 months, its taxable value is 60 percent of the manufacturer's base retail price. The tax is $2.80 for each $100 of that value.
  • Each year after that, the value drops 16.25 percent from the year before, and the rate becomes $2.89 per $100.
  • The minimum is $10 a year.

For a car with a $40,000 base retail price, the first-year value is $24,000 and the VLT is $672. The next year the value falls to $20,100 and the tax to about $581. By year five, the value is under $12,000 and the tax is about $340. The money goes to highways, counties, and cities, and the bill breaks out each recipient's share. The base retail price is the manufacturer's figure, not what you paid, so a discount doesn't lower your VLT.

Sales and use tax

A purchase from an Arizona dealer is subject to the transaction privilege tax: the state rate of 5.6 percent plus the county and city rates where the dealer is located. ADOR's motor vehicle sales page covers how dealers report it. A car you buy from a private party isn't subject to TPT or use tax. Bring the bill of sale when you register.

Arizona residents who buy from a dealer in another state owe vehicle use tax when they register here, at 5.6 percent plus the city rate. The value of a trade-in and any manufacturer rebate are subtracted from the taxable amount. ADOR's vehicle use tax calculator works out the figure. A new resident who bought the car while living elsewhere and first used it outside Arizona generally owes no use tax on moving in (A.R.S. § 42-5159).

Emissions testing

Arizona tests emissions only in two areas, according to the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality:

  • Phoenix metro (Area A): testing every two years for most vehicles.
  • Tucson metro (Area B): testing every year.

The OBD test for newer vehicles costs $16.15 in both areas. The older tailpipe test costs $15.20 in Phoenix and $12.25 in Tucson. Vehicles that run only on electricity are exempt (A.R.S. §§ 49-541 and 49-542). Arizona has no periodic safety inspection for passenger cars.

Registration periods

Arizona lets most owners register for one or two years at a time, and some qualifying vehicles can register for five years. The VLT is still calculated year by year, so a longer registration doesn't lower the tax. It just puts several years of it on one bill. In the Phoenix and Tucson areas, MVD won't renew until the vehicle's emissions test is on file. Test in the window shown on your renewal notice.
