What Connecticut requires

25/50/25: $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 bodily injury per accident, and $25,000 property damage, plus uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage of at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident.

The Connecticut Insurance Department also accepts a $50,000 single limit covering both bodily injury and property damage instead of split limits. These minimums took effect on January 1, 2018, under Public Act 17-114. Before that, the limits were 20/40/10 and UM/UIM was 20/40. Your liability coverage pays for injuries and damage you cause to others, and it never pays for your own car or your own injuries.

Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage is mandatory here, unlike in many states where you can turn it down. It covers you, relatives in your household, and your passengers when you're hurt by an uninsured driver, a hit-and-run driver, or a driver whose limits are too low to pay your losses. The Insurance Department and the DMV both recommend carrying more than the minimum, since the minimum limits may not cover a serious crash.

Continuous coverage and DMV tracking

Connecticut requires liability insurance on every registered vehicle, all the time, until you cancel the registration or turn in the plates. Insurers have to tell the DMV when a policy is canceled. Here's what happens next:

  • If your coverage lapses for more than 14 days, the DMV mails you a warning or suspension notice and you owe a $200 fine.
  • If you don't respond by the date on the notice, your registration is suspended. So is your ability to register any vehicle or renew any registration in your name.
  • You can pay the fine online through the DMV's compliance tool, by phone at 860-263-5725, or by mailing a check with the signed consent agreement from the notice.
  • If you were actually insured the whole time, for example because you switched companies, send the DMV's Insurance Compliance Unit your new declaration page or a letter of experience from your insurer, and the fine can be dropped. To request a hearing, call before the suspension date.

Storing a car or leaving the state

Dropping insurance on a car you aren't driving doesn't stop DMV enforcement. You have to deal with the plates first. You can bring them to any DMV office and put them "on hold", then drop coverage. If the car will sit unused for a year or more, you can cancel the plates and ask for a refund. For a seasonal vehicle you want to keep plated, ask your insurer in writing for a "suspension of liability" and keep comprehensive coverage. You can't drive the car while liability is suspended. If you move away, cancel your Connecticut registration once you've registered in the new state and keep the receipt. This stops lapse reports and also stops local property tax from building up on the car.

Penalties for driving uninsured

According to the General Assembly's Office of Legislative Research summary of the 2017 act:

  • Driving without the required insurance carries a fine of $100 to $1,000 (C.G.S. 14-213b).
  • Failing to maintain insurance is a class C misdemeanor (C.G.S. 38a-371).
  • Not having proof of insurance in the car is an infraction with a $50 fine (C.G.S. 14-13).
  • Your registration and license can be suspended, and an uninsured car with a suspended registration can be impounded (C.G.S. 14-12g and 14-12h).
  • For a commercially registered vehicle, an owner who knowingly drives it, or lets someone else drive it, without insurance commits a class D felony.

Things the Insurance Department wants you to know

  • You choose your repair shop. Your insurer can't require you to use a particular body shop.
  • Household drivers. Insurers generally require every licensed person in your household who can use the car to be listed on the policy, unless that person shows they have their own car and insurance. Ask whether a named-driver exclusion is available if this raises your premium.
  • Delivery and rideshare work. Personal auto policies usually exclude business use, so a crash while delivering pizza may not be covered. Read the exclusions before you start gig work.
  • Anti-theft devices may lower your premium. The Department also suggests comparing quotes at every renewal.
  • You can get a free copy of your claims history to check what insurers see.

What Connecticut drivers pay

The NAIC's 2024 average premium supplement puts Connecticut's combined average premium (liability, collision, and comprehensive) at $1,690.07, about $77 above the national figure of $1,612.81. The average expenditure, which counts drivers who carry only liability, was $1,564.17. The Insurance Research Council estimated that 11.8% of Connecticut drivers were uninsured in 2023, ranking 29th and below the 15.4% national rate (Insurance Information Institute).
